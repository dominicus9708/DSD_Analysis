# LAW-001 Cross-Jurisdiction Revalidation Source Notes

Status: source packet for revalidation.

## 1. UN General Assembly — abstention is not an affirmative or negative vote

Official source: UN General Assembly Rules of Procedure, plenary voting rules.

Relevant structure:

- Rule 82: each member has one vote.
- Rules 83 and 85: decisions use members present and voting.
- Rule 86: members present and voting are those casting an affirmative or negative vote; members abstaining are considered not voting.
- Rule 87: roll-call answers are `yes`, `no`, or `abstention`.
- Rule 67 separately makes presence relevant to quorum.

Use:

`present != voting`;

`abstention != NO`;

`absence/presence may affect quorum without changing vote identity`.

URL: https://www.un.org/en/ga/about/ropga/plenary.shtml

## 2. EU Council — abstention can permit unanimity without becoming approval

Official source: TFEU Article 238(4), EUR-Lex.

Relevant structure:

`Abstentions by Members present in person or represented shall not prevent the adoption by the Council of acts which require unanimity.`

Use:

An abstention can have a downstream procedural relation different from both a negative vote and an affirmative vote.

`abstention permits adoption under source rule != abstention is YES`.

URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/treaty/tfeu_2012/art_238/oj/eng

## 3. Australian Electoral Commission — turnout and informal ballots are separate

Official sources: AEC 2025 federal-election participation page; AEC House count guidance; compulsory-voting backgrounder.

Relevant structure:

- turnout is measured as the proportion of enrolled Australians who cast a vote;
- informality is measured separately among ballot papers/votes cast;
- informal ballot papers are not counted toward candidates and are counted/set aside separately;
- failure to vote without a valid and sufficient reason is itself an offence under the Electoral Act.

Use:

`nonparticipation != informal/invalid ballot`;

`nonparticipation can have a legal consequence without being converted into a cast invalid ballot`.

URLs:
- https://www.aec.gov.au/election/fe25/participation-rates.htm
- https://www.aec.gov.au/Voting/counting/hor.htm
- https://www.aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm

## 4. UK Electoral Commission — rejected ballot presupposes a ballot-paper object

Official source: UK Electoral Commission guidance for UK Parliamentary general elections.

Relevant structure:

A ballot paper may be rejected if unmarked, missing the official mark, marked for more than one candidate, identifying the voter, or failing to indicate intention with certainty. Rejected ballot papers are recorded as such.

Use:

A submitted/issued ballot paper that reaches adjudication is a different institutional object from a person who never forms/submits a ballot in the modeled election path.

`nonexercise != rejected ballot`.

URL: https://www.electoralcommission.org.uk/guidance-candidates-and-agents-uk-parliamentary-general-elections-great-britain/verification-and-count/how-votes-will-be-counted/what-if-vote-a-ballot-paper-not-clear

## 5. UNIDROIT — silence/inactivity does not in itself equal acceptance, but source rules can change the consequence

Official source: UNIDROIT Principles Article 2.1.6.

Relevant structure:

- a statement or conduct indicating assent is acceptance;
- silence or inactivity does not in itself amount to acceptance;
- however, agreement, established practices, or usage may make silence/inactivity legally operative in context.

Use as direct counterpressure:

The universal claim cannot be `silence never has assent effect`.

The surviving form is:

`silence/nonaction does not itself carry the identity of assent; any assent consequence requires an additional source-supplied relation`.

URL: https://www.unidroit.org/instruments/commercial-contracts/unidroit-principles-2010/chapter-2-section-1/

## 6. ICC Rome Statute Article 66 — accusation, proof, and guilt are staged

Official source: Rome Statute Article 66.

Relevant structure:

- everyone is presumed innocent until proved guilty before the Court according to applicable law;
- the onus is on the Prosecutor to prove guilt;
- conviction requires guilt beyond reasonable doubt.

Use:

`charge/accusation != guilt`;

`prosecution burden unsatisfied -> no conviction under this rule`.

URL: https://asp.icc-cpi.int/sites/asp/files/asp_docs/Publications/Compendium/RomeStatute-ENG.pdf

## 7. ECHR — presumption of innocence plus bounded presumptions

Official sources: ECHR Article 6 case-law materials; Salabiaku v. France.

Relevant structure:

- Article 6(2) protects presumption of innocence;
- ECHR materials treat in dubio pro reo as an expression of that principle;
- presumptions of fact and law are not categorically forbidden, but must remain within reasonable limits and preserve defence rights.

Use as counterpressure:

`presumption of innocence != every evidentiary burden or presumption is forbidden`.

This narrows any claim that prosecution must carry every burden on every subsidiary issue.

URLs:
- https://ks.echr.coe.int/documents/d/echr-ks/presumption-of-innocence
- https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf?filename=SALABIAKU+v.+FRANCE.pdf&id=001-76797&library=ECHR

## 8. US Ninth Circuit — not-guilty gate and affirmative-defence counterpressure

Official source: United States Courts for the Ninth Circuit model criminal jury instructions.

Relevant structure:

- indictment is not evidence;
- defendant is presumed innocent unless and until government proves guilt beyond reasonable doubt;
- defendant does not have to prove innocence;
- if government does not satisfy the burden, the legal result is not guilty;
- model instructions separately recognize some affirmative defences on which a defendant bears a source-defined burden.

Use:

`failure to prove guilt -> not guilty verdict under source rule`;

but not:

`failure to prove guilt -> positive factual proof of innocence`.

The affirmative-defence provisions also reject an overbroad `all burdens always prosecution` claim.

URLs:
- https://www.ce9.uscourts.gov/jury-instructions/criminal/chapter-1/1-2-the-charge-presumption-of-innocence/
- https://www.ce9.uscourts.gov/jury-instructions/criminal/chapter-6/6-5-reasonable-doubt-defined/

## 9. UK CPS — acquittal/not guilty is proof-threshold failure, not a contrary victim finding

Official source: Crown Prosecution Service explanation of jury outcomes in rape cases.

Relevant structure:

A not-guilty verdict means the prosecution did not meet the standard required for conviction; it does not itself mean the complainant was disbelieved or found to have lied.

Use:

This supplies an explicit institutional witness against converting an acquittal into a positive opposite factual finding.

URL: https://www.cps.gov.uk/index.php/how-cps-charges-and-prosecutes-rape-cases

## 10. Revalidation source synthesis

Independent source families now support four distinct patterns:

1. abstention/nonparticipation and affirmative/negative voting are not the same institutional input;
2. nonparticipation and formed-but-invalid/informal ballot objects are distinct even when nonparticipation itself is sanctionable;
3. silence/nonaction may receive a source-defined consequence, but that consequence is not logically contained in the nonaction itself;
4. criminal accusation, proof threshold, guilty verdict, acquittal/not-guilty result, and factual innocence must not be collapsed, while source-defined presumptions/affirmative-defence burdens remain possible.
