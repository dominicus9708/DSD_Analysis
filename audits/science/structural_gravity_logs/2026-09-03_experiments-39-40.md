# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiments 39–40

## Experiment 39 — Common-scale degeneracy and bounded-transition identifiability

### Setup

Consider two models with the same bounded state, source geometry, density profile, initial/boundary state, and state trajectory, but with commonly rescaled constitutive data.

\[
\mathcal D_1[u]=0,
\qquad
\mathcal D_\lambda[u]=\lambda\mathcal D_1[u]=0,
\qquad \lambda>0.
\]

For a second-order specialization,

\[
\mathsf M_\lambda=\lambda\mathsf M_1,
\qquad
\mathsf K_\lambda=\lambda\mathsf K_1.
\]

Then the characteristic ratio \(\mathsf K/\mathsf M\) can remain unchanged even though the absolute coefficient amplitude differs.

### Homogeneous transition conditions

Let the transition condition be \(\Phi(P)=0\), where the constitutive data are collected in \(P\), and suppose

\[
\Phi(\lambda P)=\lambda^q\Phi(P).
\]

Then

\[
\Phi(P)=0
\iff
\Phi(\lambda P)=0.
\]

Therefore the transition location cannot identify \(\lambda\).

The same obstruction applies to:

- coefficient-ratio thresholds;
- dimensionless thresholds;
- homogeneous zero conditions;
- homogeneous conservation equalities;
- homogeneous jump/balance equalities such as \([J]=0\).

### Identifiability no-go candidate

Let all available state/transition observables satisfy

\[
O(\lambda P)=O(P),
\]

while a target normalization satisfies

\[
N(\lambda P)=\lambda^qN(P),
\qquad q\neq0.
\]

Then no function of the invariant observations can uniquely reconstruct \(N\).

Proof: \(P\) and \(\lambda P\) have identical \(O\) but different \(N\).

### Verdict

- common-scale homogeneous dynamics selects absolute normalization: **REJECTED**
- homogeneous bounded-transition zero condition breaks the degeneracy: **REJECTED**
- homogeneous conservation/jump rule breaks the degeneracy: **REJECTED**
- absolute normalization from scale-invariant state/transition observables: **STRUCTURALLY UNIDENTIFIABLE**

### Consequence

The missing normalization requires an actual scale-symmetry-breaking anchor, not merely an additional homogeneous transition rule.

## Experiment 40 — Necessary forms of a scale-breaking anchor

### Route A — fixed nonzero physical threshold

If

\[
Q(\lambda P)=\lambda^qQ(P)
\]

and the transition is selected by

\[
Q(P)=Q_*,
\qquad Q_*\neq0,
\]

with \(Q_*\) fixed under the common scaling, then

\[
\lambda^qQ(P)=Q_*
\]

can determine \(\lambda\).

However, a freely supplied \(Q_*\) merely renames the missing absolute constant. It is a valid anchor only if an independent structural law determines it.

### Route B — competition between sectors with unequal homogeneity degree

Suppose two independent sectors satisfy

\[
A(\lambda P)=\lambda^qA(P),
\qquad
B(\lambda P)=\lambda^rB(P),
\qquad q\neq r,
\]

and a transition obeys

\[
A+B=0.
\]

Then

\[
\lambda^{q-r}=-\frac{B(P)}{A(P)}
\]

may select the common scale.

This route is only noncircular when the amplitudes of both sectors are independently fixed rather than free constitutive coefficients.

### Dimensionless and discrete structures

Bounded-unit count, realized rank, channel count, normalized density-shape descriptors, and other dimensionless/discrete data cannot by themselves produce an \(M/L\) dimension.
They may index regimes or modify dimensionless response factors, but an additional dimensionful anchor remains necessary.

### Descriptive normalization

An analytic convention such as normalized weights or unit-sum aggregation may fix representation scale without fixing a physical mass-per-length normalization.
Descriptive normalization is therefore not counted as a physical anchor.

### Verdict

- independently derived fixed nonzero physical threshold: **CAN BREAK DEGENERACY**
- competition of independently fixed sectors with unequal scaling degree: **PRIMARY SURVIVING ROUTE**
- dimensionless/discrete structure alone: **INSUFFICIENT FOR M/L**
- descriptive normalization: **NOT A PHYSICAL ANCHOR**
