# LAW-005 Result — Procedural Formation, Validity, Invalidity, and Effect

Status: first-pass universal analysis complete.

Primary purpose: reinterpretation.

Secondary purposes: coherence, predefinition audit, regime-boundary analysis, universal-claim falsification.

## 1. Answer-first result

No direct contradiction was found between the selected transnational/international legal witness structures and the DSD Formation distinctions after legal-act identity, stage, defect type, governing consequence rule, downstream regime, and time were kept separate.

The strongest result is narrower and more general than the initial roadmap wording.

The universal candidate is **not**:

`candidate act -> formed act -> valid act -> invalid act`

as a mandatory sequence shared by every legal system.

The stronger surviving candidate is:

**when a source legal system distinguishes formation, present effect, defect, defeasibility/remedy, recognition, enforcement, or later status change, those distinctions must not be collapsed into a single `valid/invalid` value unless the source rule itself performs that collapse.**

A second generalization also survives:

**a defect does not determine its legal consequence without the rule that assigns that consequence.**

## 2. Witness-family results

### 2.1 CISG — formation is not validity

The CISG provides unusually clean boundary evidence.

Its architecture regulates formation while Article 4 generally excludes validity of the contract or its provisions from the Convention's subject matter.

Therefore the treaty itself does not permit:

`formation rules = validity rules`.

This does not tell domestic law what validity must mean. It proves only that a major uniform-law instrument can preserve the boundary as a matter of legal architecture.

Judgment: **strong witness for source-boundary preservation**.

### 2.2 UNIDROIT — conclusion, binding effect, avoidance, condition, and illegality consequences are not one bit

The UNIDROIT Principles provide several independent counterpressures.

First, Article 3.1.2 shows that a legal regime may make formation very simple: mere agreement can conclude a contract. This prevents DSD Analysis from inventing a mandatory multi-step formation ladder.

Second, Article 1.3 and its commentary distinguish a concluded agreement from the question whether it is validly entered into and binding.

Third, Chapter 3 Section 2 regulates grounds for avoidance of an already concluded contract, together with confirmation, loss of the right to avoid, notice, and time limits.

Fourth, Articles 5.3.1-5.3.2 allow effect to depend on a future uncertain condition.

Fifth, Article 3.3.1 rejects a universal automatic consequence for every mandatory-rule infringement. The consequence is first whatever the applicable mandatory rule prescribes and, if it is silent, a further remedial analysis is required.

Thus the same harmonized instrument independently requires separation among:

`conclusion`

`binding/valid status`

`present effect`

`defect`

`avoidance right`

`confirmation`

`rule-conditioned consequence`.

Judgment: **strong structural witness; also strong counterexample to a one-bit invalidity theory**.

### 2.3 UNCITRAL Model Law on Arbitration — existence, binding force, recognition, enforcement, and refusal are separate

Articles 35-36 distinguish an arbitral award, recognition as binding, enforcement, grounds for refusing recognition or enforcement, and cases where an award has been set aside or suspended.

Therefore:

`award exists`

is not identical to

`award is enforceable in every receiving regime`.

A refusal of recognition or enforcement does not retroactively mean that no award event occurred.

Judgment: **independent procedural witness for downstream-regime separation**.

## 3. Main universal result

The witness families do not support a universal legal ontology containing one status named `invalid`.

Instead, they support a more abstract rule-indexed structure:

`source act/status + defect/condition + governing rule + regime + time -> legal consequence package`.

The consequence package may contain, depending on the source system:

- nonformation;
- formed but not yet effective;
- formed and binding;
- avoidable or defeasible;
- confirmed and no longer avoidable;
- partially ineffective or severable;
- suspended;
- set aside;
- recognition refused;
- enforcement refused;
- prospectively terminated;
- another explicitly supplied source status.

The DSD Analysis conclusion is therefore not `law has the same seven stages as Formation`.

It is:

**do not replace a source-side status lattice or partial transition structure with a total binary value before the source rule has licensed that identification.**

## 4. DSD Formation comparison

Formation is useful because it already requires discipline concerning:

- staged structure;
- partial assignment;
- defined status versus absence;
- role-bearing channel identity;
- preservation of structure beyond a downstream aggregate/result.

A faithful application can therefore keep a legal act event present while leaving a later legal-effect coordinate undefined, negative, conditional, defeasible, or regime-specific.

However:

`legal formation != Formation admission`

`legal validity != Formation realization`

`legal invalidity != DSD undefinedness`

`legal nullity != channel absence`

unless an application proves a much stronger bridge.

Judgment: **structural correspondence after explicit typed application encoding**.

## 5. The most important negative result

The word `invalid` must not be used as a universal DSD value.

The selected witness systems use different legal mechanisms whose effects are not interchangeable. Even within UNIDROIT, a mandatory-rule infringement does not have one automatic output.

Therefore a DSD application that maps every legal defect to:

- undefined;
- zero;
- absent channel;
- failed realization;

would be structurally under-typed.

This is a stronger negative result than merely saying `void != voidable`.

The terminology itself varies across systems; what must be preserved is the **source consequence structure**.

## 6. Finite witness result

The finite four-state witness contains:

1. formed and ordinarily operative;
2. formed but conditionally not yet effective;
3. formed and operative but avoidable;
4. award exists but downstream enforcement is refused.

No binary `VALID/INVALID` assignment preserves all legally relevant distinctions.

The witness does not claim every system has all four states. It proves only that the selected source families contain enough independent statuses to falsify a universal one-bit encoding.

## 7. Temporal boundary

Later confirmation, avoidance, termination, suspension, or setting aside can change a legal status without deleting the historical act event.

Formation can represent static descriptors at different times, but it does not derive the transition.

Therefore:

`S(a,t1) != S(a,t2)`

may be source-law correct while the transition

`T_legal : S_t -> S_(t+1)`

remains external legal structure.

Judgment: **Dynamics not required for the first-pass distinction; transition modeling is a later layer**.

## 8. Axis-Property and Static Aggregation audit

Axis-Property is not required because no realized-axis semantics are supplied by the legal sources.

Static Aggregation is not required because validity, defect, avoidance, recognition, and enforcement are not numerical weights or additive contributions.

Both are excluded from the core result rather than added by analogy.

## 9. Relation to LAW-001 through LAW-004

LAW-001: nonaction or nonproof could not be silently converted into an opposite positive act/fact.

LAW-002: one descriptive regime's gap could not be filled by another regime's desired result.

LAW-003: one person or underlying relation did not automatically generate every role-specific authority or legal effect.

LAW-004: existence or submission of evidence did not automatically generate legal usability, probative sufficiency, or fact finding.

LAW-005 generalizes the common rule one level further:

**neither a source act nor a defect automatically determines the downstream legal status; the governing transition/consequence rule must be retained.**

This is a new independent node because the external witnesses concern formation/validity/remedial/effect architecture rather than voting, trial information asymmetry, representation, or evidence evaluation.

## 10. Predefinition result

The case rejects all of the following as universal predefinitions:

`formed -> valid forever`;

`defect -> void`;

`not currently effective -> nonexistent`;

`avoidable -> already null`;

`recognition refused -> no source act`;

`same defect label -> same consequence in every regime`.

The surviving cross-case rule is:

**preserve the source state, defect/condition, governing rule, regime, and time separately; promote or demote legal status only through the rule that actually performs that transition.**

## 11. Generalization status

- Universal candidate: **supported by several independent transnational/international witness families**.
- Universal proof: **not established**.
- Active counterexample search: **remains open**.
- Direct contradiction with Formation: **none found**.
- Formation mapping: **meaningful after typed encoding**.
- Axis-Property required: **no**.
- Static Aggregation required: **no**.
- Dynamics required: **not for first-pass static status analysis**.
- Main result class: **external coherence node + structural reinterpretation + predefinition audit**.

## 12. Boundary statement

LAW-005 does not prove a universal natural-law theory, does not derive validity doctrine from DSD, does not claim that every legal system uses the same formation sequence, and does not declare `void`, `voidable`, `unenforceable`, `avoided`, `set aside`, or `ineffective` to be synonyms.

It shows that the selected cross-system legal instruments independently preserve enough distinct formation/effect/defect/remedial/downstream statuses to defeat a one-bit legal-validity model, and that those distinctions can coexist with the Formation system under an explicit typed bridge.
