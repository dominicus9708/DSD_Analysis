# Contradiction Audit — ADMIN-001

## Strong hypotheses tested

### H1. More detailed instructions are always safer/effective

**Rejected.**

Army mission command intentionally combines clear purpose/intent with decentralized execution and limited method specification where initiative is necessary.

FAA/FEMA show that other regimes require much more explicit specification/verification for safety-critical or coordination-critical coordinates.

Therefore no universal scalar `instruction detail` determines quality.

### H2. Issued = received = understood = executed

**Rejected.**

FAA acknowledgement/readback/correction/clarification rules exist precisely because issuance does not guarantee shared understanding.

### H3. Acknowledgement proves understanding

**Rejected as universal.**

Acknowledgement is a distinct procedural state. Readback can still be incorrect/incomplete and require correction.

### H4. Recipient discretion is a defect

**Rejected.**

Mission command treats disciplined initiative within commander's intent as an intended control design under uncertainty.

### H5. Multiple directives merely add information

**Rejected as universal.**

FEMA unity-of-command doctrine explicitly treats conflicting directives/reporting relations as a source of confusion to be controlled.

### H6. Successful outcome proves the original instruction was adequate

**Rejected.**

Success may result from recipient competence, clarification, discretionary correction, favorable conditions, or later feedback/revision.

### H7. Failed outcome identifies subordinate execution as the cause

**Rejected.**

The failure may occur at intent formulation, directive encoding, authority/routing, receipt, interpretation, verification, coordination, discretion design, execution, environment, or feedback/revision.

## New risk exposed

A DSD analyst can commit a new form of overreach by treating `more formed / more specified` as `better institutional instruction`.

This is not supported.

`greater descriptive specification != universally better operational control`.
