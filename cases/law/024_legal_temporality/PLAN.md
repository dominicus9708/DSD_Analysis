# LAW-013 Plan — Legal Temporality, Commencement, Retroactivity, Repeal, and Residual Effects

Status: active first-pass analysis.

## Question

When legal rules are enacted, enter into force, are amended, suspended, repealed or terminated, does the present legal state overwrite the past, or do source systems preserve time-indexed rights, liabilities, acts, proceedings and residual effects through explicit temporal rules?

## Strong candidates to attack

- enactment/passage = immediate legal operation;
- current validity implies past applicability;
- repeal deletes previous operation and accrued states;
- termination releases past rights/obligations as well as future performance;
- non-retroactivity is universal across all legal fields in one identical form;
- procedural/substantive labels alone always determine retroactivity;
- a later milder rule and a later harsher rule have identical temporal treatment;
- legal temporal change can be modeled as simple state replacement.

## Witness families

- Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties Arts. 24, 25, 28 and 70;
- Canada Interpretation Act ss. 43-44;
- US Constitution Annotated on the Ex Post Facto Clauses;
- ECHR Article 7 guide on criminal-law non-retroactivity.

## Method

Analyze source temporal rules independently, falsify one global timeline, then compare with DSD Dynamics only after source-side results are fixed.
