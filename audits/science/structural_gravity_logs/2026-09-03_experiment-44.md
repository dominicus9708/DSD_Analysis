# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiment 44

Date: 2026-09-03

## Experiment 44 — Separation of one absolute coefficient from calibration-free structural predictions

### Objective

Leave the source-independent absolute normalization \(K_*\) unresolved and test which structural-gravity predictions remain independently comparable.

Use the conditional response form

\[
a_X(r;S)=K_*\frac{M_S}{r^2}\,[1+\delta_S(r)],
\]

where \(\delta_S(r)\) is a dimensionless structural-correction candidate associated with density profile, bounded structure, relation/property detail, or other retained source structure.

## 1. Same-source radial ratio

For one source at \(r_1,r_2\),

\[
\mathcal R_r
=
\frac{a(r_1)r_1^2}{a(r_2)r_2^2}
=
\frac{1+\delta(r_1)}{1+\delta(r_2)}.
\]

Both \(K_*\) and \(M\) cancel.
Thus radial evolution of structural corrections can be tested without absolute calibration, conditional on the coarse \(r^{-2}\) response form.

## 2. Same-mass, different-density sources

For the same-\(M\), same-\(R\) density controls developed in Experiments 28–31,

\[
\mathcal R_{AB}(r)
=
\frac{a_A(r)}{a_B(r)}
=
\frac{1+\delta_A(r)}{1+\delta_B(r)}.
\]

Therefore density-redistribution corrections can be compared without \(K_*\).
If far-field structural-information erasure holds,

\[
\mathcal R_{AB}(r)\to1.
\]

## 3. General mass/distance ratio

For different sources and radii,

\[
\mathcal R_{AB}
=
\frac{a_A(r_A)r_A^2M_B}
{a_B(r_B)r_B^2M_A}
=
\frac{1+\delta_A(r_A)}{1+\delta_B(r_B)}.
\]

The common normalization cancels, allowing relative tests of source additivity and structural corrections.

## 4. Source-removal finite-propagation timing

Amplitude normalization is not required to test the onset/front condition

\[
t_{\rm onset}(r)-t_0\gtrsim r/c_{\rm info}
\]

or the corresponding distinguishability-support bound.
Thus propagation timing remains a separate validation target even when the absolute response amplitude is uncalibrated.

## 5. Research-layer separation

Separate

\[
\boxed{\text{absolute normalization sector }K_*}
\]

from

\[
\boxed{\text{dimensionless structural-response sector }\delta_S(r)}.
\]

The scale-anchor no-go applies to the first sector.
Density-profile dependence, near/far structural erasure, relative source response, and propagation timing can be studied in the second sector without first recovering \(K_*\).

## Verdict

- unresolved absolute normalization implies all structural predictions are unresolved: **REJECTED**
- same-source radial ratios eliminate the absolute coefficient: **CONFIRMED ALGEBRAICALLY**
- same-mass density-profile ratios eliminate the absolute coefficient: **CONFIRMED ALGEBRAICALLY**
- finite-propagation timing requires amplitude calibration: **REJECTED**
- normalization sector and structural-correction sector can be separated: **CONFIRMED AS CURRENT RESEARCH ORGANIZATION**
