# LAW-014 Source Notes

## 1. UN General Assembly

UN Charter Article 18 gives each member one vote, but collective decisions depend on the issue category and the required majority. Important questions require a two-thirds majority of members present and voting; other questions use a majority of members present and voting.

General Assembly Rule 86 defines `members present and voting` as members casting affirmative or negative votes; abstaining members are considered not voting for this calculation.

Therefore:

`abstention != NO vote identity`;

`member present != member present and voting for threshold denominator`;

`individual vote != collective decision without the governing threshold rule`.

## 2. UN Security Council

Charter Article 27 gives each member one vote but distinguishes procedural and other decisions. Official Security Council guidance states that a negative vote by a permanent member can prevent adoption on non-procedural matters, whereas a permanent-member abstention does not act as a veto and can permit adoption if nine affirmative votes are obtained.

Therefore:

`same one-member one-vote count != same institutional effect for every vote relation`;

and:

`permanent-member abstention != permanent-member negative vote`.

The institutional result depends on source-defined role and voting rule, not raw vote count alone.

## 3. Council of the European Union

Qualified majority uses a double-majority rule: normally at least 55% of member states, representing at least 65% of the EU population, must vote in favour; blocking-minority and special-participation rules also apply.

Under qualified-majority voting an abstention counts against attainment/blocking calculations, but the Council explicitly states that abstention is not the same as not participating.

The Council also uses unanimity and simple majority in other contexts. Under unanimity, abstention does not prevent adoption.

Therefore:

`same abstention identity + different decision rule -> different downstream collective effect`.

And:

`simple YES count != qualified-majority result`.

## 4. United States Constitution

Article I Section 5 states that a majority of each House constitutes a quorum to do business. This is an institutional capacity/formation condition, not itself an approval vote on a bill.

Article I Section 7 requires a bill to pass both House and Senate and then be presented to the President. Presidential approval, veto/reconsideration with two-thirds override, and specified non-return conditions provide additional source-defined paths before the bill becomes law.

Therefore:

`quorum != bill approval threshold`;

`House passage != federal law`;

`House + Senate passage != law without the source-defined presentment path`.

## 5. Combined source distinctions

Potential source coordinates include:

- member identity and eligibility;
- participation status;
- individual vote/position;
- abstention/nonparticipation status;
- quorum or capacity-to-act condition;
- issue category;
- threshold and weighting rule;
- blocking/veto relation;
- body-level result;
- second-body concurrence where required;
- presentment/confirmation/override route;
- final institutional act;
- attribution to the institution;
- time/regime.

Not every institution uses all coordinates.
