# LAW-010 Model — Responsibility, Sanction, Recovery, Emotion, and Social Cost

Status: universal-first model after source review.

## 1. Source-sensitive coordinates

For a typed event/policy instance `tau`, use a non-scalar record:

`L(tau) = (H, R, S, Rest, Comp, Rehab, Prev, E_v, E_p, C_maint, C_error, C_abuse, C_legit_keep, C_legit_use, H_state, regime, time)`.

Where:

- `H` — harm/event state;
- `R` — responsibility-attribution state;
- `S` — sanction/punishment state;
- `Rest` — restitution/restoration state;
- `Comp` — compensation/reparation state;
- `Rehab` — offender or victim rehabilitation state, source-typed before use;
- `Prev` — prevention/incapacitation/non-repetition state;
- `E_v` — victim/bereaved-family emotional and evaluative responses, actor-indexed;
- `E_p` — broader public/social attitudes, actor/group and time indexed;
- `C_maint` — institutional/procedural/fiscal/administrative maintenance burdens recognized by a source;
- `C_error` — wrongful-decision / irreversible-error risk or cost;
- `C_abuse` — abuse/discrimination/political-violence risk or historical burden;
- `C_legit_keep` — legitimacy pressure associated with non-use/abolition or perceived under-punishment;
- `C_legit_use` — legitimacy pressure associated with use/retention, arbitrariness, discrimination or coercive abuse;
- `H_state` — historically relevant state-violence / political-execution / coercive-abuse context;
- `regime` — source legal/institutional regime;
- `time` — legally relevant time.

The coordinates are not assumed independent in the statistical sense. They are structurally non-identical unless the source supplies a relation.

## 2. Core non-equivalence candidate

LAW-010 tests:

`responsibility != punishment != victim restoration != compensation/reparation != rehabilitation != prevention/non-repetition != emotional/social response != institutional/social cost`.

This is not a claim that every legal system uses these exact words or keeps every category doctrinally separate.

It is a source-fidelity claim: whenever the source system gives these functions or statuses different conditions, objects, recipients, actors, procedures or effects, the application must preserve those differences.

## 3. Punishment and reparation

The ICC Rome Statute supplies an especially clean source-side separation:

- Article 75 establishes principles and orders for reparations to victims, including restitution, compensation and rehabilitation;
- Article 77 separately defines applicable penalties for a convicted person.

Therefore:

`conviction -> possible sentence`

and

`conviction -> possible reparations proceeding`

are distinct legal routes even when they arise from the same crime and convicted person.

The fact that both may follow conviction does not identify their objects or functions.

## 4. Reparation is itself plural

UN human-rights remedial materials distinguish at least:

- restitution;
- compensation;
- rehabilitation;
- satisfaction;
- guarantees of non-repetition.

This defeats:

`reparation = money`.

It also defeats:

`victim repair = punishment of perpetrator`.

Some sources expressly preserve both bringing perpetrators to justice and victim-specific reparation as distinct remedy dimensions.

## 5. Responsibility is not always a prerequisite for victim status

UN Committee against Torture General Comment No. 3 states that a person may be a victim regardless of whether the perpetrator is identified, apprehended, prosecuted or convicted.

Therefore the universal model must not require:

`criminal responsibility established -> victim status/reparation becomes definable`.

A source may instead define victim status and redress through the harm/violation relation while criminal responsibility remains unresolved or proceeds on another route.

## 6. Emotion as defined social input

### 6.1 Victim/bereaved-family layer

`E_v` is actor-indexed.

It may include grief, anger, condemnation, demand for retribution, demand for restraint, forgiveness, opposition to execution, or other responses.

Do not infer:

`bereavement -> one punishment preference`.

Source material recognizes murder-victim family members who oppose capital punishment as a distinct victim subgroup.

### 6.2 Public layer

`E_p` may include fear, condemnation, deterrence belief, retributive demand, abolition demand, distrust of state violence, or competing constitutional/political attitudes.

South African `S v Makwanyane` is a direct boundary witness:

`public support for death penalty` may be relevant to public policy/constitutional context but is not a substitute for constitutional adjudication.

Therefore:

`strong public attitude != legal judgment rule`.

## 7. Social and institutional cost is not one monetary scalar

LAW-010 uses `social cost` as a family of source-defined burdens/risks, not as a universal monetary utility value.

At minimum preserve:

- maintenance/procedural burden;
- irreversible-error risk;
- discrimination/arbitrariness risk;
- political/state-abuse risk;
- legitimacy cost of perceived under-punishment;
- legitimacy cost of coercive overreach or abusive punishment;
- historical institutional distrust.

A later empirical model may measure some coordinates numerically, but no cross-coordinate addition is licensed without an external operator and evidence base.

## 8. Historical state-abuse context

German Article 102 provides a strong witness that historical abuse can become legally relevant constitutional context.

Official Bundestag and Federal Constitutional Court materials identify Nazi-era abuse of capital punishment as a decisive historical reason for abolition, while postwar parliamentary debate still contained retributive, deterrent and public-agitation arguments for reintroduction.

Therefore the same society can simultaneously contain:

`H_state: strong historical distrust of state killing`

and

`E_p: renewed retributive/deterrent pressure`.

No universal causal implication follows in either direction.

## 9. Law-on-the-books versus practice

Capital-punishment status requires more than one bit.

A source-sensitive record may need:

`DP = (legal_authorization, offence_scope, moratorium/non-use, sentence_practice, execution_practice, review/clemency, constitutional_status, time)`.

Current comparative reporting distinguishes:

- abolitionist for all crimes;
- abolitionist for ordinary crimes only;
- abolitionist in practice;
- retentionist.

Therefore:

`statutory retention != actual execution`

and

`non-execution != legal abolition`.

## 10. Functional P/D/J extension

For a responsibility-attribution case:

- `P` may establish or argue responsibility;
- `D` may challenge responsibility, sanction eligibility, proportionality, finality, or other source conditions;
- `J` may establish responsibility and then face a distinct source-side disposition problem.

Even after `R = established`:

`R -> S_specific`

requires the source's sentencing/disposition rule.

And even after sanction:

`S_specific -> victim restored`

is not presumed.

Victim restoration, compensation, rehabilitation, satisfaction and guarantees of non-repetition may require separate routes and actors.

## 11. Policy-choice layer

A sanction regime can be affected by social-emotional and institutional inputs only through some legal/political decision structure.

Represent this abstractly as:

`PolicyStatus = Phi(source law, constitutional constraints, institutional competence, E_v, E_p, C_*, H_state, empirical claims, time)`.

`Phi` is **not supplied by DSD** and is not assumed to be additive, democratic-majoritarian, utilitarian, or otherwise fixed.

This blocks:

`public anger -> maximum punishment required`;

`state-abuse history -> abolition required`;

`high maintenance cost -> abolition required`.

## 12. DSD bridge

### Formation

Formation is useful for preserving distinct typed states and partial transitions among responsibility, sanction, victim-remedy routes, social inputs and final policy/legal status.

Do not identify:

`criminal responsibility != Formation realization`;

`punishment != channel value`;

`grief != analytic weight`;

`social cost != DSD aggregation coefficient`;

`abolitionist in practice != channel absence`.

### Dynamics

Dynamics can be useful for source-supplied temporal transitions such as:

`harm -> adjudication -> sanction/remedy -> later recovery/rehabilitation`;

`retention -> moratorium -> de facto non-use -> legal abolition`;

or the reverse where a source permits reintroduction.

Dynamics does not provide the legal transition rules.

### Static Aggregation

Not used as a legal or moral decision rule.

No scalar sum of life, grief, public anger, deterrence, legitimacy, error risk and administrative cost is licensed by the DSD papers.

### Axis-Property

Not required for the first pass.
