# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiments 83–91

Date: 2026-09-03

## Experiment 83 — Pure spatial conformal geometry

Let the spatial geometry be

\[
g_{ij}=e^{2\phi}\delta_{ij}.
\]

Its Levi-Civita connection gives, for an affine-parameter geodesic,

\[
\mathbf a_{\rm geo}
= v^2\nabla\phi-2(\mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla\phi)\mathbf v
\]

up to the usual reparameterization term parallel to the trajectory.
Therefore pure spatial conformal geometry produces velocity-dependent path curvature and does not by itself generate a universal static response of the form \(-c^2\nabla\phi\) for a probe initially at rest.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as a complete origin of the desired velocity-independent acceleration response.

## Experiment 84 — Progression/time-scale extension

Introduce a separate progression-rate or time-scale sector through a dimensionless factor \(N(\phi)>0\), with a kinematic toy geometry

\[
d\Sigma^2=-N(\phi)^2c_*^2dt^2+H(\phi)^2g_{0,ij}dx^idx^j.
\]

In the static slow-motion limit, the leading coordinate response is

\[
\mathbf a\simeq
-c_*^2\frac{N N'}{H^2}\nabla\phi.
\]

At a normalized reference state \(N(0)=H(0)=1\),

\[
\mathbf a\simeq-c_*^2N'(0)\nabla\phi.
\]

Thus the previous dimensionless \(\beta\) is not fixed by spatial conformal geometry; it is a coupling between the progression/time-scale sector and the spatial distortion scalar.

**Verdict:** CONDITIONAL.

## Experiment 85 — Field-normalization degeneracy

For a scalar rescaling

\[
\phi'=s\phi,
\]

one has

\[
\Lambda_s'=s\Lambda_s,
\qquad
\beta'=\beta/s,
\]

while

\[
\beta'\Lambda_s'=\beta\Lambda_s.
\]

Therefore \(\beta\) and the source-to-scalar amplitude are separately meaningful only after a canonical normalization of the geometry scalar has been fixed.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED normalization degeneracy.

## Experiment 86 — Canonical progression scalar

Define

\[
\psi:=\ln N.
\]

Then the slow static response becomes

\[
\mathbf a\simeq-c_*^2\nabla\psi
\]

at the normalized reference state.
In this representation the dimensionless coefficient \(\beta\) is absorbed into the definition of the progression scalar rather than treated as an independent physical constant.

If

\[
\psi(r;M)\simeq\Lambda_t\frac{M}{r},
\]

then

\[
[\Lambda_t]=L/M.
\]

**Verdict:** beta elimination CONFIRMED as a representation choice; \(\Lambda_t\) remains unresolved.

## Experiment 87 — Spatial and progression geometry are distinct sectors

A spatial conformal scalar \(\phi_s\) and progression scalar \(\psi\) may have separate amplitudes:

\[
\phi_s\simeq\Lambda_s\frac Mr,
\qquad
\psi\simeq\Lambda_t\frac Mr.
\]

A relation \(\psi=\sigma\phi_s\) implies \(\Lambda_t=\sigma\Lambda_s\), but this relation is additional structure.
Hence the source-to-spatial-geometry normalization is not automatically the normalization controlling slow acceleration.

**Verdict:** automatic identification REJECTED.

## Experiment 88 — \(c_{\rm info}\) is not automatically the kinematic conversion speed

The current Structural Reorganization Dynamics defines \(c_{\rm info}\) as the infimal finite-propagation bound for a declared discrepancy convention.
In a symmetric-hyperbolic specialization, a characteristic bound may upper-bound \(c_{\rm info}\), but equality requires a sharp or saturating mode.
Therefore using

\[
c_* = c_{\rm info}
\]

inside the probe-motion bridge is an additional identification, not a consequence of finite structural-information propagation alone.

**Verdict:** UNRESOLVED.

## Experiment 89 — Common-cone condition

A sufficient conditional route to \(c_*=c_{\rm info}\) would require at least:

1. the same localization/time carrier for structural propagation and probe kinematics;
2. the same causal or characteristic cone;
3. a relevant mode saturating the structural propagation bound.

Without this common-cone condition, \(c_*\) and \(c_{\rm info}\) remain distinct.

**Verdict:** CONDITIONAL criterion.
