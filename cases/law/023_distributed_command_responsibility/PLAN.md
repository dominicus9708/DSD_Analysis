# LAW-012 Plan — Distributed Responsibility, Command, Organization, and State Attribution

Status: active first-pass analysis.

## Question

When one harmful event involves direct actors, commanders/superiors, organizations and States, does responsibility form one transferable chain, or do source systems create several distinct responsibility/attribution routes?

## Strong candidates to attack

- direct perpetrator responsibility automatically determines commander responsibility;
- formal rank/title automatically proves effective control;
- commander responsibility is merely vicarious identity with the subordinate's responsibility;
- lack of direct perpetration means no superior responsibility can arise;
- State attribution and individual criminal responsibility are the same legal status;
- conduct attributed to a State eliminates or absorbs individual responsibility;
- conduct outside an official's instructions is necessarily private and non-attributable;
- organization/state responsibility can be reconstructed by simply summing individual responsibility.

## Workflow

1. Analyze source law without DSD mapping.
2. Use international criminal law command-responsibility doctrine and State-responsibility attribution as independent witness families.
3. Identify counterexamples to one-chain and one-title models.
4. Only then compare with existing DSD Formation/Axis/Static/Dynamics.
5. Record actual DSD correspondence, mismatch, or non-mapping.

## Main witness families

- ICC / Rome Statute responsibility modes as reflected in current ICC materials;
- ICTY command-responsibility jurisprudence on effective control;
- ILC Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts, especially Arts. 4, 7, 8 and 58.
