## 1. David J. Chalmers, “Does Conceivability Entail Possibility?” (2002)

Author-hosted text: https://consc.net/papers/conceivability.html

### 1.1 What the source already concedes about imagination

Chalmers explicitly states that imagining a situation or a world does not entail that such a situation or world exists or is metaphysically possible. He allows, at the stage of characterizing positive conceivability, that imagination may outstrip metaphysical possibility.

Therefore the follow-up does **not** accuse him of silently identifying imagination with possibility.

### 1.2 Ideal positive conceivability

The relevant notion is demanding: missing details must in principle be fillable without contradiction under idealized rational reflection, and the imagined situation must continue to verify the target statement under such reflection.

Chalmers also acknowledges that the substantive notion of ideal reasoning is not fully reductively characterized; he treats relevant rational notions as primitive/open-ended and explicitly avoids defining better reasoning by its ability to track metaphysical possibility, because that would trivialize the bridge.

### 1.3 Strong necessities

Chalmers explicitly identifies the exact form of a counterexample to weak modal rationalism:

`ideally primarily positively conceivable S` + `S false at every metaphysically possible world`.

He calls such a case a **strong necessity** and describes the issue as whether the space of positively conceivable situations can outstrip the space of possible worlds.

Consequence for DSD analysis:

A toy model in which epistemic scenario space is strictly larger than metaphysical possibility space cannot, by itself, count as a refutation. It instantiates the type of counterarchitecture Chalmers already anticipates; a domain-specific reason is still required for believing the phenomenal case has that structure.

### 1.4 Zombie reconstruction and the primary/secondary problem

With `P` as the conjunction of physical truths and `Q` a phenomenal truth, Chalmers reconstructs the argument as:

1. `P & ~Q` is ideally primarily positively conceivable;
2. ideal primary positive conceivability yields primary possibility;
3. primary possibility is used against materialism.

He then states that this argument is **unsound as it stands**, because materialism requires a secondary-necessity claim and primary possibility does not automatically provide secondary possibility.

### 1.5 The categorical-base loophole

Chalmers explicitly acknowledges that physical concepts may have their reference fixed by structural/dispositional roles while referring to underlying categorical properties.

If so:

- the primary intension of `P` can be satisfied by a world that preserves the microphysical causal-role structure;
- the secondary intension of `P` can nevertheless fail because those causal roles have different categorical bases;
- the purported zombie world is then structurally/dispositionally isomorphic to the actual world without being a full categorical duplicate.

He identifies the resulting family with Russellian-style panprotopsychism and reformulates the conclusion as:

`materialism is false OR panprotopsychism is true`.

This is the main source basis for the descriptor-completeness attack.

## 2. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Zombies”

https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/

SEP records three relevant external response families.

### 2.1 Phenomenal Concept Strategy

Type-B / phenomenal-concept approaches attempt to explain why there can be an epistemic or conceptual gap between physical and phenomenal descriptions without a corresponding ontological gap.

This directly supports the general audit distinction:

`conceptual or epistemic non-derivability != metaphysical property distinctness`.

### 2.2 Russellian monism

SEP summarizes the view that physics may provide structural/dispositional information while leaving the intrinsic nature of physical objects underdetermined.

On this view a physicalist may:

- concede a zombie that duplicates structural properties;
- deny a zombie that is a **full physical duplicate**, if the intrinsic categorical properties relevant to consciousness are themselves counted as physical.

This is the closest established philosophical analogue of the DSD descriptor-completeness pressure.

### 2.3 Causal essentialism

SEP also records a distinct route on which certain physical properties may essentially have consciousness-producing causal powers. If so, conceivability of a zombie-like scenario need not yield genuine zombie-world possibility.

This is relevant as an independent reminder that property identity/essence assumptions matter to the modal promotion, but it is not the main DSD attack developed here.

## 3. Daniel Stoljar — two conceptions of the physical

Relevant works:

- “The Conceivability Argument and Two Conceptions of the Physical,” *Philosophical Perspectives* 15 (2001), 393–413, DOI 10.1111/0029-4624.35.s15.18.
- “Two Conceptions of the Physical,” *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 62(2), 253–281.

Stoljar distinguishes a theory-based conception of the physical from an object-based conception that includes the intrinsic nature required for a complete account of paradigmatic physical objects.

This motivates the notation used in the follow-up:

- `P_T`: theory-based / structural-dispositional physical truth base;
- `I`: intrinsic/categorical physical basis, if any;
- `P_F = P_T + I`: fuller object-based physical description.

The DSD follow-up does not claim this distinction as new. It uses the DSD formation/descriptor discipline to independently reconstruct why this distinction matters to the zombie inference.

## 4. DSD sources used for the structural comparison

### 4.1 Formation Axiom System

Relevant current facts:

- domain-specific use requires additional interpretation maps;
- realization does not itself imply configuration describability;
- candidate channels are admitted only after describability, assignment, and role conditions are all satisfied;
- full comparison preserves candidate structure rather than collapsing candidate status into successful formation.

Therefore candidate specification and admitted structure are deliberately separate layers.

### 4.2 Channel-Indexed Static Aggregation

Relevant current fact:

- equality of a reduced aggregate does not reconstruct support-tagged channel/property records or the complete typed underlying structure.

This gives a formal project-internal example of a general principle used in the audit:

`equality of a chosen descriptor/readout != equality of the complete underlying structure`.

No claim is made that a physical-theory descriptor is literally a DSD aggregate, or that consciousness is a DSD property record.

## 5. Follow-up source judgment

The strongest defensible result is not:

`Chalmers forgot that conceivable worlds may be impossible`.

He explicitly addresses that problem.

The stronger surviving challenge is:

`What exactly is fixed by P?`

If `P` is only theory-based/structural-dispositional, sameness of `P` may fail to establish full physical identity.

If `P` is expanded to a complete object-based physical description including every relevant intrinsic/categorical fact, the ideal positive conceivability of `P & ~Q` must be independently re-established for that stronger description.

This is a convergence with the Stoljar/Russellian family rather than a new independent external philosophical discovery.
