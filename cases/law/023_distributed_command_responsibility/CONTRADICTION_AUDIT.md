# LAW-012 Contradiction Audit

## Strong forms tested

### 1. Formal title automatically creates command responsibility

Rejected.

ICTY materials distinguish de jure title/authority from effective control and require the material ability to prevent or punish. High rank or influence alone can be insufficient.

### 2. Direct perpetrator guilt automatically transfers upward

Rejected as a universal rule.

Command responsibility requires its own source-defined elements such as superior-subordinate relation/effective control, knowledge standard, and failure to take required measures.

### 3. Superior responsibility requires direct perpetration by the superior

Rejected.

Source systems can define responsibility based on ordering or failure to control/prevent/repress/refer, distinct from personally committing the underlying act.

### 4. State attribution and individual criminal responsibility are one state

Rejected.

ILC Article 58 explicitly leaves individual responsibility unaffected by the State-responsibility articles. State attribution has its own rules; individual criminal responsibility has different elements and procedures.

### 5. Ultra vires conduct is automatically private/non-attributable

Rejected.

ILC Article 7 attributes conduct performed in governmental capacity even when authority is exceeded or instructions are breached, while distinguishing conduct so removed from official functions that it is private.

### 6. Collective/State responsibility is the sum of individual guilt

Not supported.

The examined source law supplies attribution and breach rules, not an additive scalar operator over individual guilt states.

## DSD comparison

### Formation

Useful only to preserve typed identities, roles, partial states and source-specific interfaces.

Do not identify:

- commander/superior with a Formation role by identity;
- effective control with Axis-Property;
- State attribution with Formation composition;
- individual responsibility with a component value;
- missing command responsibility with `defined zero` or channel absence without a bridge.

### Axis-Property

Not required. Words such as command, hierarchy, control and authority do not independently justify a realized-axis interpretation.

### Static Aggregation

Not required. No source provides one commensurable scalar `responsibility weight` whose sum creates organizational or State responsibility.

### Dynamics

Potentially records time-indexed control, knowledge, orders and later responses, but does not generate the legal responsibility rules.

## Direct DSD contradiction

None found after source roles and rule families were preserved separately.

The principal DSD-side danger is application overreach: turning a network of independently sourced attribution/liability relations into one hierarchical responsibility chain.
