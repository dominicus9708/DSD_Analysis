## B. Source reconstruction from Hume only

Hume first states the general priority claim that simple ideas are preceded by corresponding impressions.

He then introduces a person who has experienced all shades of blue except one particular shade. The remaining shades are arranged in gradual order from deepest to lightest, leaving a gap. Hume says the person would notice the gap and asks whether imagination could supply the missing shade. Hume judges that the person can, and treats this as a singular exception showing that a simple idea need not always be preceded by its corresponding impression.

The benchmark therefore distinguishes the following source-level objects:

- `I*` = the missing shade's direct corresponding impression, stipulated absent;
- `N-`, `N+` = neighboring experienced shades;
- `R` = the supplied ordering/gradation/gap relation among displayed shades;
- `G` = the subject's awareness that a shade-position is missing;
- `Q` = a candidate qualitative idea/representation of the exact missing shade.

The source stipulates:

`I* absent`.

The setup additionally supplies substantial neighboring and relational information:

`N-`, `N+`, and `R` are available.

Hume's intended conclusion is stronger than mere gap detection:

`Q` can be formed despite the absence of `I*`.

## C. DSD prediction — sealed

### C1. Predicted vulnerability 1 — missing corresponding impression is not missing structural input

The setup does not present the subject with no relevant input. It supplies a densely ordered family of neighboring shades plus the location of a discontinuity.

Therefore:

`no direct corresponding impression`

does not imply

`no formation-supporting descriptive structure`.

DSD predicts that a mature analysis will distinguish direct-copy origin from relational/interpolative formation.

If `Q` is generated from neighboring shade information and the supplied gradation relation, the case may challenge a strict one-to-one copy principle while leaving open a weaker principle under which new representations are formed from prior impressions plus structural relations.

### C2. Predicted vulnerability 2 — gap localization is not yet exact qualitative realization

From the displayed series the subject can detect:

`there is a missing position here`.

That establishes at most a descriptive address or relational constraint on the missing shade.

It does not by logic alone establish:

`the internally generated item is qualitatively identical to the exact missing shade`.

Therefore the inference

`G -> exact Q`

requires an identity/fidelity bridge.

DSD predicts that the literature or strongest defense will need to say what counts as having the exact missing simple idea rather than an approximate blend, symbolic placeholder, relational coordinate, or merely the knowledge that something is missing.

### C3. Predicted vulnerability 3 — external arrangement contributes information

The experimenter deliberately arranges the experienced shades in an ordered sequence and creates a salient gap. This arrangement is not neutral with respect to the reconstruction task.

The subject's available descriptive state after presentation contains relations that were not supplied by the missing direct impression itself.

Therefore a conclusion of the form

`Q arises without I*`

must not be silently strengthened to

`Q arises without relevant experiential/structural support`.

DSD predicts that the case will be sensitive to whether the ordering relation is treated as genuine additional input, merely an aid, or already implicit in the subject's prior color experience.

### C4. Predicted vulnerability 4 — 'simple idea' and 'structurally derived representation' need not be the same classification

Hume calls the shade idea simple.

DSD does not define psychological simplicity, but it predicts a classification question:

Can something be phenomenally or content-wise simple while being formation-wise derived from a relational neighborhood?

If yes, then:

`simple content`

does not imply

`non-derived formation history`.

The benchmark predicts that at least one serious analysis route will separate content simplicity from derivational simplicity, compositionality, interpolation, or dependence on neighboring impressions.

### C5. Predicted surviving source component

DSD does **not** predict that Hume's example is simply invalid.

If the case stipulates or independently establishes that the subject genuinely forms the exact missing qualitative idea, then the narrow source conclusion survives:

`a direct corresponding impression is not absolutely necessary for every simple idea`.

DSD's expected pressure is on what exactly has been established and what formation route is licensed, not on denying the possibility by fiat.

### C6. Predicted inapplicable attacks

The following attacks are predicted to be inapplicable unless later source material specifically justifies them:

1. `undefined = zero` is not the central issue here; absence of the missing impression must not be re-described as a zero-valued color impression.
2. Part/whole bearer attribution is not the primary mechanism.
3. A purely modal attack about conceivability versus possibility is not the primary mechanism.
4. The example does not by itself establish a physical theory of color or a psychophysical metric.

## D. DSD-derived bridge conditions

A strong defense of Hume's exact conclusion should make at least one of the following explicit:

1. **Exact-idea criterion** — what makes the generated representation identical to the missing shade rather than merely located between neighbors.
2. **Formation rule** — whether relational/interpolative construction from neighboring impressions counts as deriving the idea from impressions mediately.
3. **Simplicity criterion** — whether 'simple' classifies content, phenomenology, representational structure, or formation history.
4. **Input accounting rule** — whether the supplied gradation/order relation is independent information or already contained in the prior experienced shade family.

## E. Falsification criteria

The sealed DSD prediction is weakened or rejected if post-seal literature establishes any of the following:

1. Hume's source claim explicitly concerns only absence of the exact corresponding impression and already treats relational generation as fully compatible with his intended exception, so the DSD reconstruction adds no genuine pressure.
2. There is a source-independent criterion that guarantees exact qualitative identity of the imagined shade from the setup, eliminating the fidelity problem.
3. The neighboring-order/interpolation account is shown to presuppose the missing qualitative content and therefore cannot explain formation without circularity.
4. Serious literature shows that the central issue is elsewhere and that the sealed distinctions are peripheral.
5. The DSD attack conflates knowing where a shade lies in an ordering with possessing its qualitative idea and thereby targets a claim Hume never makes.
