# Structural Gravity Audit — Axis Tension, Prestress, Geometric Stiffness, and Support Margin

Date: 2026-09-05
Status: DSD Analysis + DSD Audit continuation

## Experiment 451 — `Axis tension` needs a deformation coordinate before it can become a mechanical quantity

A bare realized axis line does not by itself supply an extension coordinate or physical length. Therefore the old label `axis tension` cannot be identified with physical force from line geometry alone.

Two downstream cases must be separated.

1. Orientation-only interaction, with a dimensionless coordinate such as
   \[
   s_{ab}=\operatorname{tr}(P_aP_b)=\cos^2\theta_{ab}.
   \]
   If \(E_{ab}=V(s_{ab})\), then
   \[
   \tau^{(s)}_{ab}:=V'(s_{ab})
   \]
   is a generalized interaction prestress conjugate to the dimensionless descriptor \(s\). Its units are those of energy, not automatically force.

2. Extension-bearing localized axis, with a supplied physical coordinate \(\ell\). Then
   \[
   T_a:=\frac{\partial E}{\partial\ell_a}
   \]
   has force dimension and may legitimately be called a mechanical tension.

### Verdict

`Axis tension` should be retained as a candidate typed property, but its physical carrier and units must be selected only after a deformation/localization bridge is supplied.

## Experiment 452 — Dimensionless strain still requires a reference length for physical force

Let
\[
e=(\ell-\ell_*)/\ell_*.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{\partial E}{\partial e}
\]
has energy units, while
\[
\boxed{
T=\frac1{\ell_*}\frac{\partial E}{\partial e}
}
\]
has force units.

Hence a dimensionless axis-property value or angular descriptor cannot generate force units without a supplied physical scale.

### Verdict

This reinforces the earlier no-go: axis geometry alone cannot generate the universal absolute normalization.

## Experiment 453 — Tension/prestress and stiffness are distinct derivatives

For a scalar deformation coordinate \(q\),
\[
\Sigma(q)=\frac{dE}{dq},
\qquad
K(q)=\frac{d\Sigma}{dq}=\frac{d^2E}{dq^2}.
\]
At a reference state \(q_*\), define
\[
\Sigma_0:=\Sigma(q_*),
\qquad
K_0:=K(q_*).
\]
The same \(\Sigma_0\) can occur with different \(K_0\), and the same \(K_0\) can occur with different \(\Sigma_0\).

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{\text{prestress/tension state}\neq\text{tangent stiffness}}
\]

## Experiment 454 — Exact angular second variation splits material and geometric parts

For two unoriented axes, let
\[
s(\theta)=\cos^2\theta,
\qquad
E(\theta)=V(s(\theta)).
\]
Since
\[
\frac{ds}{d\theta}=-\sin2\theta,
\qquad
\frac{d^2s}{d\theta^2}=-2\cos2\theta,
\]
one obtains
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d^2E}{d\theta^2}
=V''(s)\sin^22\theta
-2V'(s)\cos2\theta
}.
\]
Interpretation:

- \(V''\)-term: tangent/material coupling stiffness;
- \(V'\)-term: geometric stiffness generated by the current prestress/interacting state.

Thus prestress can affect stability without being identical to material stiffness.

## Experiment 455 — Pure prestress can generate angular stiffness even when \(V''=0\)

Take
\[
V(s)=\kappa s.
\]
Then \(V''=0\), but
\[
E''(\theta)=-2\kappa\cos2\theta.
\]
Hence
\[
E''(\pi/2)=2\kappa,
\qquad
E''(0)=-2\kappa.
\]
For \(\kappa>0\), the same positive interaction coefficient stabilizes the orthogonal state and destabilizes the aligned state.

### Verdict

The sign of prestress contribution to support is geometry/state dependent. `Positive tension` is not universally stabilizing or destabilizing.

## Experiment 456 — Balance constraints determine a nullspace and ratios, not absolute scale

Let \(N=[n_1\ \cdots\ n_m]\) be the direction matrix and let a force-like balance specialization be
\[
N\tau=0.
\]
Then
\[
\tau\in\ker N.
\]
If \(\tau\neq0\) is a solution, so is
\[
\lambda\tau
\]
for every scalar \(\lambda\).

Moreover,
\[
\dim\ker N=m-\operatorname{rank}N.
\]
Thus nontrivial balanced tensions require a nontrivial relation among the participating directions, and the balance relation itself does not fix an absolute magnitude.

### Verdict

The old `axis-tension balance` intuition can constrain ratios or admissible relation sectors, but cannot recover a universal force scale by itself.

## Experiment 457 — Equilibrium does not imply zero pair prestress

For a network energy
\[
E=\sum_{a<b}V_{ab}(s_{ab}),
\]
stationarity of axis \(a\) requires the total rotational drive to vanish,
\[
\sum_bV'_{ab}(s_{ab})[P_a,P_b]=0,
\]
but individual terms need not vanish.

Hence one may have a static equilibrium with nonzero internal pair prestresses whose torques cancel.

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{\text{static equilibrium}\not\Rightarrow\text{zero axis tension/prestress}}
\]

---

## Experiment 458 — Tension balance does not imply support/stability

A stationary state can satisfy the first-variation balance while the Hessian has a negative eigenvalue.

Therefore
\[
\delta E=0
\]
is not sufficient for
\[
\delta^2E>0.
\]

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{\text{tension/torque balance}\neq\text{support}}
\]

The previously defined support margin must remain a second-variation/full-coupled diagnostic.

## Experiment 459 — Prestress enters the support operator through geometric stiffness

Write schematically
\[
\mathcal H_A
=
\mathsf R_A
+\mathsf K_A^{\rm material}
+\mathsf K_A^{\rm geo}(\Sigma_0)
+\mathsf C_A.
\]
Then the axis-support margin is
\[
\boxed{
m_{\rm sup}=\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal H_{\rm full})
}.
\]
The prestress state affects support only through the supplied geometric/constitutive map
\[
\Sigma_0\mapsto\mathsf K_A^{\rm geo}(\Sigma_0),
\]
not by name alone.

## Experiment 460 — Simple two-state witness for state-dependent prestress effect

Consider
\[
E(\theta)=\frac12R(\theta-\theta_*)^2+\kappa\cos^2\theta.
\]
At an orthogonal reference \(\theta_*=\pi/2\), the local curvature contribution from the pair term is \(+2\kappa\).
At an aligned reference \(\theta_*=0\), it is \(-2\kappa\).

Thus an identical pair coefficient can harden one geometry and soften another.

### Verdict

No universal monotone rule `more tension -> more support` is justified.

## Experiment 461 — Coupled structural-gravity threshold depends on the prestressed axis Hessian

In the previously derived Schur-complement reduction,
\[
L_{U,\rm eff}
=
L_U
-\beta_A^2 C\mathcal H_A^{-1}C^*.
\]
Therefore prestress can alter the self-field coupled threshold indirectly by changing \(\mathcal H_A\).

If geometric prestress softens \(\mathcal H_A\), then the susceptibility
\[
\chi_A=\langle u,C\mathcal H_A^{-1}C^*u\rangle
\]
can increase and the coupled instability may occur earlier.
If it hardens \(\mathcal H_A\), the opposite can occur.

### Verdict

Axis tension can influence structural-gravity stability, but the sign is not universal and the effect is downstream of an explicit bridge.

## Experiment 462 — Axis inertia changes timescale, not the static prestress-support criterion

With
\[
\mathsf M_A\ddot q+\mathcal H_Aq=0,
\]
rescaling
\[
\mathsf M_A\mapsto\lambda\mathsf M_A
\]
changes frequencies/growth times but leaves the static condition
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal H_A)=0
\]
unchanged.

### Verdict

`axis inertia`, `axis tension`, and `axis support` remain distinct roles.

## Experiment 463 — A scalar tension aggregate can erase directional prestress information

A candidate line-stress representation is
\[
\Sigma_A=\sum_a\tau_aP_a.
\]
Two systems can have the same scalar total
\[
T_{\rm sum}=\sum_a\tau_a
\]
but different tensors \(\Sigma_A\).

For three equal \(\tau\):

- orthonormal triad:
  \[
  \Sigma_A=\tau I;
  \]
- fully aligned triad:
  \[
  \Sigma_A=3\tau P.
  \]

Both have \(T_{\rm sum}=3\tau\), but one is isotropic and the other maximally uniaxial.

### DSD Analysis verdict

A scalar aggregate of `axis tension` cannot generally reconstruct directional prestress, geometric stiffness, or support margin.

## Experiment 464 — Tension and stiffness records may fail to admit one conservative energy

Suppose a specialization independently assigns a tension-like field \(\Sigma(q)\) and a stiffness-like operator \(K(q)\).
A single conservative energy exists only if the compatibility condition
\[
K=D\Sigma
\]
(and the corresponding symmetry/integrability conditions in multiple dimensions) holds.

An arbitrary pair of old static property records need not satisfy this.

### Verdict

There are two legitimate branches:

1. **energy-derived branch**: tension and stiffness are linked derivatives and must satisfy compatibility;
2. **nonconservative constitutive branch**: tension-like and stiffness-like operators are independently supplied, and no common potential is claimed.

The migration must declare which branch is used.

## Experiment 465 — Minimal audited tension bridge

The old typed property record should not map directly to one universal scalar. A minimal bridge is schematically
\[
\boxed{
B_{\rm ten}:
(D_{\rm ten},G_{\rm loc},q,\text{reference measure})
\longrightarrow
\Sigma_A
}
\]
where \(\Sigma_A\) is a generalized stress/prestress object in the chosen structural-gravity model.

If a physical extension coordinate is available, a further readout can produce a force-like tension. If only orientation data are supplied, the output remains an angular/generalized prestress.

---

## Experiment 466 — Minimal stiffness bridge remains separate

The stiffness bridge is
\[
\boxed{
B_{\rm stiff}:
(D_{\rm stiff},G_{\rm loc},q)
\longrightarrow
\mathsf K_A
}
\]
or, in the conservative compatible branch,
\[
\mathsf K_A=D_q\Sigma_A.
\]

Thus `axis tension` and `axis stiffness` may be related in a chosen specialization without being definitionally identical.

## Experiment 467 — Migration classification after the tension audit

- **axis tension**: static/relational property candidate -> generalized prestress/stress through explicit bridge;
- **axis stiffness**: constitutive tangent/spatial operator;
- **axis restoration**: target-directed zero-order response;
- **axis support**: derived full-system stability margin;
- **axis inertia**: kinetic/time-scale operator;
- **axis coupling**: pair/field interaction operator that may create internal prestress;
- **axis reorganization**: dynamic event/law driven by the resulting generalized forces/torques.

This classification preserves the old vocabulary while preventing synonym collapse.

## Experiment 468 — Main structural-gravity chain for tension

The strongest surviving chain is
\[
\boxed{
\text{typed tension candidate}
\to
\text{localized deformation bridge}
\to
\text{prestress }\Sigma_0
\to
\text{geometric stiffness}
\to
\mathcal H_A
\to
m_{\rm sup}
\to
\text{coupled structural-gravity threshold}
}
\]
with every arrow beyond the typed property layer explicitly supplied or derived within a declared specialization.

## Final verdict

The old `axis tension` concept should be migrated, but not as the origin of the universal gravity constant and not as a synonym for stiffness or support.

Its best current role is a **state-dependent generalized prestress** whose physical dimension and mechanical meaning depend on the supplied deformation/localization bridge. It can influence structural-gravity stability through geometric stiffness, but the sign and magnitude of that influence are geometry- and constitutive-law dependent.
