# BENCH-C01 Source Notes — Toxin Puzzle

Status: **post-seal unblinding source audit**.

## Primary source

Gregory S. Kavka, “The Toxin Puzzle,” *Analysis* 43(1), 1983, pp. 33–36.

The original setup rewards the presence of an intention at midnight, not actual toxin drinking the following afternoon. Kavka explicitly permits later change of mind and stipulates reliable intention detection.

Kavka’s own analysis already distinguishes **reasons for intending** from **reasons for acting**. He argues that intentions are not mere inner performances or commands but are constrained by reasons for action, and that the puzzle arises when reasons for intending and reasons for acting diverge.

This is important for the DSD audit: the original source already contains part of the stage separation predicted in `PREDICTION.md`. Therefore DSD cannot claim that Kavka simply conflated intention with execution.

## External literature opened after the seal

### Alfred R. Mele — 1992 / 1996

Mele’s work treats the Toxin Puzzle as a problem about the relation among intentions, reasons, beliefs, and intentional action. It explicitly examines whether a reason to intend can generate an intention when it is not also a reason for the intended action.

Mele’s `Ted` case is a major counterexample to simple principles claiming that lack of a future normative reason to act always blocks present intention formation. Ted can form and sustain the intention under unusual agency conditions.

This is a major point that the pre-seal DSD prediction did not specifically anticipate.

### Michael E. Bratman — stability of intention

Bratman’s work explicitly treats toxin cases under the heading **stability of intention** and planning agency. Prior plans normally play a role in organizing action over time and need not be reconsidered from scratch at every moment.

This strongly overlaps the DSD prediction that `formation`, `persistence`, and `execution` should not be collapsed into one event.

However, it also shows that this distinction is an established part of the literature rather than a new DSD discovery.

### David Gauthier — rationalist line

Gauthier argues that toxin drinking can be rational as part of a larger course of action or commitment adopted as a whole. Later work characterizes this as a proposal that the rationality of the prior intention can supply a backward-looking reason to execute the action.

This directly occupies the bridge between intention formation and later execution that DSD predicted would need explicit justification.

### Ken Levy — critique of the Rationalist Solution

Levy distinguishes the rationality of intending from the rationality of acting and argues that the rationality of the intention does not straightforwardly transfer to the intended action. His treatment makes the transfer principle explicit and criticizes proposed bridges such as self-promise and rational self-interest.

This is a direct external match for the DSD requirement that an intention-to-action bridge be stated rather than assumed.

### Fernando Rudy-Hiller — 2019

Rudy-Hiller proposes a more specific **motivating-explanatory reason** principle: an ordinary agent cannot now intend a future action if the agent believes that, at the future time, there will be no motivating reason capable of explaining intentional performance.

This is especially important because it refines the DSD-predicted generic bridge. The pre-seal DSD analysis predicted that some formation/execution bridge was needed, but it did not distinguish **normative reasons** from **motivating/explanatory reasons**.

This counts as a substantive DSD miss/finer external distinction.

## DSD source basis

### Formation Axiom System

Use only the general methodology:

- staged operations must not be collapsed;
- domain-specific applications require interpretation maps;
- defined/undefined statuses are distinct when a partial assignment is used;
- a downstream stage must not be silently identified with an earlier formation stage.

The Formation Axiom System itself is static and does not supply a theory of psychological intention or temporal persistence.

### Axis-Property Axiom System

If `intention` is represented as an external property, the target theory must supply the property signature, bearer profile, domain, value/status structure, and semantic coherence law. A property label by itself has no mathematical content.

### Structural Reorganization Dynamics

This is the most relevant DSD predecessor for the temporal separation. It explicitly distinguishes time-indexed states and requires stronger transitions of support/status/formation data to be declared rather than hidden inside smooth value evolution.

The methodological transfer to BENCH-C01 is limited:

`state at t0 != state at t1`, and a later transition does not by itself rewrite the earlier slice.

DSD does not thereby define intention persistence, rationality, reasons, or agency.

## Source-discipline conclusion

The literature confirms that the Toxin Puzzle is indeed centered on the interface predicted by DSD, but it also contains much richer distinctions than the pre-seal DSD analysis anticipated. The correct validation outcome is mixed-positive rather than a clean new solution.
