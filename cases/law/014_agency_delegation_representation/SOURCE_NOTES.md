# LAW-003 Source Notes

Status: source collection executed on 2026-08-22 (Asia/Seoul).

## Source selection rule

Primary statutory text and Supreme Court decisions are used before any DSD correspondence. The source-law description is kept separate from the later structural mapping.

## Terminology guard

The following terms are not treated as synonyms:

- `대리` — agency/representation by an agent under the Civil Act rules on agency;
- `대리권` — authority to act as an agent;
- `위임` — mandate contract under Civil Act Article 680;
- `대표` — representation of a juridical person by its organ/representative, including Civil Act Article 59;
- `수권` — conferral of authority, only where the source rule actually supports that reading;
- `귀속/효력` — legal effect or attribution to another person/entity;
- `추인` — ratification of an unauthorized agency act;
- `표현대리` — a statutory doctrine imposing responsibility/effect under specified reliance conditions despite a defect in actual authority.

No DSD term is inserted into these source definitions.

## External source records

### EXT-01 — Ordinary authorized agency

- Jurisdiction: Republic of Korea
- Source: Civil Act (민법)
- Current version used: effective 2026-03-17, Act No. 21454
- Provision: Article 114(1), effect of an act of agency
- Official source: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSideInfoP.do?docCls=jo&joBrNo=00&joNo=0114&lsiSeq=284415&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR
- Claim used: when an agent, within the scope of authority, makes a declaration of intention showing that it is made for the principal, the effect arises directly for the principal.
- Source status: normative rule.

This establishes that `actor`, `authority`, `capacity`, and `attributed legal effect` are not collapsed into one coordinate. Direct effect on the principal requires the source-law conditions of the agency rule.

### EXT-02 — Excess of authority and apparent agency

- Jurisdiction: Republic of Korea
- Source: Civil Act
- Current version used: effective 2026-03-17
- Provision: Article 126
- Official source: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSideInfoP.do?docCls=jo&joBrNo=00&joNo=0126&lsiSeq=284415&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR
- Claim used: where an agent acts outside the scope of authority, the principal may nevertheless be responsible if the third party had justifiable grounds to believe the authority existed.
- Source status: normative exception/protection rule.

This is important because `outside actual authority` cannot be identified globally with `no possible legal effect on the principal`. The reliance rule is an additional legal route to responsibility.

### EXT-03 — Termination of authority and post-termination apparent agency

- Jurisdiction: Republic of Korea
- Source: Civil Act
- Current version used: effective 2026-03-17
- Provisions: Articles 128-129
- Official source: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsInfoP.do?docType=JO&joNo=062900&lsiSeq=58442
- Claims used:
  - Article 128: voluntary agency authority granted by juridical act terminates when the underlying legal relationship terminates, and may also terminate by revocation of the authorization before that relationship ends.
  - Article 129: termination of authority cannot be asserted against a good-faith third party, subject to the third party's negligence rule.
- Source status: normative termination and third-party protection rules.

The legal system therefore distinguishes `actual authority status` from `effect against a protected third party`.

### EXT-04 — Unauthorized agency and ratification

- Jurisdiction: Republic of Korea
- Source: Civil Act
- Current version used: effective 2026-03-17
- Provisions: Articles 130-134
- Official sources:
  - Article 130: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSideInfoP.do?docCls=jo&joBrNo=00&joNo=0130&lsiSeq=284415&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR
  - Article 131: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSideInfoP.do?docCls=jo&joBrNo=00&joNo=0131&lsiSeq=284415&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR
  - Article 133: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSideInfoP.do?docCls=jo&joBrNo=00&joNo=0133&lsiSeq=284415&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR
- Claims used:
  - Article 130: a contract made as another's agent without authority has no effect against the principal unless the principal ratifies it.
  - Article 131: the counterparty may demand a definite answer on ratification; failure to answer within the specified reasonable period is treated as refusal.
  - Article 133: absent a different declaration, ratification has retroactive effect from the time of contract, without prejudicing third-party rights.
  - Article 134: before ratification, the counterparty has a statutory withdrawal right subject to the stated knowledge condition.
- Source status: normative status-transition and protection rules.

The unauthorized act is not simply treated as a nonexistent event. The source law separately regulates the attempted contract, its lack of effect against the principal, the ratification decision, retroactivity, and the counterparty's interim position.

### EXT-05 — Mandate contract

- Jurisdiction: Republic of Korea
- Source: Civil Act
- Current version used: effective 2026-03-17
- Provision: Article 680
- Official source: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkProc.do?joNo=068000&lnkJoNo=undefined&lsClsCd=L&lsId=001706&lsNm=%EB%AF%BC%EB%B2%95&mode=4
- Claim used: mandate takes effect when one party entrusts the handling of affairs to the other and the other accepts.
- Source status: definitional/normative contract rule.

Article 680 defines an internal task-handling relationship. It does not state the Article 114 external-effect rule. Whether a mandate relationship also carries agency authority therefore cannot be assumed merely from the word `위임`.

### EXT-06 — Supreme Court treatment of mandate relation and agency authority as distinct legal questions

- Institution: Supreme Court of Korea
- Decision: Supreme Court 2024-03-12, 2023Da288772 (물품대금)
- Official source: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/precInfoP.do?precSeq=239899
- Claim used: in the choice-of-law analysis, the Court separately examines the principal-agent underlying relationship, such as a mandate contract, and whether the agent was lawfully granted agency authority, including its existence, content, scope, and termination.
- Source status: authoritative judicial interpretation.

This supports a source-side distinction between the underlying mandate-like relationship and the legally operative agency authority question.

### EXT-07 — Juridical-person representation and appointment of a specific agent

- Jurisdiction: Republic of Korea
- Source: Civil Act
- Current version used: effective 2026-03-17
- Provisions: Articles 59-62
- Official source: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1017152173
- Claims used:
  - Article 59: directors represent the juridical person in its affairs, subject to the stated internal limits, and agency provisions apply mutatis mutandis to representation of a juridical person.
  - Article 60: a restriction on a director's representative authority cannot be asserted against a third party unless registered.
  - Article 62: within the statutory conditions, a director may cause another person to act as agent for a specific act.
- Source status: normative organizational representation rules.

The statute itself distinguishes `director representing the juridical person` from `another person appointed to act as agent for a specific act`.

### EXT-08 — Personal capacity and corporate-party identity are not interchangeable

- Institution: Supreme Court of Korea
- Decision: Supreme Court 2008-06-12, 2008Da11276 (공사대금)
- Official source: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/precInfoP.do?evtNo=2008%EB%8B%A411276
- Claim used: a lawsuit filed in the personal name of a person who had been a representative director could not, merely by party-name correction, be converted from that individual to the company because party identity was not the same. The same decision also held that authority to conclude a contract does not automatically include all later disposition or termination authority.
- Source status: authoritative judicial interpretation.

This is a strong role/capacity boundary: the same human's personal procedural position and the company's procedural position are not interchangeable, and one grant of authority does not automatically totalize into every later authority.

### EXT-09 — Modern apparent-agency boundary

- Institution: Supreme Court of Korea
- Decision: Supreme Court 2025-06-05, 2023Da232526
- Official source: https://portal.scourt.go.kr/pgp/main.on?c=900&jisCntntsSrno=2025000016396&pgDvs=1&rnum=37&srchwd=%2A&w2xPath=PGP1011M04
- Claim used: Article 126 apparent agency requires the agency/intention structure and justifiable reliance conditions described by the Court; the doctrine is not a universal conversion of every unauthorized use of another person's name into principal liability.
- Source status: authoritative judicial interpretation.

## Source-side propositions fixed before DSD mapping

The external sources support the following propositions for this case:

1. `same human actor` does not determine `same legally operative capacity`.
2. `mandate relationship` and `agency authority` are distinct questions and cannot be collapsed without additional source-law support.
3. `actual authority` and `legal effect against the principal` are related but not identical statuses because apparent-agency rules can supply effect/responsibility despite an authority defect.
4. `unauthorized act exists` and `effect against principal` are distinct; ratification can later alter the latter status.
5. `grant of authority for one act` does not automatically mean authority for all later acts.
6. juridical-person organ representation and an appointed agent for a specific act are source-law-distinct routes.
