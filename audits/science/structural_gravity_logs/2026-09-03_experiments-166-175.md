# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiments 166–175

## Experiment 166 — Orbital quadrupole of bounded components

For

\[
\rho=\sum_a\rho_a,
\]

let component \(a\) have mass \(m_a\), centroid \(\mathbf R_a\), and intrinsic quadrupole \(Q^{(a)}_{ij}\) about its own centroid. About the global origin,

\[
Q_{ij}
=
\sum_a
\left[
Q^{(a)}_{ij}
+
m_a\left(3R_{ai}R_{aj}-R_a^2\delta_{ij}\right)
\right].
\]

Thus bounded-component labels alone are invisible, but actual relative placement generates an orbital quadrupole even if every component is individually spherical.

## Experiment 167 — Moment additivity

Inside the linear density branch, monopole and higher moments add under density superposition. The two-lump quadrupole control is the special case with vanishing intrinsic component quadrupoles and nonzero orbital quadrupole.

## Experiment 168 — Bulk/surface mass sectors

Consider

\[
\nabla^2\psi
=
4\pi\left(
\Lambda_b\rho
+
\Lambda_\Sigma\sigma\,\delta_\Sigma
\right).
\]

The far-field monopole amplitude is

\[
A_0
=
\Lambda_bM_b
+
\Lambda_\Sigma M_\Sigma,
\]

where

\[
M_b=\int \rho\,d^3x,
\qquad
M_\Sigma=\int_\Sigma \sigma\,dA.
\]

## Experiment 169 — Composition-factorization test

If physical total mass is

\[
M=M_b+M_\Sigma,
\]

then the monopole amplitude factors through \(M\) for arbitrary bulk/surface partition iff

\[
\Lambda_b=\Lambda_\Sigma=\Lambda_t.
\]

Unequal couplings make composition fraction an independent far-field descriptor.

## Experiment 170 — Geometric interface-source caution

A purely geometric source such as

\[
\eta K\,\delta_\Sigma
\]

has the correct \(L^{-2}\) source dimension without a mass coefficient and can generate a length-valued monopole amplitude. However, unless its total monopole vanishes or is absorbed into a background/reference, it produces progression geometry independently of physical mass and therefore breaks mass-only universality. It does not supply the missing mass-to-geometry normalization.

## Experiment 171 — General source-sector formulation

Let coarse source-sector charges be

\[
\mathbf q=(q_1,\ldots,q_n),
\]

physical mass

\[
M=\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf q,
\]

and progression monopole amplitude

\[
A_0=\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\mathbf q.
\]

## Experiment 172 — Exact linear factorization criterion

The monopole factors through physical mass for all admissible compositions,

\[
A_0=F(M),
\]

iff

\[
\ker\mathbf w\subseteq\ker\boldsymbol\lambda.
\]

In finite-dimensional linear form this gives

\[
\boldsymbol\lambda
=
\Lambda_t\mathbf w,
\]

so

\[
A_0=\Lambda_tM.
\]

## Experiment 173 — Non-mass structural sector

If one structural sector does not contribute to physical mass,

\[
w_s=0,
\]

then mass-only monopole factorization forces

\[
\lambda_s=0.
\]

Such a sector may still contribute through higher multipoles or near-field corrections.

## Experiment 174 — Structural contribution to physical mass

If binding/formation/internal structure genuinely contributes to the physical mass functional, then \(w_s\neq0\). It may then contribute to the monopole, but total-mass universality requires

\[
\lambda_s=\Lambda_t w_s.
\]

## Experiment 175 — Verdict

Source-sector coupling universality is exactly the factorization requirement that the far-field monopole descend to the physical-mass quotient.

- bounded arrangement to quadrupole: CONFIRMED inside the linear branch;
- composition-independence criterion: EXACT in the finite-dimensional linear sector model;
- non-mass structural monopole: REJECTED under mass-only universality;
- higher-multipole structural correction: ALLOWED.
