# LAW-006 Result — Obligation, Permission, Prohibition, Exemption, and Rule Scope

Status: first-pass universal analysis complete.

Primary purpose: reinterpretation.

Secondary purposes: coherence, predefinition audit, rule-scope analysis, universal-claim falsification.

## 1. Answer-first result

No direct contradiction was found between the selected international/transnational normative structures, formal deontic counterpressure, and the DSD Formation distinctions after actor, action, role, governing rule, scope, context, purpose, time, and applicability were typed separately.

The initial roadmap wording was too simple if read as four mutually exclusive values:

`OBLIGATORY / PERMITTED / PROHIBITED / EXEMPT`.

That one-bit-style taxonomy does not survive counterpressure.

The stronger surviving universal candidate is:

**normative status is rule- and context-indexed; physical possibility, rule applicability, obligation, permission, prohibition, exception/exemption, derogation, and downstream consequence must not be collapsed unless the source system supplies the rule or logic that licenses that identification.**

## 2. Why the four-value model fails

### 2.1 Obligation and permission need not be mutually exclusive

Standard deontic systems commonly validate a consistency principle of the form:

`O(A) -> P(A)`.

Therefore a model in which an act must be either `OBLIGATORY` or `PERMITTED` but never both is not a safe universal starting point.

Other deontic theories distinguish strong permission from mere non-prohibition and contain well-known puzzles involving conflicts, contrary-to-duty obligations, normative gaps, and agency.

This formal literature is used as counterpressure, not as positive law.

### 2.2 Lack of permission is not necessarily prohibition

UNIDROIT Articles 6.1.14-6.1.16 expressly contemplate a public permission that is required but is still neither granted nor refused, while one party already bears an obligation to take measures to obtain it.

Thus the same transaction can contain:

`O(seek permission)`

and

`permission-to-perform = pending`.

Therefore:

`not granted`

is not identical to

`refused` or `prohibited`.

### 2.3 Exception is not necessarily an opposite normative value

ILO Convention No. 29 Article 2(2) excludes specified categories of compulsory work from the Convention's forced-labour definition under stated conditions.

A source-faithful model can therefore treat an exception as a rule-scope result:

`not Applies(base_prohibition, tau)`

rather than first asserting a global prohibition and then overwriting it with a global permission.

This is a major result of LAW-006.

## 3. Witness-family results

### 3.1 ILO Convention No. 29 — prohibition, definition, exception, and state obligation

The Convention combines several normative structures:

- a state undertaking to suppress forced or compulsory labour;
- a definition of the regulated category;
- explicit conditional exclusions from that category;
- a requirement that illegal exaction be punishable and that adequate penalties be enforced.

This independently demonstrates that:

`physically compulsory work`

is not automatically identical to

`work inside the Convention's prohibited forced-labour category`.

The scope rule must be applied first.

Judgment: **strong witness for applicability-before-consequence discipline**.

### 3.2 ICCPR / Human Rights Committee — right, restriction, derogation, and residual limits

The ICCPR structure provides two different forms of normative qualification.

First, rights such as freedom of expression coexist with specified restriction clauses. A protected liberty therefore does not mean unconditional permission under every circumstance.

Second, Article 4 permits temporary derogation from some Covenant obligations only under exceptional conditions and subject to safeguards, while other rights remain non-derogable.

Thus:

`base right/obligation`

`!= restriction authority`

`!= derogation authority`

`!= unrestricted cancellation`.

Judgment: **strong witness for conditional normative scope and exception power**.

### 3.3 UNIDROIT — permission and obligation are different coordinates

Articles 6.1.14-6.1.17 are especially strong because public permission and contractual obligation are simultaneously present in one legal structure.

A party can be obligated to seek public permission while the public authority has not yet granted it. If no decision arrives, a termination entitlement may arise; if permission is refused, consequences depend on whether validity or performance is affected.

Therefore:

`obligation to obtain/seek permission`

`!= permission granted`

`!= permission pending`

`!= permission refused`

`!= final contract consequence`.

Judgment: **direct witness against a single global normative-status value**.

## 4. Functional prosecution/defence/judgment result

LAW-006 also sharpens the functional `P/D/J` model from LAW-002.

Suppose the attribution/prosecution function `P` advances:

`Applies(r,tau) and Forbidden(r,tau)`.

The defence function `D` can defeat this route in several non-equivalent ways:

- the rule does not apply to this actor/action/context;
- an exception or exclusion condition applies;
- an exemption or waiver removes the asserted duty;
- a superior or special rule modifies the result;
- a required condition of prohibition is not established.

Crucially:

`P fails to establish prohibition`

is not identical to

`D establishes global affirmative permission`.

The judgment function `J` must preserve that distinction.

This is the normative-law analogue of the earlier result:

`failure to prove guilt != proof of factual innocence`.

## 5. Typed normative carrier

The bare physical act is too coarse as the application object.

Use a typed normative instance such as:

`tau = (actor, action, role, context, time, purpose, regime)`.

The normative rule is separately retained:

`r in R`.

The source-side question becomes:

`Applies(r,tau)?`

before asking whether the rule yields obligation, permission, prohibition, exception, exemption, or another consequence.

This avoids artificial contradictions where the same physical behavior has different status for different actors, purposes, times, or jurisdictions.

## 6. DSD Formation comparison

Formation is useful because it preserves:

- typed identity;
- partiality;
- staged formation;
- role information;
- absent versus defined states;
- non-totalization across channels/regimes.

However:

`legal permission != Formation admission`

`legal obligation != Formation realization`

`legal prohibition != channel absence`

`legal exemption != defined zero`.

A prohibited act can exist, be performed, be fully described, and still be prohibited. Therefore encoding prohibition as absence would erase the regulated event.

Judgment: **strong structural correspondence after explicit normative encoding; no semantic identity**.

## 7. Norm conflict boundary

A further negative result is important.

If two source rules yield:

`Obligatory(r1,tau)`

and

`Forbidden(r2,tau)`,

DSD Analysis is not entitled to repair the source conflict by choosing one value.

The application must retain both rule identities until a source-side rule supplies:

- hierarchy;
- priority;
- exception;
- lex-specialis relation;
- temporal priority;
- jurisdictional boundary;
- another conflict rule.

Thus DSD's non-totalization discipline applies not only to missing values but also to unresolved normative conflicts.

## 8. Most important negative result

The word `permission` itself is not one universal DSD status.

Different sources may distinguish:

- affirmative/strong permission;
- mere absence of prohibition;
- licence/authorization granted by an authority;
- right/liberty;
- exemption from a duty;
- exception from the scope of a prohibition;
- temporary derogation power.

Treating all of these as one `PERMITTED = 1` channel would be under-typed.

Likewise `EXEMPT` cannot be a universal opposite of `OBLIGATORY` or `PROHIBITED`.

## 9. Relation to LAW-001 through LAW-005

LAW-001: nonaction/nonproof could not be converted into an opposite positive act/fact.

LAW-002: one regime's gap could not be filled by another regime's desired positive result.

LAW-003: one person/relation did not generate every authority/effect.

LAW-004: evidence existence/submission did not generate usability/proof/finding.

LAW-005: act/defect did not determine downstream validity/effect without a consequence rule.

LAW-006 generalizes the same discipline to normativity:

**an action's factual possibility or one rule's status does not determine a global normative value; rule applicability and the source's normative consequence relation must be preserved.**

## 10. Predefinition result

The case rejects these universal predefinitions:

`possible -> permitted`;

`not permitted -> prohibited`;

`permitted -> optional only`;

`obligatory and permitted are disjoint`;

`exception -> opposite value`;

`exemption -> affirmative permission`;

`derogation -> norm deleted`;

`conflict -> choose one global status`.

The surviving rule is:

**preserve actor, action, role, context, time, governing rule, applicability, and normative consequence separately; infer a normative status only through the source rule or logic that actually licenses it.**

## 11. Generalization status

- Universal candidate: **supported by several independent international/transnational witness families and survives formal deontic counterpressure in narrowed form**.
- Universal algebra of obligation/permission/prohibition/exemption: **not established and explicitly rejected as premature**.
- Direct contradiction with Formation: **none found**.
- Formation mapping: **meaningful after typed rule-sensitive encoding**.
- Axis-Property required: **no**.
- Static Aggregation required: **no**.
- Dynamics required: **not for first-pass status analysis**; relevant later for repeal, waiver, derogation, expiry, or revocation.
- Main result class: **external coherence node + structural reinterpretation + predefinition/rule-scope audit**.

## 12. Boundary statement

LAW-006 does not derive legal obligations from DSD, does not prove one universal deontic logic, does not claim that every legal system defines permission or exemption identically, and does not resolve conflicts of law or norms without a source-side priority rule.

It establishes a narrower cross-system result: the selected witness families independently require enough distinction among applicability, obligation, permission, prohibition, exception/exemption, derogation, and consequence to defeat a total context-free normative-status encoding, while those distinctions can coexist with Formation under an explicit typed bridge.
