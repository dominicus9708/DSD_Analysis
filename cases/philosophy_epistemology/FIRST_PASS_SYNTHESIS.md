## 4. Cross-case structural recurrence

The first sequence repeatedly encountered a small set of structural mechanisms.

### A. Reduced/internal equality does not imply full-structure identity

Appears in:

- PHIL-002 hidden trust-property identification;
- PHIL-004 narrow/internal state versus broad reference;
- PHIL-005 accessible projection versus full world.

### B. A new description/record does not automatically imply a new target/entity/fact

Appears most clearly in PHIL-003 Argument 1.

### C. One-time state does not automatically propagate across time

Appears in PHIL-003 Argument 2 and the recent-envatment boundary of PHIL-005.

### D. Property/signature/regime must be declared before equality or attribution is compared

Appears in:

- PHIL-002 bearer/type and trust-property status;
- PHIL-004 Semantic-Signature Fork;
- PHIL-005 Equivalence-Signature Audit and ordinary-English/vat-English semantic regimes.

### E. Constitutive relation is not the same as inverse reconstruction

Appears especially in PHIL-004 and PHIL-005.

An external environment can be part of what constitutes a relational property without the internal descriptor uniquely reconstructing that environment.

### F. Strong conclusions require explicit bridges

Repeated bridge types include:

- modal/completion bridge;
- hidden-property identification/injectivity bridge;
- epistemic-record -> world-fact-target bridge;
- snapshot -> diachronic update/access bridge;
- linguistic reference -> broad mental content bridge;
- electrical-interface -> complete subject-level equivalence bridge;
- sentence-level semantic result -> object-level disquotation/status bridge.

This bridge discipline is the most stable cross-case operational signature of the first sequence.

## 5. What the first sequence does and does not support

### Supported, conservatively

The first philosophy sequence supports describing DSD Analysis as a **self-correcting, externally calibrated structural audit framework** that can:

- preserve failed attacks;
- reproduce established distinctions without novelty inflation;
- identify rare precedent-convergence cases;
- make some prospective structural predictions;
- discriminate a small hidden-control baseline;
- expose missing signatures, reconstruction assumptions, time propagation assumptions, and bridge premises;
- keep one source case's multiple layers separate rather than force one verdict.

### Not supported

The sequence does **not** establish that:

- Formation or Axis-Property axioms are ontologically true because external cases resemble them;
- DSD is historically prior to the convergent external arguments;
- every classical thought experiment is defective;
- one aggregate success percentage is meaningful;
- the Mode-D small baseline generalizes to real philosophical literature;
- any unaudited formal extension is historically novel.

## 6. Multi-result recording rule

PHIL-005 makes this rule explicit.

One source case may legitimately yield several retained results, for example:

- precedent convergence;
- implementation-bridge audit;
- empirical comparison layer;
- historical objection convergence.

These should all be recorded because collapsing them loses information.

However:

`multiple retained results from one source case != multiple independent evidence cases`.

Independence is assigned by genuinely distinct external formal families or structural mechanisms, not by the number of headings produced by the audit.
