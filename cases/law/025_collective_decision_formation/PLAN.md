# LAW-014 Plan — Collective Decision Formation and Institutional Attribution

Status: active first-pass cross-jurisdiction analysis.

## Question

When members cast individual votes or positions, how does a source system form a collective body's decision and, where applicable, a later institutional act? Is the collective result a simple sum of individual preferences, or a source-rule-generated status with quorum, threshold, weighting, participation and multi-body conditions?

## Strong candidates to attack

- each individual YES directly creates collective adoption;
- collective decision universally equals simple arithmetic majority;
- quorum is the same rule as approval threshold;
- abstention is identical to a NO vote;
- abstention is identical to nonparticipation;
- passage by one body automatically creates the final institutional act;
- an institutional act implies every member personally endorsed it;
- the largest preference subgroup determines institutional attribution;
- collective decision can be represented by one universal scalar sum without source-specific voting rules.

## Witness families

- UN General Assembly Charter Article 18 and Rules of Procedure 83-87;
- UN Security Council Charter Article 27 and official voting-system explanation;
- Council of the European Union qualified-majority voting rules;
- US Constitution Article I Sections 5 and 7.

## Method

Analyze source decision rules first. Only after the source-side distinctions are fixed compare them with Formation and Static Aggregation. Do not design a DSD voting theory merely to reproduce the sources.
