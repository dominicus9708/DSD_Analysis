## Immediate source gaps exposed by the matrix

1. **Advertising / commercial speech / public-channel occupancy**: add legal, media-economics, attention-economy, and outdoor-advertising research.
2. **Economic vulnerability and livelihood speech**: add street-vending, informal economy, labor voice, and proportional-regulation literature.
3. **Korean law**: add Constitutional Court/Supreme Court and statutes on expression, commercial advertising, solicitation, religious activity, minors, and platform communication where relevant.
4. **Comparative captive-audience doctrine**: expand beyond U.S. law before making general normative claims.
5. **Education**: separate formal compulsory schooling, critical thinking, media literacy, civic education, and epistemic vigilance.
6. **Child development psychology**: add empirical developmental work alongside rights frameworks.
7. **Representation**: add advocacy-group legitimacy, interest-group representation, survey nonresponse, agenda-setting, and media amplification studies.
8. **Digital publicness**: add algorithmic amplification, recommender systems, context collapse, harassment/blocking, and platform governance.
9. **Lexical audit**: retrieve exact official Korean `자유` headword and primary/historical dictionary evidence for additional languages.
10. **Novelty**: search for integrated free-speech frameworks that already combine speaker/listener capability, power asymmetry, audience uptake, exit, publicness, and developmental protection.

## Publication rule

A manuscript sentence should not cite this matrix as evidence. The matrix is an audit index. Every retained manuscript claim must point to the underlying primary/secondary source and state whether the source is theoretical, empirical, legal/normative, lexical, or an internal DSD structural result.
