# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiment 43

Date: 2026-09-03

## Experiment 43 — Inventory audit of dimensionful anchors in current DSD structures

### Objective

Audit the currently defined transition contribution, property value, constitutive/relaxation coefficient, residual magnitude, hyperbolic boundary flux, and static normalization to determine whether any of them provides a source-independent absolute \(M/L\) anchor that is not reducible to source \(M,L,c_{\rm info}\) or a freely supplied constitutive amplitude.

## 1. Transition contribution

A cross-transition balance may take the form

\[
C(S^+)-C(S^-)=J^C.
\]

A dimensionful \(J^C\) could serve as an anchor only if an independent structural law uniquely determines its absolute value.
The balance relation alone does not do so.

**Verdict: DIMENSIONALLY POSSIBLE / NOT INTERNALLY FIXED.**

## 2. Constitutive operator and relaxation coefficient

Typed property data can be mapped to dynamic coefficients or operators through a constitutive dynamic bridge.
If two admissible bridges on the same inherited typed state assign different absolute operator amplitudes, the static/property state does not identify a unique normalization.

**Verdict: NOT AN INTRINSIC ANCHOR.**

## 3. Property value

A particular property carrier may contain dimensionful values.
However, if the property value is supplied primitive/model data, its numerical magnitude is not universally selected by bounded formation itself.

**Verdict: POSSIBLE INPUT ANCHOR, NOT A DERIVED UNIVERSAL ANCHOR.**

## 4. Typed residual and residual norm

Residuals are defined relative to a target/reference relation and a compatible residual carrier.
In a linear carrier one may write \(r=z-z_*\), but the target \(z_*\), norm or metric, and any scalar weighting/normalization are additional structure.

Therefore residual magnitude is not a new absolute scale unless that calibration is independently physical and already fixed.

**Verdict: DIAGNOSTIC, NOT AN ABSOLUTE ANCHOR BY ITSELF.**

## 5. Hyperbolic boundary flux

In a symmetric-hyperbolic specialization, a localized-energy boundary flux depends on the coefficient matrices and represented field amplitude.
Under a common rescaling

\[
H\to\lambda H,
\qquad
HA^r\to\lambda HA^r,
\qquad
B\to\lambda B,
\]

the principal symbol and characteristic cone can remain unchanged while the energy/flux magnitude rescales.

Thus support propagation or characteristic speed does not identify the absolute flux normalization.

**Verdict: PROPAGATION-SHAPE INPUT, NOT AN ABSOLUTE NORMALIZATION.**

## 6. Static aggregate and normalized weights

Normalized analytic weights can fix a within-channel representation convention, but they do not independently fix the physical calibration of the local field, measure, or downstream property bridge.

**Verdict: DESCRIPTIVE/ANALYTIC NORMALIZATION ONLY.**

## Combined result

The current dimensionful candidates fall into two classes:

1. quantities derived from source \(M,L,c_{\rm info}\), which retain source-specific scaling;
2. absolute amplitudes supplied by property, constitutive, transition, residual, or representation data.

No source-independent dimensionful amplitude uniquely selected by the present bounded/formation structure has been identified.

\[
\boxed{\text{current DSD structural data do not yet break the absolute-scale degeneracy}}
\]

This conclusion follows from the actual map, transition, residual, and operator freedoms rather than from manuscript scope statements.

## Next step

Separate two research paths:

### A. Close the current-system result as a no-go theorem candidate

Show under explicit assumptions that scale-invariant formation/property/static/dynamic data cannot identify a nonzero-degree absolute normalization.

### B. Minimal structural-gravity physical specialization

Introduce at most one genuinely non-homogeneous physical law or calibration parameter and audit whether it merely renames \(G\).
Then test whether the remaining structure predicts, independently of that one calibration:

- radial distance dependence;
- source additivity / mass exponent;
- density and bounded-structure corrections;
- finite-propagation transients;
- near/far-field structural-information erasure.
