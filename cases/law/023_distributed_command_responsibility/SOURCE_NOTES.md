# LAW-012 Source Notes

## 1. ICTY — effective control is not title identity

ICTY command-responsibility jurisprudence repeatedly treats effective control as the material ability to prevent or punish subordinate criminal conduct. Formal or de jure authority may be evidence, but is not identical to effective control.

Relevant official examples:

- *Hadžihasanović and Kubura* decision on indictment: de jure power is not synonymous with effective control; effective control must be pleaded as a material fact.
- *Čelebići* appeal summary: de facto command can suffice if the relevant degree of control exists.
- *Blaškić* appeal summary: command responsibility failed where effective control over the units was not proven.
- *Halilović* judgment summary: high rank and influence fell short of the effective-control standard where the prosecution failed to prove command over the perpetrators.

Structural witness:

`formal rank/title != effective control != command responsibility`.

## 2. ICC — several individual responsibility routes can coexist

Current ICC situation materials identify distinct individual responsibility modes, including commission jointly/through others, ordering, and failure to exercise proper control over forces under command.

Structural witness:

`direct/indirect commission != ordering != command-responsibility omission`.

These are related but not one scalar responsibility value.

## 3. ILC State Responsibility — attribution is its own rule family

ILC Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts distinguish:

- Art. 4: conduct of a State organ is attributable to the State under international law;
- Art. 7: an organ/person/entity acting in governmental capacity can have conduct attributed to the State even when exceeding authority or contravening instructions;
- Art. 8: conduct of a person or group can be attributed where it acts on State instructions, direction or control;
- Art. 58: the State-responsibility articles are without prejudice to individual responsibility under international law.

Structural witness:

`individual responsibility != State attribution/responsibility`.

And:

`excess/contravention of authority != automatic non-attribution`.

## 4. Source-side separation to preserve

The witness families jointly require at least the possibility of distinguishing:

- direct underlying conduct;
- actor identity;
- command/superior relation;
- effective control;
- knowledge/required mental or notice state;
- omission to prevent/repress/refer;
- individual mode of liability;
- official/governmental capacity;
- State-attribution rule;
- State responsibility;
- organization/collective relation where independently supplied.

Do not infer one from another without the source rule.
