# LAW-005 Contradiction Audit

## Candidate C1 — formation and validity may coincide, so the staged model is false

Rejected as a direct contradiction.

A source rule may make conclusion and ordinary binding effect coincide in a simple case. The universal candidate does not require every stage to be temporally or procedurally separate in every regime. It requires only that distinct source statuses not be collapsed when the source itself distinguishes them.

UNIDROIT Article 3.1.2 is useful counterpressure here: mere agreement may conclude a contract. This narrows the universal claim and blocks any theory that insists on a mandatory long formation ladder.

## Candidate C2 — a defect always means invalidity

Rejected as overgeneralization.

UNIDROIT Article 3.3.1 expressly makes the consequence of mandatory-rule infringement dependent first on what the mandatory rule prescribes, and otherwise on a reasonableness analysis.

Therefore the universal map

`defect -> one invalid status`

is not supported.

## Candidate C3 — avoidance means the contract never existed

Rejected.

UNIDROIT separately regulates concluded contracts and grounds for later avoidance, confirmation, loss of the right to avoid, notice, and time limits. The legal act and the later remedial status must therefore be preserved separately in this witness system.

## Candidate C4 — lack of present effect equals nonformation

Rejected.

UNIDROIT conditions provide a source case in which the relevant contract or obligation may have its effect depend on a future uncertain event. Pending effect is not automatically the same as absence of the legal relation.

## Candidate C5 — refusal of enforcement means no legal act or decision existed

Rejected.

UNCITRAL Model Law Articles 35-36 distinguish award, binding recognition, enforcement, refusal, suspension, and setting-aside-related statuses.

A downstream refusal cannot faithfully be encoded as deletion of the source award event.

## Candidate C6 — CISG contradicts the model because it does not govern validity

Rejected.

The exclusion is itself useful boundary evidence. CISG Article 4 explicitly separates formation from validity by governing one and generally leaving the other outside the Convention's scope.

The DSD analysis must respect that boundary rather than fill the missing validity rules from the CISG formation rules.

## Candidate C7 — every legal defect can be represented as DSD undefinedness

Rejected as an application error.

A legal defect can be a positively defined legal status with specific consequences. `void`, `avoidable`, `suspended`, or `recognition refused` cannot automatically be represented as undefinedness or absence.

## Candidate C8 — legal effectiveness is literally Formation realization

Rejected.

The selected legal instruments supply normative consequence rules. Formation provides a structural vocabulary for preserving staged and partial distinctions, but it does not generate legal validity or effectiveness.

## Candidate C9 — temporal change contradicts static Formation identity

Rejected as a layer error.

The same legal act can have different legal status at different times under an external transition rule. Static descriptors at `t1` and `t2` can differ. Formation does not derive the transition; an application transition relation or later dynamics analysis is needed.

## Final contradiction result

No direct contradiction with the Formation Axiom System was found after act identity, legal stage, regime, defect type, consequence rule, and time were kept distinct.

The strongest counterpressure forced a narrower universal claim:

**the universal object is not a fixed sequence of legal stages and not a universal taxonomy of voidness; it is the requirement to preserve source-distinct statuses and rule-indexed consequences without premature collapse.**
