# Plan — LING-004 / Global Case 017

## Question
Can linguistic ambiguity be represented without collapsing `multiple legitimate interpretations` into either one preselected meaning or an undefined value?

## External source problem
Ambiguity is treated in philosophy of language and linguistics as a sign or sentence supporting multiple legitimate interpretations, distinct from vagueness, context sensitivity, and mere underspecification.

Primary external reference:
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Ambiguity”: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ambiguity/

## DSD targets
1. Formation staged distinction between candidate expression and downstream assignment.
2. Primitive Axiom V single-valued partial assignment.
3. Channel identity as typed tuple `(p,a,lambda,v,rho)`.
4. Predefinition audit: does unresolved ambiguity license arbitrary selection of one interpretation?

## Finite witnesses
A. Lexical ambiguity: one surface token `bank` with two legitimate lexical interpretations.
B. Scope ambiguity: `Every woman squeezed a man` with two different quantifier-scope readings.

## Stress questions
- Is ambiguity the same as undefinedness? Expected: no.
- Can one exact DSD assignment input carry two values simultaneously? Expected: no, not under the current partial-function rule.
- Can DSD encode ambiguity by separating interpretation candidates into distinct configurations/channels/tags? Expected: yes, after explicit application encoding.
- Does later disambiguation prove that the selected interpretation was already uniquely fixed beforehand? Expected: no.

## Expected classification
Primary: structural reinterpretation.
Secondary: coherence / predefinition / representation-boundary analysis.
