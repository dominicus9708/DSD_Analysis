## Experiment 70 — Two non-equivalent formulations

The previously introduced shell-conserved coarse flux \(J_q\) and the geometric distortion slope \(s_X=-\nabla X\) should not be silently identified. Two clean formulations are possible.

### Flux-primary formulation

\[
\text{source}\to J_q\to X_J\to B_{aJ}\to\mathbf a,
\]

where \(X_J\) is introduced only if \(J_q\) admits a scalar potential.

### Geometry-primary formulation

\[
\text{source}\to X\to -\nabla X,
\]

followed by separate bridges to a conserved exterior flux and to physical acceleration.

The flux-primary route has fewer constitutive steps but gives \(X_J\) only the status of a flux potential until a geometric-identification argument is supplied. The geometry-primary route preserves an independently meaningful structural-distortion field but carries a larger bridge burden.

## Experiment 71 — Potential-existence criterion

For a vector flux \(J_q\) on a metric spatial carrier, a local scalar potential satisfying

\[
J_q=-\nabla X_J
\]

requires the associated one-form to be closed:

\[
d(J_q^\flat)=0.
\]

Global existence additionally requires the relevant closed form to be exact on the exterior domain. In a smooth spherically symmetric radial exterior field, the curl-free condition is automatic, so a scalar flux potential exists up to an additive constant.

If \(J_q\) carries a persistent rotational/anisotropic component, a scalar \(X_J\) cannot encode the entire exterior flux.

## Experiment 72 — Shell conservation gives the inverse-distance flux potential

For a spherically symmetric shell-conserved coarse flux,

\[
4\pi r^2J_r(r)=Q,
\]

so

\[
J_q(r)=\frac{Q}{4\pi r^2}\hat r.
\]

If the flux potential is defined by

\[
J_q=-\nabla X_J,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{X_J(r)=\frac{Q}{4\pi r}+C}
\]

on the exterior radial domain.

This obtains the \(r^{-1}\) potential shape without an additional \(J=-\lambda\nabla X\) constitutive coefficient because the potential relation is now definitional. It does not by itself establish that \(X_J\) is the independently postulated geometric distortion field.

## Experiment 73 — Normalization degeneracy survives the flux-potential route

Under

\[
Q\to\gamma Q,
\qquad
J_q\to\gamma J_q,
\qquad
X_J\to\gamma X_J,
\]

an acceleration bridge

\[
\mathbf a=\kappa_JJ_q
\]

produces the same physical response if

\[
\kappa_J\to\kappa_J/\gamma.
\]

Thus defining a flux potential removes one intermediate constitutive coefficient but does not solve the absolute normalization problem.

If independent-source additivity gives \(Q(M)=\alpha M\), then

\[
\mathbf a(r)=\frac{\kappa_J\alpha}{4\pi}\frac{M}{r^2}\hat r
\]

under the stated conditional assumptions, and only the product \(\kappa_J\alpha\) is physically relevant until one factor is independently normalized.

## Experiment 74 — Geometry-primary conservation equation

If \(X\) is retained as an independently meaningful structural-distortion field, introduce a constitutive flux bridge

\[
J_q=-\lambda_X(X,\ldots)\nabla X.
\]

Exterior conservation gives

\[
\nabla\cdot\bigl(\lambda_X\nabla X\bigr)=0.
\]

Under spherical symmetry,

\[
r^2\lambda_X(r)X'(r)=\text{constant}.
\]

Therefore an inverse-distance \(X\) and inverse-square slope follow only when \(\lambda_X\) becomes asymptotically scale-neutral. A nontrivial radial scaling of \(\lambda_X\) changes the distortion exponent.

## Experiment 75 — Scalar distortion as an emergent coarse mediator

If the exterior flux decomposes as

\[
J_q=-\lambda_X\nabla X+J_\perp
\]

with a rotational or otherwise non-potential detail component \(J_\perp\), a scalar distortion field cannot be a complete exterior descriptor while \(J_\perp\) remains physically relevant.

A scalar-potential structural-gravity description can nevertheless emerge in a far-field regime if the non-potential/detail sector becomes response-invisible or vanishes under the exterior coarse map.

## Verdict

- shell-conserved spherical flux \(\Rightarrow r^{-2}\) flux: **CONDITIONALLY_CONFIRMED**.
- radial curl-free flux \(\Rightarrow\) scalar potential exists: **CONFIRMED under regularity/domain conditions**.
- defining \(X_J\) as the flux potential removes one constitutive bridge: **CONFIRMED as a formulation choice**.
- flux potential automatically equals independently physical geometric distortion: **UNRESOLVED**.
- flux-potential formulation solves absolute normalization: **REJECTED**.
- persistent non-potential flux detail is representable by one scalar \(X\): **REJECTED in general**.

## Current fork

The research line should keep both formulations distinct until one is selected by further structure:

1. **Flux-primary:** mathematically leaner; \(X_J\) is a potential representation of the coarse exterior flux.
2. **Geometry-primary:** physically stronger geometric interpretation; requires a source-to-geometry bridge and a geometry-to-flux/response bridge.
