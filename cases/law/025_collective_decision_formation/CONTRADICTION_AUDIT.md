# LAW-014 Contradiction Audit

## 1. Collective decision universally equals simple majority sum

Rejected.

UN General Assembly important/ordinary questions use different thresholds; the EU Council uses qualified, unanimity and simple-majority systems; qualified majority includes member-state and population conditions plus blocking-minority rules; UN Security Council non-procedural decisions include permanent-member voting effects.

## 2. Abstention is identical to opposition

Rejected.

UN General Assembly rules exclude abstentions from `present and voting`; UN Security Council practice allows a permanent-member abstention without veto; EU qualified-majority rules count abstention against attainment/blocking but explicitly distinguish abstention from nonparticipation, while EU unanimity allows abstention without preventing adoption.

Thus a downstream counting effect does not rewrite the input identity.

## 3. Quorum equals approval threshold

Rejected.

US Constitution Article I Section 5 treats quorum as capacity to do business. Article I Section 7 separately supplies the legislative passage/presentment rules.

## 4. One body's adoption automatically forms the final institutional act

Rejected.

US federal legislation requires House and Senate passage plus presentment and source-defined presidential/override paths.

## 5. Institutional act means every member personally endorsed it

Rejected.

All examined systems permit institutional decisions despite dissent, abstention, or other non-affirmative individual positions when the governing rule is satisfied.

`institutional attribution != personal endorsement by each member`.

## 6. The largest preference group always determines institutional output

Rejected.

EU double-majority/blocking rules, Security Council permanent-member veto structure and multi-stage US legislation show that raw largest-group status is insufficient.

## 7. DSD comparison

### Formation

Useful for preserving typed individual inputs, participation/quorum states, body-level results, and final institutional-act states.

Do not identify:

- individual vote with collective Formation composition;
- abstention with defined zero merely because it can have no affirmative contribution;
- quorum absence with channel absence by identity;
- institutional act with unanimity among component roles.

### Static Aggregation

This case gives the strongest temptation to use Static Aggregation because voting systems contain counts, percentages and weighted thresholds.

The mapping is still not automatic.

Source rules define the actual aggregation/decision operator. EU qualified majority is a Boolean decision rule over multiple conditions, not one universal linear weighted sum. US bicameralism/presentment is a staged institutional rule, not a single sum.

Therefore:

`legal/institutional voting operator != DSD Static Aggregation operator by identity`.

### Axis-Property

Not required. Veto status, member-state population weight and institutional hierarchy do not establish realized axes.

### Dynamics

May represent multi-stage temporal decision states but does not generate quorum, voting, veto or presentment rules.

## 8. Direct contradiction

No direct DSD axiom contradiction was found after source-defined collective-formation operators were kept external.

The main DSD application risk is treating composition/aggregation as if the source's collective act were a generic sum of individual declarations.
