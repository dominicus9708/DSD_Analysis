# LAW-008 Result — Rule Conflict, Exception, Priority, and Resolution

Status: first-pass universal analysis complete.

Primary purpose: reinterpretation.

Secondary purposes: coherence, predefinition audit, conflict-resolution boundary analysis, universal-claim falsification.

## 1. Answer-first result

No direct contradiction was found between the selected international-law conflict structures and the DSD Formation distinctions after rule identity, subject, act/issue, applicability, scope, parties, time, regime, exception conditions and source-supplied conflict rules were typed separately.

The initial roadmap phrase `rule conflict -> exception/priority -> one result` is too coarse as a universal model.

The stronger surviving universal candidate is:

**a difference between rules is not yet an unresolved legal contradiction, and a conflict does not determine one universal legal consequence. Source-side applicability, interpretation, harmonization, speciality, hierarchy, temporal/party relations, invalidity and any residual unresolved conflict must be preserved until the source system supplies the relation that governs them.**

## 2. Counterpressure result — what counts as conflict

The ILC Fragmentation Conclusions distinguish:

- relationships of interpretation, where one norm assists application/clarification/modification of another and both can be applied together;
- relationships of conflict, where two valid and applicable norms point to incompatible decisions so that a choice must be made.

The same conclusions also state a harmonization principle: where several norms bear on a single issue, they should, to the extent possible, be interpreted so as to produce compatible obligations.

Therefore LAW-008 rejects:

`different wording -> contradiction`;

`same broad subject matter -> contradiction`;

`two relevant norms -> contradiction`.

A conflict candidate begins only after the relevant source rules are valid/applicable to the same typed issue and their outputs remain incompatible after the source's interpretation/harmonization structure is respected.

## 3. Witness 1 — VCLT Article 30: no context-free later-rule ordering

Article 30 of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties regulates successive treaties concerning the same subject matter.

Its structure is already strongly indexed:

- it is subject to UN Charter Article 103;
- an express treaty clause can make one treaty subject to another;
- where all earlier-treaty parties are also parties to the later treaty, the earlier treaty continues to apply to the extent compatible with the later treaty;
- where party sets differ, the governing treaty relation differs depending on which States are in the bilateral/mutual relation.

Therefore:

`later treaty > earlier treaty`

is not a source-faithful global value independent of parties, provisions and compatibility.

A more faithful object is:

`Priority/Compatibility(T1,T2,party_relation,subject,time)`.

Judgment: **strong witness for party- and relation-indexed conflict resolution**.

## 4. Witness 2 — UN Charter Article 103: priority is not automatically invalidity

Article 103 states that when a UN Member's Charter obligations conflict with obligations under another international agreement, the Charter obligations prevail.

LAW-008 records exactly the source relation:

`Charter obligation prevails in the conflict`.

It does not silently rewrite this as:

`the other treaty never existed`;

`the other treaty is globally void`;

`the other treaty is absent from every other legal relation`.

Judgment: **strong witness that conflict can yield a priority relation without one universal deletion operator**.

## 5. Witness 3 — VCLT Article 53: some conflicts yield invalidity

Article 53 provides a different source consequence. A treaty is void if, at the time of conclusion, it conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law under the Article's definition.

Thus the selected sources contain at least:

`conflict -> priority`

and

`conflict -> invalidity`.

Therefore there is no single universal DSD/legal value named `CONFLICTED` from which one downstream effect can be inferred without retaining the governing source rule.

Judgment: **strong independent witness for consequence non-totalization**.

## 6. Witness 4 — ILC lex specialis: special governance is not general-law erasure

The ILC treats `lex specialis derogat legi generali` as a generally accepted international-law technique of interpretation and conflict resolution, while emphasizing contextual appreciation.

Crucially, it states that application of special law does not normally extinguish the relevant general law. The general law may remain valid/applicable as interpretive background and for situations not covered by the special rule.

Therefore:

`SpecialGoverns(r_s,r_g,tau)`

is not identical to:

`Deleted(r_g)`.

Judgment: **strong witness against encoding exception/speciality as source-rule absence**.

## 7. Finite witness result

Six states were constructed:

1. two rules differ but harmonize;
2. special rule governs while general rule remains;
3. Charter obligation prevails over conflicting treaty obligation;
4. treaty conflicts with jus cogens and is void;
5. later treaty governs only to the extent of incompatibility with an earlier treaty for common parties;
6. the same treaty pair yields different governing relations for different party pairs.

No context-free encoding such as

`{NO_CONFLICT, R1_WINS, R2_WINS, BOTH_INVALID}`

preserves all six.

The selected sources therefore require a typed relation package rather than one global conflict bit/output.

## 8. Functional prosecution/defence/judgment result

Suppose the attribution/prosecution function `P` relies on rule `r1` to establish a prohibited or required legal status.

The defence function `D` may attack different links:

- `r1` is not applicable to this typed instance;
- an exception removes the instance from `r1`'s scope;
- `r2` is a source-recognized special rule;
- a hierarchy/priority rule governs the relation;
- the alleged conflict can be harmonized;
- the party/time relation changes which rule governs;
- the source itself leaves a genuine unresolved conflict.

Therefore:

`P proves existence of r1`

is not identical to

`P proves r1 controls the case after all conflict rules`.

And:

`r1 loses priority`

is not identical to

`r1 never existed`.

The judgment function `J` must preserve the chain:

`rule existence -> applicability -> conflict relation -> source resolution -> case consequence`.

## 9. DSD Formation comparison

The Formation paper supplies a useful structural discipline because it independently distinguishes typed stages, partial assignments, role-bearing channel identity, undefined assignment, defined zero, channel absence and zero contribution.

The project source also states that failure of channel conditions yields channel absence rather than zero, that a defined zero can still support a channel, and that multiple roles can yield distinct channels.

LAW-008 uses only those source-supported distinctions to preserve legal rule/status identity.

It does not identify:

`legal rule validity != Formation admission`;

`legal applicability != Formation realization`;

`exception != channel absence`;

`priority loss != channel deletion`;

`jus-cogens invalidity != defined zero`;

`unresolved legal conflict != DSD undefined assignment`.

A source-side unresolved conflict is itself legal information and should be encoded as such rather than erased because the DSD layer cannot decide it.

Judgment: **meaningful correspondence after explicit typed legal encoding; no semantic identity**.

## 10. Most important negative result

The word `conflict` must not be used as one universal downstream legal status.

The selected sources distinguish at least:

- apparent difference;
- harmonized co-application;
- scope exception;
- special-over-general governance;
- priority of one obligation;
- provision-level compatibility/incompatibility;
- party-indexed governing relation;
- invalidity;
- potentially unresolved conflict.

These are not interchangeable.

## 11. Cross-case generalization

LAW-001 through LAW-007 showed that actions, proof states, roles, evidence states, defect consequences, normative statuses, and legal powers cannot be silently promoted, demoted, activated, erased or globally normalized.

LAW-008 adds:

**even when two source rules are both present, their relationship and the legal consequence of incompatibility must not be pre-assigned. The relation itself is a source-governed status.**

The domain-level rule becomes:

**preserve source-defined identity, applicability, scope, relation, priority and consequence separately; resolve a legal relationship only through the source rule that actually licenses harmonization, exception, priority, invalidity, suspension or another result.**

## 12. DSD layer boundary

- Formation: sufficient for first-pass typed separation.
- Axis-Property: not required; legal hierarchy is not realized-axis geometry.
- Static Aggregation: not required; priority is not an additive numerical weight.
- Dynamics: not required for first pass; later useful for repeal, succession, suspension, reactivation and temporal transitions.

- Universal conflict-resolution algorithm: **not established and explicitly rejected**.
- Direct contradiction with Formation: **none found**.
- Formation mapping: **meaningful after typed source-sensitive encoding**.

## 14. Boundary statement

LAW-008 does not derive lex specialis, lex posterior, Charter priority, jus cogens or any legal hierarchy from DSD. It does not claim all legal systems define conflict identically or that all conflicts are resolvable.

It establishes a narrower result: the selected sources independently require enough distinction among difference, applicability, harmonization, speciality, priority, party/time relation, invalidity and residual conflict to defeat a context-free conflict value, while those distinctions coexist with Formation under an explicit typed bridge.
