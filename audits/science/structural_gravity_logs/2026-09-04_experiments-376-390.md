## Experiment 376 — The field-driven anisotropy pattern is radial, not a fixed global quadrupole

For a spherical progression field \(\psi=\psi(r)\),

\[
G_\psi
:=
[\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi]^{\rm TF}
=
\psi'(r)^2
\left(n\otimes n-\frac13I\right).
\]

Therefore a local axis flow driven by \(G_\psi\) naturally generates a **radially aligned axis second moment**, not the fixed-orientation tensor \(a(r)S\) used in Experiments 356–363.

This distinction matters because the radial tensor is rotationally covariant as a field and can affect the spherically symmetric ground mode at first order.

## Experiment 377 — Energy-descending projector-flow sign

The weak axis-metric contribution to the progression-field gradient energy is

\[
E_{\psi A}^{(1)}
=-\frac{\beta_A\mu_0c_*^2}{2}
\int \mathcal A^{ij}G_{\psi,ij}\,d^3x.
\]

For one rank-one projector \(P\), use the represented reorientation law

\[
\dot P=[\Omega,P].
\]

If the axis dynamics is chosen to descend the above coupling energy, the natural sign is

\[
\boxed{
\Omega=-\eta[P,G_\psi],
\qquad \eta>0.
}
\]

Holding \(G_\psi\) fixed instantaneously,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{tr}(PG_\psi)
=-\eta\operatorname{tr}([P,G_\psi]^2)
=\eta\|[P,G_\psi]\|_F^2\ge0.
\]

Thus this sign aligns realized axes with the largest-eigenvalue direction of \(G_\psi\), i.e. with the local progression-field gradient.

The commutator fixes the reorientation direction; the mobility \(\eta\) remains constitutive.

## Experiment 378 — Radial axis-anisotropy family

Write

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(r)
=a(r)
\left(n\otimes n-\frac13I\right)

with \(0\le a(r)\le1\). Then

\[

\[
q_r=\frac{1+2a}{3},

so it is a valid normalized axis second moment.

For

\[
h_A=e^{\beta_A\mathcal A},

the radial inverse-metric coefficient is

\[
\boxed{

For a radial transformed field \(U(r)\), the threshold eigenproblem becomes

\[
-\frac1{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}
\left(r^2e^{-2\beta_Aa/3}\frac{du}{dr}\right)
=\lambda(\beta_A)d(r)u.

## Experiment 379 — First-order radial-alignment theorem

At \(\beta_A=0\), for the spherical ground state \(u_0\), the Hellmann–Feynman derivative is

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1'(0)
=-\frac23
\frac{\int_0^1a(s)s^2u_0'(s)^2ds}
{\int_0^1d(s)s^2u_0(s)^2ds}.
}
\]

Therefore, if

\[
a(s)\ge0
\]

and is not identically zero,

\[
\boxed{\lambda_1'(0)<0},
\qquad
\boxed{\epsilon_c'(0)<0}.
\]

Thus **radial field-aligned axis anisotropy lowers the collapse threshold at first order**.

This is stronger than the fixed global quadrupole branch, whose first-order shift vanishes by angular orthogonality.

## Experiment 380 — Smooth admissible radial witness

To avoid the undefined radial direction at the center, choose

\[
a(s)=s^2(1-s^2).
\]

Then \(a(0)=a(1)=0\), the tensor field is smooth at the center, and \(0\le a\le1/4\).

For the uniform critical sphere,

\[
u_0(s)=\frac{\sin(\pi s/2)}{(\pi/2)s},
\qquad
\epsilon_0=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\]

The first-order threshold coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_c(\beta_A)
=\frac{\pi^2}{6}
-0.03429195957\,\beta_A
+O(\beta_A^2).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\epsilon_c'(0)
=
-\frac{14}{27}
-\frac{2\pi^2}{135}
+\frac{56}{9\pi^2}
\approx-0.03429195957.
\]

Thus a fully smooth, axis-second-moment-admissible radial anisotropy gives a definite destabilizing first-order shift.

## Experiment 382 — Why the field-driven flow has the destabilizing sign

Near a static transformed solution,

\[
\psi=-2\ln U
\]

implies

\[
[\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi]^{\rm TF}
=\frac{4}{U^2}
[\nabla U\otimes\nabla U]^{\rm TF}.
\]

Thus \(G_\psi\) and the critical-mode gradient tensor have the same eigendirections.

The energy-descending projector flow increases the axis second moment along the gradient direction. In the weak metric branch, that decreases the radial inverse stiffness \(h^{rr}\), and the Rayleigh quotient then decreases.

Therefore the locally field-aligned projector flow is **spectrally destabilizing** for \(\beta_A>0\) in the radial spherical branch.

## Experiment 383 — Rigid-frame obstruction

Axis reorientation does **not** automatically produce \(\mathcal A\neq0\).

If three equally weighted axes remain an orthonormal frame and rotate rigidly,

\[
P_i(t)=R(t)P_i(0)R(t)^T,
\]

then

\[
Q(t)=\frac13\sum_{i=1}^3P_i(t)=\frac13I
\]

for all \(t\), hence

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(t)=0}.
\]

Therefore the radial destabilization mechanism requires **relative axis reorientation, unequal weighting, nonorthogonality, or another mechanism that changes the second moment \(Q\)**.

A rigid orthonormal-frame constraint completely blocks this branch.

## Experiment 384 — Structural role of axis support/closure/restoration

The previous result gives a concrete role to axis-support or closure-like constraints:

- rigid closure that fixes \(Q=I/3\): suppresses progression-induced axis anisotropy;
- relaxable relative axes: permit radial alignment and threshold reduction;
- restoration toward isotropy: opposes the field-induced drive.

Thus axis support/restoration need not generate the absolute gravitational coupling \(\mu_0\); instead they can control **stability against anisotropy-assisted spectral collapse**.

## Experiment 385 — Minimal tensor restoration model

Use the local axis energy density

\[
\mathcal E_A
=\frac{\mathcal R_A}{2}\mathcal A:\mathcal A
-\frac{\beta_A\mu_0c_*^2}{2}\mathcal A:G_\psi.
\]

In the quasistatic weak-coupling limit,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_*
=\frac{\beta_A\mu_0c_*^2}{2\mathcal R_A}G_\psi.
}
\]

For a spherical field this is exactly radial-axis anisotropy.

Define the dimensionless axis susceptibility at source scale \(R\),

\[
\boxed{
\chi_A
:=\frac{\mu_0c_*^2}{\mathcal R_A R^2}.
}
\]

Large \(\mathcal R_A\) means small \(\chi_A\) and suppresses field-induced anisotropy.

## Experiment 386 — Self-induced threshold shift is second order in the metric coupling

If the source begins axis-isotropic, the field-induced anisotropy itself is \(O(\beta_A)\). The metric perturbation is \(\beta_A\mathcal A_*\), so the collapse-threshold shift begins at \(O(\beta_A^2)\).

For the uniform critical sphere and the local quadratic restoration model,

\[
\boxed{
\delta\epsilon_c
\simeq
-0.259060728\,\chi_A\beta_A^2
}
\]

in the weak induced-anisotropy approximation.

The sign is negative: field-induced radial alignment plus finite axis susceptibility lowers the threshold.

This numerical coefficient belongs only to the stated uniform-sphere/local-restoration toy.

## Experiment 389 — No local divergent axis drive at the static threshold

Although the response-mass coefficient \(C\) diverges at the static spectral threshold, the local logarithmic gradient does not.

Since

\[
U=A u,
\qquad
\psi=-2\ln A-2\ln u,
\]

one has

\[
\nabla\psi=-2\frac{\nabla u}{u},
\]

so the divergent normalization \(A\) cancels from the local gradient.

For the uniform critical sphere the surface value is finite:

\[
R\,|\psi_r(R)|=2.
\]

Therefore the local axis-driving term \(G_\psi\propto|\nabla\psi|^2\) does **not** automatically diverge as the static threshold is approached.

The self-response divergence arises because the strongly distorted exterior region extends to ever larger radius, not because the near-source gradient becomes infinite.

This removes an otherwise tempting but incorrect runaway argument.

## Experiment 390 — Consolidated axis-feedback verdict

### Confirmed within the present conditional branch

1. progression-gradient descent naturally aligns relaxable axes with the local field gradient;
2. in spherical symmetry this produces radial axis anisotropy;
3. radial anisotropy lowers the collapse threshold at first order when prescribed;

8. the local axis drive remains finite at the static threshold.
