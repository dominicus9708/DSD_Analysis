# LAW-012 Result — Distributed Responsibility, Command, Organization, and State Attribution

Status: first-pass cross-jurisdiction analysis complete.

Primary purpose: reinterpretation + falsification + predefinition audit.

## 1. Answer-first result

A single harmful event does **not** justify one universal responsibility chain of the form:

`direct actor -> commander -> organization -> State`.

The selected international criminal-law and State-responsibility sources instead support multiple source-defined routes that may coexist, overlap, or fail independently.

The strongest surviving candidate is:

**where a source system distinguishes direct conduct, ordering/inducement, superior-subordinate relation, effective control, knowledge, failure to prevent/repress/refer, official capacity, attribution to an organization/State, and individual/entity responsibility, preserve those relations separately. Do not transfer responsibility merely because actors are connected by hierarchy, membership, command, employment, office or the same underlying event. Each upward, downward or lateral attribution requires the source rule that creates it.**

Compactly:

`same event != same responsibility basis`.

`formal hierarchy != effective control != individual liability != State attribution/responsibility`.

## 2. Command responsibility counterpressure

ICTY jurisprudence rejects the equation:

`formal commander = effective control`.

The relevant command-responsibility relation depends on effective control in the sense of a material ability to prevent or punish subordinate criminal conduct. De jure authority may be evidence but is not itself the legal conclusion.

The Halilović and Blaškić materials are especially strong counterexamples: high rank, influence, or formal command did not suffice where effective control over the relevant perpetrators was not proven.

Therefore:

`rank/title -> command responsibility`

is not a valid universal promotion rule.

## 3. Distinct individual responsibility routes

ICC materials identify several individual responsibility modes, including direct/joint/through-another commission, ordering, and failure to exercise proper control over forces under command.

This defeats a one-dimensional model in which all responsibility is simply inherited from the direct perpetrator.

`direct perpetration != ordering != superior omission`.

The same person may be analyzed under more than one source-defined mode, but non-identity does not mean mutual exclusion.

## 4. State attribution is another legal layer

The ILC Articles on State Responsibility distinguish State attribution from individual responsibility.

Article 4 attributes conduct of State organs to the State.

Article 7 shows that official-capacity conduct may remain attributable even when the organ/person/entity exceeds authority or contravenes instructions.

Article 8 supplies a separate instruction/direction/control route for conduct of persons or groups.

Article 58 expressly leaves individual responsibility under international law unaffected by the State-responsibility articles.

Therefore:

`conduct attributed to State != individual criminal guilt established`;

and:

`individual guilt established != State attribution automatically established`.

These may coexist, but through distinct rule families.

## 5. Predefinition significance

LAW-012 adds a distributed-responsibility version of the project's recurring rule.

Do not predefine:

`subordinate guilty -> commander guilty`;

`commander title -> effective control`;

`official exceeded authority -> private act`;

`State attribution -> individual criminal guilt`;

`individual guilt -> organizational/State responsibility`;

`many responsible persons -> one aggregate responsibility value`.

The opposite negatives are also forbidden:

`superior did not directly commit act -> no possible superior responsibility`;

`official acted contrary to instruction -> no possible State attribution`.

The source rules decide which responsibility/attribution routes exist.

## 6. DSD comparison

Formation can preserve typed actor, role, source-state and interface distinctions, but it does not generate legal liability or attribution.

The legal network is better represented as a source-indexed graph than a chain:

`G_R = (V, E_R)`.

Each edge in `E_R` is licensed by a source rule such as direct participation, ordering, effective-control superior responsibility, State-organ attribution, ultra vires attribution, or instruction/direction/control attribution.

Do not identify:

`legal hierarchy != DSD axis`;

`effective control != Axis-Property`;

`State attribution != Formation composition`;

`responsibility intensity != Static Aggregation weight`.

Dynamics may record time-indexed command, knowledge, omission and response states but does not supply the legal transition.

No direct DSD axiom contradiction was found after these boundaries were preserved.

**one event may generate several distinct responsibility and attribution relations, but institutional connectedness alone does not supply those relations.**

## 9. Boundary statement

LAW-012 does not provide a universal theory of command responsibility, corporate criminal liability, organizational fault or State responsibility. It does not claim that every legal system recognizes all listed responsibility modes.

It establishes the narrower structural result that where source systems assign different responsibility or attribution conditions to direct actors, superiors, organizations or States, those relations cannot be collapsed into one chain or scalar merely because the same event connects the actors.
