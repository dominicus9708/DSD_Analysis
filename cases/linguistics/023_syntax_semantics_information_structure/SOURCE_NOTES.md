## External literature

### Rooth 1992 — A theory of focus interpretation
Mats Rooth, *Natural Language Semantics* 1(1):75–116, DOI 10.1007/BF02342617.

Relevant structural point: focus interpretation can be modeled through focus-determined alternatives and contextual licensing. A focused constituent therefore contributes more than ordinary lexical denotation alone.

### Kratzer & Selkirk 2020 — Deconstructing Information Structure
Angelika Kratzer and Elisabeth Selkirk, *Glossa*, DOI 10.5334/GJGL.968.

Relevant structural point: parts of information structure can be analyzed through morphosyntactic features whose effects cross syntax–semantics and syntax–phonology interfaces. Their analysis distinguishes focus-related and givenness-related contributions rather than treating information structure as one undifferentiated value.

### Sevi & Kadmon — Without 'Focus'
This work argues that the traditional grammatical notion of focus may be less systematic than often assumed and explores analyses without a dedicated focus primitive.

Boundary consequence: the external field itself does not force one unique ontology of focus. DSD therefore should preserve the source-theory distinction chosen by an application rather than declaring a universal DSD focus property.

## DSD sources
### Formation Axiom System
Formation is a static typed framework and preserves structured configuration, typed assignments, and channel identity. It can therefore host distinct application-supplied configurations even when some material coordinates coincide.

### Channel-Indexed Static Aggregation
The static paper explicitly states that aggregate equality does not reconstruct channel support or complete typed property structure. This gives a downstream warning: lexical or scalar reduction cannot be assumed to reconstruct information-structural organization.

### Axis-Property
Axis-Property permits tag-sensitive profiles, but the current linguistic witness does not require treating focus/topic as realized-axis properties. Doing so without independent need would add unnecessary structure.
