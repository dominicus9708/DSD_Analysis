## External literature

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Ambiguity
URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ambiguity/

Relevant points:
- ambiguity is generally treated as a sign bearing multiple legitimate interpretations;
- ambiguity is distinguished from vagueness, context sensitivity, and underspecification;
- lexical ambiguity and syntactic/scope ambiguity are distinct families;
- one surface sentence may correspond to multiple logical forms or interpretations;
- formalization is often used to disambiguate natural-language expressions;
- ambiguity can create invalid equivocation if one reading is silently substituted for another.

Illustrative scope case from the source:
`Every woman squeezed a man` can receive readings corresponding roughly to
`forall x exists y` and `exists y forall x`.

## Mapping discipline
Do not identify linguistic ambiguity with Formation undefinedness.
An ambiguous expression may have multiple well-formed, meaningful candidate interpretations. The unresolved state is therefore better represented by a family/set of candidate interpretation structures, each of which can be individually defined.
