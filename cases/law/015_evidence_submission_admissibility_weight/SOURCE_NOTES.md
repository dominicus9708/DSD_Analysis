## EXT-01 — Criminal Procedure Act, Article 294

Current text checked at the National Law Information Center, effective 2026-07-01.

Claim used: prosecutor, accused, or defense counsel may submit documents or objects as evidence and may request examination of witnesses, experts, interpreters, or translators. A deliberately late application that delays completion of trial may be rejected.

Source URL:
https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1029127213

Source-side implication: possession/existence of information is not identical to formal evidentiary submission/application, and application is not identical to acceptance or use.

## EXT-02 — Criminal Procedure Act, Articles 307, 308, 308-2

Current text checked at the National Law Information Center, effective 2026-07-01.

Claims used:

- Article 307: fact-finding must be based on evidence; criminal facts require proof beyond reasonable doubt.
- Article 308: probative force is determined by the judge's free evaluation.
- Article 308-2: evidence collected without following lawful procedure may not be used as evidence.

Sources:
https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1009267611

Source-side implication: legal usability/admissibility and probative force are distinct questions. Even usable evidence does not by its mere existence establish the ultimate fact.

## EXT-04 — Civil Procedure Act, Articles 288-292

Current operative evidence chapter checked at the National Law Information Center. The displayed operative version is effective 2025-07-12; a 2026 amendment shown by the Center has an effective date in 2028 and does not displace these provisions as of retrieval.

Claims used:

- Article 288: admitted facts and notorious facts need no proof.
- Article 289: an evidence application must identify the fact to be proved.
- Article 290: the court may decline to investigate evidence it finds unnecessary, subject to the statutory proviso for a party's sole evidence.
- Article 291: evidence may be left uninvestigated if it is uncertain whether or when investigation is possible.
- Article 292: the court may conduct evidence investigation ex officio under the stated conditions.

Source:
https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1000266100

Source-side implication: application, investigation, and factual determination remain separate procedural coordinates.

## EXT-05 — Civil Procedure Act, Article 202

Claim used: the court evaluates truth or falsity of factual allegations by free conviction based on the whole purport of pleadings and results of evidence investigation, under logic and experience.

Source:

## EXT-06 — Supreme Court 2024Da222212, 2026-04-30

Official Supreme Court case bulletin published 2026-05-11.

Claims used:

1. A recording of a non-public conversation made by a third party in violation of the Protection of Communications Secrets Act lacks evidentiary admissibility because the special statute expressly bars its use.
2. Unlike criminal procedure, civil procedure does not contain a general statutory exclusion rule for unlawfully collected evidence. Where no separate statute determines admissibility, unlawfully collected civil evidence is not automatically inadmissible; admissibility is decided by a case-specific balancing of fairness, protected interests, truth-finding, circumstances of collection, need, urgency, and related factors.
3. In the case, one category of recording evidence was inadmissible under the special statute, while another category of unlawfully collected photographed digital material was not automatically excluded and was accepted after balancing.

Source:
https://lawclerk.scourt.go.kr/portal/news/NewsViewAction.work?gubun=4&searchOption=&searchWord=&seqnum=11061

Source-side implication: the same broad label `unlawfully collected` does not determine a single admissibility value across source regimes or statutory routes.
