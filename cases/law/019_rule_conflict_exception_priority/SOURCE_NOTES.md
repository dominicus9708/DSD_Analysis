# LAW-008 Source Notes

## Source discipline

The first-pass universal analysis uses international/transnational materials as independent witness families. They are not treated as proof that every legal system uses the same conflict rules.

## 1. Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969)

Official UN text:
https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/1_1_1969.pdf

### Article 30 — successive treaties on the same subject matter

Article 30 does not provide one context-free `later treaty always deletes earlier treaty` rule.

It distinguishes:

- Article 103 of the UN Charter as an external priority condition;
- express treaty clauses making one treaty subject to another;
- the case where all parties to the earlier treaty are also parties to the later treaty;
- the case where party sets differ;
- compatibility of earlier and later provisions;
- continuing responsibility questions.

Important structural consequence:

`same broad subject + successive treaties != one global winner independent of parties and compatibility`.

### Article 31 — interpretation

Treaty interpretation is contextual and in light of object and purpose. Relevant international-law rules applicable between the parties are also taken into account.

This supports an interpretation-before-unresolved-conflict discipline.

### Article 53 — conflict with jus cogens

A treaty is void if, at the time of conclusion, it conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law.

This is not merely a `priority` result. It is a source-supplied invalidity consequence.

Therefore:

`conflict -> consequence` is rule-indexed.

## 2. United Nations Charter Article 103

Official UN Charter text:
https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text

Article 103 provides that, in the event of a conflict between a Member's Charter obligations and obligations under another international agreement, the Charter obligations prevail.

The text supplies a priority relation.

For LAW-008, do not silently rewrite `prevail` as `other treaty never existed` or `other treaty is universally void`. The source consequence is priority of Charter obligations in the conflict.

## 3. International Law Commission — Fragmentation Conclusions (2006)

Official UN/ILC text:
https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/draft_articles/1_9_2006.pdf

Relevant structural points:

### Relationships of interpretation vs relationships of conflict

The ILC distinguishes situations where one norm assists interpretation/application of another from situations where two valid and applicable norms point to incompatible decisions so that a choice must be made.

This is a strong source-side warning against calling every normative difference a conflict.

### Harmonization

Where several norms bear on a single issue, they should, to the extent possible, be interpreted so as to give rise to a single set of compatible obligations.

LAW-008 uses this as counterpressure against premature contradiction declarations.

### Lex specialis

The ILC treats `lex specialis derogat legi generali` as a generally accepted interpretation/conflict-resolution technique in international law, but also emphasizes contextual appreciation.

The effect of applying special law does not normally extinguish the relevant general law. General law may remain valid and applicable and can continue to guide interpretation or apply to matters not covered by the special rule.

Therefore:

`special rule governs this case != general rule deleted from the legal system`.

## 4. DSD Formation source basis

Project source: `DSD_Formation_Axiom_System_EN.pdf`.

Relevant source-supported distinctions:

- assignment can be undefined without being zero;
- channel formation requires describability, a defined assignment and a role;
- failure of required channel conditions yields channel absence, not zero-valued channel;
- a defined zero assignment may still support a channel;
- absent channel is not a zero term;
- role multiplicity yields distinct channels;
- Stage V through VII preserve undefined assignment, channel absence and zero contribution as distinct.

LAW-008 uses only this source-side type/status discipline. It does not claim that legal rules literally are Formation channels or that priority is a Formation operator.

## 5. Negative source conclusions

The selected sources do not justify these universal propositions:

- every conflict is resolved by lex specialis;
- every later rule defeats every earlier rule;
- every priority relation invalidates the lower-priority norm;
- every exception deletes the general rule;
- every apparent inconsistency is a genuine unresolved contradiction;
- DSD supplies a legal conflict hierarchy.
