## Experiments 237–244 — From bare axes to continuum inertia

A realized axis represented only by a one-dimensional subspace

\[
\ell=\operatorname{span}(n)=\operatorname{span}(\alpha n)
\]

has no physical segment length. With a metric one may choose a unit representative and the orientation-invariant projector

\[
P=n\otimes n,
\]

but this is direction data, not a mass/length scale. Localizing \(P_i(x)\) can create inverse-length variation scales through derivatives, but it still introduces no mass dimension.

To test a stronger literal-line interpretation, assume localized line supports \(\Gamma_a\) and a microscopic action

\[
S_{\rm ax}=\sum_a\int dt\int_{\Gamma_a}
\left[
\frac{\eta_a}{2}(\partial_t\psi)^2
-\frac{\kappa_a}{2}(D_{n_a}\psi)^2
\right]d\ell.
\]

For dimensionless \(\psi\),

\[
[\eta_a]=ML,\qquad [\kappa_a]=ML^3/T^2.
\]

With line-length density \(\mathscr L_a=d\ell_a/dV\), \([\mathscr L_a]=L^{-2}\), the coarse coefficients are

\[
\mu_{\rm eff}=\sum_a\eta_a\mathscr L_a,
\]

\[
\mathcal K_{\rm eff}^{ij}
=\sum_a\kappa_a\mathscr L_a n_a^in_a^j.
\]

Thus the desired dimensions appear only because the microscopic coefficients and line measure already carry dimensional information. Isotropy can fix tensor shape and dimensionless factors such as

\[
\langle n^in^j\rangle=\frac13h^{ij},
\]

but not the common amplitude.

For a fixed finite self-similar line network, total line length scales as \(L\) while volume scales as \(L^3\), so \(\mathscr L\sim L^{-2}\). A source-independent line density therefore requires an additional microscopic spacing/correlation scale or line-number scaling law.

### Verdict

- bare axis geometry -> physical length scale: **REJECTED**
- axis geometry -> mass dimension: **REJECTED**
- localized line network -> finite line-density measure: **CONDITIONAL**
- isotropy -> tensor shape/dimensionless factor: **CONDITIONAL / CONFIRMED**
- homogenization itself -> new absolute \(M/L\) scale: **REJECTED**

## Experiments 245–250 — Mass measure and homogenization non-identifiability

If a physical length measure \(d\ell\) and mass measure \(dm\) are supplied on an axis support with \(dm\ll d\ell\), then

\[
\iota=\frac{dm}{d\ell},\qquad [\iota]=M/L.
\]

This defines a local linear-mass descriptor, but it already carries the target dimension and is generally source dependent. Requiring \(\iota=\mu_0\) universally is a new constitutive law.

Moreover, because \(\psi\) is dimensionless, the literal line-action kinetic coefficient is \(\eta[ML]\), not ordinary line mass density \([M/L]\). Converting one into the other requires an additional length-squared scale. Hence `axis inertia = ordinary line mass density` is not a justified identification in the progression-field branch.

Static normalized aggregation can average or sum already dimensionful values, but cannot create a mass dimension from dimensionless axis records.

An explicit scaling family fixes all axis supports, projectors, ranks, closure data, and line-density geometry while setting

\[
\eta_a^{(\lambda)}=\lambda\eta_a,
\qquad
\kappa_a^{(\lambda)}=\lambda\kappa_a.
\]

Then

\[
\mu_{\rm eff}^{(\lambda)}=\lambda\mu_{\rm eff},
\qquad
\mathcal K_{\rm eff}^{(\lambda)}=\lambda\mathcal K_{\rm eff},
\]

but the ratio \(\mathcal K_{\rm eff}/\mu_{\rm eff}\), characteristic cone, rank, axis directions, and closure remain unchanged. Under fixed minimal matter coupling, the static progression response amplitude changes as \(1/\mu_{\rm eff}\).

### Conditional theorem candidate — axis-homogenization scale non-generation

Dimensionless axis geometry, normalized weights, supplied metric/time data, and localization data carrying no mass dimension cannot generate a universal coefficient with dimension \(M/L\). At least one upstream mass-dimensional assignment or equivalent physical anchor is required. If that mass input is taken from the source itself, universality needs an additional source-independent constitutive condition.

### Verdict

- physical mass + line measures -> local \(M/L\) descriptor: **CONDITIONAL**
- universality of that descriptor: **UNRESOLVED / NOT DERIVED**
- normalized aggregation -> absolute scale generation: **REJECTED**
- axis geometry + cone data uniquely determine \(\mu_\psi\): **REJECTED by explicit scaling family**

## Experiments 251–255 — Minimal one-parameter weak structural-gravity closure

Introduce a source-independent positive progression-sector inertial coefficient

\[
\mu_0[M/L]
\]

as an **additional physical input**, not as a DSD-derived value. With universal minimal proper-time matter coupling and a common cone,

\[
\mathcal L_{\rm weak}
=
\frac{\mu_0}{2}(\partial_t\psi)^2
-
\frac{\mu_0c_*^2}{2}|\nabla\psi|^2
\pm c_*^2\rho\psi.
\]

Up to sign convention,

\[
\frac1{c_*^2}\partial_t^2\psi-\nabla^2\psi
=
\pm\frac{\rho}{\mu_0}.
\]

The static compact-source monopole is

\[
\psi\sim\mp\frac{M}{4\pi\mu_0r},
\]

and with

\[
\mathbf a=-c_*^2\nabla\psi
\]

the effective coupling magnitude is

\[
\boxed{|K_*|=\frac{c_*^2}{4\pi\mu_0}}.
\]

### Self-field bookkeeping after the normalization correction

For static field energy density

\[
u_\psi=\frac{\mu_0c_*^2}{2}|\nabla\psi|^2
\]

and conditional self-source mass density

\[
\rho_\psi=u_\psi/c_*^2
=\frac{\mu_0}{2}|\nabla\psi|^2,
\]

the field equation acquires

\[
\nabla^2\psi
=
\pm\frac{\rho}{\mu_0}
+\frac12|\nabla\psi|^2.
\]

Thus the earlier dimensionless self-coupling \(\gamma=1/2\) survives, while \(\mu_0\) remains undetermined.

For a spherical bounded source, the leading linear-field self-mass is

\[
M_\psi
=
\frac1{8\pi\mu_0}
\left[
\int_0^R\frac{M(<r)^2}{r^2}\,dr
+\frac{M^2}{R}
\right].
\]

For a uniform sphere,

\[
\frac{M_\psi}{M}=\frac35\epsilon,
\qquad
\epsilon:=\frac{M}{4\pi\mu_0R}.
\]

For the earlier same-\(M,R\) piecewise-density controls, the leading coefficients \(M_\psi/M=(I/2)\epsilon\) are approximately

- core-heavy: \(0.76531\epsilon\),
- uniform: \(0.60000\epsilon\),
- envelope-heavy: \(0.58347\epsilon\).

Hence linear exterior monopole equality can coexist with profile-sensitive self/backreaction corrections.

### Verdict

- adding one universal \(\mu_0\) closes the weak structural-gravity branch: **CONDITIONAL**
- current DSD determines numerical \(\mu_0\): **REJECTED / NOT DERIVED**
- self-consistency fixes \(\gamma=1/2\): **CONDITIONAL**
- self-consistency fixes \(\mu_0\): **REJECTED**

## Consequence

The absolute-normalization problem is now most sharply expressed as the origin of

\[
\boxed{\mu_0[M/L]\text{ — universal progression-sector inertia}}
\]

rather than as axis-tension magnitude or boundedness-induced quantization. Axis data are better positioned as candidates for tensor shape, anisotropy, mode mixing, and near-field corrections unless an independent physical axis-inertia scale is supplied.
