# LAW-007 Contradiction Audit

## C1. Does recognized legal capacity imply immediate practical exercise without support or conditions?

No. CRPD Article 12 recognizes legal capacity while separately requiring access to support and safeguards for its exercise.

A source-faithful DSD encoding must therefore not treat support need as absence of capacity.

## C2. Must `capacity` and `exercise` always be separate legal concepts?

No. This proposed universal wording fails counterpressure.

CRPD General Comment No. 1 uses legal capacity broadly to include both legal standing and legal agency. The universal candidate is therefore vocabulary-neutral and preserves only distinctions actually made by the source.

## C3. Does existence of a right imply it is presently exercisable?

No. UNIDROIT Chapter 10 explicitly distinguishes a right from the time when it can be exercised.

## C4. Does exercisability imply actual exercise?

No. UNIDROIT Article 7.3.2 requires a notice event to exercise the termination right.

## C5. Does expiration of a limitation period destroy the underlying right?

Not under the selected UNIDROIT regime. Article 10.9 preserves the right while allowing enforcement to be barred when the limitation defence is asserted.

## C6. Does entity-level capacity imply actor-level authority?

No. VCLT Article 6 recognizes State treaty capacity; Article 7 separately governs a person's representative authority/full powers.

## C7. Does an attempted exercise without authority mean no event occurred?

No. VCLT Article 8 describes an act performed without authorization and assigns it no legal effect unless later confirmed.

Encoding the attempted act as channel absence would erase a source-side event required for the confirmation rule.

## C8. Does later confirmation prove the earlier act was already effective?

No. Article 8 supplies a later rule that changes the legal effect. It does not justify retroactively totalizing the pre-confirmation state into the post-confirmation state at the descriptive level.

## C9. Does DSD Formation force a universal legal-capacity taxonomy?

No. Formation supplies typed staged/partial structure but does not define legal capacity, competence, authorization, notice, enforceability, or confirmation.

## C10. Is Axis-Property required because legal powers are 'properties'?

No. The source domain supplies no realized-axis semantics. Applying Axis-Property merely because law uses the words property, status, power or capacity would violate the DSD layer boundary.

## Result

After matching subject, right/power, actor, role, authority/support conditions, time, exercise event, regime and legal effect, no direct contradiction with the Formation distinctions was found.

The principal danger is under-typing: collapsing possession, exercisability, exercise and effect into one global `power` bit.
