## A. Allowed pre-seal input

Only the original problem setup and neutral bibliographic information are treated as admissible input:

- an eccentric billionaire will pay the agent one million dollars tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, the agent **intends** to drink a nonlethal but painful toxin tomorrow afternoon;
- actual drinking is not required for payment;
- the money arrives before the scheduled drinking time;
- after payment the agent is free to change their mind;
- a stipulated reliable scanner determines whether the relevant intention is present at midnight;
- drinking the toxin later remains physically within the agent’s power.

## C. DSD prediction

### C1. Predicted vulnerabilities

#### Prediction 1 — formation, persistence, and execution are distinct stages

Let:

- `I_0` = the intention state at midnight;
- `R_1` = reward receipt the following morning;
- `I_1` = any later persistence/renewal of the intention;
- `A_2` = the afternoon drinking action.

DSD predicts that the puzzle becomes unstable if it silently treats these as one event:

`Formation(I_0) != Persistence(I_1) != Execution(A_2)`.

The billionaire’s stated reward condition targets `I_0`, not `A_2`.

Therefore failure to execute `A_2` after the reward does not by itself imply that `I_0` was absent at midnight.

#### Prediction 2 — anticipated future preference change requires an explicit bridge to present intention formation

The reward changes the later decision context:

`S_0 -> I_0 -> R_1 -> S_1 -> A_2`.

In general `S_0 != S_1`.

DSD predicts that any inference of the form

`the agent expects not to drink at S_1 -> the agent cannot genuinely form I_0 at S_0`

requires an explicit intention-formation bridge. The bridge may be philosophically defensible, but it is not supplied merely by temporal ordering or by the reward contract.

#### Prediction 3 — later rational revision does not automatically retroactively falsify an earlier formed state

If `I_0` is genuinely formed and then the agent rationally revises at `S_1`, DSD predicts:

`Revision(I_1) -/-> not Formation(I_0)`.

A later change can terminate or replace a previously formed state without showing that the earlier state never existed.

Any theory that denies this must state a persistence or diachronic-coherence condition as part of what counts as intention.

#### Prediction 4 — the crucial question is not “can the action be performed?” but “what makes the intention assignment defined?”

The action is physically possible by stipulation. The DSD-relevant issue is whether the property/status `Intends(agent, drink_tomorrow)` has a justified formation criterion at midnight.

The puzzle therefore depends on the semantic/functional signature of intention:

- Is intention constituted by a present practical commitment?
- Does it require expected persistence?
- Does it require the agent to expect eventual execution?
- Can an intention be genuine even if the agent foresees a later rational reconsideration?

DSD predicts that without fixing this signature, “cannot intend” risks mixing **failure of assignment** with a stronger metaphysical incapacity claim.

### C2. Predicted surviving components

1. The distinction between a genuine intention and merely uttering, imagining, wishing, or commanding “I will drink” should survive DSD analysis. DSD does not identify a label or verbal output with a formed property/state.
2. The stipulated scanner removes a major observer-identification problem: if it is accepted as perfectly reliable, the puzzle is not primarily about whether an external observer can detect `I_0`.
3. If a defensible theory of intention builds future-directed commitment, anticipated execution, or stability into the constitutive conditions of intention, the puzzle may retain genuine force after DSD stage separation.

### C3. DSD attacks predicted to be inapplicable

1. `undefined != zero` is not by itself a solution. If the theory supplies a clear intention domain and the scanner checks it, the main issue is formation criteria, not merely status bookkeeping.
2. Part/whole attribution is not central to this case.
3. External-output non-identifiability of the Human/AI-room type is not central because the scanner is stipulated to identify the target intention accurately.
4. The puzzle is not refuted merely because the reward is received before the action; the difficulty concerns whether the earlier intention can be genuinely formed under known future incentives.

### C4. Required bridge conditions

DSD predicts that a decisive solution must make at least one of the following bridges explicit:

1. **Formation bridge** — necessary and sufficient conditions for `I_0` to count as a genuine future-directed intention rather than a wish, decision-like performance, or verbal act.
2. **Diachronic bridge** — whether genuine intention at `t_0` requires expected persistence through later preference/reason changes.
3. **Execution bridge** — whether genuine intention requires the agent at `t_0` to expect eventual execution, and how strong that expectation must be.
4. **Revision bridge** — whether foreseen rational revision at `t_1` is compatible with genuine intention at `t_0`.

### C5. Falsification criteria

The DSD prediction is weakened or rejected if unblinding establishes that:

1. Kavka’s original argument already cleanly separates formation, persistence, and execution and the main puzzle remains unchanged after that separation;
2. a standard and independently justified theory of intention shows that foreknown later non-execution is constitutively incompatible with present intention, making the DSD stage separation merely terminological;
3. the strongest historical solutions locate the puzzle elsewhere and the DSD-predicted bridges fail to engage the actual inferential burden;
4. the DSD analysis collapses completely into one already standard objection with no useful additional formal separation;
5. the original setup or strongest replies show that the reward-induced state change is irrelevant to the possibility of forming the intention.

## D. Sealed prospective thesis

The main DSD prediction is:

> The Toxin Puzzle is not fundamentally a contradiction between rationality and physical ability. Its pressure arises at the interface between a presently formed future-directed intention and the rules governing persistence, expected execution, and rational revision across a known state change. The puzzle cannot move from “I know I will have no later reason to drink” to “I cannot now genuinely intend to drink” without an explicit diachronic intention bridge.

This thesis is now sealed. Subsequent literature comparison must not rewrite Predictions 1–4 or the falsification criteria.
