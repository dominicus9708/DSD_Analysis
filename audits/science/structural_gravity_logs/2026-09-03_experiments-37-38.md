## Experiment 37 — Audit of mass-bearing candidates in current DSD

Goal: determine whether the current DSD layers already contain an independently derived quantity capable of supplying the missing universal \(M/L\) dimension without importing a gravitational measurement.

### Tension-, stiffness-, inertia-, restoration-like properties

In the current Property/Dynamics hierarchy these are typed property labels or data. They are not automatically identified with physical force, energy, mass, or wave speed. A separate constitutive dynamic bridge is required before they become specified dynamic operators.

**Verdict: REJECTED as automatically physical mass-bearing invariants.**

### Hyperbolic operators \(\mathsf M,\mathsf D,\mathsf K,\mathsf R,\mathsf C\)

In the current dynamics specialization these are supplied typed operators. The DSD core does not uniquely select their numerical values or physical dimensions.

**Verdict: REJECTED as independently derived universal normalization; status = supplied constitutive data.**

### \(c_{\rm info}\)

\(c_{\rm info}\) is a finite propagation bound for distinguishability support and supplies only an \(L/T\)-type dimension in a physical specialization. It carries no mass dimension by itself.

**Verdict: REJECTED as a sole \(M/L\) normalization source.**

### Static aggregation measures, weights, and analytic realization

The measure, weights, fields, and Banach realizations are application-specific analytic data. They are not automatically physical mass or a universal mass scale.

**Verdict: REJECTED as automatic physical normalization.**

### Density, radial moments, and bounded-transition increments

These are source-dependent quantities or dimensionless structural descriptors. They remain useful correction or source-description sectors but do not currently provide a source-independent absolute normalization.

**Verdict: RETAINED as structural input/correction; REJECTED as universal normalization.**

### Experiment 37 result

No independently derived universal mass-bearing dimensional invariant was found inside the current four DSD layers.

\[
\boxed{
\text{current native DSD data}
\not\supset
\text{derived universal mass-bearing normalization}
}
\]

This identifies the missing constitutive target rather than establishing a failure of the broader structural program.

## Experiment 38 — Hyperbolic common-scale degeneracy and impedance-like target

Consider the principal part of a simple scalar second-order hyperbolic specialization:

\[
\mathsf M\,\partial_t^2u
-\nabla(\mathsf K\nabla u)=0.
\]

The characteristic propagation scale depends schematically on a ratio such as

\[
c^2\sim\frac{\mathsf K}{\mathsf M}.
\]

Under the common rescaling

\[
\mathsf M\mapsto\lambda\mathsf M,
\qquad
\mathsf K\mapsto\lambda\mathsf K,
\]

the ratio and characteristic speed remain unchanged.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{propagation speed / characteristic cone}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{absolute constitutive normalization}
}
\]

within the declared specialization.

This restates the earlier speed-versus-tension obstruction at the operator level: even an exact propagation bound cannot determine the common absolute scale of the kinetic/stiffness pair.

The missing quantity is therefore better characterized as an **absolute response normalization** that breaks the common-scale degeneracy, not another propagation-speed quantity.

Dimensional escape routes, if independently derived, could include

\[
\mu_*=\frac{\mathcal T_*}{c_{\rm info}^2}
\]

for a universal structural tension \(\mathcal T_*\), or an impedance-like quantity \(Z_*\) with \([Z_*]=M/T\):

\[
\mu_*=\frac{Z_*}{c_{\rm info}}.
\]

Neither \(\mathcal T_*\) nor \(Z_*\) is currently derived by DSD; these are dimensional target forms only.

### Verdict

- propagation bound determines absolute constitutive scale: **REJECTED**
- common-scale degeneracy in the declared hyperbolic specialization: **CONFIRMED**
- missing target = absolute constitutive normalization / impedance-like sector: **RETAINED CANDIDATE**
