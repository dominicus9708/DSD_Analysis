# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiments 186–195

Date: 2026-09-03
Status: conditional probe-response audit

## Experiment 186 — Point-probe response family

Allow

\[
\mathbf a_A
=
-\beta_A c_*^2\nabla\psi
\]

for probe type \(A\). Universal point-probe response requires \(\beta_A\) to be the same for every allowed probe.

## Experiment 187 — Charge/inertia factorization

If a probe carries progression-response charge \(q_\psi(A)\) and inertial coefficient \(I(A)\), define

\[
\beta_A=\frac{q_\psi(A)}{I(A)}.
\]

Universal acceleration is equivalent to \(q_\psi/I\) being constant across probe descriptors.

## Experiment 188 — Single-metric normalization

If every point probe follows the same progression metric and \(\psi=\ln N\) is the canonical progression variable, the common coefficient can be absorbed by normalization, giving

\[
\mathbf a
=
-c_*^2\nabla\psi
\]

in the low-speed point limit.

## Experiment 189 — Source and probe universality are independent

Universal source monopole coupling does not by itself force universal probe response, and universal probe response does not force universal source coupling. Both sides must be checked independently unless a single action/metric specialization links them.

## Experiment 190 — Tidal response

Relative acceleration between nearby probe elements is governed at leading order by

\[
T_{ij}
=
-c_*^2\partial_i\partial_j\psi.
\]

A probe center may therefore have zero absolute acceleration while nonzero internal tidal response remains.

## Experiment 191 — Center connection

In the spherical density branch,

\[
\mathbf a(0)=0,
\qquad
\nabla\nabla\psi(0)
=
\frac{4\pi}{3}\Lambda_t\rho(0)I.
\]

Relative probe acceleration can remain density-sensitive at the source center.

## Experiment 192 — Exact spherical-probe COM result in a harmonic exterior

Assume the whole probe lies inside a source-free region where

\[
\nabla^2\psi=0,
\]

and the probe mass density is spherically symmetric about its center. Each component of \(\nabla\psi\) is harmonic. By the spherical mean-value property, mass-weighted averaging over concentric shells gives exactly

\[
\mathbf a_{\rm CM}
=
-c_*^2\nabla\psi(\mathbf x_{\rm CM}),
\]

independent of probe radius and radial density profile, in the test/backreaction-free limit.

## Experiment 193 — Anisotropic finite-size correction

For a centered extended probe,

\[
a_{{\rm CM},i}
=
-c_*^2\left[
\partial_i\psi
+
\frac12 I_{jk}\partial_i\partial_j\partial_k\psi
+\cdots
\right],
\]

where \(I_{jk}\) is the normalized second moment of the probe density. In a harmonic exterior, the isotropic part of \(I_{jk}\) contracts to

\[
\partial_i\nabla^2\psi=0,
\]

so the traceless shape anisotropy supplies the leading finite-size correction.

## Experiment 194 — Scaling

For a monopole-like exterior, anisotropic finite-size COM corrections scale relative to the point response as

\[
O\!\left((\ell_{\rm probe}/r)^2\right).
\]

Tidal differential acceleration is governed by the Hessian and is one spatial derivative stronger than the point acceleration.

## Experiment 195 — Verdict

Universal point response and structure-sensitive finite-size/tidal response are compatible.

- point-probe universality requires coupling universality: YES;
- same-metric specialization can enforce it conditionally: YES;
- spherical extended-probe COM universality in a harmonic exterior: YES;
- arbitrary anisotropic extended-probe universality: NO.
