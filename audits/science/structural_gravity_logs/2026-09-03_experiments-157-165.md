# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiments 157–165

Date: 2026-09-03
Status: conditional branch audit

## Experiment 157 — Explicit centered two-lump quadrupole control

Take two equal compact masses \(M/2\) centered at \(z=\pm a\). The total dipole vanishes. The quadrupole components are

\[
Q_{zz}=2Ma^2,
\qquad
Q_{xx}=Q_{yy}=-Ma^2.
\]

Along the symmetry axis and equatorial axis, respectively,

\[
\psi_z
=
-\Lambda_tM\left(\frac1r+\frac{a^2}{r^3}+\cdots\right),
\]

\[
\psi_x
=
-\Lambda_tM\left(\frac1r-\frac{a^2}{2r^3}+\cdots\right).
\]

## Experiment 158 — Acceleration anisotropy

With \(K_*=c_*^2\Lambda_t\),

\[
|a_z|
=
K_*\frac{M}{r^2}
\left[1+3(a/r)^2+\cdots\right],
\]

\[
|a_x|
=
K_*\frac{M}{r^2}
\left[1-\frac32(a/r)^2+\cdots\right].
\]

Hence the directional difference relative to the monopole is

\[
\frac{|a_z|-|a_x|}{K_*M/r^2}
=
\frac92(a/r)^2+\cdots.
\]

Finite-distance anisotropy survives but vanishes quadratically in distance.

## Experiment 159 — Distance/tolerance dependent sufficiency

For a centered compact source, total mass is sufficient only relative to a chosen observation distance and tolerance. Adding the quadrupole descriptor controls the next anisotropic order. Therefore the minimal sufficient descriptor is a function of \((r,\varepsilon)\), not one globally fixed scalar.

Approximate ladder:

\[
M
\prec
(M,Q)
\prec
(M,Q,O,\ldots)
\prec
\rho(\mathbf x).
\]

This is a finite-distance approximation hierarchy, not an ontology claim.

## Experiment 160 — Explicit structural source extension

Generalize the conditional scalar branch to

\[
\nabla^2\psi
=
4\pi\Lambda_t\rho
+\mathcal S_{\rm str}.
\]

Relation/property sectors can affect progression geometry independently of density only through such an explicit additional source or through additional coupled fields.

## Experiment 161 — Zero-monopole structural correction route

If

\[
\mathcal S_{\rm str}
=
\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf J_{\rm str}
\]

has compact support and zero boundary flux, then

\[
\int \mathcal S_{\rm str}\,d^3x=0.
\]

Therefore it cannot change the \(1/r\) monopole amplitude, while it may affect near-field and higher multipoles.

This supplies a concrete mathematical route for

\[
\psi
=
-\Lambda_t\frac Mr
+\delta\psi_{\rm struct}
\]

without contaminating the universal mass monopole.

## Experiment 162 — Moment-neutral hierarchy

If only the monopole of \(\mathcal S_{\rm str}\) vanishes, the leading correction is generically dipolar:

\[
\delta\psi\sim r^{-2},
\qquad
\delta a\sim r^{-3}.
\]

If monopole and dipole both vanish, the leading correction is quadrupolar:

\[
\delta\psi\sim r^{-3},
\qquad
\delta a\sim r^{-4}.
\]

More generally, if moments through order \(l-1\) vanish, the first structural correction occurs at multipole order \(l\).

## Experiment 163 — Compact Laplacian special case

If

\[
\mathcal S_{\rm str}=\nabla^2\chi
\]

with compactly supported \(\chi\), then all exterior harmonic multipole moments vanish by integration by parts. With compatible boundary data, the correction can be strictly confined to the source region:

\[
\delta\psi=\chi
\]

inside its support and zero outside after the homogeneous solution is fixed accordingly.

This is an extreme example of internal structural distortion with no exterior correction.

## Experiment 164 — Descriptor ladder

Centered-source finite-distance response can be approximated with a hierarchy of moments. Each added moment controls an additional order in \(R/r\). The minimal descriptor depends on distance and target tolerance.

## Experiment 165 — Verdict

- explicit quadrupole witness: CONFIRMED inside the conditional linear branch;
- finite-distance anisotropy with asymptotic mass-only universality: CONFIRMED;
- zero-monopole structural correction: mathematically viable;
- direct physical origin of \(\mathcal S_{\rm str}\): OPEN.

## Impact

The linear density branch does not force structural corrections to vanish identically. Instead, relation/property corrections can coexist with a universal mass monopole if their effective source is moment-neutral, especially zero-monopole. This sharply separates absolute mass coupling from higher-order structural detail.
