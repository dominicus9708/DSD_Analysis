# LING-010 / Global Case 023 — Result

## Question
Can lexical identity and linear order determine complete meaning once focus/information structure is relevant? Which DSD layer is needed to preserve the distinction without claiming a DSD theory of focus?

## External result
The literature supplies an independent structural node.

Under Rooth-style focus semantics, focus marking can determine an alternative set used in interpretation. Thus identical lexical material and linear order can support different focus-sensitive interpretations when the focused constituent differs.

Kratzer–Selkirk provide a different but compatible pressure: information-structural features can participate across morphosyntax, semantics, phonology, and discourse. Conversely, critical work such as *Without 'Focus'* shows that the status of focus as a dedicated grammatical primitive is itself contested.

Therefore the source field supports preservation of information-structural distinctions, but not one uniquely mandatory theory or representation.

## Strongest finite result
For a selected Rooth-style toy interpretation, the two markings

`John only introduced BILL to Sue`

and

`John only introduced Bill to SUE`

can share the same words, linear order, and ordinary base proposition while generating different alternative sets. A finite world can make the first `only` claim true and the second false.

Hence:

`same lexical material + same linear order` does not imply `same complete focus-sensitive interpretation`.

A second result is independent of truth-value change:

`same at-issue truth conditions` does not imply `same information-structural state or discourse compatibility`.

## Predefinition restraint
The relevant restraint is structure-sensitive:

> A surface string, lexical inventory, or coarse truth-conditional representation does not justify preassigning a unique focus/topic structure, alternative set, or discourse function when the source theory requires additional prosodic, syntactic, contextual, or discourse information.

Likewise, successful later disambiguation of focus structure does not prove that the same structure was uniquely recoverable from the reduced earlier representation.

## Formation correspondence
Formation is sufficient for the core DSD application if the linguistic analysis supplies focus/prosodic/information-structural distinctions as part of the application-level structured configuration.

The important point is not to overload the operational-channel role coordinate `rho` with linguistic focus by default. Focus is not automatically a DSD role. Instead, the source representation should distinguish the configurations or typed material from which the later assignment/channel structure is formed.

Thus Formation provides a preservation framework, not the linguistic rule that generates focus structure.

## Static Aggregation correspondence
The static aggregation paper is directly relevant as a preservation warning: equality of a reduced aggregate does not reconstruct complete channel support or typed property structure.

At the linguistic application level, a bag-of-words representation or a reduction to one at-issue proposition can likewise identify U1 and U2 even when their information-structural organization differs.

The correspondence is structural, not theorem identity.

## Axis-Property
Axis-Property is **not required** for the core witness.

Although it can represent tag-sensitive typed properties, declaring focus/topic as axis properties would add a realized-axis interpretation that the linguistic source theory does not require. Unless a later application has a genuine reason to promote selected linguistic channels into the axis-property extension, Formation-level structured encoding is cleaner.

This is an important restraint after LING-007–009: the availability of Axis-Property should not cause every relational or tagged external phenomenon to be forced into that layer.

## Relation to LING-004
LING-004 concerned multiple legitimate semantic parses/interpretations awaiting selection.

LING-010 instead holds lexical material and surface order fixed and varies information-structural organization or prominence. The issue is not merely unresolved ambiguity but the fact that structure beyond lexical inventory and linear order can be semantically/pragmatically active.

## Verdict
- Formation contradiction: **not found**.
- Structural reinterpretation: **established**.
- Predefinition restraint: **independently corroborated at the syntax/information-structure interface**.
- Formation: **sufficient for the core source-preserving encoding**.
- Static Aggregation: **useful as a downstream information-loss analogy/warning**.
- Axis-Property: **not required; avoid over-application**.
- Unique DSD theory of focus/topic: **not claimed**.
- New audit item: **confirmed — do not infer complete structural interpretation from lexical identity, linear order, or reduced truth-conditional output when the source theory treats focus/topic/prosody/discourse structure as active data.**
