# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiments 98–99

Date: 2026-09-03

## Experiment 98 — Constitutive mass–geometry non-identifiability theorem candidate

Let one structural state family admit a physical-mass bridge \(\mathcal M\) and a progression-geometry far-field bridge

\[
\psi(r;S)=\Lambda_t\frac{\mathcal M(S)}{r}+\cdots.
\]

Assume the current structural axioms and descriptors do not independently fix the absolute calibration of \(\mathcal M\) or \(\Lambda_t\). For every \(\beta>0\), define another admissible constitutive specialization

\[
\mathcal M_\beta(S)=\beta\mathcal M(S),
\qquad
\Lambda_{t,\beta}=\frac{\Lambda_t}{\beta}.
\]

Then

\[
\Lambda_{t,\beta}\mathcal M_\beta(S)
=\Lambda_t\mathcal M(S),
\]

so the progression geometry \(\psi\), its gradient, scale-free source-structure comparisons, and any probe response depending only on the product are unchanged.

Therefore the current structural premises cannot identify a unique absolute mass-to-geometry normalization unless an additional condition fixes one factor independently.

**Verdict:** CONDITIONAL THEOREM CANDIDATE.

---

## Experiment 99 — Exact ways the non-identifiability can be broken

The theorem candidate fails only if the theory supplies an additional non-rescalable anchor. Sufficient classes include:

1. an independently derived absolute physical mass calibration from structural state;
2. independently derived universal length and mass quanta whose ratio gives \(L/M\);
3. an absolute transition flux, tension, impedance, or rate scale not constructed from the source's own \(M,L,c\);
4. a nonhomogeneous cross-sector balance whose two terms have different rescaling weights and whose coefficients are themselves fixed;
5. an independently normalized progression/clock observable tied to source structure by a law that is not invariant under \(\mathcal M\mapsto\beta\mathcal M\).

Dimensionless descriptors, boundedness alone, source-specific \(L/M\), homogeneous transition equations, and finite propagation bounds do not break the degeneracy.

### Consequence

Under the present ingredients, a measurement-free numerical reconstruction of the absolute gravitational normalization is structurally non-identifiable under the stated rescaling freedom.
Future work should either derive one of the non-rescalable anchors above or retain the absolute normalization as an independent physical parameter while continuing to derive scale-free structural predictions.
