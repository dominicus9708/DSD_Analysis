# LAW-001 through LAW-010 — Predefinition Cross-Case Result

Status: cross-case result complete.

## Main result

The law campaign refines predefinition criticism into a two-sided, source-indexed rule.

**A prior definition is not unjustified merely because it is prior. It becomes analytically suspect when it silently excludes source-permitted alternatives, promotes or demotes a status without the rule that performs that change, pre-fills either a positive or negative downstream value, or back-infers an upstream identity from a later effect.**

## Two-sided prohibition

Do not predefine positive downstream states such as:

- nonaction -> opposition/assent;
- submission -> admissibility;
- admissibility -> proof;
- right/power possession -> successful exercise/effect;
- responsibility -> one required sanction;
- rule difference -> unresolved contradiction.

Do not predefine negative downstream states either:

- lack of ordinary authority -> no possible attribution/effect;
- lack of ordinary party-submitted proof -> no possible procedural fact-treatment;
- failure of one proof route -> opposite fact established;
- failure of one conflict-resolution route -> no alternative source route.

## Back-inference prohibition

Do not infer:

- later attributed effect -> original ordinary authority existed;
- acquittal/not-guilty -> positive historical innocence proved;
- final factual finding -> one unique evidentiary path;
- downstream sanction/practice -> one upstream legal or moral identity.

A source may expressly provide legal retroactivity, ratification, confirmation, presumption, default, judicial notice, or another relation. That rule changes the source-defined legal state; it does not license the analyst to rewrite historical identity beyond what the source says.

## Graph rule

Predefinition auditing should not assume one universal chain.

Use a source-indexed interface graph:

`G = (V, E_R)`

where vertices are source-distinct states and each edge is licensed by a source or independently justified bridge rule `R`.

Branches, bypasses, loops, review, later confirmation and substitute routes are legitimate when the source supplies them.

## Analysis/design separation

The analyst must not design the external field to fit DSD or design DSD application mappings to force agreement.

A stronger DSD-style application assumption that fails under external counterpressure is narrowed or rejected while the source result is preserved.

## Scope

This result is a methodological cross-case conclusion from the legal campaign, not a claim that law proves the DSD axioms or that DSD supplies legal doctrine.
