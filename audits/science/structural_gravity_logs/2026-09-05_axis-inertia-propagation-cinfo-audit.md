## 487 — Source-status audit of structural inertia

The earlier realized-axis property system treated a `structural inertia record` as a static resistance descriptor and explicitly did not identify it with physical mass. The current structural-reorganization layer likewise allows an inertia-like property to map to a second-order operator `M` only through an explicit constitutive dynamic bridge.

### Verdict

`axis inertia` survives migration as a candidate dynamic inertia role, not as a primitive mass quantity.

---

## 488 — Projector tangent-space kinetic metric

For an unoriented realized line represented by

\[
P=nn^{\mathsf T},\qquad n^{\mathsf T}n=1,
\]

one has

\[
\dot P=\dot n n^{\mathsf T}+n\dot n^{\mathsf T},
\qquad n\cdot\dot n=0,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\|\dot P\|_F^2=2\|\dot n\|^2}.
\]

A rotational kinetic specialization

\[
T_A=\frac{J_A}{2}\|\dot n\|^2
\]

can consequently be written as

\[
\boxed{T_A=\frac{J_A}{4}\|\dot P\|_F^2}.
\]

Thus a clean realization of axis inertia is a positive metric/coefficient on the tangent space of the line/projector manifold.

### Verdict

This gives a concrete bridge slot for a structural-inertia record without interpreting it as matter mass.

## 489 — Positive definiteness is a constitutive admissibility condition

For a second-order axis field

\[
M_A q_{tt}-\partial_r(K_A^{rs}\partial_s q)+\cdots=0,
\]

a conventional finite-speed hyperbolic specialization requires the kinetic form to be positive definite on the propagating subspace:

\[
\boxed{M_A\succ0}.
\]

If a nonzero mode `v` satisfies `M_A v=0` but has positive stiffness in some direction, then the generalized characteristic quotient is singular and the ordinary second-order finite-speed interpretation fails. If both kinetic and stiffness forms vanish on the mode, it must instead be treated as a constraint/degenerate sector.

### Verdict

A zero or negative static `inertia property value` cannot automatically be inserted as a kinetic coefficient. The constitutive bridge must enforce the analytic conditions required by the chosen dynamics.

## 490 — Static support threshold is independent of positive inertia in the conservative specialization

Let

\[
L^{(2)}=\frac12\langle\dot q,M_A\dot q\rangle-\frac12\langle q,H_A q\rangle.
\]

Static equilibrium and its Hessian are determined by `H_A`. Normal modes satisfy

\[
\boxed{H_A e=\omega^2 M_A e}.
\]

If `M_A` is positive definite, the sign change that produces the conservative static support threshold occurs when `H_A` loses positive definiteness, not when the magnitude of `M_A` changes.

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{\text{axis inertia changes rates, not the conservative static support boundary}}
\]

provided `M_A` remains positive and nonsingular.

## 491 — Uniform inertia scaling changes the clock but not static support

Under

\[
M_A\mapsto\lambda M_A,
\qquad\lambda>0,
\]

with the stiffness/restoration operator fixed,

\[
\boxed{\omega\mapsto\omega/\sqrt\lambda}.
\]

The same scaling applies to characteristic speeds generated by the same principal stiffness operator. Static equilibria and the Hessian sign remain unchanged.

## 492 — Anisotropic inertia is not merely a global time rescaling

Take normalized principal matrices

\[
K=\operatorname{diag}(4,9).
\]

For

\[
M_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,100),
\]

the two characteristic speeds are

\[
(2,0.3).
\]

For

\[
M_2=\operatorname{diag}(100,1),
\]

they are

\[
(0.2,3).
\]

The stiffness geometry is unchanged, but the fastest mode switches.

### Verdict

Tensorial axis inertia can alter mode ordering and the directional propagation cone rather than only multiplying one global time scale.

## 493 — Exact characteristic-speed role

For a propagation direction `n`, define

\[
K_A(n)=\sum_{r,s}n_r n_sK_A^{rs}.
\]

The directional squared speeds satisfy

\[
\boxed{K_A(n)e=c^2M_Ae},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
c_{A,\mathrm{char}}
=\sup_n\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(M_A^{-1/2}K_A(n)M_A^{-1/2})}
}.
\]

This is the structural-gravity axis-sector specialization of the second-order characteristic formula already allowed by Structural Reorganization Dynamics.

## 494 — Axis characteristic speed is not automatically `c_info`

The DSD dynamics defines `c_info` only after a localization carrier, propagation metric, metric time, evolution law, discrepancy convention, and support-faithful comparison are supplied. Therefore

\[
\boxed{c_{A,\mathrm{char}}\neq c_{\mathrm{info}}\quad\text{by definition}}.
\]

If the axis field is admitted as a support-faithful component of one model whose global structural-information bound is `c_info`, causal compatibility requires

\[
\boxed{c_{A,\mathrm{char}}\le c_{\mathrm{info}}}.
\]

## 495 — Loewner-order causal admissibility criterion

For positive `M_A`, the directional bound

\[
c_A(n)\le c_{\mathrm{info}}
\]

for every mode is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
K_A(n)\preceq c_{\mathrm{info}}^2 M_A
\quad\text{for every admitted propagation direction }n.
}
\]

This is a useful constitutive audit condition. It restricts an axis inertia/stiffness bridge but does not derive either coefficient from `c_info`.

Equality with `c_info` additionally requires a reachable support-faithful mode that actually saturates the bound.

## 496 — `mu_A` and `mu_0` can share dimensions without being the same parameter

For the previously used dimensionless continuum anisotropy field,

\[
[\mu_A]=M/L,
\qquad
[\mu_0]=M/L.
\]

This dimensional coincidence does not imply identity.

Countermodel A: hold `mu_0` fixed and rescale `mu_A`. The static progression monopole normalization is unchanged while axis-reorganization frequencies and speeds change.

Countermodel B: hold `mu_A` fixed and change `mu_0`. Free axis dynamics can remain unchanged while the progression-field source normalization changes.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mu_A\not\equiv\mu_0}
\]

without an additional physical bridge.

A dimensionless ratio such as `mu_A/mu_0` may enter a later coupled specialization, but its value is not fixed by the present hierarchy.

## 497 — Axis inertia does not automatically source static gravity

Even if a later universal-energy source rule is supplied, the kinetic energy density of a dimensionless axis field has the form

\[
u_{A,\mathrm{kin}}
=\frac{\mu_A}{2}\|\partial_t\mathcal A\|^2.
\]

It vanishes in a static configuration. Hence the existence of nonzero `mu_A` does not by itself generate a static monopole source.

A rest-energy or static-energy contribution would require a separate constitutive/source rule.

## 498 — Two distinct origins of slow response

For a normalized soft mode `phi`, define

\[
m_{\mathrm{eff}}=\langle\phi,M_A\phi\rangle,
\qquad
k_{\mathrm{eff}}=\langle\phi,H_A\phi\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\omega^2=k_{\mathrm{eff}}/m_{\mathrm{eff}}}.
\]

A small frequency may therefore arise from either

1. `k_eff -> 0+` — genuine approach to a spectral support boundary; or
2. `m_eff -> infinity` at fixed positive stiffness — large inertia/kinetic arrest.

### Verdict

Observed or calculated slowness alone does not diagnose proximity to support loss.

---

## 499 — Inertia changes post-threshold growth rate, not the sign of conservative instability

When

\[
k_{\mathrm{eff}}<0,
\]

the unstable conservative mode grows with

\[
\boxed{\sigma^2=|k_{\mathrm{eff}}|/m_{\mathrm{eff}}}.
\]

Larger positive inertia slows the instability but does not restore static stability.

## 500 — Undamped overshoot audit

For a suddenly applied constant drive

\[
m\ddot q+kq=f_0,
\qquad q(0)=\dot q(0)=0,
\]

one obtains

\[
q(t)=q_{\mathrm{eq}}(1-\cos\omega t),
\qquad
q_{\mathrm{eq}}=f_0/k,
\qquad
\omega=\sqrt{k/m}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{q_{\max}=2q_{\mathrm{eq}}}
\]

is independent of inertia in the undamped model, whereas the time of the first maximum

\[
\boxed{t_{\max}=\pi\sqrt{m/k}}
\]

is inertia-dependent.

With a fixed damping coefficient, inertia also changes the damping ratio and can therefore affect overshoot amplitude. Thus the earlier `inertia may cause overshoot` statement requires the damping/forcing protocol to be specified.

## 501 — Finite propagation versus overdamped reduction

A telegraph/hyperbolic axis specialization

\[
M_A q_{tt}+D_Aq_t-\nabla\cdot(K_A\nabla q)+\cdots=0
\]

retains a finite characteristic front set by the principal pair `(M_A,K_A)` when hyperbolicity assumptions hold.

If one instead deletes the inertial term and obtains a parabolic diffusion-type equation, the standard continuum model generally has instantaneous noncompact support tails.

### Verdict

In a structural-gravity specialization that must be compatible with a finite `c_info`, a purely parabolic reorganization law cannot automatically be used as a literal support-propagation law. Either a hyperbolic/inertial completion or another finite-speed mechanism is required.

## 502 — Safe inertial completion of projector reorganization

The rank-preserving kinematic equation remains

\[
\boxed{\dot P=[\Omega,P]}.
\]

Rather than writing an unconstrained equation directly for `P_tt`, a geometrically safe inertial specialization can evolve the skew generator:

\[
\boxed{
J_A\dot\Omega+\Gamma_A\Omega
=\tau_A^{\mathrm{skew}}+\text{spatial/coupling terms},
\qquad
\dot P=[\Omega,P].
}
\]

This keeps the projector constraints in the kinematic layer while assigning inertia to the tangent angular motion.

### Verdict

`axis inertia` is naturally attached to reorientation velocity/generator space, not to unconstrained Cartesian acceleration of `P`.

## 503 — Equivalent unit-vector constrained form

For a representative unit vector `n`, a second-order specialization may be written schematically as

\[
J_A(\ddot n+\|\dot n\|^2n)+\Gamma_A\dot n
=P_{n^\perp}F_A,
\]

with

\[
n\cdot n=1.
\]

The projector/generator formulation is preferable when the unoriented identification `n ~ -n` must remain explicit.

## 504 — Restoration opens a frequency gap but does not set the principal front speed

For an isotropic small-angle continuum model

\[
\mu_A\theta_{tt}-\mathcal T_A\Delta\theta+\mathcal R_A\theta=0,
\]

the dispersion relation is

\[
\boxed{
\omega^2
=\frac{\mathcal T_A}{\mu_A}|k|^2
+\frac{\mathcal R_A}{\mu_A}.
}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{c_A^2=\mathcal T_A/\mu_A}
\]

comes from the principal stiffness/inertia ratio, while restoration supplies a low-wave-number gap. This further separates axis restoration from axis inertia and stiffness.

## 505 — Prestress may affect propagation only through the operator slot it actually modifies

If an axis-tension/prestress state contributes only a zero-order geometric-stiffness correction, it shifts frequencies/support margins without changing the principal characteristic cone.

If the constitutive linearization instead contributes to the principal spatial-gradient operator, it can modify `K_A(n)` and hence the propagation speed.

### Verdict

There is no universal rule `tension changes/does not change axis propagation speed`; the answer depends on which operator block the audited prestress bridge modifies.

## 506 — Mixed inertia profiles can generate off-diagonal kinetic blocks

The general property architecture permits mixed and higher-order typed property records. Therefore a structural-gravity constitutive bridge need not produce only unary diagonal axis inertias. A coupled kinetic form

\[
T_A=\frac12\sum_{a,b}\langle\dot q_a,M_{ab}\dot q_b\rangle
\]

is admissible when the typed bridge and positivity conditions are supplied.

The characteristic problem then depends on the full kinetic block matrix, not on one scalar `mu_A` per axis.

## 507 — Collective axis rank and kinetic-rank degeneracy are independent

The collective axis rank is determined by the span/Gram or, for positive weights, by the rank of the second moment `Q`. The kinetic operator `M_A` is supplied by a distinct constitutive bridge.

Hence a collective rank transition can occur while `M_A` remains positive definite on the surviving tangent variables, and `M_A` may become degenerate even when the geometric axis span retains full rank.

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{\text{axis-rank loss}\neq\text{inertia-operator degeneracy}.}
\]

---

## 508 — Kinetic-energy positivity is not inherited from a property label

If the structural-gravity specialization uses a conservative action, negative eigenvalues of `M_A` produce an indefinite kinetic energy and fall outside the intended positive-energy hyperbolic branch. Zero eigenvalues require separate constraint treatment.

Thus the dynamic bridge should explicitly impose

\[
\boxed{M_A\succ0}
\]

on the propagating tangent sector even if the upstream property carrier allowed more general ordered or signed values.

## 509 — `c_info` cannot determine the absolute axis-inertia scale

The simultaneous rescaling

\[
M_A\mapsto\lambda M_A,
\qquad
K_A^{rs}\mapsto\lambda K_A^{rs}
\]

leaves

\[
M_A^{-1/2}K_A(n)M_A^{-1/2}
\]

and therefore every characteristic speed unchanged.

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{
\text{a propagation cone fixes only stiffness/inertia ratios, not their common absolute scale.}
}
\]

Thus neither `c_info` nor common-cone matching can by itself determine the absolute magnitude of axis inertia.

---

## 510 — Same cone, different driven susceptibility countermodel

Consider a fixed external drive `f` with

\[
M_Aq_{tt}+K_Aq=f.
\]

Under the common rescaling

\[
(M_A,K_A)\mapsto(\lambda M_A,\lambda K_A),
\]

the free characteristic cone is unchanged, but the static driven response becomes

\[
q_{\mathrm{eq}}\mapsto q_{\mathrm{eq}}/\lambda.
\]

Therefore models with identical axis propagation speeds can have different reorganization susceptibility to the same supplied drive.

This is an explicit no-go against extracting the complete constitutive normalization from speed data alone.

## 511 — Consolidated migration verdict for axis inertia

The strongest surviving structural-gravity interpretation is

\[
\boxed{
\text{structural-inertia record}
\xrightarrow{B_{\rm dyn}}
\text{positive tangent kinetic operator }M_A
}
\]

with the following separations:

- `M_A` is not matter mass;
- `M_A` is not the progression-source normalization `mu_0`;
- `M_A` does not set the conservative static support boundary while positive;
- `M_A` controls frequencies, growth times, and together with principal stiffness the axis characteristic cone;
- `c_info` can constrain the ratio `K_A/M_A` but cannot select the common absolute scale;
- projector-preserving inertial dynamics is most safely formulated through tangent angular velocity/generator variables;
- observed stasis/slowness does not by itself distinguish large inertia from proximity to a support zero mode.
