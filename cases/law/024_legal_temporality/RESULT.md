# LAW-013 Result — Legal Temporality, Commencement, Retroactivity, Repeal, and Residual Effects

Status: first-pass cross-jurisdiction analysis complete.

Primary purpose: reinterpretation + falsification + predefinition audit.

## 1. Answer-first result

Legal time is not faithfully represented by one current-state bit such as `law active / law inactive`.

The selected sources independently distinguish adoption/enactment, consent or commencement, provisional operation, event time, retrospective scope, repeal/termination, transition/savings rules, and residual rights/liabilities/proceedings.

The strongest surviving candidate is:

**where a source system distinguishes adoption/enactment, commencement/entry into force, provisional application, event/accrual time, amendment/repeal/termination, retrospective scope, savings/transition rules, and residual legal states, preserve those temporal coordinates separately. Do not infer past applicability from current force, erase historical operation because a rule is now repealed, or infer present force merely because an earlier legal effect survives.**

Compactly:

`historical operation != current force != residual effect`.

## 2. Entry into force is not adoption

VCLT Article 24 makes entry into force depend on the treaty's terms or agreement, and allows a State joining later to have a different effective date.

Article 25 permits provisional application before full entry into force when the source so provides.

Therefore:

`adopted != in force`;

and:

`not fully in force != no possible legal operation`.

A binary timeline loses the provisional path.

## 3. Present law does not automatically rewrite earlier events

VCLT Article 28 supplies a default non-retroactivity rule for acts/facts and situations preceding entry into force unless a different intention appears.

US Ex Post Facto doctrine independently prohibits specified disadvantageous retroactive criminal/penal legislation but does not establish a universal ban on all retrospective legislation.

ECHR Article 7 likewise prohibits retrospective criminal-law application to an accused's disadvantage and applies the rule to offence definition and penalty.

Thus:

`rule in force now != rule governing all past conduct`.

But also:

`non-retroactivity != one identical universal temporal doctrine across all legal fields`.

The source regime determines temporal scope.

## 4. Repeal/termination does not erase history

VCLT Article 70 distinguishes release from future treaty performance from rights, obligations and legal situations already created through prior execution.

Canada's Interpretation Act s. 43 similarly provides that repeal does not ordinarily erase previous operation, accrued/accruing rights and liabilities, offences/penalties, or related investigations and proceedings.

Therefore:

`repealed/terminated now != never legally operated`;

`repealed/terminated now != all past consequences extinguished`.

Canada s. 44 further shows that repeal-and-substitution can have its own continuation and mitigation rules.

## 5. Predefinition significance

Do not predefine:

`enacted -> operative immediately`;

`current law -> past event governed`;

`repeal -> all prior legal states deleted`;

`termination -> all prior rights/obligations gone`;

`surviving old-law consequence -> old law still generally in force`;

`non-retroactivity -> universal identical rule in every field`.

The opposite negatives are also unsafe:

`not yet fully in force -> no possible provisional application`;

`repealed -> no possible proceeding based on earlier operation`.

## 6. Revised temporal model

Use a source-indexed temporal graph:

`G_T = (V_T, E_R)`.

Vertices are time-stamped legal states; edges are source-authorized temporal relations such as commencement, provisional application, retrospective application, savings, continuation, mitigation, suspension, repeal, termination and residual effect.

This avoids treating legal change as destructive overwrite.

## 7. DSD comparison

Formation can preserve typed time-indexed state differences, but source law supplies the temporal relation.

Dynamics is the DSD layer most plausibly relevant here, but only as representation of source-defined change.

Do not identify:

`legal commencement != DSD formation`;

`legal repeal != structural deletion`;

`legal savings/residual effect != automatically derived persistence`;

`retroactive legal application != backward physical causation or generic DSD backward dynamics`.

Axis-Property and Static Aggregation are not required.

No direct DSD axiom contradiction was found after legal temporal rules were kept source-indexed.

## 8. Relation to prior cases

LAW-005 showed that formation, effect, defect and later status cannot be collapsed into one valid/invalid bit.

LAW-008 showed that later rule priority does not necessarily delete the earlier rule's identity/history.

LAW-009 distinguished finality/review and irreversible consequences over time.

LAW-013 generalizes the temporal boundary:

**a legal rule's historical existence, current normative force, applicability to a particular event, and residual consequences are separate coordinates.**

## 9. Generalization status

First-pass status:

**`cross-jurisdictionally corroborated legal-temporality non-totalization candidate; enactment=operation, current-force=past-applicability, repeal=historical-erasure, termination=all-effect-extinction, and one universal non-retroactivity doctrine rejected; source-indexed temporal graph retained; active falsification remains open`.**

## 10. Boundary statement

LAW-013 does not supply a universal retroactivity doctrine and does not claim that every legal field preserves the same residual states. It establishes only that legal temporal transitions cannot be inferred from chronological order alone; source rules determine commencement, retrospective reach, repeal consequences, savings, continuation and residual effects.
