# DSD Analysis — Linguistics / Formal Semantics First-Pass Synthesis

## Status

**First-pass domain campaign complete.**

Scope: `LING-001` through `LING-010`, corresponding to Global Cases `014`–`023`.

This synthesis does **not** claim that DSD created a new theory of linguistics, semantics, pragmatics, speech acts, institutions, or social power. The campaign asks a narrower question: when independent linguistic and adjacent institutional theories preserve distinctions among formation, applicability, interpretation, update, inference, role, relation, and effect, can DSD preserve those distinctions without collapsing source-theory structure or importing conclusions prematurely?

The answer across the ten cases is: **yes, with important representation boundaries and with explicit external bridge rules retained.** No contradiction with the tested DSD layers was found in this campaign.

## Campaign principle

The recurring methodological rule is:

> Preserve the source discipline first. Use DSD only to keep distinct states, typed inputs, formation stages, relations, and transformations distinct. Do not treat structural correspondence as a claim that the external theory is derived from DSD.

A useful generic pattern is:

`source material / prerequisite / candidate`
`-> source-theory licensing or bridge`
`-> formed interpretation / act / relation`
`-> downstream inference / update / effect`

No arrow is inferred merely from the existence of the preceding node.

## 2. What is genuinely repeated, and what is a new node?

The cases repeatedly support **predefinition restraint**, but they do not all instantiate the same problem.

### 2.1 Status / applicability family

`LING-001`, `LING-004`, and `LING-005` all resist premature completion, but the states are different.

- `LING-001`: a semantic application or reference may be unavailable/undefined while a genuinely defined false output is a separate state.
- `LING-004`: several interpretations may each be legitimate candidates while none has yet been selected. This is **not** mere undefinedness.
- `LING-005`: a borderline case does not itself determine whether the source semantics uses a truth-value gap, hidden bivalence, a graded value, or another representation.

Therefore the campaign must not collapse these into one generic `unknown` state.

A useful audit vocabulary is:

`unknown != unavailable != undefined != defined zero/false != graded value != multiple candidates unresolved`.

These are an application-level audit vocabulary, not new Formation axioms.

### 2.2 Context / inference / history family

`LING-002`, `LING-003`, and `LING-006` all involve context, but at different boundaries.

- `LING-002`: a missing prerequisite may later be repaired or accommodated; the repair is not evidence that the prerequisite was originally present.
- `LING-003`: inferred pragmatic content is not retroactively part of the explicit source utterance.
- `LING-006`: a later discourse state can license a reading that an earlier state did not license; update order and history can therefore matter.

The common higher-level rule is:

> A downstream state, repair, inference, or accessibility condition may not be projected backward into an earlier stage without an explicit source-theory rule.

But the three cases remain independent nodes because they concern prerequisite repair, pragmatic inference, and ordered state transition respectively.

### 2.3 Constitutive / institutional relation family

`LING-007`, `LING-008`, and `LING-009` form a progressive cluster rather than three copies of one result.

- `LING-007`: asks whether a meaningful utterance successfully forms the intended speech act and whether downstream effects follow.
- `LING-008`: adds the institutional bridge — role, active delegation, jurisdiction, procedure, and constitutive/regulative rule.
- `LING-009`: moves from act formation to the structure of authority relations themselves — direction, scope, normative legitimacy, actual compliance, and conflict resolution.

The cluster yields the layered distinction:

`role`
`!= authority relation`
`!= delegation / jurisdiction / scope`
`!= valid constitutive act`
`!= normative legitimacy`
`!= observed compliance / downstream effect`
`!= conflict resolution`.

The cases converge with earlier law analyses, but they originate from separate linguistic, social-institutional, and authority-theory source literatures. They should therefore be recorded as additional external nodes, not as proof of a universal DSD theorem.

### 2.4 Structural reduction family

`LING-010` adds a different kind of pressure:

`same reduced representation != same complete source structure`.

A bag-of-words representation, surface string, or one coarse at-issue proposition can erase focus/information-structural organization. This structurally converges with Static Aggregation's warning that an aggregate does not in general reconstruct complete support/tagged structure.

This is an information-loss correspondence, not identity between linguistic semantics and the DSD aggregation theorem.

## 3. Cross-case DSD audit rules

The first-pass linguistics campaign supports the following reusable audit questions.

### A. Status fidelity

Before assigning an ordinary value, ask whether the source state is:

- unavailable;
- undefined;
- defined and false/zero;
- a defined graded value;
- one of several unresolved candidates;
- merely unknown to an observer.

Do not merge these unless the source theory explicitly supplies the completion/encoding rule.

### B. Bridge explicitness

For every step of the form

`X -> Y`,

ask which rule actually supplies the transition.

Examples include:

- semantic type/application rule;
- reference interpretation;
- accommodation rule;
- pragmatic implicature bridge;
- disambiguation rule;
- discourse update map;
- felicity condition;
- institutional constitutive/regulative rule;
- authority-conflict resolution rule;
- focus/information-structure interpretation rule.

If the rule belongs to linguistics, pragmatics, law, social ontology, or an institution, it remains external application data. DSD must not manufacture it.

### C. No retrospective completion

Do not infer an earlier state from a later successful result when the mapping is non-injective or requires additional conditions.

Examples:

- later accommodation does not establish original presupposition support;
- later disambiguation does not establish original uniqueness;
- later discourse accessibility does not establish earlier accessibility;
- observed compliance does not establish normative authority;
- valid-looking downstream effect does not, by itself, reconstruct role/delegation/jurisdiction/procedure history.

### D. Preserve ordered relations

When the source relation is directional, preserve the ordered typed input.

`Authority(s,h,a,C)` need not equal `Authority(h,s,a,C)`.

Do not convert ordered pairs to unordered sets, or relational properties to unary participant labels, without a separate justification.

### E. Preserve unresolved multiplicity

Several legitimate candidates or several simultaneously valid relations do not automatically determine one output.

- multiple semantic candidates require disambiguation/selection;
- multiple valid authority relations may require conflict resolution.

The existence of alternatives is not the same as a unique resolved state.

### F. Audit reduction / aggregation loss

If two source structures become equal after a reduction,

`R(x1) = R(x2)`,

that equality alone does not establish

`x1 = x2`.

Record which distinctions the reduction discards before using the output for reconstruction or responsibility attribution.

### G. Use the weakest sufficient DSD layer

The campaign also supplies a restraint on DSD itself:

> availability of a stronger DSD layer does not imply that the layer should be used.

Do not convert every external tag into an Axis-Property, and do not identify linguistic dynamic semantics with Structural Reorganization Dynamics merely because both use the word `dynamic`.

## 4. DSD layer usage across the campaign

### Formation Axiom System

**Core layer for the entire first-pass campaign.**

It is useful because it supplies typed carriers, staged formation, partial assignment, channel identity, and distinctions among absence / undefinedness / defined values. In linguistic applications it acts as a **preservation and staging framework**, not as a grammar, lexical semantics, reference theory, truth theory, pragmatic theory, or institution theory.

### Axis-Property Axiom System

**Not generally required.**

Its use becomes meaningful when the external source itself requires relation-sensitive structure over formed/tagged entities.

- `LING-001`–`006`: core results do not require it.
- `LING-007`: optional and structurally relevant for role/authority relations.
- `LING-008`: natural/recommended optional extension for a full institutional relation model.
- `LING-009`: strongly useful for ordered binary/higher-order authority/power relations.
- `LING-010`: not required; promoting focus/topic into axis properties would be an unnecessary extra interpretation for the core witness.

The campaign therefore provides both positive and negative evidence about where Axis-Property is useful.

### Channel-Indexed Static Aggregation

**Not a core linguistic semantics layer.**

It becomes directly useful in `LING-010` as a structural warning about reduction and information loss: equal downstream aggregates or coarse summaries need not reconstruct complete typed source structure.

### Structural Reorganization Dynamics

**Not directly required in the first-pass linguistic campaign.**

`LING-006` explicitly distinguishes linguistic dynamic semantics from DSD Structural Reorganization Dynamics. A discourse sequence is most faithfully represented, if a DSD encoding is desired, as static Formation snapshots joined by explicit source-theory update bridges. The physical/mathematical dynamics layer must not be imported by name alone.

## 5. What the campaign supports

The ten cases jointly support the following limited conclusions.

1. **Structural reinterpretation is viable.** Independent linguistic theories repeatedly preserve distinctions that can be represented without contradiction by DSD staging/typing when the source theory remains primary.
2. **Predefinition restraint receives multiple independent corroborations.** Prematurely filling unavailable, unresolved, later-repaired, inferred, relational, or reduced states can change the source problem.
3. **The strongest cross-case value is preservation discipline, not new linguistic content.** DSD helps ask what was formed, what was merely a candidate, which bridge was used, which relation direction was active, and what information was lost.
4. **Layer selection matters.** Formation is broadly sufficient; Axis-Property is useful only in relation-sensitive cases; Static Aggregation supplies a reduction warning; Structural Reorganization Dynamics is not automatically relevant.
5. **External theory plurality must be preserved.** Free-logical treatments, vagueness theories, presupposition accounts, focus theories, institutional theories, and authority theories do not collapse to one mandatory semantics. DSD must not choose among them without an application reason.

---

## 6. What the campaign does not support

The campaign does **not** establish:

- a DSD grammar of natural language;
- a DSD lexical or truth-conditional semantics;
- a unique DSD theory of reference, presupposition, implicature, ambiguity, vagueness, focus, or discourse;
- a DSD derivation of speech-act felicity conditions;
- a DSD derivation of social, legal, or institutional authority;
- empirical validation of linguistic predictions;
- proof of the DSD axioms from linguistic agreement;
- universal validity of the same encoding across all linguistic theories.

These boundaries are part of the positive result: the campaign remains a cross-domain analysis and preservation test rather than an appropriation of the source disciplines.

## 7. Campaign closure and follow-up rule

The **first-pass linguistics/formal-semantics campaign is closed at LING-010 / Global Case 023**.

New linguistic cases should be added only when at least one of the following holds:

1. the source phenomenon introduces a genuinely new structural node not already represented by LING-001–010;
2. an existing DSD mapping is placed under meaningful counterpressure or falsification pressure;
3. a real-world administrative, military, corporate, or institutional case provides independent evidence that requires the linguistic distinctions in an applied setting;
4. an empirical or computational benchmark can test a concrete prediction rather than merely restate structural similarity.

Routine variants of an existing ambiguity, implicature, role, or authority example should not be counted as new independent nodes.

## Overall verdict

**First-pass linguistic campaign: complete.**

- Formation contradiction found: **no**.
- Structural reinterpretation across the selected phenomena: **supported**.
- Predefinition restraint: **repeatedly and independently corroborated**.
- Axis-Property: **selectively useful, especially for ordered relation-sensitive institutional cases; not universal**.
- Static Aggregation: **useful for explicit reduction/information-loss auditing**.
- Structural Reorganization Dynamics: **not directly required**.
- Unique DSD linguistic theory: **not claimed and not supported by this campaign**.

The main value of the domain is therefore a network of independent external nodes that makes the DSD analysis discipline more constrained and more precise, rather than a claim that DSD invented those distinctions.
