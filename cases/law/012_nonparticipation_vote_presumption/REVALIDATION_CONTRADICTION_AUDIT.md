# LAW-001 Cross-Jurisdiction Revalidation — Contradiction Audit

Status: complete for this revalidation pass.

## Candidate 1 — `nonaction never has consequences`

Rejected.

Australian compulsory voting assigns legal consequences to failure to vote without sufficient reason.

UNIDROIT also recognizes contexts in which silence/inactivity can acquire an acceptance consequence through agreement, established practice, or usage.

Result:

`nonaction can be legally operative`.

This does not establish:

`nonaction has the identity of the opposite/affirmative action`.

## Candidate 2 — `abstention = NO whenever abstention hurts adoption`

Rejected.

UN General Assembly Rule 86 expressly distinguishes abstention from affirmative/negative voting.

EU Council unanimity rules can allow adoption despite abstention.

Thus downstream effect does not determine input identity.

## Candidate 3 — `abstention = YES whenever adoption proceeds despite abstention`

Rejected.

EU Council rules expressly speak of abstention while allowing the act to be adopted.

A rule can make abstention non-blocking without converting it into affirmative assent.

## Candidate 4 — `failure to vote = invalid/informal vote`

Rejected.

AEC turnout and informality are separately measured. Informal ballot papers are separately counted/set aside. Failure to vote can also trigger a separate non-voter process.

Thus nonparticipation, ballot formation/submission, informality, and downstream counting remain distinct.

## Candidate 5 — `silence can never amount to acceptance`

Rejected as universal.

UNIDROIT Article 2.1.6 states that silence/inactivity does not **in itself** amount to acceptance, while its commentary preserves agreement/practice/usage cases in which silence can be operative.

Surviving rule:

`silence/nonaction does not acquire assent/opposition identity without a source-supplied relation`.

## Candidate 6 — `presumption of innocence means prosecution bears every burden on every issue`

Rejected as too strong.

ECHR doctrine permits bounded presumptions of fact/law, and US federal model instructions recognize source-defined affirmative-defence burdens on defendants while preserving the government's burden on offence elements.

Surviving rule:

The charge/guilt relation and source-defined burden for criminal responsibility must remain distinct from subsidiary presumptions or affirmative-defence burdens.

## Candidate 7 — `not guilty = factual innocence proved`

Rejected.

US model instructions make not guilty the verdict when the government fails to establish guilt beyond reasonable doubt. UK CPS materials explicitly explain that acquittal means the conviction standard was not met and does not itself amount to a contrary finding about the complainant.

Thus:

`failure to establish guilt -> no guilty verdict under source rule`

but not:

`failure to establish guilt -> positive proof of factual innocence`.

## Candidate 8 — `charge/accusation = evidence/guilt`

Rejected.

ICC Article 66 and US model instructions preserve presumption of innocence and place a proof gate between accusation and guilty judgment.

## Candidate 9 — `lack of guilty verdict = DSD undefined`

Rejected.

Acquittal/not guilty, pending trial, hung jury, dismissed charge, and other legal statuses can each be fully defined source-side states.

DSD partiality must not replace legal procedural status.

## Candidate 10 — direct contradiction with Formation

None found after type matching.

The revalidated sources support a source-fidelity rule compatible with Formation's resistance to premature totalization. They do not establish that legal voting or criminal-procedure states literally are Formation stages/channels.

## Final audit judgment

The original core `nonaction != opposite action` survives only if read as an **identity/non-totalization claim**, not as a claim that nonaction can never have downstream legal effect.

The original responsibility claim also survives in narrowed form:

`failure to establish responsibility under the governing burden/threshold != positive proof of the opposite factual proposition`.

No universal claim is made that all burdens, presumptions, or verdict taxonomies are identical across legal systems.
