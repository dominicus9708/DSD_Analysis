# PHIL-001 / Global Case 044 — Source Notes

## Primary / authoritative sources used

### David J. Chalmers, *Does Conceivability Entail Possibility?* (2002)
Author-hosted HTML: https://consc.net/papers/conceivability.html

This is the main source for the mature modal bridge rather than relying on the bare three-line zombie slogan.

Relevant source structure:

- Chalmers explicitly describes the general three-step form: epistemic claim -> modal claim -> metaphysical claim.
- He distinguishes prima facie vs. ideal, positive vs. negative, and primary vs. secondary conceivability.
- He warns that defining conceivability directly in terms of possibility would trivialize the bridge.
- His central defensible bridge is: ideal primary positive conceivability -> primary possibility.
- Applied to zombies, with `P` the physical truths and `Q` a phenomenal truth, he reconstructs the argument as:
  1. `P & ~Q` is ideally primarily positively conceivable;
  2. if so, `P & ~Q` is primarily possible;
  3. if so, materialism is false.
- He then states that the argument is unsound *as it stands* because the route from primary possibility to the secondary/modal requirement relevant to materialism is not immediate, and develops a more elaborate two-dimensional rescue with further assumptions and a Russellian-monist loophole.

### David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind* author page (1996)
https://consc.net/the-conscious-mind/

The author describes the book as arguing that reductive explanation of consciousness is impossible and that strict materialism must be exceeded. This establishes the original argumentative context but the full zombie chapter is not reproduced on the author page.

### David Chalmers, “Zombies on the Web”
https://consc.net/zombies-on-the-web/

Chalmers defines the philosophical zombie as physically/behaviorally identical to a normal conscious human while lacking conscious experience. He also explicitly calls the logical possibility of zombies controversial and says zombies are a vivid illustration rather than the only possible presentation of the anti-materialist argument.

## Secondary authoritative synthesis

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Zombies”
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/

Used to cross-check the historical and argumentative structure.

SEP reconstructs the simple argument:

1. zombies are conceivable;
2. whatever is conceivable is possible;
3. therefore zombies are possible.

SEP emphasizes that the argument is valid in form but both premises are controversial once clarified. It also records that Chalmers's 1996 presentation treated the zombie description as apparently coherent while recognizing that hidden contradiction remains a legitimate challenge.

## Source-discipline consequence

The full Chalmers argument must not be reduced to:

`define zombie -> zombie possible -> physicalism false`.

The developed argument contains independent epistemic and modal bridge theses. A premise-loading audit must therefore test those theses rather than accuse the definition itself of proving possibility.
