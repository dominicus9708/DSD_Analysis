# LAW-006 Direct Contradiction Audit

## Audit rule

A direct contradiction is counted only after action identity, actor, role, governing rule, jurisdiction/institution, context, purpose, time, applicability, and normative semantics are aligned.

## C1 — DSD requires one status, while law gives obligation plus permission

**Rejected.**

Formation does not require every application object to have one global normative value. A typed application can preserve separate rule-conditioned coordinates or channels.

Standard deontic logic itself commonly accepts `O(A) -> P(A)`, so a mutually exclusive `OBLIGATORY/PERMITTED` coding would be an application error rather than a DSD requirement.

## C2 — Exception creates both prohibition and permission and therefore contradiction

**Rejected as a universal reading.**

Some source exceptions alter the scope or definition of the base prohibition. ILO Convention No. 29 Article 2(2) is a clear witness: specified work is excluded from the Convention's forced-labour definition under conditions.

The correct source-side question is whether the prohibition rule applies, not whether one global Boolean must be overwritten.

## C3 — Lack of permission must equal prohibition

**Rejected.**

UNIDROIT Articles 6.1.14-6.1.16 explicitly contemplate a permission that is neither granted nor refused while other obligations already exist.

Therefore:

`not yet granted`

is not identical to

`refused/prohibited`.

This parallels LAW-001's distinction between nonformation and defined negative value.

## C4 — Obligation must imply physical possibility

**Not adopted as a DSD claim.**

Legal and deontic theories can contain impossible or conflicting obligations, and actual compliance can fail. DSD Formation does not derive `ought implies can`.

Physical possibility and normative obligation therefore remain different predicates unless the source supplies the bridge.

## C5 — Exemption must equal positive permission

**Rejected as a universal identity.**

An exemption can remove a duty, burden, or applicability condition without imposing the opposite duty and without necessarily using a strong-permission operator.

The exact result is source-dependent.

## C6 — Derogation negates the original norm globally

**Rejected.**

ICCPR Article 4 and General Comment No. 29 make derogation conditional, temporary, limited to strict necessity, and subject to safeguards; some rights remain non-derogable.

Derogation is therefore modeled as a rule-conditioned scope modification, not universal deletion of the base normative structure.

## C7 — Conflicting norms contradict Formation because one action receives incompatible values

**Rejected unless the application first collapses distinct rule coordinates.**

If rule `r1` requires an action and rule `r2` forbids it, the source regime contains a normative conflict. Formation does not authorize DSD Analysis to repair that conflict by choosing one value.

The faithful representation retains the rule identities and records the conflict until a source-side priority rule is supplied.

## C8 — Prohibition is channel absence

**Rejected.**

A prohibited action can be perfectly describable, physically possible, actually performed, and legally regulated. Mapping prohibition to channel absence would erase the very event to which the norm attaches.

## C9 — Permission is Formation admission

**Rejected.**

Formation admission is an internal DSD formation concept. Legal permission is an application-level normative relation. Similar staged behavior does not make the concepts identical.

## C10 — Defence defeat of prohibition proves global permission

**Rejected.**

Under the functional `P/D/J` model, defence may defeat the prosecution/attribution route by showing that the asserted prohibition does not apply, an exception applies, or a required element is unformed. This does not automatically establish an affirmative permission under every other rule.

This is consistent with LAW-002's cross-regime non-totalization result.

## Final audit

No direct contradiction with Formation remains after the normative act, rule, actor, scope, context, time, and applicability coordinates are retained.

The strongest rejected naive mappings are:

- `possible = permitted`;
- `permitted = obligatory` as a mutually exclusive classification;
- `not permitted = prohibited`;
- `exception = opposite value`;
- `exempt = affirmative permission`;
- `prohibited = absent`;
- `conflict = choose one value`.

The result is structural correspondence plus a strong predefinition/type-discipline boundary, not derivation of deontic law from DSD.
