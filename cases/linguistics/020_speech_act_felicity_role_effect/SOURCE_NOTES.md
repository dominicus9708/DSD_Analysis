# LING-007 / Global Case 020 — Source Notes

## External sources

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Speech Acts
Current archived entry checked: Summer 2023.

Relevant source-theory points:
- saying can sometimes constitute doing, but not every utterance by every speaker succeeds as the intended speech act;
- institutional acts such as appointing, declaring proceedings open, christening, or marrying require appropriate authority, time, place, and circumstances;
- failures can be `misfires`, in which the intended act fails to be performed;
- a speech act can instead be performed but remain defective, e.g. an insincere promise, classed as an `abuse` in the Austinian framework;
- speech acts can be rescinded/retracted in some cases, without changing the historical fact that the earlier act occurred.

URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2023/entries/speech-acts/

### Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy — J. L. Austin
Relevant points:
- locutionary act: saying something meaningful;
- illocutionary act: the act performed in saying something, such as ordering, promising, warning, appointing;
- perlocutionary act: contingent effects on hearers' thoughts, feelings, or actions;
- Austinian felicity conditions include accepted procedure, appropriate persons/circumstances, correct and complete execution, and sincerity/consequent conduct conditions;
- exercitives such as appointing or ordering presuppose authority or influence;
- the conventionality of speech acts is strongest in institutional cases but is not a single universal mechanism for every illocutionary act.

URL: https://iep.utm.edu/john-austin/

## DSD source notes

### Formation Axiom System
The operational channel has identity

`c = (p, a, lambda, v, rho)`

and the role `rho` is retained in channel identity. Formation traces preserve the restriction-realization witness history without adding that history as an extra channel coordinate.

Implication for this case:
- a role-sensitive utterance/event can be represented without erasing the role;
- however Formation does not state that a role label such as `chair` entails a legal/social authority relation or felicity condition.

Source: `DSD_Formation_Axiom_System_EN.pdf`, Definition 3.5 / Theorem 3.6 and the Stage-VI interface.

### Axis-Property Axiom System
The axis-property layer:
- distinguishes bare realized lines from formation-tagged channels;
- supports tag-sensitive unary, binary, higher-order, and mixed profiles;
- uses partial property assignments;
- distinguishes unavailable input, undefined application, defined zero, and defined value;
- explicitly states that a property name alone has no mathematical content; profile, value carrier, assignment, and compatibility data must be supplied.

Implication for this case:
An application may introduce an authority/eligibility relation as a typed tag-sensitive property only after specifying its formal profile and assignment. The published system does not contain a built-in `authority` semantics and does not derive speech-act felicity.

Source: `DSD_Axioms for the Property Structure of Realized Axes_EN.pdf`, Sections 3.3–3.4 and Discussion/Conclusion.

## Source-discipline warning
Do not collapse all speech acts into institutional declarations. Promising, warning, requesting, appointing, declaring, and persuading do not share one identical set of success conditions. The case uses institutional declaration as a finite witness, not as a universal speech-act template.
