# Structural Gravity Research Log — Experiments 100–104

Date: 2026-09-03

## Experiment 100 — nonhomogeneous transition balance can select a dimensionless rescaling

Consider

\[
F_\lambda=\lambda^pA+\lambda^qB=0,\qquad p\neq q,
\]

with \(A\) and \(B\) of the same physical dimension and opposite signs at the transition.
Then

\[
\lambda_*^{p-q}=-\frac{B}{A}.
\]

Thus unequal scaling weights can remove a common dimensionless normalization freedom.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED.

However \(\lambda_*\) is dimensionless. This is normalization selection (gauge fixing), not generation of a missing \(L/M\) dimension.

---

## Experiment 101 — mass–geometry bridge necessity

If one competing sector carries physical mass dimension while the other is purely geometric/kinematic (built from \(L,T\) quantities only), the two terms cannot be added or equated without either:

1. an independently normalized mass-bearing quantity in the geometric sector; or
2. an inter-sector bridge coefficient carrying the missing mass dimension.

For structural gravity the latter is precisely a coefficient of the \(L/M\)-type needed to map source mass into a geometric length amplitude.

**Verdict:** direct mass-vs-pure-geometry balance without a mass–geometry bridge is REJECTED.

---

## Experiment 102 — scale breaking is not dimensional-anchor generation

If \(A\) and \(B\) are built only from source \(M,L\), one propagation/kinematic speed, and dimensionless structural descriptors \(\Pi\), then the ratio \(B/A\) can be dimensionless only after all base-dimension exponents cancel.
Hence

\[
\lambda_*=f(\Pi)
\]

can select only a dimensionless structural normalization.
It cannot create a universal quantity with dimensions \(L/M\).

**Verdict:** nonhomogeneous scale selection automatically produces an absolute gravitational normalization — REJECTED.

---

## Experiment 103 — exponent selection vs amplitude selection

A scale-free bounded-transition law can determine a critical scaling exponent,

\[
M_c(L)\propto L^\alpha,
\]

while leaving the dimensionful amplitude free:

\[
M_c(L)=C_\alpha L^\alpha.
\]

For \(\alpha=1\),

\[
[C_1]=M/L.
\]

Thus a theory may derive the linear critical exponent while still failing to derive the absolute mass–length conversion amplitude.

This is the useful distinction:

\[
\boxed{\text{universal exponent}\neq\text{universal dimensional amplitude}.}
\]

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED.

---

## Experiment 104 — status of the nonhomogeneous-balance route

- unequal scaling weights can break a dimensionless rescaling degeneracy: **CONFIRMED**
- the resulting \(\lambda_*\) supplies \(L/M\): **REJECTED**
- pure geometry and physical mass can compete without a bridge coefficient: **REJECTED unless an independent mass-bearing scale already exists**
- bounded transition may select a scaling exponent while leaving its dimensional amplitude free: **CONFIRMED**
- an absolute structural-gravity normalization still requires an independently normalized dimensionful anchor or a genuinely scale-generating mechanism: **UNRESOLVED / REQUIRED**

## Consequence

The remaining question is no longer whether nonhomogeneous transition dynamics can select a branch; they can.
The sharper target is whether DSD contains a mechanism that generates a dimensionful mass–geometry anchor rather than merely fixing a dimensionless normalization freedom.
