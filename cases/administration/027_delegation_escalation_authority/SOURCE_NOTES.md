# Source Notes — ADMIN-002

## 1. U.S. Army ADP 6-0

Official doctrine distinguishes delegation of decision-making authority from retained command responsibility.

Relevant points:

- commanders delegate appropriate authority based on subordinate capability and experience;
- delegated authority allows subordinates to decide and act in specified areas;
- some decisions remain the commander's sole responsibility and are not delegated;
- ADP 6-0 states that commanders can delegate authority but cannot delegate responsibility;
- decentralized execution changes the location of decision making without erasing the higher commander's responsibility.

Source:
- https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/1FE33715-CFD1-4614-A489-B3E0480C3F80-1428688882108/adp6_0.pdf

Counterpressure:

`delegated authority = transferred responsibility` is false at least in this organizational doctrine.

## 2. FEMA/NIMS ICS

Delegation of Authority specifies the duration and limits of an ICS organization's authority and may include fiscal, geographic, tactical, temporal, and other constraints.

Not every incident requires a new delegation; authority can pre-exist under jurisdictional procedures.

Transfer of command has an explicit process:

- briefing of essential incident information;
- communication of effective date/time;
- transition designed to minimize disruption;
- reasons for transfer can include incident complexity, qualifications, duration, agency direction, or legal requirement.

Sources:
- FEMA ICS Position Training Reference Guide: https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/icsresource/assets/ics_training_reference_guide.pdf
- Transfer of Command: https://emilms.fema.gov/_is_0200c/groups/191.html
- Transfer review: https://emilms.fema.gov/_is0200c/groups/247.html

Counterpressure:

`authority is unbounded once delegated` is false.

`change of commander = instantaneous deletion of prior organizational state` is false.

## 3. NASA governance / Technical Authority / dissent

NASA governance uses multiple authority structures and explicit checks and balances.

Relevant points:

- Engineering, Safety and Mission Assurance, and Health/Medical Technical Authorities can approve/disapprove waivers or deviations within their authority;
- independent reviews are designed to be outside project supervision/advocacy/control;
- dissenting opinions can be elevated successively up authority chains, potentially to the Administrator;
- risk acceptance has identified decision authority and technical concurrence requirements;
- NASA distinguishes Programmatic Authority from Institutional/Technical Authority.

Sources:
- NPD 7120.4E: https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?c=7120&s=4E&t=NPD
- NPR 7120.8 dissent and Technical Authority: https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7120_008A_&page_name=Chapter5
- NPD 8700.1F: https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?c=8700&s=1F&t=NPD

Counterpressure:

`one organization = one authority line` is false.

`dissent/escalation = organizational defect` is false in a source system that deliberately institutionalizes dissent and independent technical oversight.

## Source-side synthesis

The witness families jointly motivate separation among:

- formal position/rank;
- ordinary reporting relationship;
- delegated decision authority;
- scope/limits/duration of delegation;
- retained responsibility/accountability;
- technical/review authority;
- risk-acceptance authority;
- dissent/escalation right;
- transfer-of-command state and effective time;
- implementation authority.

No universal rule makes all of these coextensive.
