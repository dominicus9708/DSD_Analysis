# PHIL-005 / Global 048 — Brain in a Vat: Source Notes

Status: **first-pass source reconstruction**.

## Source distinction

PHIL-005 treats two historically distinct targets separately.

1. **Generic skeptical BIV**: a subject's internally accessible experiences are stipulated to be qualitatively indistinguishable from those of an ordinary embodied subject although the external causes differ radically.
2. **Putnam's always-envatted BIV**: Hilary Putnam, *Reason, Truth and History* (1981), Chapter 1, uses semantic/content externalism to argue that an always-envatted subject cannot use `brain` and `vat` with ordinary-world reference and therefore cannot straightforwardly state the skeptical hypothesis in ordinary English.

These are not the same argument.

## Putnam boundary

Putnam's target is the radical **always-envatted** case. A recently envatted subject may retain ordinary causal-historical reference for some time, so the semantic argument does not automatically eliminate recent-envatment skepticism.

Representative literature also notes a major reconstruction problem: deriving the object-level conclusion `I am not a BIV` from a semantic claim about the truth/falsity of the sentence requires a regime-appropriate disquotation/truth-condition bridge. Brueckner-style objections argue that using the ordinary-English bridge can be epistemically circular if knowing that it is the correct bridge already requires knowing that one is not a BIV.

## Neuroscience comparison boundary

The user's lived experience is used only as a **hypothesis generator**, not as medical evidence or a general causal claim.

The closest research families located are:

- reality monitoring / perceptual reality monitoring;
- source monitoring and externalizing bias;
- self/other monitoring and corollary-discharge/efference-copy models;
- sleep/wake sensory disconnection and hypnagogic/hypnopompic reality-monitoring difficulty;
- artificial sensory feedback and intracortical microstimulation.

The literature supports the following limited points:

1. Perceptual reality monitoring concerns discrimination of internally generated from externally triggered experience.
2. No single cue is known to unambiguously mark internal versus external origin; higher-level monitoring integrates multiple sensory and cognitive factors.
3. Hallucinations in several clinical samples are associated with source-monitoring impairment or externalizing bias, but this is not universal across all hallucination-prone populations and is not a single-cause theory.
4. Sleep and sleep-onset states alter environmental coupling and the balance of internal/external processing; sleep does not imply absolute sensory shutdown.
5. Current neural-interface technology can evoke artificial percepts, but naturalness, neural coding, tissue response, multimodal integration, and full bidirectional integration remain active technical problems.

Therefore the project does **not** infer:

`reduced arousal -> hallucination`

or

`electrical stimulation equality -> full experiential equality`.

## DSD source constraints

The DSD Formation Axiom System distinguishes reduced/composite coincidence from strict full-structure equivalence and keeps channel identity typed.

The Axis-Property Axiom System requires explicit property signatures, input profiles, carriers, domains, and assignments; a property name alone does not determine a comparison.

Channel-Indexed Static Aggregation explicitly shows that equal aggregates need not reconstruct support-tagged or complete typed structure without an injectivity/reconstruction condition.

Structural Reorganization Dynamics represents time-indexed component-resolved states and does not identify aggregate equality with dynamic-state equality. Physical or biological interpretation requires explicit constitutive bridges.

These rules are used as audit discipline only; no DSD primitive is identified with consciousness, hallucination, reality monitoring, neural stimulation, or semantic reference.
