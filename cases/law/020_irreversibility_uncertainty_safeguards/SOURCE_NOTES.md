# LAW-009 Source Notes

## 1. Human Rights Committee — General Comment No. 36 (Article 6, right to life)

Official source: OHCHR / UN treaty-body document CCPR/C/GC/36.

Relevant structure:

- Article 6 protects the right to life and the Committee treats capital punishment under especially strict constraints.
- Paragraph 41 states that violations of ICCPR Article 14 fair-trial guarantees in proceedings resulting in the death penalty render the sentence arbitrary and contrary to Article 6.
- Paragraph 43 states that executing a person whose guilt has not been established beyond reasonable doubt constitutes arbitrary deprivation of life, and calls for feasible measures to avoid wrongful convictions and reconsider past convictions in light of new evidence.
- Paragraph 46 requires a final judgment, opportunity to use all judicial appeal procedures, and resolution of other available avenues before execution.
- Paragraph 42 recognizes that serious procedural flaws beyond Article 14 may also render a death sentence contrary to Article 6.

## 2. UN ECOSOC 1984/50 — Safeguards for those facing the death penalty

Official source: United Nations Digital Library, E/RES/1984/50.

Relevant structure:

- death penalty only for the most serious crimes under the resolution's safeguards;
- guilt must rest on clear and convincing evidence leaving no room for an alternative explanation of the facts;
- execution only pursuant to a final judgment by a competent court after legal process providing all possible fair-trial safeguards;
- adequate legal assistance is required;
- appeal/review and pardon/commutation protections are part of the safeguard architecture.

Source URL:
https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/70601

## 3. UN ECOSOC 1989/64 — strengthened capital-case safeguards

Official source: United Nations Digital Library, E/RES/1989/64.

This is a particularly strong comparative witness because it expressly recommends special protection in capital cases **above and beyond** the protection afforded in non-capital cases, including time and facilities for defence and counsel at every stage. It also recommends mandatory appeal/review and clemency or pardon provisions in all capital cases.

Source URL:
https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/75549?ln=en

## 4. European Court of Human Rights — Rule 39 interim measures

Official ECHR source:
https://www.echr.coe.int/web/echr/applying-for-interim-measures

Relevant structure:

- interim measures may be indicated where there is an imminent risk of irreparable harm to a Convention right;
- their function includes avoiding irreversible situations that would prevent national courts or the ECHR from properly examining a complaint or securing the practical and effective benefit of asserted Convention rights;
- they are exceptional and follow rigorous examination of relevant circumstances;
- irreparable harm therefore matters, but is not by itself a universal sufficient condition.

The current Rule 39 request portal also states that interim measures apply only where there is an imminent risk of irreparable harm.

Portal:
https://r39.echr.coe.int/

## 5. International Court of Justice — provisional measures

Official ICJ source example: Order of 1 December 2023.

Relevant structure:

- provisional measures may be indicated where irreparable prejudice could be caused to rights in issue or alleged disregard may entail irreparable consequences;
- the Court also requires urgency: a real and imminent risk that irreparable prejudice will occur before final decision;
- this separates `irreparable prejudice` from `urgency` and from the availability of a provisional measure.

Source URL:
https://www.icj-cij.org/index.php/node/203341

## 6. Source-level negative findings

The selected sources do **not** establish a universal scalar quantity of legal irreversibility.

They do **not** establish the universal theorem:

`if consequence A is more irreversible than consequence B, every legal system must assign A a strictly stronger safeguard package than B`.

Instead they show narrower relations:

- capital punishment can trigger expressly heightened procedural protection;
- risk of irreparable harm can trigger interim preservation mechanisms;
- irreparability generally operates together with other source conditions such as urgency, protected-right status, finality, evidentiary sufficiency, and institutional jurisdiction.

## 7. DSD source boundary

Formation Axiom System is a static typed framework and expressly requires domain-specific interpretation maps. It distinguishes undefined assignment, defined zero, channel absence and zero contribution, which is useful for preserving legal state distinctions but does not itself define legal irreversibility.

Structural Reorganization Dynamics supplies time-indexed admissible structural states and transition/succession structure, but states no universal conservation or legal restoration law. Any legal recoverability/irreparability relation must therefore be added by the application.
