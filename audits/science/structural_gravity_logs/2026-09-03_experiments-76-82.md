## Experiment 76 — Geometric distortion is not scalar by default

Let \(g_0\) be a supplied reference geometry and \(g\) the source-affected geometry on a common comparison carrier. A direct local geometric contrast is tensor-valued:

\[
\Delta g=g-g_0.
\]

A scalar structural distortion

\[
X=\Psi(\Delta g)
\]

therefore requires a scalarization map \(\Psi\) unless the admissible geometry is already restricted to a one-scalar family.

## Experiment 77 — Scalarization collision

A single trace-, norm-, determinant-, or other scalar readout need not reconstruct the full distortion tensor. For example, a nonzero trace-free anisotropic distortion can have the same trace as the zero tensor. Thus

\[
\boxed{X\text{ scalar equality}\not\Rightarrow\Delta g\text{ equality}}
\]

in a general geometry-primary model.

If physical response can depend on anisotropic distortion components, a scalar \(X\) is incomplete.

## Experiment 78 — Conformal/isotropic specialization

If the admissible distortion is restricted to

\[
\boxed{g=e^{2\phi}g_0}
\]

then the dimensionless scalar \(\phi\) completely determines the metric within that specialization. In a weak-distortion regime,

\[
g\approx(1+2\phi)g_0.
\]

Thus a scalar distortion field becomes a complete geometric descriptor when isotropic/conformal reduction is independently justified.

## Experiment 79 — Geometry-to-acceleration dimensional closure

For dimensionless conformal distortion \(\phi\),

\[
[\nabla\phi]=L^{-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathbf a=-\beta c_{\rm info}^2\nabla\phi}
\]

has the dimensions of acceleration for dimensionless \(\beta\).

This does not derive the response law or the value of \(\beta\), but it shows that once the distortion field is genuinely dimensionless geometry, the geometry-to-acceleration bridge need not introduce a new dimensionful constant beyond the propagation-speed scale.

## Experiment 80 — The missing normalization moves upstream to mass-to-geometry conversion

Suppose the far-field conformal distortion has the radial shape

\[
\phi(r;M)\sim\frac{A(M)}r.
\]

Because \(\phi\) is dimensionless,

\[
[A]=L.
\]

If independent-source additivity and regularity yield a linear source amplitude,

\[
A(M)=\Lambda_*M,
\]

then

\[
[\Lambda_*]=L/M.
\]

The acceleration becomes

\[
\mathbf a
\sim
\beta c_{\rm info}^2\Lambda_*
\frac{M}{r^2}\hat r
\]

up to the selected orientation convention.

Thus the unresolved absolute coupling is localized in the source-to-geometric-amplitude conversion \(\Lambda_*\), not necessarily in the geometry-to-acceleration step.

## Experiment 81 — Normalization relocation principle

Different choices for the physical units/normalization of the distortion variable move the same missing scale between bridges.

- If \(X\) is defined with potential-like units \(L^2/T^2\), the source-to-\(X\) map already contains the absolute coupling scale and \(\mathbf a=-\nabla X\) may be a calibrated definition.
- If \(\phi\) is a dimensionless geometric deformation, the response may be written with \(c_{\rm info}^2\), but the source amplitude requires \(\Lambda_*[L/M]\).

Hence

\[
\boxed{\text{changing the normalization of the distortion variable relocates the missing scale; it does not remove it.}}
\]

## Experiment 82 — Scalar geometry as an emergent far-field sector

A general metric distortion may be decomposed schematically into an isotropic/conformal sector and anisotropic detail:

\[
\Delta g
=\Delta g_{\rm iso}+\Delta g_{\rm aniso}.
\]

A scalar geometry-primary description becomes an effective far-field description if the anisotropic sector either vanishes or becomes response-invisible under the exterior map. This is the geometric analogue of the earlier detail-erasure criterion.

## Verdict

- general geometric distortion is one scalar by necessity: **REJECTED**.
- conformal/isotropic specialization permits a complete scalar geometric descriptor: **CONFIRMED conditionally**.
- dimensionless geometry plus \(c_{\rm info}^2\) closes the dimensions of acceleration: **CONFIRMED dimensionally**.
- this solves the absolute mass-to-geometry normalization: **REJECTED**.
- missing \(L/M\) scale can be localized in the source-to-geometric-amplitude map: **CONFIRMED as a structural decomposition**.
- scalar structural distortion may emerge as a far-field coarse geometry after anisotropic-detail erasure: **CONDITIONAL**.
