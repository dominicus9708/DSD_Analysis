# PHIL-001 / Global Case 044 — Philosophical Zombie Premise Loading Audit

Status: **first-pass analysis completed**.

Final judgment: **mixed / conditional validity**. The naive premise-loading objection fails against Chalmers's mature formulation, but the argument's anti-materialist force depends on independently defended modal bridge principles and cannot be obtained from the zombie stipulation alone.

## 1. Separate the layers

Let `P` abbreviate the relevant total physical truths and `Q` a phenomenal truth such as someone's being conscious.

The mature structure is not simply:

`define P & ~Q -> P & ~Q is possible -> materialism false`.

A more faithful reconstruction from Chalmers 2002 is:

1. epistemic premise: `P & ~Q` is **ideally primarily positively conceivable**;
2. modal bridge: ideal primary positive conceivability entails **primary possibility**;
3. metaphysical bridge: the relevant modal separation, together with further intension assumptions, undermines materialism.

These are distinct claims and must be audited separately.

## 2. Premise-loading test

### Naive formulation

If one merely defines a zombie as a physical duplicate without consciousness and then treats the successful verbal definition itself as proof that such a world is metaphysically possible, the reasoning is under-supported. The disputed modal status has been promoted without an independent bridge.

### Mature Chalmers formulation

The stronger accusation — that Chalmers simply hides the anti-physicalist conclusion inside the definition — does **not** survive source checking.

Chalmers explicitly distinguishes kinds of conceivability, refuses to define conceivability directly in terms of metaphysical possibility because that would trivialize the link, and supplies a separate modal-rationalist argument for a restricted conceivability-to-possibility bridge.

Therefore the definition of the zombie is a scenario specification; the controversial work is performed by the epistemic premise and modal bridge, not by definition alone.

**Verdict on premise loading:** not confirmed against the mature formulation; confirmed only as a risk in compressed or undefended presentations.

## 3. Conceivability status test

The weak claim

`the zombie description initially appears coherent`

is not yet the strong premise needed by the mature argument.

Chalmers distinguishes prima facie from ideal conceivability precisely because an apparently coherent thought experiment may conceal a contradiction that better reasoning would expose.

The relevant premise is therefore much stronger:

`P & ~Q is ideally primarily positively conceivable`.

This premise is substantive and defeasible. Materialists can attack it by arguing that ideal reflection would reveal an a priori connection between the physical and phenomenal.

Thus the audit locates a genuine open pressure point rather than a definitional circularity.

## 4. Modal bridge test

The simple principle

`whatever is conceivable is metaphysically possible`

is too coarse. Chalmers's 2002 framework narrows it to a bridge from ideal primary positive conceivability to primary possibility.

This is significant for the DSD audit because an intermediate admission rule is explicitly supplied rather than silently assumed.

But supplying a bridge is not the same as proving it beyond dispute. The bridge remains a philosophical thesis, defended through modal rationalism and by arguing against strong necessities.

Therefore:

`description/coherence -> possibility`

is **not automatic** even inside the source theory; it requires a defended modal rule.

## 5. Materialism-conclusion test

Chalmers explicitly notes that the zombie argument, in the reconstructed primary/secondary framework, appears **unsound as it stands**: primary possibility does not immediately give the secondary possibility relevant to the strongest materialist necessity claim.

He then develops a rescue using two-dimensional semantics, assumptions about the primary and secondary intensions of phenomenal and physical truths, and a restricted remaining loophole associated with Russellian monism.

Therefore the strongest anti-materialist conclusion is not licensed by the bare zombie scenario or by a one-line conceivability claim.

The argument is layered and its final force depends on additional bridge assumptions.

## 6. Observer-access audit

The planned DSD observer-access criticism does **not** hit the core zombie argument.

The argument does not infer zombiehood from observations made by the zombie or from an internal subject's inability to distinguish worlds. The relevant epistemic subject is the philosopher/conceiver assessing whether a complete scenario is coherently conceivable.

Thus:

`internal observer cannot identify zombie world`

is not, by itself, a refutation of Chalmers's modal inference.

**Verdict:** observer-access objection is inapplicable to the core argument and must not be counted as a failure of the thought experiment.

## 7. Inverse non-identifiability audit

Likewise, the core argument is not principally an inverse reconstruction of external ontology from identical empirical outputs.

Physical and behavioral indistinguishability helps specify the zombie contrast, but Chalmers does not argue:

`same internal/behavioral observations -> therefore zombie world`.

So the generic DSD inverse-non-identifiability criticism is not the primary pressure point here.

**Verdict:** mostly inapplicable to the central modal argument.

## 8. DSD comparison

The current Formation Axiom System explicitly separates candidate structural data from admitted/describable configurations and states that domain-specific applications require additional interpretation maps. It also retains unsuccessful candidate configurations in the full formation descriptor rather than identifying candidacy with successful formation.

A limited structural comparison is therefore legitimate:

`candidate description != admitted structure without an admissibility/bridge condition`.

The zombie audit shows that the mature source itself recognizes an analogous methodological need: a description that appears coherent must pass stronger conceivability criteria, and conceivability must be connected to possibility by an explicit bridge thesis.

However, the following identifications are prohibited:

- DSD structural admission = metaphysical possibility;
- DSD describability = conceivability;
- DSD formation witness = modal world;
- DSD observer regime = Chalmers's conceiver;
- DSD strict equivalence = physical duplication.

The comparison is methodological only.

## 9. What the audit falsified

### Hypothesis A — the zombie argument simply defines its conclusion into existence

Rejected for Chalmers's mature formulation.

### Hypothesis B — mere prima facie coherence is sufficient for the source argument

Rejected. Chalmers explicitly distinguishes prima facie from ideal conceivability.

### Hypothesis C — the conceivability-to-possibility step is left completely unstated

Rejected. Chalmers gives an explicit restricted bridge and a substantial defense of it.

### Hypothesis D — the mature argument therefore becomes uncontroversially sound

Rejected. The ideal-conceivability premise, the modal-rationalist bridge, and the primary/secondary route to anti-materialism remain disputable.

### Hypothesis E — internal observer access is a decisive objection to the core zombie argument

Rejected as inapplicable.

## 10. Final classification

**Conditional illustration + contested modal argument; no simple premise-loading conviction.**

The zombie scenario is legitimate as a conditional contrast and intuition-generating device. Its stronger anti-materialist use requires additional epistemic and modal bridge principles. Chalmers's mature work explicitly supplies and defends such bridges, so DSD analysis does not expose a trivial circularity.

What DSD analysis does successfully expose is the exact location of the burden:

`scenario specification`

`-> ideal conceivability`

`-> primary possibility`

`-> relevant metaphysical possibility / anti-materialist consequence`.

The transitions, rather than the verbal zombie definition, are where the philosophical dispute must be decided.

## 11. DSD consequence

PHIL-001 is therefore a useful **negative-control case** for DSD Analysis. The audit method does not merely criticize famous thought experiments: when the source already separates candidate description from modal admission and explicitly defends its bridge principles, the method must record that the naive DSD-friendly criticism fails.

No contradiction with the current DSD Formation Axiom System was found. This case is not independent evidence for the DSD axioms; it is evidence that the audit procedure can distinguish a genuine hidden-premise problem from an explicitly acknowledged and defended bridge premise.
