# LAW-003 Cross-Jurisdiction Revalidation Result

Status: cross-jurisdiction revalidation complete.

Original historical `RESULT.md` is preserved.

## 1. Answer-first result

The original non-collapse insight survives, but the rigid chain

`person/entity -> role -> authority -> act -> attributed effect`

must not be read as one universal one-way pipeline.

Rejected strong forms include:

- `no actual authority -> no possible legal effect or attribution`;
- `some authority -> authority for every related act`;
- `official status -> every act attributable`;
- `unauthorized act -> no legally relevant act exists`;
- `later ratification/confirmation -> original authority must have existed`.

The surviving universal candidate is:

**when a source legal regime distinguishes the underlying person/entity, operative role or capacity, actual authority and its scope, apparent or official capacity, the performed act, attribution/effect, and later ratification/confirmation, those coordinates must be preserved until the source rule expressly links or transforms them. Authority status alone does not universally determine attribution or effect.**

Compactly:

`person/entity != role/capacity != authority/scope != act occurrence != attribution/effect`.

But this is a non-identity rule, not a claim that every pair is always disjoint or that every legal system uses the same taxonomy.

## 2. UNIDROIT witness — ordinary authority, apparent authority, ratification

UNIDROIT Principles Chapter 2 Section 2 provides a high-quality transnational witness.

Article 2.2.5 gives the ordinary rule that an agent acting without or beyond authority does not affect principal-third-party legal relations, but also supplies an apparent-authority route when the principal caused reasonable belief in authority.

Article 2.2.9 separately permits later ratification and gives the act the same effects as if initially authorized.

Therefore:

`no actual authority != no possible principal effect under every rule`;

`act occurred != current principal effect`;

`ratification changes legal effect without proving original authority existed`.

This independently supports the project's predefinition rule: do not fill `authority defect` with either `permanent no effect` or `automatic effect` before reading the source rule.

## 3. Vienna Convention witness — representation is purpose- and function-sensitive

VCLT Article 7 treats representation of a State as source-defined and purpose-sensitive. Certain officials may represent the State by virtue of function, while other representatives require appropriate powers or relevant practice/circumstances.

Article 8 then provides that a treaty-related act by a person lacking Article 7 authority is without legal effect unless later confirmed by the State.

This yields:

`person/status != unlimited authority`;

`performed act != present legal effect`;

`present no effect != impossibility of later confirmation`.

The treaty witness is important because it reproduces the same structural distinction outside domestic private agency law.

## 4. ILC State-responsibility witness — ultra vires conduct can still be attributed

The strongest counterpressure comes from State responsibility.

Article 4 attributes conduct of State organs to the State under international law according to the governing attribution rules.

Article 7 goes further: conduct of a State organ or an entity exercising governmental authority can remain attributable to the State when performed in that capacity even if authority is exceeded or instructions are contravened.

The commentary distinguishes such unauthorized-but-official conduct from private conduct of an individual who happens to be an official.

Therefore:

`authority exceeded != State attribution absent`;

and simultaneously:

`person is official != every private act attributable`.

This directly falsifies any universal model in which legal attribution is computed from one actual-authority bit.

## 5. Revised finite structural model

A source-faithful record may require at least:

`(actor/entity, role/capacity, authority basis, authority scope, apparent/official-capacity status, act token, attribution/effect status, ratification/confirmation status, governing rule, time, regime)`.

The same actor may support several role-indexed act tokens.

Different legal regimes may assign different consequences to the same authority defect.

A later transition may alter effect status without altering the historical fact that authority was absent or exceeded when the act occurred.

## 6. Relation to LAW-001 and LAW-002

LAW-001 revalidation established that an input's identity must not be inferred from downstream effect and that nonaction can acquire consequences only through a source rule.

LAW-002 revalidation established that office identity and procedural-function identity are many-to-many and that cross-regime transfer requires source-defined interfaces.

LAW-003 now extends both principles to authority and attribution:

`actor identity != role identity`;

`role/status != authority scope`;

`authority defect != one universal effect`;

`effect/attribution != proof that ordinary authority existed`.

## 7. DSD Formation result

The current Formation system is useful only as a typed preservation discipline.

Its Stage-VI operational channel retains `(p,a,lambda,v,rho)` and therefore preserves role as part of channel identity. The Formation source also permits distinct channels differing only in role, which is structurally compatible with one underlying application object participating under multiple role tags.

However:

`legal person != Formation material item automatically`;

`legal role != rho by identity`;

`legal authority != channel admission`;

`unauthorized legal act != channel absence`;

`legal attribution/effect != component term`.

No direct contradiction with Formation was found after these bridge assumptions were removed.

Axis-Property remains unnecessary for the core result because no realized-axis semantics are supplied by the legal sources.

Static Aggregation is unnecessary.

Dynamics is optional only for time-indexed ratification/confirmation bookkeeping; it does not generate legal retroactivity or attribution rules.

## 8. Main negative results

Rejected:

- binary `authority yes/no -> effect yes/no`;
- person or office as a complete legal-role state;
- authority for one purpose as authority for all related purposes;
- unauthorized act as non-event;
- ultra vires act as necessarily unattributable;
- official status as sufficient for every private act to be State conduct;
- later confirmation as historical rewriting of the original authority state.

## 10. Cross-case significance

LAW-003 supplies a particularly strong external criticism of unjustified predefinition:

**do not infer authority from role, effect from authority alone, or original authority from later effect. Preserve the source rule that creates apparent authority, attribution, ratification, confirmation or another exceptional route.**

This is also a direct example of the project's analysis/design separation: the external legal systems were not redesigned to fit DSD, and the DSD application model was narrowed where the external sources defeated its stronger binary assumptions.
