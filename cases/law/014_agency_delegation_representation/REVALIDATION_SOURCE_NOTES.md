# LAW-003 Cross-Jurisdiction Revalidation Source Notes

Status: source audit complete for first revalidation pass.

## 1. UNIDROIT Principles — Authority of Agents

Official UNIDROIT Chapter 2 Section 2 supplies several independent states.

### Article 2.2.1

The Section distinguishes external authority relations from the underlying internal principal-agent relationship. It also notes that company organs/officers may be governed by special rules.

### Article 2.2.2

Authority may be express or implied and has a scope tied to its purpose.

### Article 2.2.5

General rule:

`agent acts without/exceeds authority -> no principal-third-party effect`.

But paragraph (2) supplies an apparent-authority route where the principal's conduct creates a reasonable belief of authority.

Therefore:

`no actual authority != no possible principal effect under every source rule`.

### Article 2.2.9

An unauthorized or excess-of-authority act may later be ratified. Ratification gives the act the same effects as if initially authorized, subject to the source-defined conditions.

Therefore:

`act existed before ratification`;

`pre-ratification effect status != post-ratification effect status`;

`later ratification != proof that actual authority already existed at act time`.

## 2. Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties 1969

### Article 7

A person's capacity to represent a State for treaty acts depends on full powers, relevant practice/circumstances, or specified functions such as Head of State, Head of Government, Minister for Foreign Affairs, diplomatic mission head for a limited purpose, or accredited representative for a limited purpose.

This supplies strong scope sensitivity:

`person/status != unlimited treaty authority`.

### Article 8

An act by a person not authorized under Article 7 is without legal effect unless afterwards confirmed by the State.

This is an independent international-law witness for:

`performed act != current legal effect`;

`current lack of effect != impossibility of later source-defined confirmation`.

## 3. ILC Articles on State Responsibility

### Article 4

Conduct of a State organ is considered an act of the State under international law when the person/entity has organ status under the relevant rule.

This confirms that attribution depends on a source-defined status/function relation rather than mere physical authorship.

### Article 7

A State organ or entity empowered to exercise governmental authority may have conduct attributed to the State when acting in that capacity even if it exceeds authority or contravenes instructions.

The commentary emphasizes the distinction between unauthorized-but-official conduct and private conduct of a person who happens to be an official.

Therefore:

`authority exceeded != attribution necessarily absent`;

but also:

`person is official != every private act attributable to State`.

The decisive coordinate is source-defined official capacity/attribution rule, not a binary authority flag alone.

## 4. Korean first-pass witness

The original Korean Civil Act/Supreme Court analysis remains valid as a detailed domestic implementation of ordinary agency, apparent agency, mandate/authority separation, unauthorized act, ratification and role-sensitive legal capacity.

It is no longer the basis for generalization.

## 5. Cross-source synthesis

Independent witness families jointly reject a one-bit model:

`authority yes -> effect/attribution yes`

`authority no -> effect/attribution no`.

The source-sensitive structure is instead closer to:

`(actor, role/capacity, actual authority/scope, apparent/official status, act, governing rule, time, regime) -> source-defined effect/attribution`.

No universal scalar or single boolean can preserve the relevant distinctions.
