# LAW-008 Finite Witnesses — Conflict Consequences Are Not One Bit

## Goal

Construct a small witness family showing that a single binary field

`CONFLICT in {YES,NO}`

plus one universal consequence cannot preserve the selected source structures.

## Witness A — apparent difference resolved by harmonization

Two valid rules bear on one issue but can be interpreted together so that compatible obligations remain.

State:

- `Valid(r1)=true`;
- `Valid(r2)=true`;
- both relevant to `tau`;
- initial wording differs;
- source interpretation produces compatible obligations.

Required result:

`different/relevant rules != unresolved conflict`.

ILC harmonization supplies this witness form.

## Witness B — special rule governs but general rule remains

Let `r_g` be general and `r_s` special.

For a typed instance inside the special scope:

`SpecialGoverns(r_s,r_g,tau)=true`.

But the general rule is not thereby globally extinguished and may remain relevant outside the special scope or as interpretive background.

Required distinction:

`special priority != deletion of general law`.

ILC lex-specialis conclusions supply this witness form.

## Witness C — explicit priority without universal invalidity

Let `r_C` be a UN Charter obligation and `r_T` an obligation under another international agreement.

If they conflict for a UN Member, Article 103 says Charter obligations prevail.

Required distinction:

`priority in conflict != source statement that the other treaty never existed`.

This witness is encoded only as the source supplies it: `prevail`.

## Witness D — conflict producing invalidity

Let treaty `T` conflict at conclusion with a peremptory norm under VCLT Article 53.

Required result:

`T is void`.

This differs from Witness C.

Therefore:

`conflict -> one universal priority consequence`

is false for the selected sources.

## Witness E — successive treaties with same parties

Let all parties to earlier treaty `T1` also be parties to later treaty `T2`, with `T1` still in operation.

Under VCLT Article 30(3), `T1` applies only to the extent compatible with `T2`.

Required distinction:

`later treaty relationship can be provision/compatibility-sensitive rather than global deletion of T1`.

## Witness F — successive treaties with different party sets

Let States A and B be parties to both `T1` and `T2`, but State C be party only to `T1`.

Under Article 30(4):

- A/B relations may use the later-treaty compatibility rule;
- A/C relations are governed by the treaty to which both are parties.

Thus the same pair of treaty texts cannot be represented by one context-free ordering

`T2 > T1`

for all legal relationships.

## Compression obstruction

Consider an encoder

`K(r1,r2) in {NO_CONFLICT, R1_WINS, R2_WINS, BOTH_INVALID}`.

This still loses information:

- Witness A needs harmonized co-application;
- Witness B needs special governance with continuing general-law validity;
- Witness C needs source priority without an automatic universal invalidity statement;
- Witness D needs invalidity;
- Witness E needs partial compatibility by provision;
- Witness F needs party-indexed governing relations.

Therefore the selected sources require at least a rule/context-indexed relation package rather than one global conflict output.

## Minimal typed witness table

| Witness | Both source rules/instruments present? | Relation | Loser deleted/void? | Index needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | yes | harmonized | no | issue/context |
| B | yes | special governs | normally no | scope/context |
| C | yes | Charter obligation prevails | not stated as universal voidness by Art.103 | obligation/regime |
| D | treaty + peremptory norm | conflict causes invalidity | treaty void | norm status/time |
| E | yes | later compatibility rule | earlier survives compatible extent | parties/provisions/time |
| F | yes | relation differs by party pair | no single global answer | party relation |

## Conclusion

A legally meaningful conflict model must preserve at least rule identity, applicability, scope, party/regime indices, and the specific source relation that determines the consequence.
