# Source Notes — LING-005

## External sources

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Vagueness
- Vagueness is widely characterized by borderline cases.
- It is distinguished from ambiguity.
- Rival treatments include many-valued approaches, supervaluationism, contextualism, and epistemic theories.
- Supervaluationism treats simple borderline predications as lacking a truth value while allowing compounds to be supertrue/superfalse across admissible precisifications.
- URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/vagueness/

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Sorites Paradox
- Epistemicism retains bivalence and a sharp boundary while treating its location as unknowable or inaccessible to ordinary knowledge.
- URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sorites-paradox/

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Fuzzy Logic
- Fuzzy logics use defined truth degrees, often in `[0,1]`, and are commonly motivated by vague predicates.
- Intermediate degrees are values, not assignment failure.
- URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-fuzzy/

## DSD Formation source
Formation Axiom System §5 distinguishes:

Corollary 5.3: undefined assignment is not a value.
Proposition 5.4: zero-padding erases assignment-domain status.

## Interpretation rule
No external theory of vagueness is identified with DSD status vocabulary by default. Each theory requires an explicit application bridge.

## Key caution
An analyst's inability to classify a borderline case is not enough to infer any one of:
- semantic truth-value gap,
- hidden but determinate Boolean value,
- intermediate truth degree.
The three are externally distinct theories despite similar surface uncertainty.
