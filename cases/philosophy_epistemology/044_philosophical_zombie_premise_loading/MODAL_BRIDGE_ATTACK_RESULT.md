# PHIL-001 Follow-up Result — Modal Bridge and Physical-Descriptor Completeness Attack

Status: **follow-up attack completed; no blanket refutation of the mature Chalmers position**.

## 1. Target actually tested

The first PHIL-001 premise-loading attack failed. This follow-up therefore tested two narrower claims:

1. whether epistemic/ideal conceivability may be promoted to metaphysical possibility without an additional truth-preserving bridge; and
2. whether the physical truth-base `P` used in the zombie argument is guaranteed to be a complete description of the underlying physical structure rather than only a structural/dispositional descriptor.

Narrative naming, biological implementation, shared-neural-network models, signal latency, and transmission mechanisms were excluded because they change the physical base or do not alter the inferential form.

## 2. Source result: the simple modal-space attack is anticipated

Chalmers explicitly states that a counterexample to

`ideal primary positive conceivability -> primary possibility`

would be a **strong necessity**: a case in which a positively conceivable scenario is nevertheless false at every metaphysically possible world considered as actual.

He also explicitly formulates the issue as whether the space of positively conceivable situations can outstrip the space of possible worlds.

Therefore a bare DSD-style claim

`epistemic scenario space may be larger than metaphysical possibility space`

is **not a new refutation** of Chalmers. It restates a possibility that his modal-rationalist defense is designed to reject.

## 3. Finite witness A — separation of epistemic and metaphysical spaces

The reproducibility script constructs the finite spaces

`E = {(0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1)}`

and

`M = {(0,0), (1,1)}`,

where the second space has an additional admissibility constraint `q = p`.

The candidate `(1,0)` belongs to `E` but not to `M`.

This proves only the general logical point:

`scenario-level coherence does not entail modal admissibility without a bridge rule`.

It does **not** establish that psychophysical dependence has the form `q = p`, and therefore does not itself refute Chalmers.

The source comparison strengthens this restraint: Chalmers already labels the relevant counterarchitecture a strong-necessity view and argues that no clear example establishes such a necessity in the phenomenal domain.

## 4. Stronger target — what does `P` actually fix?

The more productive pressure point appears later in Chalmers's own reconstruction.

He notes a loophole when physical concepts have their reference fixed by structural/dispositional roles while referring to underlying categorical properties. In that case, a world can satisfy the **primary** intension of the physical description `P` while failing the **secondary** intension of `P` because the same causal roles are realized by different categorical bases.

The purported zombie world may then be only structurally/dispositionally isomorphic to the actual world rather than a full duplicate in categorical nature.

This is exactly the point at which the zombie argument ceases to straightforwardly establish:

`same complete physical structure + different consciousness`.

It may instead establish only:

`same structural/dispositional physical descriptor + different categorical base and/or consciousness`.

## 5. Finite witness B — reduced physical descriptor is not full structure

The reproducibility script uses three Boolean coordinates:

- `d`: a reduced structural/dispositional descriptor;
- `i`: an intrinsic/categorical coordinate omitted by `d`;
- `q`: a downstream property fixed in the witness by `i`.

The two full states

`(d,i,q) = (1,0,0)`

and

`(1,1,1)`

share the same reduced descriptor `d = 1`, yet they are not equal as full underlying states.

Therefore:

`same reduced physical descriptor != same complete underlying structure`.

This is a finite structural witness for descriptor incompleteness. It is not a model of consciousness and does not prove Russellian monism.

## 6. The completeness–conceivability squeeze

Let:

- `P_T` = theory-based or structural/dispositional physical truths;
- `I` = intrinsic/categorical physical basis, if such a basis exists;
- `P_F = P_T + I` = a fuller object-based physical description;
- `Q` = phenomenal truth.

Then the zombie argument faces a two-horn pressure test.

### Horn A — use `P_T`

If `P` contains only the structural/dispositional truths supplied by physical theory, then the conceivability of

`P_T & ~Q`

may be granted.

But this does not establish the possibility of a **full physical duplicate** without consciousness if an omitted intrinsic/categorical coordinate is part of the physical nature of the actual system.

The scenario may preserve only a reduced descriptor.

### Horn B — use `P_F`

If `P` is strengthened to include every intrinsic/categorical physical fact required for full physical identity, then the burden changes. One must establish that

`P_F & ~Q`

is **ideally primarily positively conceivable**.

But if the intrinsic/categorical basis is not captured by our current physical concepts or is epistemically inaccessible, the original intuitive zombie construction does not by itself certify ideal positive conceivability of this fully specified scenario.

Thus:

- the more easily conceivable structural `P` may be incomplete;
- the more complete `P` is made, the less the original conceivability intuition by itself establishes ideal positive conceivability.

## 7. Relation to existing philosophy

This pressure is not claimed as an independently invented refutation.

It converges with established objections and escape routes:

- Stoljar's distinction between theory-based and object-based conceptions of the physical;
- phenomenal-concept / Type-B physicalist strategies that separate an epistemic gap from an ontological gap;
- Russellian monism, on which physics may characterize structure and dynamics without exhausting the intrinsic categorical nature of matter.

The Stanford Encyclopedia summary explicitly notes that a Russellian physicalist can concede zombies that duplicate us only structurally while denying zombies that are full physical duplicates, if the relevant intrinsic properties are classified as physical.

Chalmers himself acknowledges this loophole and reformulates his conclusion as roughly:

`materialism is false OR panprotopsychism is true`.

Therefore the follow-up attack does not reveal an unnoticed contradiction in the mature source.

## 8. DSD contribution

The DSD Formation Axiom System supplies a legitimate methodological analogy, not an identification of modalities.

Its staged framework separates candidate data, admission, realization, configuration describability, and later channel formation. Realization alone does not imply configuration describability, and a candidate channel is admitted only when additional conditions are satisfied.

The current static aggregation layer supplies a second relevant separation: equality of a reduced aggregate does not reconstruct the support-tagged or complete typed structure.

Together these support the audit rule:

`equality/completeness of a chosen descriptor must not be assumed to be equality/completeness of the underlying structure`.

The DSD contribution is therefore a **descriptor-completeness audit** of the zombie argument, not a new metaphysics of consciousness.

## 9. What survived and what failed

### Failed attack 1

`Conceivable scenario space could exceed metaphysical space, therefore Chalmers is refuted.`

**Rejected.** Chalmers explicitly anticipates this as the strong-necessity issue and defends weak modal rationalism against it.

### Surviving attack 2

`The physical truth-base P must be audited for descriptor completeness before a structural duplicate is promoted to a full physical duplicate.`

**Survives.** Chalmers himself recognizes the categorical-base loophole.

### Surviving attack 3

`If P is strengthened to include unknown intrinsic/categorical physical facts, ideal positive conceivability of P & ~Q requires a new justification.`

**Survives as an under-support challenge.** It does not prove the scenario impossible; it shows that the intuitive structural-zombie construction does not by itself certify the fully specified case.

### Failed overclaim

`DSD has now proved physicalism true or proved all zombies impossible.`

**Rejected.** Neither finite witness establishes a theory of consciousness.

## 10. Final follow-up judgment

**The mature zombie argument is not refuted wholesale, but its force against physicalism simpliciter is substantially narrowed by a descriptor-completeness dilemma.**

The strongest defensible DSD formulation is:

`If the zombie's P is only a structural/dispositional descriptor, equality of P is insufficient for full physical identity. If P is expanded to a complete object-based physical description, the ideal positive conceivability of P & ~Q must be independently re-established.`

This is close to the Russellian/Stoljar family of objections and should be counted as convergence with that external family rather than as a new independent philosophical discovery.

## 11. Next decision point

A further attack would have to choose one of two projects:

1. **P-completeness project:** determine whether a non-question-begging notion of complete physical truth can be fixed while preserving the zombie conceivability premise; or
2. **phenomenal-concept project:** test whether distinct modes of access to one property can explain the epistemic gap without ontological duplication.

Either project should be treated as a new follow-up subcase rather than silently folded into the original premise-loading audit.
