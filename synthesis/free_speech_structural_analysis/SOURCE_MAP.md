# Multidisciplinary Source Map — Freedom, Speech, Listening, and Structural Non-Equivalence

Status: source pool for later publication; **not yet a frozen bibliography**.

Purpose: identify external theories, empirical findings, normative sources, and lexical references that independently preserve distinctions relevant to the DSD structural analysis. No source below is treated as proof of DSD. Before publication, each retained claim must be pinned to edition/DOI/page/section in a claim-source matrix.

## A. Political philosophy and theories of freedom

1. Isaiah Berlin, *Two Concepts of Liberty* — classic negative/positive liberty distinction. Freeze edition/pages before citation.
2. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Positive and Negative Liberty” — current overview of negative/positive freedom and related triadic analyses. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberty-positive-negative/
3. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Republicanism” — republican freedom as non-domination; absence of actual interference is not sufficient when one remains exposed to arbitrary/uncontrolled power. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/
4. Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970) — exit and voice as distinct response modes. Freeze edition before publication.
5. Mark E. Warren, “Voting with Your Feet: Exit-based Empowerment in Democratic Theory,” *American Political Science Review* 105(4), 2011. DOI: 10.1017/S0003055411000323.

DSD use: keep interference, dependence, exit, voice, and domination structurally distinct. Do not choose one philosophical family as the DSD definition of freedom.

## Source-audit rules

1. Philosophical theories are not empirical effect evidence.
2. A single empirical study is not promoted to a universal rule; reviews/meta-analyses are preferred where available.
3. Official legal/human-rights sources are external normative bridges, not DSD theorems.
4. Comparative cases remain jurisdiction-specific unless a separate comparative-law argument is supplied.
5. Dictionary definitions do not represent a whole nation's political philosophy or psychology.
6. Translation equivalence is not conceptual equivalence.
7. `freedom`, `liberty`, `Freiheit`, `liberté`, `libertad`, `自由`, and `자유` are not reduced to national essences.
8. Publication manuscript must use a claim-source matrix with claim, source, source type, independence, DOI/URL, edition, page/section, and verification date.
9. DSD novelty, if any, must be claimed only at the level of integrated structural decomposition/audit after prior-art comparison, not at the level of distinctions already known in external fields.
