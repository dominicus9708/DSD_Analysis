# BENCH-C01 Result — Toxin Puzzle Prospective DSD Audit

Status: **post-seal literature comparison completed**.

Validation mode: **Mode C prospective prediction — partial blind integrity**.

Overall judgment: **DSD successfully localized the main structural interface of the Toxin Puzzle, but did not produce a new solution. The strongest DSD stage-separation claim was already substantially present in Kavka and later intention literature. DSD also missed an important external refinement: the distinction between normative reasons and motivating/explanatory reasons, plus the Ted counterexample to simple reason-based impossibility principles.**

## 1. Prediction integrity

The DSD prediction was committed before dedicated objection/reply literature was searched.

Blindness is downgraded from `clean` to `partial` because one secondary lecture snippet accidentally exposed, before the seal, the idea that the puzzle is connected to a reasons-for-action constraint on intention.

No named Toxin-Puzzle solution family was examined before the seal.

## 2. Prediction-by-prediction comparison

### Prediction 1 — formation, persistence, and execution are distinct stages

Sealed prediction:

`Formation(I_0) != Persistence(I_1) != Execution(A_2)`.

### External comparison

This is structurally correct, but not new.

Kavka already explicitly distinguishes reasons for **intending** from reasons for **acting**, and his setup explicitly allows later change of mind after payment. Later literature makes the temporal decomposition even more explicit:

- Gauthier separates deliberation about forming the intention from subsequent execution;
- Bratman explicitly treats **stability of intention** in toxin/temptation cases;
- Mele distinguishes intention formation, persistence, and resulting intentional action.

### Classification

**C2 — established family, DSD gives a clean explicit stage decomposition but does not discover a new fault line.**

### Falsification impact

Pre-seal falsification criterion 1 is **partially triggered**. Kavka did not simply collapse intention and action. Therefore `stage separation alone solves the puzzle` is rejected.

The DSD contribution at this point is organizational/formal, not a refutation.

---

### Prediction 2 — anticipated future preference/reason change requires an explicit bridge to present intention formation

Sealed prediction:

`expects no later reason to act -/-> cannot form present intention`

unless an intention-formation bridge is supplied.

### External comparison

This prediction hits the central literature directly.

Kavka supplies one such bridge: intentions are constrained by reasons for action. Later authors dispute or refine the bridge.

Rudy-Hiller gives a more specific candidate bridge based on **motivating/explanatory reasons** rather than merely normative reasons: an ordinary agent cannot now form a deliberation-based intention when she believes there will be no future motivating reason capable of explaining the intentional action.

This confirms the DSD prediction that the inferential burden lies in an explicit relation between present intention formation and anticipated future action conditions.

### Classification

**C1 — established external match.**

The DSD prediction correctly identified the bridge location before the dedicated literature search.

---

### Prediction 3 — later rational revision does not retroactively falsify an earlier formed intention

Sealed prediction:

`Revision(I_1) -/-> not Formation(I_0)`.

### External comparison

This is broadly compatible with mainstream intention theory: intentions can be revocable, and later reconsideration need not mean the earlier intention never existed.

Bratman’s work on planning and stability explicitly studies when prior intentions should persist and when reconsideration is rational.

However, this point does **not** by itself solve Kavka’s problem, because Kavka’s central claim is that the relevant intention may fail to form in the first place once the agent correctly anticipates the later reason structure.

### Classification

**C2 — valid temporal sharpening, but secondary to the core puzzle.**

It blocks a retroactive-state conflation but does not answer the constitutive question of whether `I_0` can form.

---

### Prediction 4 — the decisive issue is what makes the intention assignment well-defined

Sealed prediction:

The puzzle depends on the semantic/functional signature of `intention`, not merely on physical ability to drink.

### External comparison

This is strongly confirmed.

Kavka says the puzzle concerns the nature of intentions. Mele explicitly frames subsequent work around the nature of intention and rational intention. The literature asks which reasons can ground intentions, what relation intention bears to intentional action, whether confidence in execution matters, and how plans remain stable over time.

### Classification

**C1/C2 — direct external match with DSD-style formal sharpening.**

DSD’s `property signature + formation condition + transition rule` vocabulary is its own formal organization, but the philosophical issue itself is established.

## 3. Major DSD miss — C3

The pre-seal DSD analysis correctly predicted that a bridge would be required but did not predict an important distinction the literature uses to refine that bridge.

### Normative reasons vs motivating/explanatory reasons

The DSD prediction spoke generically of `reason`, `future preference`, and `rational revision`.

Rudy-Hiller argues that a simple principle based on future **normative** reasons is too strong. Mele’s `Ted` case shows that an agent can sometimes form and sustain the intention despite lacking a future normative reason to drink.

Rudy-Hiller therefore shifts to **motivating/explanatory reasons** as the relevant constitutive constraint for ordinary deliberation-based intentions.

DSD did not predict this distinction in advance.

### Ted counterexample

Mele’s Ted is an extraordinary agent whose intention, once formed, predictably persists and issues in intentional drinking under unusual conditions. This undermines a simple universal bridge of the form:

`no future normative reason to A -> cannot now intend to A`.

The pre-seal DSD prediction did not construct or anticipate such an extraordinary-agent counterexample.

### Classification

**C3 — meaningful external distinction/counterexample missed by the sealed DSD prediction.**

This miss must be retained in the validation record.

## 4. What DSD got right

The prospective analysis located the controversy at the correct structural interface:

`present intention formation`

vs

`future reasons and motivational structure`

vs

`persistence/reconsideration`

vs

`eventual execution`.

This is not a superficial analogy. The post-seal literature is in fact organized around these relations.

The strongest successful prospective claim is therefore:

> DSD identified, before dedicated reply search, that the decisive work must be done by an explicit diachronic bridge connecting intention formation to expected later agency conditions.

## 5. What DSD did not establish

DSD did **not** establish that:

- Kavka’s puzzle is invalid;
- the agent can in fact form the intention;
- later revision alone resolves the problem;
- the puzzle is merely a confusion of stages;
- the DSD formulation is historically novel.

Kavka already distinguished reasons for intending from reasons for acting, and later work gives sophisticated theories of stability, rational commitment, normative reasons, motivating reasons, and unusual agents.

## 6. DSD-specific interpretation

The useful DSD transfer is limited and methodological.

A domain-specific notion such as `Intention(agent, action, t)` is not supplied by the Formation Axiom System. A target interpretation must specify its bearer, application domain, formation criterion, and any temporal coherence/transition law.

The relevant DSD rule is:

`a later state transition does not silently rewrite an earlier state, and a future-action condition must not be inserted into present property formation unless the interpretation explicitly requires it`.

The Structural Reorganization Dynamics paper provides the closest internal analogy because it distinguishes time-indexed admissible slices and requires stronger status/support/formation changes to be explicitly declared.

This remains an analogy/analysis rule, not a DSD theory of intention.

## 8. Overall BENCH-C01 judgment

**Mixed-positive prospective validation.**

DSD’s describability/stage discipline succeeded at **problem localization**: it predicted where an explicit bridge must exist and correctly separated formation, persistence, revision, and execution.

It did not outperform the mature external literature and did not generate a novel solution. The literature also exposed a real omission in the sealed prediction.

Because blind integrity is only partial, BENCH-C01 should be treated as a **prospective pilot**, not the project’s first fully clean C-mode benchmark.

A fully clean BENCH-C02 is still required.
