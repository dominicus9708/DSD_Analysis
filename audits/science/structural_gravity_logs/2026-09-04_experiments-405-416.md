## Experiment 405 — Coupled threshold occurs on a finite background

If the field-axis Schur-complement instability occurs at

\[
\epsilon_c^{\rm coupled}<\epsilon_c^{(0)},
\]

then the uncoupled progression field is still on its finite positive normalized branch at the coupled crossing. Therefore a local nonlinear reduction around the coupled zero mode is better posed than an expansion directly at the pure self-field endpoint, where the normalization coefficient diverges.

---

## Experiment 406 — Generic reduced amplitude equation

Let \(z\) denote the amplitude of the coupled zero mode and let

\[
\delta:=\epsilon_c^{\rm coupled}-\epsilon.
\]

After eliminating stable transverse modes, the local reduced potential has the generic form

\[
V_{\rm eff}(z;\delta)
=
\frac12 a\delta z^2
+\frac13 b z^3
+\frac14 c z^4
+\cdots,
\qquad a>0,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
m_{\rm eff}\ddot z
+a\delta z
+bz^2
+cz^3
+\cdots=0.}
\]

No exact \(z\mapsto-z\) symmetry has been established for the coupled progression/axis system, so \(b\) need not vanish.

### Consequence

Linear instability does not uniquely determine nonlinear collapse. Depending on \(b,c\), admissible-domain constraints, and higher terms, the post-crossing state may approach a nearby branch, saturate, undergo another structural transition, or continue toward a collapse-like regime.

## Experiment 407 — Local nonzero branch when the quadratic-force term is present

If \(b\neq0\), stationary nonzero solutions near the coupled crossing satisfy

\[
a\delta+bz+cz^2=0.
\]

To leading order,

\[
\boxed{z_*\simeq-\frac{a\delta}{b}.}
\]

Thus a nearby finite-amplitude branch is locally allowed. The existence of this branch shows that exponential growth immediately after the linear crossing is not, by itself, a proof of runaway.

No sign or global stability statement is claimed without the actual nonlinear constitutive coefficients.

If an additional symmetry forces \(b=0\), then

For \(c>0\), a supercritical saturated branch is possible; for \(c<0\), quartic order destabilizes and higher terms are required. Thus the nonlinear fate remains a constitutive question.

## Experiment 409 — Exact admissible range of radial axis anisotropy

For

\[
\mathcal A
=a(r)\left(n\otimes n-\frac13I\right)
\]

and

\[
Q=\frac13I+\mathcal A,
\qquad Q\succeq0,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}Q=1,
\]

the radial and tangential eigenvalues of \(Q\) are

\[
q_r=\frac{1+2a}{3},
\qquad
q_t=\frac{1-a}{3}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{-\frac12\le a\le1.}
\]

The field-driven radial-alignment branch uses \(a\ge0\), hence

\[
\boxed{0\le a\le1.}
\]

This is a geometric saturation bound inherited from the axis second-moment interpretation, not an arbitrary nonlinear cutoff.

## Experiment 410 — Nonperturbative lower bound on the critical threshold

For the volume-preserving axis metric

\[
h_A=e^{\beta_A\mathcal A},
\]

the radial inverse metric coefficient is

\[
h_A^{rr}=e^{-2\beta_Aa/3}.
\]

For a spherical matter profile the critical s-wave Rayleigh quotient is

\[
\lambda_1[a]
=
\min_u
\frac{\int_0^1 e^{-2\beta_Aa(s)/3}s^2u'(s)^2ds}
{\int_0^1 d(s)s^2u(s)^2ds}.
\]

For \(\beta_A>0\) and \(0\le a\le1\),

\[
e^{-2\beta_A/3}\le e^{-2\beta_Aa/3}\le1.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
e^{-2\beta_A/3}\lambda_1[0]
\le
\lambda_1[a]
\le
\lambda_1[0]
}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
e^{-2\beta_A/3}\epsilon_c^{(0)}
\le
\epsilon_c[a]
\le
\epsilon_c^{(0)}.}
\]

This is nonperturbative within the radial axis-metric branch.

## Experiment 411 — Necessary condition for axis-only threshold crossing

A fixed matter source with compactness \(\epsilon\) cannot be driven across the spectral boundary by any admissible radial axis alignment if

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon<e^{-2\beta_A/3}\epsilon_c^{(0)}.
}
\]

Equivalently, axis-only crossing requires at least

\[
\boxed{
\beta_A
\ge
\frac32\ln\left(\frac{\epsilon_c^{(0)}}{\epsilon}\right)
}
\]

as a necessary geometric-coupling condition, before restoration/stiffness restrictions are even imposed.

For the uniform-sphere baseline \(\epsilon_c^{(0)}=\pi^2/6\), a source only \(10^{-3}\) below the uncoupled threshold requires at least \(\beta_A\approx9.12\times10^{-4}\) from this absolute admissibility bound. The actual required value is generally larger because the axis field need not achieve \(a=1\) throughout the source.

## Experiment 412 — Restoration and stiffness tighten the geometric bound

The admissible bound \(a\le1\) is only a kinematic maximum. A quasistatic axis response satisfies schematically

\[
L_Aa
=\beta_A F_U,
\]

subject to the admissible interval. Thus an accessible amplitude estimate is

\[
a_{\max}^{\rm dyn}
\le
\min\left(1,\,\|\beta_A L_A^{-1}F_U\|_\infty\right).
\]

Replacing \(1\) by the actually reachable \(a_{\max}^{\rm dyn}\) gives the tighter necessary bound

\[
\epsilon_c[a]
\ge
\exp\left(-\frac{2\beta_Aa_{\max}^{\rm dyn}}3\right)
\epsilon_c^{(0)}.
\]

Therefore axis restoration and stiffness suppress not only the perturbative susceptibility but also the maximum nonperturbative threshold reduction.

## Experiment 413 — Axis inertia affects timing, not the static admissible floor

The coefficient \(\mu_A\) does not enter the static Rayleigh quotient or the geometric admissibility bound. It sets the characteristic reorganization time through

\[
c_A^2=\mathcal T_A/\mu_A
\]

and the normal-mode growth/oscillation rates. Hence a source can satisfy the static crossing condition but fail to cross on a given finite observation interval if axis reorganization is too slow.

## Experiment 414 — Transient overshoot witness

In a one-mode conservative axis response with a suddenly applied approximately constant field drive,

\[
m_A\ddot y+k_Ay=f_0,
\qquad y(0)=\dot y(0)=0,
\]

one has

\[
y(t)=y_{\rm eq}(1-\cos\omega_At),
\qquad
y_{\rm eq}=f_0/k_A.
\]

Thus the undamped transient reaches \(2y_{\rm eq}\). Conditioned on the admissible axis bound, a transient threshold crossing can therefore occur even when the final static axis displacement would remain subcritical.

This is a synthetic witness only. Relaxation/damping changes the overshoot and must be supplied by the chosen dynamic bridge.

## Experiment 415 — Static boundary, dynamic crossing, and nonlinear fate remain distinct

The current hierarchy is

\[
\boxed{
\text{admissible axis geometry}
\to

## Experiment 416 — Consolidated result

Two complementary constraints now govern axis-driven collapse-like criticality:

1. **Soft-mode feedback:**
\[
L_{U,\mathrm{eff}}=L_U-\beta_A^2CL_A^{-1}C^*
\]
shows that a stable relaxable axis sector lowers the progression-field stability margin.

2. **Geometric saturation:**
\[
Q\succeq0,\quad \operatorname{tr}Q=1
\]
forces \(0\le a\le1\) for radial alignment and therefore
\[
\epsilon_c[a]\ge e^{-2\beta_A/3}\epsilon_c^{(0)}.
\]

Hence field-axis feedback can trigger an earlier instability, but it cannot lower the critical compactness without bound. Sources sufficiently far below the pure-field threshold are protected from axis-only crossing within this branch.
