## Experiment 34 — Density-shape dependence of bounded-transition critical slope

Represent a spherical bounded source as

\[
\rho(r;L,M,\zeta)=\frac{M}{L^3}f\!\left(\frac rL;\zeta\right),
\]

with

\[
4\pi\int_0^1 f(u;\zeta)u^2du=1.
\]

The dimensionless variable \(\zeta\) denotes density-shape data such as normalized radial moments.

Let a bounded-transition critical surface be

\[
\Phi(L,M,\zeta)=0,
\]

and suppose it can locally be solved as

\[
M=M_c(L,\zeta).
\]

For fixed shape define

\[
\mu_c(L,\zeta):=\partial_LM_c(L,\zeta).
\]

A source-independent asymptotic critical slope requires

\[
\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}
\left|\partial_LM_c(L,\zeta)-\mu_*\right|
\to0.
\]

A useful synthetic classification is

\[
M_c(L,\zeta)=\mu_*L+a(\zeta)L^p+\cdots.
\]

- \(p<1\): \(pa(\zeta)L^{p-1}\to0\); density shape can shift finite-size thresholds while preserving the asymptotic slope.
- \(p=1\): density shape changes the slope itself; source-independent universality fails unless the coefficient is shape-independent.
- \(p>1\): the correction dominates the linear term; no linear asymptotic critical curve remains.

If the physical transition sequence also changes shape, \(\zeta=\zeta(L)\), then

\[
\frac{dM_c}{dL}
=
\partial_LM_c
+
\nabla_\zeta M_c\cdot\frac{d\zeta}{dL}.
\]

Thus fixed-shape universality is not sufficient for universality along a physical path. One additionally needs

\[
\nabla_\zeta M_c\cdot\frac{d\zeta}{dL}\to0.
\]

This can occur if the density shape approaches a structural fixed point or if critical-surface sensitivity to shape vanishes asymptotically.

### Verdict

- density changes only threshold location while preserving asymptotic slope: **CONDITIONALLY POSSIBLE**
- density must always change the slope: **REJECTED AS NECESSITY**
- fixed-shape slope universality alone guarantees physical-path universality: **REJECTED**

## Experiment 35 — Critical-density exponent and linear mass–length transition

For a 3D spherical source with characteristic length \(L\), define

\[
\bar\rho_c(L,\zeta)
=
\frac{3M_c(L,\zeta)}{4\pi L^3}.
\]

If

\[
M_c(L,\zeta)\sim\mu_*L,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\bar\rho_c(L,\zeta)
\sim
\frac{3\mu_*}{4\pi}L^{-2}
}.
\]

More generally, if

\[
\bar\rho_c(L,\zeta)
\sim A(\zeta)L^{-q},
\]

then

\[
M_c(L,\zeta)
\sim
\frac{4\pi}{3}A(\zeta)L^{3-q}.
\]

Therefore a linear critical relation \(M_c\propto L\) in 3D requires

\[
\boxed{q=2}.
\]

However,

\[
M_c\propto L
\Longleftrightarrow
\bar\rho_c\propto L^{-2}
\]

is only an algebraic equivalence. Assuming the \(L^{-2}\) law in order to recover a constant \(M/L\) simply relocates the missing normalization unless that density scaling is independently derived.

If the amplitude \(A(\zeta)\) depends on density shape, then the asymptotic slope is shape-dependent unless \(A\) becomes shape-independent or the physical shape flow satisfies

\[
A(\zeta(L))\to A_*.
\]

### Verdict

- 3D linear \(M_c(L)\) and \(L^{-2}\) mean critical-density scaling: **CONFIRMED AS EQUIVALENT**
- \(L^{-2}\) critical-density scaling is independently derived by current DSD: **NOT DERIVED**
- density-shape amplitude can vary arbitrarily while preserving a universal slope: **REJECTED**

## Experiment 36 — Mass-dimension deficit no-go for absolute normalization

Assume the structural-gravity specialization has, as source-independent dimensional input, only

- dimensionless or typed formation/property/describability data;
- \(c_{\rm info}\) with dimension \(L/T\);

and otherwise uses source-dependent \(M,L,\rho\) and functions derived from them.
Assume no independent source-independent quantity carrying mass dimension has been supplied.

Then a source-independent quantity

\[
\mu_*\qquad[\mu_*]=M/L
\]

cannot be constructed by dimensional closure alone.

Reason: the dimensional algebra generated by \(c_{\rm info}\) and dimensionless data contains no mass dimension. If mass dimension is taken from the source \(M\), \(\rho\), or another source-derived quantity, the result generally changes under source rescaling and is not universal without an additional theorem or invariant.

Hence an actual derivation of a universal critical slope requires at least one independently selected mass-bearing dimensional sector.

Examples of admissible dimensional forms, if their inputs were independently derived, include

\[
\mu_*=\frac{\mathcal T_*}{c_{\rm info}^2}
\]

for a universal structural tension \(\mathcal T_*\), or

\[
\mu_*=\rho_*\ell_*^2
\]

for an independently selected density scale and intrinsic length scale.

These examples are dimensional escape routes, not current DSD derivations.

The current no-go boundary is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{Formation + Property + Static Aggregation + finite }c_{\rm info}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{universal numerical }M/L\text{ normalization}
}
\]

without an additional constitutive/dynamic dimensional invariant.

### Verdict

- critical-curve reinterpretation clarifies the source-specific \(L/M\) problem: **CONFIRMED**
- the critical curve alone creates the missing absolute normalization: **REJECTED**
- an independently selected mass-bearing constitutive/dynamic invariant is required under the stated assumptions: **CURRENT NECESSITY CANDIDATE**
