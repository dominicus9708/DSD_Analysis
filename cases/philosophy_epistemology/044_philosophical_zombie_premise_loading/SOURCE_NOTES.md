# PHIL-001 / Global Case 044 — Source Notes

## Primary / authoritative sources used

### David J. Chalmers, *Does Conceivability Entail Possibility?* (2002)
Author-hosted HTML: https://consc.net/papers/conceivability.html

This is the main source for the mature modal bridge rather than relying on the bare three-line zombie slogan.

Relevant source structure:

- Chalmers explicitly describes the general three-step form: epistemic claim -> modal claim -> metaphysical claim.
- He distinguishes prima facie vs. ideal, positive vs. negative, and primary vs. secondary conceivability.
- He warns that defining conceivability directly in terms of possibility would trivialize the bridge.
- His central defensible bridge is: ideal primary positive conceivability -> primary possibility.
- Applied to zombies, with `P` the physical truths and `Q` a phenomenal truth, he reconstructs the argument as:
  1. `P & ~Q` is ideally primarily positively conceivable;
  2. if so, `P & ~Q` is primarily possible;
  3. if so, materialism is false.
- He then states that the argument is unsound *as it stands* because the route from primary possibility to the secondary/modal requirement relevant to materialism is not immediate, and develops a more elaborate two-dimensional rescue with further assumptions and a Russellian-monist loophole.

### David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind* author page (1996)
https://consc.net/the-conscious-mind/

The author describes the book as arguing that reductive explanation of consciousness is impossible and that strict materialism must be exceeded. This establishes the original argumentative context but the full zombie chapter is not reproduced on the author page.

### David Chalmers, “Zombies on the Web”
https://consc.net/zombies-on-the-web/

Chalmers defines the philosophical zombie as physically/behaviorally identical to a normal conscious human while lacking conscious experience. He also explicitly calls the logical possibility of zombies controversial and says zombies are a vivid and provocative illustration rather than the only possible presentation of the anti-materialist argument.

This source is also important for scope cleanup: Chalmers himself says most arguments using zombies can be reformulated in zombie-free terms. Therefore the word `zombie` and its popular-culture biological associations are not legitimate primary inferential targets.

## Secondary authoritative synthesis

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Zombies”
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/

Used to cross-check the historical and argumentative structure.

SEP reconstructs the simple argument:

1. zombies are conceivable;
2. whatever is conceivable is possible;
3. therefore zombies are possible.

SEP emphasizes that the argument is valid in form but both premises are controversial once clarified. It records multiple physicalist responses, including necessary-a-posteriori identity approaches, phenomenal-concept strategies, and causal-essentialist objections to the transition from conceivability to metaphysical possibility.

SEP also stresses that a genuine anti-physicalist zombie must be an exact physical duplicate, not merely a behavioral or functional duplicate. This is why shared-neural-network, remote-control, parasite, hive-mind, latency, or altered-signal models change the physical base and are not direct countermodels to the zombie premise.

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Two-Dimensional Semantics” supplement: “The 2D Argument Against Materialism”
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/two-dimensional-semantics/supplement.html

This gives a useful explicit reconstruction of Chalmers's more developed route:

1. `Q` is true;
2. `(P & ~Q)` is a priori coherent;
3. a priori coherence yields an epistemically possible scenario;
4. every relevant epistemically possible scenario corresponds to a metaphysically possible centered world;
5. the relevant epistemic and subjunctive intensions are sufficiently aligned/stable;
6. therefore `(P & ~Q)` is metaphysically possible;
7. if so, materialism is false.

For follow-up attack design, the main pressure points are therefore premise 2 (a priori/ideal coherence), premise 4 (modal rationalism), premise 5 (semantic stability, though Chalmers has fallback machinery), and the general step from epistemic separation to ontological separation.

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Functionalism”
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/functionalism/

Used to locate the phenomenal-concept / Type-B materialist family of responses. These approaches can concede an epistemic or conceptual gap while denying that the gap establishes a distinct metaphysically possible physical duplicate without consciousness.

This is directly relevant to the next DSD audit target: descriptive or epistemic nonrecoverability need not automatically entail underlying ontological variation.

## Post-audit scope cleanup

The following points are now recorded as out-of-scope for a serious refutation of the mature argument:

- the historical naming choice `zombie`;
- biological undead/infection/parasite taxonomies;
- shared-neural-network or hive-mind mechanisms that add causal structure;
- hormonal/electrical transmission details, latency, bandwidth, synchronization, or why such a network exists;
- the blanket charge that Chalmers merely defines his conclusion into the scenario.

These may be useful for intuition or terminology criticism, but they do not engage the modal inference at the required level.

## Source-discipline consequence

The full Chalmers argument must not be reduced to:

`define zombie -> zombie possible -> physicalism false`.

The developed argument contains independent epistemic and modal bridge theses. A premise-loading audit must therefore test those theses rather than accuse the definition itself of proving possibility.

The strongest follow-up target is now:

`epistemic/descriptive separation -> metaphysical/ontological separation`.

A serious DSD contribution would need to show, without presupposing physicalism, that non-derivability or distinct modes of description can occur while the underlying structure remains fixed; then Chalmers must independently justify why the phenomenal case escapes that general counter-architecture.
