# Source notes — LING-003 / Global Case 016

## External sources

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Implicature (2024 revision)
- Implicature is distinct from what is said.
- Conversational implicatures depend on conversational context rather than being fixed by the conventional meaning of the uttered sentence.
- Generalized conversational implicatures such as `some` -> `not all` are characteristically cancelable.
- Example pattern: `Some athletes smoke` can be followed by `Indeed, all do` without contradiction, showing that `not all` is not part of the asserted semantic content.

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Pragmatics / Paul Grice
- Gricean conversational implicatures are inferred from how something is said and from rational/cooperative assumptions.
- Typical conversational implicatures are cancelable and are not equivalent to semantic entailments.

## DSD source
Formation Axiom System:
- Primitive Axiom V uses partial assignment rather than total zero-filled assignment.
- Corollary 5.3: undefined assignment is not a value and cannot be inferred to equal zero.
- Proposition 5.4: zero-padding is not assignment-faithful.
- Stage VI channel formation retains role and other conditions rather than inferring a channel from a mere candidate.

## Mapping caution
Do not identify semantic assertion, pragmatic implicature, and DSD channel/value states literally.
The comparison is structural: a later/context-sensitive inferred status should not be silently written back as an earlier-stage literal value without the bridge that licenses it.
