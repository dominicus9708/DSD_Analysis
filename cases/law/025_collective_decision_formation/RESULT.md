# LAW-014 Result — Collective Decision Formation and Institutional Attribution

Status: first-pass cross-jurisdiction analysis complete.

Primary purpose: reinterpretation + falsification + predefinition audit.

## 1. Answer-first result

Collective and institutional decisions are not faithfully represented by the equation:

`collective decision = simple sum of individual positions`.

The selected sources independently show quorum conditions, issue-dependent thresholds, abstention rules, weighted/double-majority calculations, blocking/veto relations, multi-body concurrence, presentment and override paths.

The strongest surviving candidate is:

**where a source system distinguishes member eligibility/participation, individual vote or position, abstention/nonparticipation, quorum, issue category, threshold or weighting rule, blocking/veto relation, body-level result, inter-body concurrence, presentment/confirmation, and final institutional act, preserve those states and source-defined operators separately. Do not infer the collective act from raw preference counts or infer every member's personal endorsement from the institutional act.**

Compactly:

`individual position != collective decision != final institutional act`.

## 2. UN General Assembly — input identity and denominator are separate

UN Charter Article 18 gives each member one vote, but important questions require two-thirds of members present and voting while other questions require a majority of members present and voting.

General Assembly Rule 86 defines members present and voting as those casting affirmative or negative votes; abstaining members are not counted as voting for this threshold.

Therefore:

`abstention != NO vote`;

and:

`present member != member included in the present-and-voting denominator`.

A collective result requires the source-defined decision rule in addition to individual votes.

## 3. UN Security Council — equal votes can have role-sensitive institutional effects

Article 27 gives each Security Council member one vote, but non-procedural decisions have additional permanent-member conditions. Official Security Council guidance explains that a permanent-member negative vote acts as a veto, whereas a permanent-member abstention can allow adoption if the required affirmative votes are obtained.

Thus:

`one member = one vote`

does not imply

`every vote has the same institutional relation under every decision rule`.

And:

`permanent-member abstention != permanent-member negative vote`.

## 4. EU Council — collective output is not raw YES count

Qualified majority ordinarily requires both:

- at least 55% of member states in favour;
- those states representing at least 65% of the EU population.

Blocking-minority and special-participation rules add further conditions.

Under qualified majority, abstention counts against attainment/blocking calculations, but the Council explicitly distinguishes abstention from nonparticipation. Under unanimity, abstention does not prevent adoption.

Therefore the same broad input identity can receive different downstream effects under different source rules without changing its identity.

`abstention + QMV rule -> one counting effect`;

`abstention + unanimity rule -> different collective effect`.

This is a direct cross-case witness for the project's predefinition rule.

## 5. US Constitution — quorum, body passage and final law are different states

Article I Section 5 makes a majority of each House a quorum to do business. Quorum is therefore a capacity-to-act condition, not itself passage of a bill.

Article I Section 7 requires passage by both House and Senate and then presentment to the President. Approval, veto, reconsideration/override and specified non-return conditions provide source-defined routes to final legal status.

Thus:

`quorum != bill approval`;

`House passage != federal law`;

`House + Senate passage != final law without the source-defined presentment path`.

## 6. Institutional attribution does not rewrite member positions

All selected systems can generate institutional decisions despite dissent, abstention or other non-affirmative positions when their governing rules are satisfied.

Therefore:

`institutional decision != unanimous personal endorsement`.

The institution's act is attributed under the constitutive decision rule. That attribution does not transform dissenting or abstaining members into supporters.

## 7. Predefinition significance

Do not predefine:

`individual YES -> collective adoption`;

`largest subgroup -> institutional result`;

`abstention -> NO identity`;

`abstention -> nonparticipation`;

`quorum -> approval`;

`one body adopts -> final institutional act`;

`institutional adoption -> every member endorsed`;

`numerical voting language -> one universal scalar aggregation rule`.

The source rule forms the collective status.

## 8. DSD comparison

Formation can preserve typed member inputs, participation/quorum states, body-level results and final institutional-act records.

But Formation composition is not the source voting rule by identity.

Static Aggregation is not automatically the correct layer merely because votes are counted or weighted. The EU qualified-majority rule is a multi-condition Boolean threshold system; the US legislative process includes bicameral concurrence and presentment; Security Council voting includes role-sensitive veto effects.

Thus:

`source collective-choice operator != DSD Static Aggregation operator by identity`.

Axis-Property is not required.

Dynamics may represent multi-stage temporal progression but does not generate quorum, vote-counting, veto, presentment or override rules.

No direct DSD axiom contradiction was found after the source operators were preserved externally.

## 11. Boundary statement

LAW-014 does not provide a universal voting theory or social-choice function. It does not claim that every collective institution uses the same coordinates or procedure. It establishes only that collective acts are source-rule-generated statuses whose relation to individual inputs, participation, thresholds and later institutional stages must be preserved rather than inferred from raw counts alone.
