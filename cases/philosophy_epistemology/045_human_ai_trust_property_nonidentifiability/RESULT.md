# PHIL-002 Result — Human/AI Room Trust Attribution Trilemma

Status: **new DSD-constructed rebuttal executed; historical novelty not established**.

Validation status: **new active PHIL-002 case replacing the retired Chinese Room convergence case**.

Judgment: **the rebuttal is valid as a DSD-specific formal synthesis/sharpening of established distinctions between trust, trustworthiness, trusting behavior, anthropomorphic attribution, and AI applicability. The exact DSD trilemma and undefined/zero observer-relative formulation were not located in the dedicated search, but the core ingredients have substantial prior art; therefore no unqualified novelty claim is made.**

## 1. Setup

Let:

- `H` = a human room;
- `A` = an AI room;
- `E` = an external observer;
- `O_E(x)` = the externally admitted interaction descriptor of room `x` under regime `E`;
- `T_E(x)` = the status/value assigned by the selected trust-property interpretation to bearer `x` under regime `E`.

The rooms are externally opaque. The observer can supply the same histories and receive the same promises, cooperative choices, defections, explanations, risk-taking decisions, and other admitted outputs.

Assume:

`O_E(H) = O_E(A)`.

The target question is not whether the outputs are trustworthy. The question is whether the observer is entitled to assign an internal/attitudinal property called `trust` to either bearer solely from those outputs and substrate labels.

## 2. Core non-implications

Without an additional property-identification bridge:

`O_E(H) = O_E(A)`

does not imply

`T_E(H) = T_E(A)`.

It also does not imply the asymmetric pair

`T_E(H)=1 and T_E(A)=0`.

More generally, equality of the observable descriptor does not establish equality of internal mechanism:

`O_E(H)=O_E(A) -/-> M(H)=M(A)`.

The same outward cooperation may arise from trust, sanction avoidance, reputation maximization, expected-utility optimization, fixed policy, learned policy, or other internal structures.

## 3. DSD Trust Attribution Trilemma

Any attempt to infer `trust` from the Human/AI Room must choose or justify one of three structurally different routes.

### Horn I — Behavioral constitutivism

Define trust by the admitted behavioral descriptor itself:

`T_E(x) := B(O_E(x))`.

Then

`O_E(H)=O_E(A) -> T_E(H)=T_E(A)`

by construction.

This route is logically valid, but the conclusion is **definitional/operational**. It does not establish that the rooms share one deeper psychological, phenomenal, motivational, or implementation-level state unless the target theory explicitly identifies trust with that behaviorally defined construct.

Thus behavioral equality can justify equal **behavioral trust** while leaving stronger hidden-state claims untouched.

### Horn II — Substrate- or type-gated attribution

Suppose a theory declares the human bearer eligible for a trust property but excludes the AI bearer because trust requires a human-specific, conscious, normative, affective, biological, or other typed prerequisite.

Then the asymmetry comes from the **property signature/domain rule**, not from the equal observed behavior.

Moreover, DSD status discipline forbids replacing exclusion from the property domain with a numerical zero.

If `A` is not an admissible bearer of the property, the proper status may be `inapplicable` or `unavailable input`, not

`T_E(A)=0`.

Likewise, mere eligibility of `H` does not establish

`T_E(H)=1`;

a defined assignment is still required.

Therefore:

`human/AI type difference` may justify different **applicability statuses** under a supplied theory, but it does not by itself justify the value pair `(1,0)`.

### Horn III — Non-constitutive property without an identification bridge

If trust is not defined purely by behavior and neither substrate/type rules nor a validated measurement map identify its value, then the admitted observation may leave the assignment unresolved.

In that case the correct state is not forced falsity:

`T_E(x) = undefined`

rather than

`T_E(x) = 0`.

This is the central DSD-specific status claim.

## 4. The rebuttal

The Human/AI Room rebuts any argument that attempts either of the following solely from externally equivalent behavior plus bearer labels:

### Anthropomorphic promotion

`humanlike/trust-compatible AI behavior -> AI has the same internal trust property as a human`.

This requires behavioral constitutivism or another identification bridge.

### Anti-anthropomorphic demotion

`AI substrate -> trust is absent/zero despite the same admitted behavior`.

This requires an explicit type/applicability theory, and even then `inapplicable`, `unavailable`, `undefined`, and `defined zero` must remain distinct.

The symmetric conclusion is:

> Equal externally admitted trust-compatible behavior does not license equal, unequal, positive, or zero-valued hidden-property assignment unless the property-identification rule is independently supplied.

## 5. Finite non-identifiability witness

The reproducibility script fixes one observation vector and four candidate full states:

1. human + defined trust value `1`;
2. human + defined trust value `0` generated by sanction avoidance;
3. AI + trust assignment `undefined` under the selected interpretation;
4. AI + a defined trust-like internal property value `1` under another admissible interpretation.

All four candidates have the same external observation vector.

Therefore the observation map is many-to-one and does not identify a unique internal mechanism or trust record.

This witness does **not** claim that these are the true psychological states of real humans or AI systems. It proves only the logical non-identifiability of the hidden property record from the chosen output descriptor.

## 6. Relation to DSD sources

### Formation Axiom System

The Formation system explicitly distinguishes undefined assignment from defined zero and requires domain-specific interpretation maps for external applications. It also distinguishes reduced/composite equality from strict full-structure equivalence.

Methodological transfer:

`observable/reduced equality != full typed-structure equality`.

### Axis-Property System

A property name alone has no mathematical content. A property requires a typed profile, value carrier, partial assignment/domain, optional zero structure, and any additional semantic coherence rule.

The application statuses include unavailable input, undefined, defined zero, and defined nonzero/value.

Methodological transfer:

`bearer label + property name != property assignment`.

### Static Aggregation

Aggregate equality reconstructs the selected records only under an injectivity condition. Without it, equal downstream output need not identify support-tagged or complete property structure.

Methodological transfer:

`same output -> same hidden record` requires a reconstruction/identification condition.

No DSD primitive is identified with psychological trust, human mentality, machine mentality, or consciousness.

## 7. Dedicated precedent search after the prediction seal

The dedicated search found strong prior art for the **ingredients** of the argument.

### Trust vs trusting behavior

Trust research has long distinguished cognitive/attitudinal trust from trusting behavior. Work going back at least to Pearce (1973), later trust-construct literature, and modern behavioral research explicitly warns that cooperative or vulnerability-taking behavior is not automatically identical with a cognitive trust state.

### AI trust, trustworthiness, and behavior

Recent AI-trust reviews explicitly distinguish `trust`, `trustworthiness`, and `trusting behaviour`, and describe trust in AI as inferred rather than directly observed.

### AI anthropomorphism and mental-state attribution

AI/HCI literature distinguishes enacted social behavior from psychological anthropomorphism and studies how behavioral cues change mental-state and trust attributions without establishing that the attributed inner states actually exist.

### Can artificial agents genuinely trust?

Philosophy of AI contains both permissive operational/reliance accounts and restrictive accounts requiring intentionality, normativity, affect, first-person perspective, or other capacities. Some sources state explicitly that if trust is defined as mere reliance/expectation it may apply to artificial agents, whereas stronger accounts can classify such attribution as a category mistake.

### Behavioral AI trust experiments

Research that measures an AI agent's 'trust' in humans often uses careful wording such as **behavior consistent with trust**, precisely because observed game choices need not settle the stronger philosophical property question.

## 8. Novelty classification

The following are **not new**:

- trust vs trusting behavior;
- trust vs trustworthiness;
- behavior vs internal cognition;
- anthropomorphic mental-state attribution to AI;
- the debate over whether artificial agents are eligible bearers of genuine trust;
- behavioral tests that operationalize trust without settling ontology.

The dedicated search did not locate, in the retrieved materials, the exact combined DSD formulation:

1. human and AI are placed under one externally identical behavioral descriptor;
2. both positive anthropomorphic attribution and negative AI-exception attribution are audited symmetrically;
3. the result is organized as a three-horn choice among behavioral constitution, substrate/type gating, and unresolved assignment;
4. `inapplicable/unavailable/undefined/defined-zero` are separated explicitly;
5. the same-output inference is tied to an injectivity/reconstruction condition.

That absence is insufficient for a priority claim.

Conservative classification:

**DSD-specific new constructed rebuttal and formal synthesis/sharpening of established neighboring ideas; historical novelty unproven.**

## 9. What the argument establishes

### Established

1. Equal external trust-compatible behavior does not logically identify a unique internal mechanism.
2. Equal behavior does not by itself identify a unique trust-property status/value unless the property is behaviorally constitutive or another identification bridge is supplied.
3. Human/AI substrate asymmetry cannot be converted automatically into `trust=1` versus `trust=0`.
4. If a theory excludes AI from the trust-property domain, the status is not automatically defined zero.
5. If the property remains unidentifiable under the admitted regime, `undefined` is a legitimate outcome rather than forced falsity.

### Not established

1. That humans genuinely possess a particular metaphysical trust state.
2. That AI systems can or cannot genuinely trust.
3. That consciousness is required or not required for trust.
4. That behavioral definitions of trust are wrong.
5. That substrate-gated theories of trust are wrong.
6. That this trilemma is historically unprecedented.

## 10. Final judgment

**PHIL-002 replacement survives as a genuinely new DSD case construction and rebuttal format, but not yet as a proven historically novel philosophical objection.**

Its main contribution is to force any human/AI trust attribution to disclose which bridge is doing the work:

- behavior defines the property;
- bearer type restricts the property domain;
- or the property remains unresolved under the current descriptive regime.

The prohibited shortcut is:

`same observable trust-like behavior + human/AI labels -> hidden trust values assigned by intuition`.

The DSD rule is:

`fix the property signature, bearer domain, observation regime, and identification map before assigning the value; preserve undefined/inapplicable states instead of totalizing them to zero.`
