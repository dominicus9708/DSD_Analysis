# LAW-010 Contradiction Audit

Status: completed first-pass audit.

## 1. Audit target

Check whether the source structures on punishment, reparation, victim status, public opinion, historical state abuse, death-penalty law/practice and victim-family heterogeneity directly contradict the DSD Formation/Dynamics distinctions once type, role, regime and time are matched.

## 2. Source-side distinctions preserved

### Responsibility versus sanction

A responsibility finding does not by itself specify one sanction. Sentencing/disposition remains source-rule governed.

### Sanction versus reparation

ICC Article 75 reparations and Article 77 penalties are separate legal routes.

No contradiction appears if DSD encodes them as distinct role-bearing application channels rather than one `justice` value.

### Reparation versus compensation

UN remedial sources distinguish restitution, compensation, rehabilitation, satisfaction and guarantees of non-repetition.

No contradiction appears if each is encoded separately.

### Victim status versus conviction

UN Committee against Torture material recognizes victim status regardless of whether the perpetrator is identified, apprehended, prosecuted or convicted.

Therefore an application that made `victim` a downstream value only after conviction would be source-inaccurate. This is an application error, not a contradiction in Formation.

### Public opinion versus legal judgment

`S v Makwanyane` treats public opinion as potentially relevant but not a substitute for constitutional adjudication.

No contradiction appears if public attitude is encoded as contextual/social input rather than legal decision rule.

### Historical abuse versus current public pressure

German materials preserve Nazi-era capital-punishment abuse as a decisive constitutional-historical context while documenting postwar reintroduction arguments.

No contradiction appears if historical context and current political sentiment are separately typed.

### Law versus practice

Formal retention, moratorium/non-use, de facto abolition and legal abolition are not one state.

No contradiction appears if legal authorization and execution practice are separately indexed over time.

### Victim-family heterogeneity

Bereaved families need not share one punishment preference.

No contradiction appears if emotional/evaluative states are actor-indexed.

## 3. DSD misuse checks

### Formation misuse

Reject:

`criminal conviction = Formation realization`;

`punishment = DSD channel value`;

`victim compensation = defined zero/nonzero` as a semantic identity;

`de facto abolition = channel absence`.

Formation supplies typing/stage discipline, not legal content.

### Axis-Property misuse

Not required.

Words such as severity, hierarchy, social pressure or state power do not justify realized-axis semantics.

### Static Aggregation misuse

Reject any direct use of Static Aggregation to compute:

`grief + public anger + life + deterrence + wrongful-execution risk + legitimacy + fiscal cost -> optimal punishment`.

No justified common codomain or application-supplied aggregation operator has been established.

### Dynamics misuse

Dynamics may carry temporal transitions, but does not derive:

- victim healing;
- offender rehabilitation;
- deterrence;
- moratorium;
- abolition;
- reintroduction.

Those transitions require source/empirical rules.

## 4. Direct contradiction test

After source-side distinctions are preserved:

- no source requires undefined and defined zero to be identical;
- no source requires absence of a legal channel to equal non-use of an existing legal power;
- no source requires punishment and reparation to be one role/value;
- no source requires all victim/public attitudes to collapse to one observer value;
- no source requires social-cost dimensions to share one scalar codomain;
- no source requires legal status and practical use to be identical over time.

Result: **no direct contradiction with Formation was found**.

For Dynamics, no direct contradiction was found under the boundary that application-specific temporal transition and restoration semantics remain external.

## 5. Counterpressure on the universal candidate

The strong claim

`responsibility, punishment, reparation, rehabilitation and prevention are always doctrinally separate in every legal system`

is too strong and is rejected.

A legal system may combine functions in one order, sentence, tribunal, restorative process or institutional package.

The surviving claim is source-sensitive:

**if the source gives different actors, recipients, conditions, procedures, temporal states or effects to responsibility, sanction, reparation, rehabilitation, prevention, emotion/social response or institutional cost, those differences must not be collapsed without the source rule that identifies or aggregates them.**

## 6. Social-input boundary

Emotion and social cost are neither automatically irrelevant nor automatically decisive.

Reject both:

`emotion -> irrational -> ignore`

and

`emotion -> legal outcome`.

Likewise reject:

`historical abuse -> abolition necessarily`

and

`public anger -> retention necessarily`.

The legal/political decision function is source-institutional and may accept, filter, override or exclude such inputs.

## 7. Audit judgment

- Strong universal doctrinal-separation theorem: **rejected as too strong**.
- Source-fidelity non-totalization candidate: **survives**.
- Punishment/reparation non-identity: **strongly witnessed**.
- Victim-status/conviction non-identity: **strongly witnessed**.
- Emotion/public-opinion as relevant-but-not-decisive input: **strongly witnessed in selected systems**.
- Historical state-abuse context as legally relevant input: **strongly witnessed in Germany, not universalized causally**.
- Law/practice distinction for death penalty: **strongly witnessed comparatively**.
- Direct contradiction with Formation: **none found**.
- Direct contradiction with Dynamics under explicit source bridge: **none found**.
