# LAW-003 Result — Agency, Mandate, and Representation

Status: first-pass analysis complete.

Primary purpose: reinterpretation.

Secondary purposes: coherence, predefinition audit, role/regime boundary analysis.

## 1. Answer-first result

No direct contradiction was found between the selected Korean agency/representation structure and the DSD Formation distinctions after the compared objects, roles, legal regimes, and effect statuses were typed separately.

The strongest result is **not** that DSD derives representation law. It is that the source law independently requires several separations that DSD can preserve without contradiction:

- same human performer vs legally operative capacity;
- underlying mandate relation vs agency authority;
- actual authority/scope vs attributed legal effect;
- existence of an unauthorized attempted act vs effect against the principal;
- ordinary agency route vs apparent-agency exception route;
- pre-ratification vs post-ratification legal status;
- organ representation vs appointment of a separate agent for a specific act.

## 2. Source-law result

### 2.1 Ordinary agency is conditional

Civil Act Article 114 ties direct effect on the principal to an agent acting within authority and making the relevant principal-directed manifestation.

Therefore:

`human performed act`

is not identical to

`act has direct effect for principal`.

### 2.2 Authority defect does not have one universal output

Civil Act Articles 126, 129, 130, and 133 prevent a one-bit model such as:

`authority present -> effect`

`authority absent -> no effect forever`.

The source law contains multiple routes:

- ordinary authorized agency;
- apparent agency after excess of authority under specified reliance conditions;
- protection after termination of authority under specified conditions;
- unauthorized agency lacking effect against the principal absent ratification;
- later ratification with the statutory retroactivity rule.

Thus `authority status` and `effect/attribution status` must remain separate.

### 2.3 Mandate and authority are not one source-side coordinate

Civil Act Article 680 defines mandate as entrustment and acceptance of handling affairs. Supreme Court 2023Da288772 separately treats the underlying principal-agent legal relation, including mandate, and whether agency authority was lawfully granted and what its scope/termination are.

Accordingly, this case records:

`Mandate != AgencyAuthority`

as a source-side non-collapse rule unless a concrete source rule establishes the additional link.

### 2.4 Personal capacity and representative capacity are not interchangeable

Civil Act Articles 59-62 distinguish juridical-person representation by a director from appointment of another person as agent for a specific act.

Supreme Court 2008Da11276 provides a sharper boundary: an individual who had been representative director and the company were not the same procedural party for ordinary party-name correction. The decision also rejects automatic expansion from authority to conclude a contract into all later disposition/termination authority.

The external law therefore independently supports:

`same natural person != same legal party/capacity/action status`.

## 3. DSD comparison

### 3.1 Formation role identity is genuinely useful

The Formation operational channel retains role `rho` as part of channel identity.

For this case, a faithful application bridge can map role-sensitive legal act tokens to channels whose `rho` distinguishes, for example:

- self-capacity;
- agent-capacity;
- director-representative capacity.

This does not require treating the underlying humans as different persons.

Judgment: **direct structural correspondence after explicit application encoding**.

### 3.2 The material carrier must be typed correctly

A naive mapping that uses the human person alone as the Formation material item and then tries to assign different legal-effect values solely because that person acts under different roles can collide artificially with Primitive Axiom V's regime-global partial-assignment discipline.

The source law does not assign contradictory legal effects to `the human as such`. It assigns effects to distinct acts under distinct capacities, contexts, scopes, and authority relations.

The corrected mapping therefore uses a typed legal act-instance as the application material item and preserves the common human as provenance/annotation.

This is one of the main analytical findings of LAW-003.

### 3.3 Unauthorized act must not be encoded as channel absence

Civil Act Articles 130-134 regulate an unauthorized attempted contract as an existing legal event even before principal effect is established.

Therefore the DSD comparison must preserve:

`described attempted act`

separately from

`effect against principal`.

Encoding the act itself as absent would lose source-law information.

Judgment: **strong structural correspondence to DSD's non-collapse discipline**, but not identity of legal validity with Formation admission.

### 3.4 Apparent agency is not a contradiction

At first glance, Article 126 can look hostile to a stage model because principal responsibility can arise even though actual authority was exceeded.

The apparent conflict disappears once the legal rule is typed correctly:

- actual authority/scope is one source status;
- protected-reliance/apparent-agency requirements are another rule layer;
- principal responsibility/effect is a downstream legal conclusion.

DSD does not state that only ordinary actual authority may ever support an externally supplied legal effect. It requires the application not to silently identify distinct statuses.

Judgment: **apparent conflict resolved by rule/regime separation**.

### 3.5 Ratification is a layer boundary

Formation is static. Article 133 gives a legal rule by which later ratification changes the legal status and, subject to its conditions, gives retroactive effect.

Formation can represent fixed pre- and post-ratification descriptors, but it does not derive the transition or legal retroactivity.

Judgment: **complementary different layer**. A temporal model would require an additional dynamic or application transition relation.

## 4. Axis-Property audit

The roadmap initially treated tag-sensitive Axis-Property structure as a plausible tool for this domain.

After source-first analysis, it is **not required for LAW-003's main result**.

Reason:

- the Formation channel already retains role identity;
- the legal sources require authority/attribution relations but do not independently provide a realized-axis interpretation;
- forcing principal-agent relations into the realized-axis property system merely because they are role-sensitive would add an unsupported semantic bridge.

Therefore the Axis-Property layer is classified here as:

**no meaningful direct mapping required without additional realized-axis semantics**.

This negative mapping is retained rather than inflating the application.

## 5. Direct contradiction audit

### Candidate C1 — same human must be one DSD object, but law gives different effects

Rejected as a direct contradiction.

The legal effects attach to different typed acts/capacities, not to the natural person in isolation. Preserving the human as shared provenance while using distinct act-instances and roles is faithful to the source.

### Candidate C2 — no actual authority but principal can still be responsible

Rejected as a direct contradiction.

Apparent agency is an explicit external exception/protection rule. DSD does not require `actual authority defect` to be numerically or structurally identical to `no downstream effect under every possible external rule`.

### Candidate C3 — ratification retroactively changes effect

Not a contradiction with the static Formation system.

It is outside the static layer unless the application supplies cross-time transition/lineage structure.

### Candidate C4 — mandate and agency authority might be the same

Rejected by the selected source structure as a required identity. The source materials support treating the mandate-like underlying relationship and lawful agency authority as distinct legal questions.

### Candidate C5 — one authority grant should cover all related acts

Rejected by the source law and Supreme Court 2008Da11276. Authority is scope-sensitive and need not totalize into later rescission, termination, or other disposition authority.

This is compatible with DSD's typed/partial discipline.

## 6. Relation to LAW-001 and LAW-002

LAW-001 found that nonparticipation, invalid input, and downstream result must not be collapsed.

LAW-002 found that one legal regime's descriptive gap cannot be silently filled with another regime's desired positive result.

LAW-003 adds an independent institutional node:

**one actor's existence or one underlying relationship does not automatically generate every role-specific authority or downstream legal effect.**

The recurring pattern is now:

`underlying entity/relation`

`!= role/capacity`

`!= authority/applicability`

`!= legally operative act`

`!= attributed downstream result`.

This is a new source-domain instance rather than a reanalysis of the same meeting or criminal-trial facts.

## 7. Predefinition relevance

LAW-003 strengthens the project's predefinition audit in a more nuanced way than a simple `missing value must stay missing` rule.

The source law shows both directions:

1. one must not predefine `mandate -> all agency authority` or `authority for act x -> authority for every related act`;
2. one must also not predefine `authority defect -> no possible principal responsibility`, because explicit apparent-agency or ratification rules can create another legal route.

Therefore the defensible rule is:

**do not promote a source status into another status without the rule that performs that promotion.**

That rule may be ordinary authority, apparent agency, ratification, registration/protection rules, or another source-law mechanism.

## 8. Final classification

- Source discipline preserved: **yes**.
- Formation mapping meaningful: **yes, with explicit typed application bridge**.
- Formation direct contradiction found: **no**.
- Axis-Property required: **no**.
- Axis-Property direct contradiction found: **not reached; no direct realized-axis mapping asserted**.
- Apparent conflicts: **resolved by role/type/rule separation**.
- Main result class: **external coherence node + structural reinterpretation**.
- Secondary result: **predefinition/non-totalization support**.

## 9. Boundary statement

LAW-003 does not prove that Korean agency law follows from DSD, that DSD supplies legal authority, that a DSD channel is a legal act, or that Formation admission equals legal validity.

It shows only that, under an explicit typed bridge, the selected source-law distinctions can be preserved jointly with the relevant Formation distinctions without a direct contradiction, while several tempting naive mappings are rejected.
