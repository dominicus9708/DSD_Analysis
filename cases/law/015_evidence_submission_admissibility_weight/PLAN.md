# LAW-004 / Global Case 015 — Evidence Submission, Admissibility, Probative Weight, and Finding

Status: active analysis.

## 1. Purpose

Test whether legal evidence structures require the following states to remain distinct:

- information/evidence exists in the world;
- a party offers or submits it;
- the court decides whether it may be used or investigated under the applicable procedural regime;
- an admitted/usable item receives some probative weight;
- the court reaches a factual finding under the governing standard.

The case does not assume one universal evidence pipeline. Criminal and civil procedure are compared as distinct legal regimes.

## 2. Primary question

After source-law types and procedural regimes are aligned, can DSD Formation preserve the source-side separation of existence, submission, admissibility/use, weight, and finding without direct contradiction?

## 3. Source-first scope

Primary Korean sources:

- Criminal Procedure Act: Articles 294, 307, 308, 308-2, 310-2 and related evidentiary provisions.
- Civil Procedure Act: Articles 202, 288-292.
- Supreme Court 2026. 4. 30. judgment 2024Da222212 on illegally collected evidence in civil litigation.

The 2026 civil judgment is used to test regime sensitivity: a collection defect does not produce one universal admissibility result across criminal and civil procedure.

## 4. DSD scope

Primary layer: Formation Axiom System.

Candidate comparison targets:

- staged formation and domain restriction;
- partial assignment rather than silent totalization;
- admitted operational channel versus absent channel;
- defined zero versus absence/undefinedness;
- strict structure versus downstream composite coincidence.

Static Aggregation is audited only as a possible downstream analogy for evidential weight. It is not assumed that legal probative weight equals DSD analytic weight.

Axis-Property and Dynamics are excluded unless a source-side requirement forces their use.

## 5. Stress cases

1. Evidence exists but is never offered.
2. Evidence is offered but the court does not investigate/use it because the applicable rule excludes or declines it.
3. Evidence is legally usable but carries weak probative force and does not establish the asserted fact.
4. Two items with similar collection defects receive different admissibility treatment because different procedural/statutory regimes apply.
5. A factual conclusion cannot be reconstructed merely from the existence or number of submitted items.

## 6. Direct-contradiction criterion

A direct contradiction candidate exists only if, after matching the same evidence item, procedural regime, legal stage, and comparison status, source law requires two states to be identical that DSD necessarily separates, or DSD requires an identity that prevents faithful representation of a source-required distinction.
