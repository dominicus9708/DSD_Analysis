# LAW-005 Source Notes

Status: source lock complete for first-pass analysis.

## 1. Source policy

This case uses transnational and international instruments designed to operate across legal traditions. They are not treated as proof of universality. They are witness families against which the universal structural candidate is tested.

## 2. CISG / UNCITRAL witness

### Source
United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (CISG), official UNCITRAL materials.

### Claims used

- The CISG separately regulates formation of the contract in Part II.
- Article 4 states that, except as otherwise expressly provided, the Convention is not concerned with the validity of the contract or any of its provisions.
- The official UNCITRAL overview likewise separates formation from validity and other effects.

### Structural use

The treaty itself therefore refuses the identity:

`contract formation = contract validity`.

This is especially useful because the separation is jurisdiction-neutral inside the treaty's own architecture.

### Sources

- https://uncitral.un.org/en/texts/salegoods/conventions/sale_of_goods/cisg
- UNCITRAL Digest, Article 4 discussion.

## 3. UNIDROIT Principles witness

### 3.1 Conclusion and binding character

Article 1.3 states that a contract validly entered into is binding. The official commentary explains that binding force presupposes both that an agreement has been concluded and that it is not affected by a ground of invalidity.

Article 3.1.2 provides that a contract is concluded, modified, or terminated by mere agreement unless another rule applies.

Structural use:

`agreement/conclusion` and `binding validity` are distinguishable even where a particular rule makes them coincide in the ordinary case.

### 3.2 Avoidance

Chapter 3 Section 2 provides grounds on which a party may avoid an already concluded contract, including mistake, fraud, threat, and gross disparity.

Article 3.2.9 provides that confirmation may exclude avoidance. Article 3.2.11 regulates exercise of avoidance by notice. Article 3.2.12 regulates timing.

Structural use:

`contract concluded` does not imply `incapable of later avoidance`.

The right of avoidance is itself a separate status and can be lost.

### 3.3 Mandatory-rule infringement

Article 3.3.1 states that when a contract infringes an applicable mandatory rule, the effects are first those expressly prescribed by that rule. If the rule does not prescribe the effects, the Principles use a reasonableness analysis and allow different possible consequences.

Structural use:

`rule infringement -> void` is not a universal total function even within this harmonized instrument.

### 3.4 Conditions

Articles 5.3.1 and 5.3.2 distinguish a contract or obligation whose effect depends on a future uncertain event. A suspensive condition can leave the relevant contract or obligation without operative effect until the condition occurs.

Structural use:

`formed legal relation` and `operative effect now` may be separated by an explicit condition rule.

## 4. UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration witness

### Source
UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration (1985), with amendments adopted in 2006.

### Claims used

- Article 35 provides that an arbitral award shall be recognized as binding and enforced upon application subject to Articles 35 and 36.
- Article 36 separately provides grounds on which recognition or enforcement may be refused.
- Article 36 includes cases where an award has not yet become binding, or has been set aside or suspended.
- The official explanatory note distinguishes recognition from enforcement.

### Structural use

The following are not identical source states:

`award rendered`

`!= binding status`

`!= recognition`

`!= enforcement`

`!= refusal of recognition/enforcement`

`!= set aside or suspended`.

A later refusal or setting-aside-related status does not mean that no award event ever occurred.

### Sources

- https://uncitral.un.org/en/texts/arbitration/modellaw/commercial_arbitration
- official text / explanatory note for Articles 35-36.

## 5. Source-side terminology guard

Do not universalize the English labels `void`, `voidable`, `invalid`, `annulled`, `avoided`, `set aside`, `unenforceable`, or `ineffective` as synonyms.

The universal candidate is about preservation of distinct source statuses and rule-conditioned transitions, not about imposing one vocabulary on all legal systems.

## 6. Source-side first conclusion

Across the selected witness families, a one-bit `valid/invalid` representation loses legally relevant structure.

The strongest source-side rule is:

**a defect does not determine its legal consequence without the rule that assigns that consequence.**

This remains a candidate generalization, not a proved universal law.
