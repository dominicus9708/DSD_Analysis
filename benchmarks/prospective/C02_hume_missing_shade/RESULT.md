## 2. Prediction-by-prediction comparison

### Prediction 1 — no direct corresponding impression does not mean no formation-supporting structure

Sealed prediction:

`I* absent`

does not imply

`no relevant experiential/structural input`.

The subject has neighboring shades plus an ordered gradation relation.

### External comparison

This is directly matched by the established literature.

- SEP's detailed Hume discussion explicitly proposes **mental mixing** of neighboring simple shade ideas.
- It states that the missing shade lacks a direct antecedent impression while remaining dependent on impressions through the neighboring ideas.
- Fogelin's treatment models colors as a resemblance space in which gaps can be identified and filled.
- Garrett's solution family is summarized as relying on natural resemblance among simple ideas.

### Classification

**C1 — direct established external match.**

The DSD prediction located the same formation route before unblinding.

---

### Prediction 2 — gap localization is not automatically exact qualitative realization

Sealed prediction:

`G -> exact Q`

requires an identity/fidelity bridge.

The subject may know where a shade belongs without that fact alone establishing possession of the exact qualitative idea.

### External comparison

The literature contains closely related distinctions.

- Cummins' influential route interprets possession of the idea in terms of **recognitional capacity**, which is weaker/different from simply assuming an exact phenomenal duplicate of the missing impression.
- Empirical work by Finch treats the ability to recover missing shades as something testable rather than logically guaranteed, and observed substantial performance differences.
- The broader literature repeatedly distinguishes the ability to locate/fill a gap from the interpretation of what kind of idea has thereby been acquired.

### Classification

**C1/C2 — established family with DSD-specific fidelity/identification sharpening.**

The exact DSD phrasing in terms of an identity map is not historical novelty; it formalizes a real pre-existing distinction.

### Prediction 3 — the ordered arrangement supplies substantive relational input

Sealed prediction:

The descending series and salient gap contribute information; therefore

`Q arises without I*`

must not be promoted to

`Q arises without relevant experiential/structural support`.

### External comparison

This is strongly confirmed.

- Fogelin's color-space account depends precisely on degrees of resemblance among shades.
- SEP's mental-mixing account depends on neighboring shade ideas.
- Garrett's family is based on natural resemblance among simple ideas.
- Current SEP discussion emphasizes the unusual role of **gradation**, helping explain why the example does not generalize freely.

### Classification

**C1 — direct external match.**

### Prediction 4 — simple content need not mean non-derived formation history

Sealed prediction:

`simple content`

does not automatically imply

`non-derived formation history`.

### External comparison

This is the closest and strongest convergence in the case.

SEP explicitly describes the missing shade as mentally produced from neighboring simple ideas while the result remains a **simple idea**. Fogelin likewise emphasizes that color ideas can stand in graded resemblance relations without thereby ceasing to be simple.

Kelahan's later interpretation goes further by arguing that Humean simple ideas should be treated as theoretical posits, again showing that the status of 'simple' cannot simply be identified with a naive non-derived phenomenal atom.

### Classification

**C1/C2 — near-exact historical convergence plus DSD formation-history sharpening.**

---

### Prediction 5 — DSD should not simply invalidate Hume's example

Sealed prediction:

If exact qualitative formation is granted, the narrow conclusion that a direct corresponding impression is not universally necessary can survive.

### External comparison

This is also supported.

Hume himself treats the example as a singular exception. Later scholarship, including SEP and Earp, contains approaches that allow the counterexample while maintaining the Copy Principle as an empirical/general methodological principle whose larger use need not collapse because of one restricted gradational exception.

### Classification

**C1 — source/literature match.**

## 3. Major DSD miss — C3

### Resemblance thesis versus causal thesis

The sealed DSD prediction discussed direct-copy origin, relational support, exact qualitative identity, and the meaning of simplicity. It did **not** explicitly predict a decomposition of Hume's first principle into distinct theses about:

- resemblance/correspondence; and
- causal derivation/origin.

Masato Narita's analysis explicitly distinguishes a resemblance thesis from a causal thesis and argues that the Missing Shade targets the latter but not the former.

This matters because it changes the exact logical object being counterexampled.

### Classification

**C3 — meaningful external refinement missed by the sealed prediction.**

The miss does not overturn the main DSD localization, but it shows that the pre-seal representation of 'copy principle' was too coarse.

## 4. Secondary unpredicted refinements

Two additional literature routes were not predicted in their exact form:

1. **Theoretical-posits reading** — Kelahan argues that Humean simple ideas are best treated as theoretical posits, which changes the dialectical status of the case.
2. **Empirical-performance question** — Finch tests actual shade reconstruction and finds heterogeneous performance, showing that the assumed capacity itself can be investigated empirically.

These are retained as refinements rather than separate independent C3 nodes because they overlap the sealed simplicity/identity and exact-idea criteria.

## 6. DSD-specific formal organization

The useful DSD organization is:

- `I*` — missing direct corresponding impression;
- `N-, N+` — neighboring experienced shade inputs;
- `R` — ordering/resemblance relation;
- `G` — gap localization state;
- `Q` — candidate exact qualitative idea.

The key non-identities are:

`I* absent != all relevant support absent`;

`G != exact Q` unless an identification criterion is supplied;

`simple(Q) != nonderived(Q)` unless the target theory defines simplicity that way.

This is a formal organization of an established philosophical problem, not a DSD theory of color perception.
