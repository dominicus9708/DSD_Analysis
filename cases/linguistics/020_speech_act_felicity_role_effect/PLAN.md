# LING-007 / Global Case 020 — Speech Acts, Felicity, Role, and Effect

## Primary purpose
Structural reinterpretation.

## Secondary purposes
- coherence with Formation role-preserving channel identity;
- test whether Axis-Property is actually required for role/authority-sensitive speech acts;
- predefinition audit for utterance -> force -> valid act -> downstream effect;
- preserve source-theory distinctions between misfire, abuse, and perlocutionary consequence.

## External target
Austinian and post-Austinian speech-act theory, with emphasis on:

1. locutionary / illocutionary / perlocutionary distinctions;
2. felicity conditions for performatives;
3. appropriate authority, procedure, time, place, and circumstances;
4. misfire versus abuse;
5. institutional declarations versus ordinary acts such as promising.

## DSD questions

1. Does formation of an utterance or meaningful locution imply successful formation of the intended illocutionary act?
2. Does a speaker-role label itself imply authority to perform an institutional act?
3. Does a successful illocution imply a desired perlocutionary consequence?
4. Is Axis-Property necessary, optional, or irrelevant for the core distinction?
5. If authority is modeled as an axis-property record, what application-supplied profile/carrier/assignment is required?
6. Does later institutional effect or audience response retroactively prove that the earlier felicity conditions were present?

## Minimal witness plan
Use the same surface utterance in one meeting regime:

- authorized chair says `I declare the meeting open`;
- unauthorized visitor says the same words;
- both produce a locution;
- only the authorized case satisfies the supplied institutional felicity bridge;
- successful declaration is separated from a contingent audience reaction.

Add a second micro-witness:

- an insincere promise may still count as a promise while being an Austinian abuse rather than a misfire.

## Expected DSD boundary
Formation can preserve configuration, value, and role in channel identity, but `role = chair` does not itself derive an authority relation or a speech-act felicity rule. Axis-Property may optionally encode tag-sensitive relational authority after explicit declaration, but its property names have no semantics without a supplied profile, carrier, and partial assignment. Speech-act theory remains external.
