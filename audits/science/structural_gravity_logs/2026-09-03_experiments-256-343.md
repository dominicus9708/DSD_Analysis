## Experiments 256–259 — Location of the universal progression coefficient

### Result

The remaining universal coefficient is best represented as a property of a source-independent progression/background sector rather than as a property attached separately to each source channel.

\[
X_{\rm aux}(\text{prog-bg})=\{b_\psi\},

with

\[
[\mu_0]=M/L.

### Verdict

- universal \(\mu_0\) as progression/background property: **CONDITIONAL / STRUCTURALLY CLEAN**
- per-source-channel summation of \(\mu_0\): **REJECTED**
- numerical value of \(\mu_0\) derived from current DSD: **UNRESOLVED**

### Consequence

The absolute normalization problem is separated from source composition and from axis geometry.

## Experiments 260–263 — Axis second moment and causal principal stiffness

Define the orientation second moment

\[
Q^{ij}=\sum_a w_a n_a^i n_a^j,
\qquad \sum_a w_a=1.
\]

For isotropic three-axis data,

\[
Q^{ij}=\frac13 h^{ij}.
\]

A simple family of principal stiffness maps is

\[
A_\varepsilon^{ij}
=(1-\varepsilon)h^{ij}+3\varepsilon Q^{ij}.
\]

Every \(\varepsilon\) gives the same isotropic value \(A_\varepsilon=h\), so isotropic recovery does not determine the dimensionless axis-response strength.

Moreover, \(\operatorname{tr}A_\varepsilon=3\). If an anisotropic state has unequal eigenvalues while preserving this mean, at least one eigenvalue exceeds one. If the reference speed \(c_*\) is interpreted as a fixed outer causal cone for every state, a trace-preserving anisotropic principal stiffness therefore generically produces a direction with a super-reference characteristic speed.

A causal normalization such as

\[
A(Q)=\frac{Q}{\lambda_{\max}(Q)}
\]

avoids this by slowing other directions, but is not uniquely selected: multiple maps satisfy locality/covariance/causal-bound requirements.

### Verdict

- axis geometry can enter principal stiffness: **CONDITIONAL**
- isotropic recovery fixes coupling strength: **REJECTED**
- causal bound uniquely fixes axis-to-stiffness map: **REJECTED**

## Experiments 264–278 — Zero-monopole axis source and action realization

Define the traceless axis-anisotropy field

\[
\mathcal A^{ij}(x)=W(x)\left(Q^{ij}(x)-\frac13h^{ij}\right).
\]

In a flat/asymptotically flat weak specialization, the candidate equation

\[
\boxed{
\nabla^2\psi
=\frac{\rho}{\mu_0}
+\gamma_A\partial_i\partial_j\mathcal A^{ij}
}
\]

has the correct dimension because \(\mathcal A\) is dimensionless and the double divergence has dimension \(L^{-2}\).

For compact support and sufficient boundary decay,

\[
\int \partial_i\partial_j\mathcal A^{ij}\,d^3x=0,
\]

and the dipole also vanishes. Hence the axis term does not alter the mass monopole; its first exterior contribution is quadrupolar or higher:

\[
\delta\psi=O(r^{-3}),
\qquad
\delta a=O(r^{-4}).
\]

Under locality, linearity in \(\mathcal A\), rotational covariance, scalar output, absence of a new length scale, and zero monopole/dipole, the leading scalar operator is strongly restricted to the form

\[
\partial_i\partial_j\mathcal A^{ij},
\]

but an arbitrary dimensionless multiplier \(\gamma_A\) remains.

The same source form can be generated from the interaction term

\[
\mathcal L_A
=-\mu_0c_*^2\gamma_A\mathcal A^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j\psi
\]

or, up to a boundary term,

\[
\mathcal L_A\simeq
\mu_0c_*^2\gamma_A
(\partial_i\mathcal A^{ij})(\partial_j\psi).
\]

If \(\mathcal A\) is treated as external/source structural data, this coupling changes the source side without changing the principal kinetic/stiffness operator of \(\psi\).

### Verdict

- zero-monopole axis structural source: **CONDITIONAL / SURVIVES**
- leading operator form strongly constrained by symmetry: **CONFIRMED WITHIN THE STATED SPECIALIZATION**
- coefficient \(\gamma_A\) fixed by symmetry/covariance: **REJECTED**
- common cone preserved when axis correction is source-like rather than principal-stiffness-like: **CONDITIONAL**

## Experiments 284–291 — Linear curvature correspondence and volume-preserving axis metric

For a small spatial metric perturbation

\[
h_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\beta_A\mathcal A_{ij},
\]

linear scalar curvature is

\[
R^{(1)}
=\partial_i\partial_j(\beta_A\mathcal A^{ij})
-\nabla^2(\beta_A\operatorname{tr}\mathcal A).
\]

Because \(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal A=0\),

\[
\boxed{
R^{(1)}=\beta_A\partial_i\partial_j\mathcal A^{ij}
}.
\]

Thus the zero-monopole source operator coincides with the linear scalar-curvature form of a traceless spatial anisotropy. This is a geometric correspondence, not a derivation of a physical curvature-to-gravity law.

A nonlinear positive-definite metric candidate is

\[
\boxed{
h_A=\exp(\beta_A\mathcal A)
}.
\]

Since \(\mathcal A\) is symmetric and traceless,

\[
h_A\succ0,
\qquad
\det h_A=1,
\]

and isotropy \(\mathcal A=0\) gives \(h_A=I\).

Three branches must remain separate:

1. **axis-metric branch**: \(\Delta_{h_A}\psi=\rho/\mu_0\);
2. **zero-monopole axis-source branch**: explicit double-divergence source;
3. **curvature-source branch**: e.g. \(\Delta_{h_A}\psi=\rho/\mu_0+\lambda_R R[h_A]\).

The curvature-source branch introduces a new dimensionless coupling and can generate nonlinear integrated curvature, so its monopole behavior requires a separate audit.

### Verdict

- double-divergence ↔ linear traceless curvature form: **CONFIRMED MATHEMATICALLY**
- \(h_A=e^{\beta_A\mathcal A}\) positive-definite and volume preserving: **CONFIRMED**
- geometry alone fixes \(\beta_A\): **REJECTED**
- metric/source/curvature branches may be identified without further assumptions: **REJECTED**

## Experiments 292–303 — Far-field flux robustness and independence of \(\mu_0\) and \(\beta_A\)

In the axis-metric branch,

\[
\Delta_{h_A}\psi=\frac{\rho}{\mu_0},
\qquad
h_A\to I\quad(r\to\infty),
\]

and physical mass is

\[
M=\int\rho\,dV_{h_A}.
\]

The divergence theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{r\to\infty}
\oint_{S_r}\nabla_{h_A}\psi\cdot n\,dA_{h_A}
=\frac{M}{\mu_0}
}.
\]

If the metric is exactly Euclidean outside the compact source region, the exterior is harmonic and

\[
\psi=-\frac{M}{4\pi\mu_0r}+\text{higher multipoles}.
\]

Therefore localized axis geometry can modify internal structure and multipoles while preserving the leading mass-only far field.

Two models with identical \(\rho,\mu_0,Q,\mathcal A\) but different

\[
h_{\beta_1}=e^{\beta_1\mathcal A},
\qquad
h_{\beta_2}=e^{\beta_2\mathcal A}
\]

agree in the isotropic state but differ for anisotropic configurations. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mu_0:\text{ absolute leading amplitude},
\qquad
\beta_A:\text{ dimensionless anisotropic geometry response}
}
\]

are independent parameters.

At weak anisotropy,

\[
\frac{\delta a}{a_0}=\beta_A F_a[\mathcal A,\rho]+O(\beta_A^2),
\]

so the absolute \(\mu_0\) cancels in the relative correction.

### Verdict

- localized axis geometry preserves total far-field flux: **CONDITIONAL CONFIRMED**
- \(\mu_0\) and \(\beta_A\) are the same normalization: **REJECTED**
- relative anisotropic response can be tested without knowing \(\mu_0\): **CONFIRMED AT WEAK ORDER**

## Experiments 307–315 — Spherical matter with anisotropic axes

Take a spherical baseline

\[
\psi_0=\psi_0(r)
\]

and an axis anisotropy

\[
\mathcal A^{ij}(x)=a(r)S^{ij},
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}S=0.
\]

For the weak axis-metric correction,

\[
\Delta\psi_1
=\partial_i\left(\mathcal A^{ij}\partial_j\psi_0\right).
\]

Writing \(f(r)=a(r)\psi_0'(r)\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta\psi_1
=\left(f'(r)-\frac{f(r)}r\right)S_{ij}n^in^j
}.
\]

The angular factor \(S_{ij}n^in^j\) is a traceless \(l=2\) sector. Hence a perfectly spherical matter density can carry a quadrupolar exterior correction generated by axis geometry alone.

Matter quadrupole and axis quadrupole therefore form distinct upstream descriptors; one exterior quadrupole does not generally reconstruct which internal sector produced it.

### Verdict

- spherical density forces spherical response in axis-metric branch: **REJECTED**
- axis-only quadrupole at weak order: **CONFIRMED CONDITIONALLY**
- exterior quadrupole uniquely identifies matter redistribution: **REJECTED**

## Experiments 316–325 — Dynamic distinction and energy exchange

Use the causal weak equation

\[
\frac1{c_*^2}\partial_t^2\psi
-\partial_i\left(h_A^{ij}\partial_j\psi\right)
=\pm\frac{\rho}{\mu_0}.
\]

With \(h_A=e^{\beta_A\mathcal A}\) and small \(\beta_A\),

\[
\left(\frac1{c_*^2}\partial_t^2-\nabla^2\right)\psi_1
=-\partial_i\left(\mathcal A^{ij}\partial_j\psi_0\right).
\]

Two perturbation classes are therefore

\[
\Box\,\delta\psi_M
=\pm\frac{\delta\rho}{\mu_0},
\]

and

\[
\Box\,\delta\psi_A
=-\beta_A\partial_i\left(\delta\mathcal A^{ij}\partial_j\psi_0\right).
\]

Only their combined effective source may be visible in one exterior \(\psi\) signal, so external field data alone need not identify whether the cause was matter redistribution or axis reorganization.

For

\[
E_\psi
=\frac{\mu_0}{2}\int\left[(\partial_t\psi)^2+c_*^2h_A^{ij}\partial_i\psi\partial_j\psi\right]d^3x,
\]

one obtains, neglecting boundary flux,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{dE_\psi}{dt}
=\pm c_*^2\int\rho\,\partial_t\psi\,d^3x
+\frac{\mu_0c_*^2}{2}\int\dot h_A^{ij}\partial_i\psi\partial_j\psi\,d^3x
}.
\]

The second term is a direct energy-exchange channel between changing axis geometry and the progression field. Full conservation requires a dynamical axis sector that receives the opposite exchange.

### Verdict

- exterior signal alone generally separates matter and axis reorganization: **REJECTED**
- common propagation cone can make front timing identical: **CONDITIONAL**
- time-dependent axis metric exchanges energy with \(\psi\): **CONFIRMED WITHIN THE TOY ACTION**

## Experiments 326–333 — Field-induced axis drive and local tensor dynamics

Variation with respect to traceless axis anisotropy gives the weak generalized driving tensor

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_A^{ij}
=\frac{\beta_A\mu_0c_*^2}{2}
\left[
\partial^i\psi\partial^j\psi
-\frac13h^{ij}|\nabla\psi|^2
\right]
}.
\]

For a spherical \(\psi(r)\), this distinguishes radial and tangential directions.

A local small-anisotropy axis action may be written

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathcal A}
=\frac{\mu_A}{2}\|\partial_t\mathcal A\|^2
-\frac{\mathcal T_A}{2}\|\nabla\mathcal A\|^2
-\frac{\mathcal R_A}{2}\|\mathcal A\|^2
+\frac{\beta_A\mu_0c_*^2}{2}
\mathcal A^{ij}\partial_i\psi\partial_j\psi.
\]

The coefficient roles are

\[
[\mu_A]=M/L,
\qquad
[\mathcal T_A]=ML/T^2,
\qquad
[\mathcal R_A]=M/(LT^2),
\]

with axis-reorganization propagation scale

\[
c_A^2=\mathcal T_A/\mu_A.
\]

However an actual second-moment anisotropy obeys

\[
Q=\mathcal A+\frac13I\succeq0,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}Q=1,
\]

so

\[
-\frac13\le\lambda_i(\mathcal A)\le\frac23.
\]

An unconstrained linear tensor wave equation can leave this admissible set. Thus the \(\mathcal A\)-action is only a tangent/local approximation near admissible states.

### Verdict

- axis inertia/stiffness/restoration obtain concrete tensor-dynamics roles: **CONDITIONAL / SURVIVES**
- unconstrained \(\mathcal A\) field is a globally valid axis model: **REJECTED**

## Experiments 334–343 — Projector reorientation and rank-collapse regularity

For each realized line define the rank-one projector

\[
P_i=n_in_i^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Let

\[
G=[\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi]^{\rm TF}.
\]

Under an infinitesimal rotation generator \(\Omega_i^T=-\Omega_i\),

\[
\delta P_i=[\Omega_i,P_i].
\]

The natural skew reorientation direction generated by the axis-field coupling is

\[
\boxed{[P_i,G]}.
\]

Thus an admissible candidate flow is

\[
\Omega_i\parallel[P_i,G],
\qquad
\dot P_i=[\Omega_i,P_i].
\]

This preserves projector form:

\[
P_i^2=P_i,
\qquad
P_i^T=P_i,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}P_i=1.
\]

The commutator fixes reorientation direction but not the rate; axis inertia/friction/stiffness data are still needed to determine temporal response.

For the axis metric

\[
h_A=e^{\beta_A\mathcal A},
\]

and second-moment eigenvalue range

\[
-\frac13\le\lambda_i(\mathcal A)\le\frac23,
\]

all metric eigenvalues remain positive. In particular the metric condition number is uniformly bounded by a finite exponential in \(|\beta_A|\) (a conservative bound is \(\kappa(h_A)\le e^{|\beta_A|}\)).

Therefore a loss of realized-axis rank or an increase of linear dependence among realized lines does **not** by itself force metric degeneracy, causal collapse, or a black-hole-like singular state in this branch.

### Verdict

- commutator projector flow preserves realized-line projector constraints: **CONFIRMED**
- geometry fixes reorientation direction but also the rate: **REJECTED**
- axis-rank collapse implies axis-metric singularity: **REJECTED**
- strong collapse must be sought in an additional nonlinear/self-field/support sector: **CONFIRMED AS NEXT AUDIT TARGET**
