# LAW-008 Contradiction Audit — Rule Conflict, Exception, Priority

## Audit rule

A direct contradiction with DSD is counted only after the same source rules, typed legal instance, applicability conditions, time, parties/jurisdiction, and conflict-resolution rule are matched.

## Candidate contradiction C1

`Two rules differ, therefore DSD must represent a contradiction.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: ILC distinguishes interpretive relationships from relationships of conflict and endorses harmonization where possible. Difference/relevance alone is insufficient.

## Candidate contradiction C2

`If a special rule governs, the general rule must be absent/deleted.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: ILC states that application of special law does not normally extinguish the relevant general law. A DSD encoding can keep both source records while marking the source-supplied scope relation.

## Candidate contradiction C3

`Every conflict has the same downstream legal consequence.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: UN Charter Article 103 supplies a priority relation, whereas VCLT Article 53 supplies treaty invalidity for conflict with a peremptory norm. VCLT Article 30 supplies party/compatibility-sensitive application rules for successive treaties.

## Candidate contradiction C4

`Later law always wins globally.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: VCLT Article 30 is conditioned by Article 103, express treaty clauses, party overlap and compatibility. Different state pairs may be governed differently.

## Candidate contradiction C5

`Priority loss = channel absence.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: a lower-priority or displaced legal rule/instrument may remain an existing legal source and can remain relevant outside the conflict scope. Formation itself distinguishes channel absence from a defined channel/status; no source bridge licenses deletion.

## Candidate contradiction C6

`Exception = defined zero or affirmative permission.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: an exception may alter applicability/scope rather than supply one universal opposite normative value. LAW-006 already established this boundary; LAW-008 retains the exception rule identity.

## Candidate contradiction C7

`If the source system contains a genuine unresolved normative conflict, DSD must automatically repair it.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: DSD Analysis does not supply legal hierarchy. A source-side unresolved conflict can be represented as a defined relation/status. Treating it as missing/undefined merely because DSD cannot resolve it would erase source information.

## Candidate contradiction C8

`A priority relation can be represented as a numeric legal weight through Static Aggregation.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: no selected source supplies a numerical additive bridge. Priority, speciality, invalidity and harmonization are legal relations, not automatically scalar weights.

## Candidate contradiction C9

`Rule hierarchy requires Axis-Property.`

Result: rejected.

Reason: legal hierarchy does not independently provide realized-axis semantics. A metaphorical vertical ordering is insufficient.

## DSD source fidelity check

The Formation source explicitly preserves distinctions among undefined assignment, defined zero, channel absence and zero contribution, and preserves role in channel identity. It also permits multiple distinct channels. LAW-008 uses these facts only to avoid premature status collapse.

It does not derive:

- lex specialis;
- lex posterior;
- Charter supremacy;
- jus cogens;
- treaty invalidity;
- a universal legal conflict resolver.

## Final audit

After rule identity, applicability, scope, parties, time, regime and source conflict rules are matched:

- direct contradiction with Formation: **none found**;
- apparent conflicts caused by under-typing: **multiple, resolved by source-faithful encoding**;
- source-side genuine conflict: **must be preserved if unresolved by the source system**;
- Axis-Property required: **no**;
- Static Aggregation required: **no**;
- Dynamics required: **not for first pass**.
