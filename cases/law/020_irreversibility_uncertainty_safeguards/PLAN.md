# LAW-009 Plan — Irreversibility, Uncertainty, and Procedural Safeguards

Law ID: LAW-009
Global case: 020
Primary purpose: reinterpretation
Secondary purposes: coherence, predefinition audit, irreparability/safeguard boundary analysis, universal-claim falsification

## Initial hypothesis under test

The motivating hypothesis is that more irreversible legal intervention should require stronger justification and procedural safeguards.

This is **not** assumed as a universal theorem.

The first task is to test whether the following monotone form survives source pressure:

`greater irreversibility -> stronger safeguards`.

## Questions

1. Do legal systems expressly treat irreparable or irreversible harm as a reason for ex ante preservation, heightened review, or procedural protection?
2. Is irreparability alone sufficient, or do urgency, protected-right status, evidentiary posture, jurisdiction, and institutional purpose also matter?
3. Does the death penalty supply a direct witness that a more irreversible sanction receives safeguards beyond ordinary criminal cases?
4. Do interim-measure regimes supply a distinct witness that later adjudication can be protected against irreparable defeat before final judgment?
5. Can DSD Formation and Dynamics represent the relevant distinctions without deriving the legal norm itself?

## Witness families

- Human Rights Committee General Comment No. 36 on ICCPR Article 6.
- UN ECOSOC Safeguards for persons facing the death penalty, including resolutions 1984/50 and 1989/64.
- European Court of Human Rights Rule 39 interim-measure structure.
- International Court of Justice provisional-measures doctrine on irreparable prejudice and urgency.

## Counterpressure

The universal monotone hypothesis must be rejected or narrowed if:

- irreparability is not sufficient for a safeguard;
- no universal scalar ordering of irreversibility is source-supported;
- different institutions react to irreparability with different safeguards;
- stronger safeguards arise for reasons other than irreversibility;
- a legal system recognizes irreversible consequences without a corresponding monotonic procedural scale.

## DSD boundary

Formation may preserve typed legal statuses and non-totalization.

Structural Reorganization Dynamics may supply a time-indexed state-transition carrier, but legal irreversibility, restoration criteria, standards of proof, and procedural safeguards remain application-supplied legal data.

Axis-Property and Static Aggregation are not presumed necessary.
