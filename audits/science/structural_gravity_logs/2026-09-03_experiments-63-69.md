## Experiment 63 — Relative acceleration is controlled by distortion curvature

For the local isotropic bridge

\[
\mathbf a=-\kappa(X)\nabla X,
\]

the spatial derivative is

\[
D\mathbf a
=-\kappa(X)H_X
-\kappa'(X)\,\nabla X\otimes\nabla X,
\]

where \(H_X=\nabla^2X\).
For nearby points separated by \(\xi\),

\[
\delta\mathbf a
\approx
D\mathbf a\,\xi.
\]

At a critical point of the distortion field, \(\nabla X=0\), this reduces to

\[
\boxed{D\mathbf a=-\kappa(X_*)H_X}.
\]

Therefore zero absolute acceleration at a symmetric center does not imply zero relative acceleration or zero structural effect.

## Experiment 64 — Smooth spherical-center expansion

For a smooth radial distortion field near the center,

\[
X(r)=X_0+\frac12X_2r^2+O(r^4).
\]

Then

\[
\nabla X=X_2\mathbf r+O(r^3),
\]

and under a smooth local bridge

\[
\mathbf a=-\kappa_0X_2\mathbf r+O(r^3).
\]

The center has \(\mathbf a(0)=0\), while the first spatial growth of acceleration is controlled by the center Hessian. For a smooth radial scalar,

\[
H_X(0)=X_2I.
\]

## Experiment 65 — Density relevance may appear first in the curvature sector

The earlier same-\(M\), same-\(R\), different-density controls all permit zero central slope by spherical symmetry. Their density differences can nevertheless be relevant to a candidate curvature map

\[
E_H:\text{source structure}\to H_X.
\]

No specific density-to-Hessian law is derived here. The correct sufficiency test for a chosen density descriptor \(D_\rho\) is

\[
D_\rho(S_1)=D_\rho(S_2)
\Rightarrow
H_X(S_1)=H_X(S_2).
\]

In a linear specialization this becomes

\[
\ker D_\rho\subseteq\ker E_H.
\]

Thus density may influence distortion curvature even where the distortion slope and acceleration vanish.

## Experiment 66 — Field universality does not imply slope or Hessian universality

Use the synthetic far-field pair

\[
X_A(r)=\frac{A}{r},
\qquad
X_B(r)=\frac{A}{r}+\frac{\sin(r^3)}{r^2}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{|X_B-X_A|}{|A|/r}
\le \frac{1}{|A|r}
\to0,
\]

so the distortion profiles become relatively indistinguishable in amplitude. However,

\[
\frac{d}{dr}(X_B-X_A)
=3\cos(r^3)-\frac{2\sin(r^3)}{r^3},
\]

which does not tend to zero. The second derivative can be even less controlled.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
X\text{ universality}\not\Rightarrow
\nabla X\text{ universality}\not\Rightarrow
H_X\text{ universality}
}
\]

without additional regularity/derivative-control assumptions.

## Experiment 67 — Derivative-order universality profile

For the same coarse-source fiber define separate scale-free spreads

\[
U_0(r;M)=\sup d_{\rm sf}(X_A,X_B),
\]

\[
U_1(r;M)=\sup d_{\rm sf}(\nabla X_A,\nabla X_B),
\]

\[
U_2(r;M)=\sup d_{\rm sf}(H_{X_A},H_{X_B}),
\]

where the supremum is over source pairs with the same selected coarse descriptor and the symmetric norm-distance is used whenever the denominator is nonzero.

These are logically separate tests. A source descriptor sufficient for \(X\) need not be sufficient for \(\nabla X\) or \(H_X\).

## Experiment 68 — Geometry universality and response-bridge universality are separate

For two sources,

\[
\mathbf a_A=-\kappa_A\nabla X_A,
\qquad
\mathbf a_B=-\kappa_B\nabla X_B.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbf a_A-\mathbf a_B
=-\kappa_A(\nabla X_A-\nabla X_B)
-(\kappa_A-\kappa_B)\nabla X_B.
\]

Hence

\[
\|\mathbf a_A-\mathbf a_B\|
\le
|\kappa_A|\,\|\nabla X_A-\nabla X_B\|
+|\kappa_A-\kappa_B|\,\|\nabla X_B\|.
\]

Far-field acceleration universality therefore requires control of both the geometric-slope spread and the response-coefficient spread. If \(\kappa=F'(X)\) with continuous \(F'\), convergence of the relevant \(X\) data can force coefficient convergence. If \(\kappa\) depends independently on hidden density/property/relation data, the bridge may retain source detail even when the geometry has already converged.

## Experiment 69 — Completeness criterion for structural distortion as the gravity mediator

Define the first jet of the distortion field at a response point by

\[
J^1X(S)=(X(S),\nabla X(S)).
\]

The acceleration response factors completely through the structural distortion first jet iff there exists a map \(B_{\rm acc}\) such that

\[
\mathbf a=B_{\rm acc}(J^1X).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
J^1X(S_1)=J^1X(S_2)
\Rightarrow
\mathbf a(S_1)=\mathbf a(S_2)
}
\]

for every admissible source pair in the tested regime.

If two sources have the same distortion value and slope but different accelerations, then \(X\) is not a complete mediator: an additional exterior structural sector must enter the physical-response bridge.

A stronger curvature-sensitive response may instead require the second jet

\[
J^2X=(X,\nabla X,H_X).
\]
