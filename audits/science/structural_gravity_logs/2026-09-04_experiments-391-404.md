## Experiment 391 — Joint quadratic stability form

Let \(U_0\) be a subcritical static progression-field background and let the axis sector be represented by a perturbation variable \(A\). Around a coupled equilibrium, write the quadratic perturbation energy abstractly as

\[
Q[\delta U,\delta A]
=
\frac12\langle\delta U,L_U\delta U\rangle
+
\frac12\langle\delta A,L_A\delta A\rangle
+
\beta_A\langle\delta U,C\,\delta A\rangle .
\]

Here \(L_U\) is the progression-field stability operator, \(L_A\) is the axis restoration/stiffness operator, and \(C\) is the linearized field-axis coupling.

Assume \(L_A>0\) on the admissible axis perturbation space.

---

## Experiment 392 — Schur-complement criterion

Minimizing over the relaxable axis sector gives

\[
\delta A_*=-\beta_A L_A^{-1}C^*\delta U,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
L_{U,\mathrm{eff}}
=
L_U-
\beta_A^2 C L_A^{-1}C^*
}.
\]

Because

\[
C L_A^{-1}C^*\succeq0,
\]

the relaxable axis correction is negative semidefinite.

### Verdict

A dynamically relaxable axis sector can only lower or leave unchanged the static progression-field stability margin at leading \(O(\beta_A^2)\), provided \(L_A>0\). It cannot raise the threshold through this relaxation mechanism.

## Experiment 393 — Earlier-than-pure-field threshold

Let the lowest uncoupled progression-field stiffness be

\[
k_U(\epsilon)>0
\]

for \(\epsilon<\epsilon_c^{(0)}\), with

\[
k_U(\epsilon_c^{(0)})=0.
\]

For the soft mode \(u_0\), define

\[
\chi_A
:=
\langle u_0,
C L_A^{-1}C^*u_0\rangle
\ge0.
\]

Then the coupled critical condition is, to leading order,

\[
\boxed{
k_U(\epsilon_c^{\rm coupled})
=
\beta_A^2\chi_A}.
\]

If \(\chi_A>0\), then

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_c^{\rm coupled}
<
\epsilon_c^{(0)}
}.
\]

Thus an axis-reorganization instability can appear before the pure \(U\)-field spectral threshold.

## Experiment 394 — Near-critical threshold shift

Suppose

\[
k_U(\epsilon)
=
s_U(\epsilon_c^{(0)}-\epsilon)
+O((\epsilon_c^{(0)}-\epsilon)^2),
\qquad s_U>0.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_c^{\rm coupled}
=
\epsilon_c^{(0)}
-
\frac{\beta_A^2\chi_A}{s_U}
+O(\beta_A^4)
}.
\]

This identifies the previously obtained quasistatic negative axis-feedback correction as the zero-frequency Schur-complement limit of the full coupled stability problem.

## Experiment 395 — One-mode dynamic witness

Take one progression mode \(x(t)\) and one axis mode \(y(t)\):

\[
m_U\ddot x+k_Ux+gy=0,
\]

\[
m_A\ddot y+k_Ay+gx=0,
\]

with \(m_U,m_A,k_A>0\).

The normal-mode frequencies satisfy

\[
(k_U-m_U\omega^2)(k_A-m_A\omega^2)-g^2=0.
\]

Hence

\[
\omega_{\pm}^2
=
\frac{k_Um_A+k_Am_U
\pm
\sqrt{(k_Um_A-k_Am_U)^2+4g^2m_Um_A}}
{2m_Um_A}.
\]

The exact static stability condition is

\[
\boxed{k_Uk_A>g^2}.
\]

Thus the coupled mode destabilizes while \(k_U\) is still positive whenever \(g\neq0\).

Synthetic example: \(m_U=m_A=k_A=1\), \(g=0.1\). The pure field becomes critical at \(k_U=0\), while the coupled system becomes critical already at

\[
\boxed{k_U=0.01}.
\]

## Experiment 396 — Finite-time linear growth after coupled crossing

For a growing mode \(e^{\sigma t}\),

\[
(k_U+m_U\sigma^2)(k_A+m_A\sigma^2)-g^2=0.
\]

Immediately beyond threshold, define

\[
\Delta:=g^2-k_Uk_A>0.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sigma^2
\simeq
\frac{\Delta}{k_Um_A+k_Am_U}
}
\]

near the crossing.

Therefore the static coupled threshold is also the point where one linear normal mode changes from oscillatory to exponentially growing.

### Boundary

This establishes finite-time **linear instability**, not nonlinear blow-up, horizon formation, or irreversible collapse. Nonlinear saturation and post-threshold evolution remain separate questions.

## Experiment 397 — Static and dynamic role separation of axis coefficients

Write the positive axis operator schematically as

\[
L_A
=
\mathcal R_A
+
\mathcal T_A\,\mathcal K_A,
\]

where \(\mathcal K_A\) is a nonnegative spatial shape operator.

Then:

- \(\mathcal R_A\) and \(\mathcal T_A\) determine the static susceptibility \(L_A^{-1}\) and therefore the threshold shift;
- \(\mu_A\) does not enter the static Schur complement but controls the dynamical response time and growth rate;
- \(\beta_A\) controls the field-axis coupling strength.

Thus the previously named axis properties receive distinct operator roles rather than one shared `gravity-strength` role.

## Experiment 398 — Fixed anisotropy versus relaxable anisotropy

A prescribed axis anisotropy changes \(L_U\) directly and may shift the critical eigenvalue either upward or downward at second order, as found previously for a fixed quadrupole.

A **relaxable** stable axis sector instead contributes

\[
-\beta_A^2CL_A^{-1}C^*,
\]

whose sign is fixed.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{fixed anisotropy}
\neq
\text{dynamically induced anisotropy}
}
\]

at the level of stability theory.

## Experiment 399 — Axis-only crossing at fixed matter \(M,R,\rho\)

For fixed matter source data, \(k_U(\epsilon)\) is fixed. Axis evolution can nevertheless change the susceptibility and coupling overlap:

\[
\chi_A(t)
=
\langle u_0,C(t)L_A(t)^{-1}C(t)^*u_0\rangle.
\]

A crossing occurs when

\[
\boxed{
\beta_A^2\chi_A(t)=k_U(\epsilon).
}
\]

Thus, conditionally, total matter mass, radius, and radial density may remain fixed while axis reorganization alone drives the coupled system through its stability boundary.

## Experiment 400 — Null cases

Axis evolution does **not** shift the threshold when any of the following holds:

1. \(C^*u_0=0\) by symmetry or selection rule;
2. the realized-axis second moment remains isotropic, e.g. rigid rotation of an equal-weight orthonormal triad;
3. \(L_A^{-1}\to0\) in the infinite-restoration/infinite-stiffness limit;
4. \(\beta_A=0\).

These provide direct control models.

## Experiment 401 — Principal cones of the coupled system

In the weak coupled model, the off-diagonal field-axis terms generated by the background gradient are lower spatial derivative order than the diagonal second-order propagation operators.

Therefore the high-frequency principal symbol remains block-diagonal to leading order:

\[
P_U\sim-\omega^2/c_*^2+|k|^2,
\]

\[
P_A\sim-\mu_A\omega^2+\mathcal T_A|k|^2.
\]

The axis characteristic speed is

\[
\boxed{c_A^2=\mathcal T_A/\mu_A}.
\]

The low-frequency coupled instability does not by itself modify the outer high-frequency causal cone.

## Experiment 402 — Causal compatibility condition

If a global structural-information bound is retained, a sufficient sectorwise condition is

\[
\boxed{
c_*\le c_{\rm info},
\qquad
c_A\le c_{\rm info}.
}
\]

Equality is not required. If both sectors share one physical metric cone, then a stronger specialization may set \(c_A=c_*=c_{\rm info}^{(G)}\), but this is additional structure.

## Experiment 403 — Adiabatic crossing versus dynamical instability

Three distinct claims must remain separated:

1. **Static sequence:** the family of stationary solutions reaches the Schur-complement boundary.
2. **Linear dynamics:** beyond that boundary one normal mode has \(\omega^2<0\) and grows exponentially.
3. **Nonlinear fate:** growth proceeds to a new stable branch, saturation, structural transition, or collapse-like state.

Only (1) and (2) are established by the present coupled linear analysis.

## Experiment 404 — Consolidated coupled-instability result

Within the conditional progression-field / realized-axis specialization:

\[
\boxed{
\text{progression soft mode}
+
\text{relaxable axis sector}
\Rightarrow
\text{Schur-complement softening}
}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
L_{U,\mathrm{eff}}
=L_U-\beta_A^2CL_A^{-1}C^*.
}
\]

Hence a field-axis coupled instability may occur **before** the pure bounded-source self-field spectral threshold. The static threshold lowering depends on axis restoration/stiffness and coupling overlap; axis inertia controls the time scale after crossing. The result does not fix \(\mu_0\) and does not by itself establish nonlinear gravitational collapse.
