# LAW-010 Result — Responsibility, Sanction, Recovery, Emotion, and Social Cost

Status: first-pass universal analysis complete.

Primary purpose: reinterpretation.

Secondary purposes: coherence, predefinition audit, punishment/reparation boundary analysis, social-input decomposition, universal-claim falsification.

## 1. Answer-first result

LAW-010 confirms a broad non-totalization structure but rejects a rigid universal doctrinal taxonomy.

The strong form

`responsibility, punishment, restoration, compensation, rehabilitation and prevention are always legally separate categories in every system`

is too strong and is rejected.

The surviving source-sensitive candidate is:

**when a legal or institutional source gives responsibility, sanction, victim restoration/reparation, rehabilitation, prevention/non-repetition, social-emotional response, institutional/social cost, legal status, or practical use different actors, recipients, conditions, procedures, temporal states or effects, those distinctions must be preserved until the source rule explicitly identifies, combines, prioritizes or transforms them.**

This applies equally to emotional and social-cost inputs: they may be relevant, filtered, decisive, overridden or excluded depending on the governing political/legal decision structure, but DSD does not preassign their weight or direction.

## 2. Witness family 1 — ICC punishment and reparations are distinct routes

The Rome Statute places reparations to victims in Article 75 and applicable penalties in Article 77.

Article 75 includes restitution, compensation and rehabilitation. Article 77 separately defines imprisonment and other penalties for convicted persons.

This is a direct witness against:

`punishment = victim reparation`.

The same crime and conviction can support both routes without making them the same legal object.

## 3. Witness family 2 — reparation is itself plural

UN human-rights remedial materials distinguish:

- restitution;
- compensation;
- rehabilitation;
- satisfaction;
- guarantees of non-repetition.

Therefore:

`reparation != compensation alone`.

The materials also recognize that bringing perpetrators to justice can coexist with, but is not identical to, victim-specific reparation.

This is a strong witness for:

`punishing wrongdoer != fully repairing victim`.

It also shows that prevention/non-repetition may be one part of a broader remedy structure without being identical to compensation or restoration.

## 4. Witness family 3 — victim status does not universally wait for conviction

UN Committee against Torture General Comment No. 3 states that a person should be considered a victim regardless of whether the perpetrator is identified, apprehended, prosecuted or convicted.

Therefore:

`criminal responsibility established -> victim status becomes legally available`

is not a universal sequence.

This is important for LAW-010 because it prevents responsibility attribution from becoming the universal source of every downstream victim-remedy status.

## 5. Witness family 4 — public emotion may matter without controlling judgment

In South Africa, `S v Makwanyane` expressly assumes for argument that a majority might support the death penalty in extreme murder cases, but states that public opinion is not a substitute for constitutional adjudication.

The Court simultaneously considers the irrevocability of death, error/arbitrariness, inequality, dignity and life.

Therefore:

`strong public condemnation / retention support != constitutionally required death penalty`.

LAW-010 records public emotion and opinion as a real social/political input, not as noise, but does not confuse that input with the legal judgment rule.

## 6. Witness family 5 — historical state violence and present retributive pressure can coexist

German official materials provide a strong historical witness.

Article 102 of the Basic Law abolishes capital punishment. Bundestag and Federal Constitutional Court materials identify Nazi-era abuse of capital punishment as a decisive historical reason for the provision.

Yet postwar parliamentary debate still contained arguments grounded in retribution, deterrence and intense public reaction to serious crimes.

Therefore one society can simultaneously contain:

`historical distrust of state killing`

and

`present retributive/deterrent pressure`.

Neither automatically determines the legal result.

This supports preserving `H_state` and `E_public` as different coordinates.

## 7. Witness family 6 — legal retention and execution practice are not one status

Current comparative death-penalty reporting distinguishes:

- abolitionist for all crimes;
- abolitionist for ordinary crimes only;
- abolitionist in practice;
- retentionist.

As of the end of 2025, Amnesty International reported 113 countries abolitionist for all crimes and 145 abolitionist in law or practice.

The structure matters more than the count:

`death penalty legally retained != executions occur`;

`long-term non-execution != legal abolition`.

Thus a source-faithful analysis must separately encode legal authorization, scope, moratorium/non-use, sentence practice and execution practice.

## 8. Witness family 7 — victim/bereaved-family emotion is heterogeneous

US Office of Justice Programs materials document murder-victim family members who oppose capital punishment and identify them as a distinct subgroup whose interests can conflict with a prosecution seeking death.

Therefore:

`victim-family grief/anger != one mandatory punishment preference`.

This does not deny that other bereaved families may strongly support capital punishment. It establishes precisely the opposite: the emotional/evaluative input is actor-indexed and heterogeneous.

## 9. Social-cost decomposition

The phrase `social cost` is too coarse if treated as one monetary quantity.

LAW-010 distinguishes at least:

- `C_maint` — fiscal, procedural, administrative or institutional maintenance burden;
- `C_error` — wrongful-conviction / wrongful-execution risk and irreparable error;
- `C_abuse` — political killing, discriminatory enforcement, authoritarian abuse or state-violence risk/history;
- `C_legit_keep` — legitimacy pressure from perceived under-punishment or non-use;
- `C_legit_use` — legitimacy pressure from perceived arbitrariness, discrimination, disproportionality or coercive abuse;
- `H_state` — historically relevant memory of state violence and coercive misuse.

These coordinates may interact, but no common unit is supplied.

A monetary policy analysis may quantify some of them. A moral/political model may assign weights to others. LAW-010 does neither without an external empirical and normative bridge.

## 10. Symmetric policy pressure result

The same sanction regime may face pressure in opposite directions.

### Retention/use pressure

Possible source-side inputs include:

- severe public condemnation;
- bereaved-family retributive demand;
- perceived deterrence or incapacitation benefit;
- legitimacy concern that lesser punishment is insufficient.

### Non-use/abolition pressure

Possible source-side inputs include:

- wrongful-execution risk;
- political/state-abuse history;
- discriminatory/arbitrary enforcement;
- irreversibility;
- procedural/institutional burden;
- constitutional/human-rights constraints;
- legitimacy concern about state killing.

The universal result is **not** that these inputs must balance equally or that one side is correct.

The result is that both kinds of input can be legally/politically meaningful and must reach an outcome through the governing decision structure rather than through a DSD-supplied weight.

## 11. Responsibility, punishment and restoration

The most important human-centered result is:

`responsibility recognition != punishment != restoration of the harmed person`.

For irreversible harms, punishment cannot literally reverse the historical event.

Even where punishment has retributive, expressive, preventive or incapacitative functions, those functions do not automatically amount to restitution, compensation, rehabilitation or satisfaction for the victim.

Conversely, victim compensation or rehabilitation does not necessarily exhaust questions of responsibility, sanction or non-repetition.

## 12. Functional P/D/J result

LAW-010 extends the functional model after responsibility is found.

`P` may seek responsibility and a particular disposition.

`D` may challenge not only responsibility but also sanction eligibility, proportionality, finality, applicability or source conditions.

`J` can establish responsibility yet still face distinct decisions concerning:

- sanction;
- reparation;
- compensation;
- rehabilitation;
- prevention/non-repetition;
- review and execution.

Therefore:

`R_established != S_specific_required`.

And:

`S_imposed != victim_restored`.

## 13. DSD Formation result

Formation remains useful as a typed-separation discipline.

It does not derive punishment theory, victim rights or social preferences.

Do not identify:

`responsibility != Formation realization`;

`punishment != channel value`;

`grief/public anger != analytic weight`;

`social cost != DSD aggregation coefficient`;

`abolitionist in practice != channel absence`.

No direct contradiction with Formation was found after explicit legal/social encoding.

## 14. Dynamics result

Dynamics is meaningfully useful for temporal trajectories such as:

`harm -> adjudication -> sanction/reparation -> later recovery/rehabilitation`;

and

`retention -> moratorium/non-use -> de facto abolition -> legal abolition`.

It may also encode reverse transitions where a legal system permits reintroduction.

However, Dynamics supplies neither the legal transition rule nor the empirical causal model of healing, rehabilitation, deterrence or recidivism.

No direct contradiction was found under that boundary.

## 15. Static Aggregation result

Static Aggregation is **not** used as a social-choice or sentencing formula.

The analysis expressly rejects an unsupported scalar such as:

`grief + public anger + deterrence + victim restoration + wrongful-execution risk + legitimacy + fiscal cost -> punishment choice`.

Such a calculation would require:

1. a common codomain;
2. measurement rules;
3. empirical evidence;
4. normative conversion/aggregation rules;
5. decision thresholds.

None is supplied by the current DSD papers.

## 16. Finite witness result

Seven finite states show that no one-dimensional `justice`, `death penalty`, `social support`, or `social cost` value preserves all source distinctions.

The minimum source-faithful package needs separate coordinates for responsibility, sanction, reparation/restoration, prevention, actor-indexed emotion, institutional/social-cost family, legal status, practice status, regime and time.

## 17. Cross-case result after LAW-010

LAW-009 showed that irreversibility does not yield a universal monotone safeguard law.

LAW-010 now shows that post-responsibility legal and social responses also do not collapse to one global `justice` output.

The cross-case rule becomes:

**preserve source-defined responsibility, disposition, remedy, recovery, prevention, social-emotional input, institutional cost, legal status and practice status separately; perform any attribution, punishment, restoration, aggregation, policy selection or temporal transition only through the source/empirical/normative rule that actually licenses it.**

## 18. Main negative results

Rejected:

- `punishment restores the victim`;
- `responsibility establishes one mandatory sanction`;
- `reparation is just money`;
- `victim status universally requires perpetrator conviction`;
- `public anger determines constitutional judgment`;
- `bereaved families have one punishment preference`;
- `historical state abuse automatically causes abolition`;
- `formal death-penalty retention means execution`;
- `non-execution means legal abolition`;
- `social cost is one monetary scalar`;
- `DSD can numerically select the correct punishment`.

## 20. Boundary statement

LAW-010 does not decide whether capital punishment, life imprisonment, restorative justice, retribution, deterrence or rehabilitation is morally or legally optimal.

It establishes a narrower structural result: responsibility, punishment, victim-directed remedy, future-risk management, social emotion, historical institutional context, social cost, formal legal status and practical use are independently distinguishable in the selected sources, and none should be silently converted into another without the source or externally justified rule that performs that conversion.
