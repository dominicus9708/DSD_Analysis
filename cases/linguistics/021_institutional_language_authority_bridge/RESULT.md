# LING-008 / Global Case 021 — Result

## Question
Can institutional language be represented faithfully by identifying meaningful utterance, role, authority, valid institutional act, and institutional effect? Does the DSD Formation/Axis stack add a useful decomposition without pretending to derive the institution itself?

## External result
Social-institution and social-ontology literature independently preserves a layered institutional structure. Institutions involve differentiated roles, rules, authority and power relations. On Searle-style accounts, constitutive rules can take the form `X counts as Y in context C`; declarative speech acts can create or alter institutional status. Other theorists dispute how central or ontologically deep such constitutive rules are.

Therefore DSD must preserve the source-theory plurality. The source field supports the existence of institutional bridges, not one uniquely mandatory theory of those bridges.

## Finite witness
Two actors may share:

- the same role label (`manager`),
- the same meaningful utterance (`Approved.`),
- the same target request,

while differing only in whether a relevant delegation remains active. Their institutional effect can then differ.

Likewise, an actor can possess a general role and some active authority while lacking jurisdiction over a particular target.

Hence:

`Role(s)=R` does not imply `Authority(s,a,C)`;

`Authority(s,a,C)` does not imply `Jurisdiction(s,target,C)`;

and

`meaningful utterance + role` does not imply `valid institutional effect`.

## Strongest structural chain
A faithful decomposition is:

`linguistic material`
`-> interpreted utterance`
`-> role-tagged actor/event`
`-> authority/delegation/jurisdiction relations`
`-> procedure/constitutive rule`
`-> valid institutional act`
`-> downstream institutional consequences`.

No arrow is supplied merely by the existence of the preceding node.

## Predefinition restraint
The new restraint is institutional-rule indexed:

> The existence of a role, utterance, or even a general authority relation does not justify preassigning a particular institutional effect before target, jurisdiction, delegation state, procedure, and constitutive/regulative rules are fixed.

This is independent corroboration of predefinition restraint from institutional theory rather than a DSD invention.

## Formation correspondence
Formation directly helps preserve typed event/channel identity and the role coordinate. It blocks erasure of the distinction between otherwise similar events carried by different roles.

But Formation does not derive:

- what powers a named role has;
- whether delegation is active;
- which targets lie in jurisdiction;
- what institutional rule converts an utterance/action into an institutional status.

These are application data.

## Axis-Property correspondence
For the **full institutional-language model**, Axis-Property becomes structurally natural and recommended, though still not logically mandatory.

It can encode explicitly declared tag-sensitive relations such as:

- delegated authority,
- representation,
- supervision,
- jurisdiction,
- actor-institution relations,
- target-scoped eligibility.

However, the Axis-Property paper itself requires declared property kinds, typed profiles, value carriers, application domains, partial assignments, and compatibility structure. Therefore an authority relation is not obtained merely by naming a property `authority`.

## The constitutive bridge remains external
Even if Formation supplies role-tagged identity and Axis-Property supplies authority/jurisdiction relations, the rule

`X counts as Y in context C`

or any alternative institutional rule theory remains external. DSD can host the typed inputs and preserve their distinctions, but it does not create the institution.

## Independent convergence with law
The result structurally converges with earlier law cases:

`eligibility/capacity != exercise != valid act != downstream judgment/effect`.

This convergence is useful because the institutional-language result comes from a different source literature. It is one additional external node, not a second count of the same legal case.

## Verdict
- Formation contradiction: **not found**.
- Structural reinterpretation: **established**.
- Predefinition restraint: **independently corroborated at the institutional-rule layer**.
- Formation: **core for role-tagged event identity**.
- Axis-Property: **recommended optional extension for full relation-sensitive institutional modeling**.
- Constitutive/institutional rule: **external bridge, not DSD theorem**.
- DSD theory of institutions: **not claimed**.
- New audit item: **confirmed — distinguish role, authority, delegation, jurisdiction, procedure, valid institutional act, and downstream effect.**
