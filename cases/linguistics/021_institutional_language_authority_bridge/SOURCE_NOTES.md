# Source Notes — LING-008 / Global Case 021

## External theory
### Social Institutions — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
The entry treats institutions as structures containing differentiated roles, task rules, interdependencies, hierarchy, authority, and power relations. It also surveys Searle's constitutive-rule account, in which `X counts as Y in context C`, while noting competing accounts that reduce or downgrade the centrality of constitutive rules.

Relevant consequence: institutional status and deontic power are not exhausted by the physical act or linguistic form alone; the institutional rule/context matters.

### Social Ontology — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
The entry reviews the view that some institutional/social facts may be generated through performative or declarative speech acts, while also noting disagreement about how literally such acts create social entities or statuses.

Relevant consequence: the source field itself is theoretically plural. DSD must not silently choose Searle's account as the unique institutional ontology.

### Speech Acts — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
Institutional declarations are standard cases in which authority, conventional procedure, time/place, and uptake may matter. This supports separation of locution from institutional success.

## DSD sources
### Formation Axiom System
Operational channel identity retains `(p,a,lambda,v,rho)`; the role coordinate is part of channel identity. Formation therefore supports preserving who/what role an event belongs to, but it does not derive social authority from a role label.

### Axis-Property Axiom System
The extension permits tag-sensitive unary, binary, higher-order, and mixed-profile partial properties/relations. It is therefore a natural optional carrier for authority/delegation/jurisdiction relations, provided the application explicitly supplies property kinds, profiles, carriers, domains, assignments, and compatibility rules.

## Source-preservation rule
Do not identify:
- institutional-role label with authority;
- authority with exercise;
- exercise with valid institutional effect;
- failed institutional effect with Formation channel absence;
- `X counts as Y in C` with a theorem of DSD.
