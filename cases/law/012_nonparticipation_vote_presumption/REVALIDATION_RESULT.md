# LAW-001 Cross-Jurisdiction Revalidation Result

Status: cross-jurisdiction revalidation complete.

Original historical `RESULT.md` is preserved.

## 1. Answer-first result

The original core survives, but only in a more precise source-sensitive form.

Rejected overbroad forms:

`nonaction never has legal/institutional consequences`;

`silence can never produce assent effect`;

`presumption of innocence means every burden on every criminal issue always rests on prosecution`.

The surviving universal candidate is:

**a nonformed/nonperformed act does not by itself carry the identity or value of an affirmative, negative, opposite, invalid, or otherwise formed act; if a legal or institutional regime assigns nonaction a consequence, default, sanction, assent effect, quorum effect, or other downstream result, that consequence must be attributed to the source rule that creates it rather than read back into the identity of the missing act.**

For responsibility-attribution settings, the companion candidate is:

**failure to establish responsibility under the governing burden and proof threshold blocks the source-defined responsibility/guilt result, but does not by itself prove the opposite factual proposition; any subsidiary presumption, affirmative-defence burden, or contrary finding must come from an additional source rule or evidence path.**

## 2. UN General Assembly witness

UN General Assembly Rules 83-87 distinguish:

- presence;
- affirmative vote;
- negative vote;
- abstention;
- members present and voting.

Rule 86 expressly defines members present and voting as those casting affirmative or negative votes and treats abstaining members as not voting.

Rule 67 separately makes presence relevant to quorum.

Therefore:

`present != voting`;

`abstention != NO`;

`quorum effect != vote identity`.

This is a strong international-institutional witness for the original meeting analysis.

## 3. EU Council witness

TFEU Article 238(4) provides that abstentions by members present or represented do not prevent adoption of acts requiring unanimity.

Therefore:

`abstention != blocking NO`;

but also:

`abstention != affirmative YES`.

The rule assigns abstention a specific downstream relation without changing its identity.

This is particularly useful because it defeats the intuition that an institutional input must be reconstructed solely from its downstream effect.

## 4. Election witness — Australia and the United Kingdom

The Australian Electoral Commission separately records:

- turnout/participation;
- formal votes;
- informal votes.

Informal ballot papers are separated from formal votes, while failure to vote without a valid reason may trigger a distinct legal penalty process.

Thus:

`failure to participate != informal ballot`;

and:

`failure to participate can be sanctionable != failure to participate becomes a cast ballot`.

UK Electoral Commission guidance independently records and adjudicates rejected ballot papers as ballot-paper objects.

The combined witness supports:

`no ballot formed/submitted != formed ballot later rejected`.

## 5. Silence/default counterpressure — UNIDROIT

UNIDROIT Article 2.1.6 states that silence or inactivity does not **in itself** amount to acceptance.

Its commentary also recognizes that agreement, established practices, or usage can make silence operative as acceptance in context.

This is decisive counterpressure against a naive universal statement that silence can never have assent consequences.

The source instead supports the DSD Analysis form:

`nonaction + no completion rule != formed assent`;

`nonaction + source completion/default rule -> source-defined consequence`.

The second expression does not license retroactive identification of the silence with an independently formed affirmative act.

## 6. Presumption-of-innocence witnesses

### ICC

Rome Statute Article 66 separates accusation from guilt and places the burden of proving guilt on the Prosecutor, requiring proof beyond reasonable doubt for conviction.

### ECHR

ECHR Article 6(2) doctrine protects presumption of innocence and in-dubio-pro-reo principles, but Salabiaku and later materials show that presumptions of fact/law are not categorically prohibited if bounded and defence rights are preserved.

### United States

Ninth Circuit model jury instructions state that the indictment is not evidence, the accused need not prove innocence, and the government must prove every element beyond reasonable doubt. If that burden is not satisfied, the verdict is not guilty.

The same model-instruction system also recognizes some affirmative defences carrying a burden on the defendant.

Therefore the stronger claim

`every criminal burden always lies on prosecution`

is false as a universal formulation.

The narrower charge/guilt distinction survives.

## 7. Not-guilty versus factual innocence

US federal model instructions make `not guilty` the required verdict when the prosecution has not met its proof burden.

UK CPS materials explicitly explain that an acquittal/not-guilty verdict means the proof standard required for conviction was not met; it does not itself amount to a finding that the complainant lied or that the opposite factual narrative was proved.

Thus:

`Guilt_not_proved -> Not_Guilty_verdict`

under the governing source rule,

but not:

`Guilt_not_proved -> Factual_Innocence_proved`.

This materially strengthens the original LAW-001 responsibility claim across independent systems.

No single binary value preserves all of these states.
