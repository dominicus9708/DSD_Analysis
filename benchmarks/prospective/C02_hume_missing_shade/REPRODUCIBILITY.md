## 3. Allowed pre-seal source

Primary source only:

David Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature*, Book I, Part I, Section I.

Relevant structure to recover from the primary source:

1. direct corresponding impression of one shade is absent;
2. neighboring shades are available;
3. shades are explicitly arranged by gradation;
4. the subject notices a gap / larger distance;
5. Hume says imagination can supply the missing idea;
6. Hume calls the case a singular exception to the general maxim.

No dedicated Missing-Shade response literature is permitted before the seal commit.

## 4. Sealed DSD predictions to reproduce

The auditor should recover the following without consulting post-seal files:

1. `no direct corresponding impression != no formation-supporting structure`;
2. `gap localization != exact qualitative idea` without a fidelity/identity bridge;
3. the arranged gradation contributes relational input;
4. `simple content != non-derived formation history` unless the target theory defines simplicity that way;
5. DSD should not simply declare Hume's example invalid if exact idea formation is independently granted.

## 7. Important non-results

The benchmark does **not** establish:

- that Hume was wrong;
- that mental mixing is psychologically real for all subjects;
- that DSD defines color phenomenology;
- that the DSD formulation is historically novel;
- that a successful Mode-C prediction proves the Formation or Axis-Property axioms ontologically true.

## 8. Retrieval-integrity limitation

`clean` is defined operationally by the project protocol: dedicated objection/reply literature for this case was not retrieved before the prediction seal.

This does not claim literal erasure of all background knowledge from a language model. The benchmark tests disciplined source-ordering and prediction commitment within the project workflow.
