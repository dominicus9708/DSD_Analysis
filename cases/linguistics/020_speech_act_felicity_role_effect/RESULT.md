# LING-007 / Global Case 020 — Result

## Question
Does formation of a meaningful utterance determine the intended speech act, its institutional validity, and its downstream effect? Does role sensitivity require the Axis-Property system?

## External result
Speech-act theory supplies an independent layered structure:

1. **locutionary level** — meaningful saying;
2. **illocutionary level** — act performed in saying something;
3. **felicity/success conditions** — conditions under which the intended act is successfully performed;
4. **perlocutionary level** — downstream effects on hearers or behavior.

Institutional acts such as appointing or declaring proceedings open are especially clear finite witnesses: identical words uttered by an authorized office-holder and by an unauthorized person need not produce the same illocutionary/institutional result.

Austin's further distinction between **misfire** and **abuse** also shows that `act failed to form` and `act formed but defective/insincere` are not the same status.

## Strongest structural statements

For suitable institutional contexts,

`Locution(u,s,C) does not imply SuccessfulIllocution(u,s,C)`.

More specifically,

`Role(s)=chair` does not by itself imply `Authority(s,act,C)`

unless the application supplies the institution-specific authority rule.

Likewise,

`SuccessfulIllocution(u,s,C) does not imply DesiredPerlocutionaryEffect(u,s,C)`.

The same surface sentence can therefore correspond to different complete speech-act structures.

## Predefinition restraint
The relevant restraint is now **force/effect indexed**:

> The existence and semantic interpretation of an utterance do not justify preassigning a particular illocutionary force, institutional validity, or downstream effect without the contextual, conventional, role, authority, and procedural bridge required by the source theory.

This is not merely `undefined != false`. It prevents an analyst from jumping from linguistic material to an institutional act or consequence before the act's felicity conditions are supplied.

## Formation correspondence
Formation is directly useful at the first stage because the operational channel retains

`(configuration, item, quantity-kind, value, role)`.

Thus a DSD application can preserve role-sensitive channel identity instead of erasing whether an utterance occurred under one role or another.

However, Formation does not contain a theorem stating that a named role has a specific social/legal power. Therefore an authority relation and the rules connecting that relation to speech-act success remain application-supplied.

## Axis-Property correspondence
This case is the first linguistic case in which Axis-Property is **structurally relevant but not required**.

Why relevant:
- the extension distinguishes bare realization from formation-tagged identity;
- tag-sensitive unary/binary/higher-order properties are available;
- relational authority/eligibility can therefore be encoded, if the application declares an appropriate property kind and typed profile.

Why not required:
- Formation already preserves the speaker role;
- the minimal success bridge can be supplied directly at the application level;
- speech-act felicity includes conventional procedure, context, uptake, intention, and other conditions that are not automatically axis properties;
- the Axis-Property paper explicitly states that a property name has no mathematical content until profile, carrier, assignment, and compatibility data are supplied.

Therefore:

`Axis-Property available != Axis-Property mandatory`.

## Important non-correspondence
Do not encode `misfire` automatically as Formation channel absence or `abuse` automatically as defined zero. Those would be interpretation choices, not source-preserving identities.

A more faithful application should retain the source-theory status first and only then declare any DSD mapping.

## Verdict
- Formation contradiction: **not found**.
- Structural reinterpretation: **established**.
- Predefinition restraint: **independently corroborated at the force/authority/effect level**.
- Formation role coordinate: **useful and sufficient for the minimal role distinction**.
- Axis-Property: **optional but meaningfully applicable for typed authority/relational structure**.
- DSD derivation of speech-act theory: **not claimed**.
- DSD derivation of social/legal authority: **not claimed**.
- New DSD-analysis audit item: **confirmed — distinguish locution, constitutive illocutionary success, defect status, and downstream effect; never infer authority from a role label without an explicit bridge.**
