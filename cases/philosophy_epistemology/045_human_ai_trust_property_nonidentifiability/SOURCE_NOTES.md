# PHIL-002 Source Notes — Human/AI Trust-Property Non-Identifiability

Status: **DSD formulation sealed before dedicated precedent search; source comparison completed**.

## DSD source basis

### Formation Axiom System

Use only the following structural roles:

- candidate/declared data and admitted/describable formation are not automatically identical;
- undefined assignment is distinct from defined zero and defined nonzero;
- channel absence is distinct from a zero-valued channel;
- operational identity preserves typed/role structure;
- equality of reduced/composite output does not by itself establish strict descriptive equivalence of the full descriptor;
- domain-specific applications require additional interpretation maps.

### Axis-Property Axiom System

Use only the following structural roles:

- a property may be globally declared while being inapplicable, unavailable, undefined, defined zero, or defined nonzero/value for a specific typed input;
- property application is represented by a partial assignment with an explicit domain;
- a property name alone has no mathematical content;
- bare carrier/line identity does not determine a property value;
- equal reduced geometric information does not imply strict property equivalence.

### Static Aggregation

Use only the following structural role:

- equal aggregate/reduced output does not reconstruct support-tagged records or complete typed property structure without an injectivity/reconstruction condition.

## External-domain target

The term `trust` is not imported as a DSD primitive. It is an externally interpreted property label whose semantics must be fixed by the target theory or measurement model.

The Human/AI Room case therefore does not claim that DSD defines psychological trust, machine trust, consciousness, belief, intentionality, reliability, or trustworthiness.

## Dedicated precedent search — retained sources

### 1. Pearce (1973), *Trust in Interpersonal Communication*

Relevant point: explicitly distinguishes a **cognitive state of trust** from **trusting behavior**. This establishes that behavior/state separation is old prior art and cannot be claimed as DSD novelty.

Classification: **same broad trust-state/behavior family**.

### 2. McKnight & Chervany / trust-construct tradition, summarized in *The Meanings of Trust*

Relevant point: distinguishes trusting intention, trusting behavior, and indicators such as cooperation or information sharing. Non-defection/cooperation need not itself be identical with trust.

Classification: **same broad construct/indicator family**.

### 3. Fetchenhauer et al. (2025), *Behavioral Trust in Competence Versus Morality*

Relevant point: distinguishes trust as cognition from trust as behavior and reports that these can be only weakly correlated.

Classification: **empirical support for non-identity of internal/cognitive and behavioral trust**.

### 4. Everett et al. (2026), *Principles for understanding trust in artificial intelligence*

Relevant point: identifies as separate constructs **trustworthiness, trust, and trusting behaviour** and states that trust in AI is inferred. This is a close modern AI-trust neighbor of the Human/AI Room.

Classification: **strong neighboring precedent, but not the exact DSD trilemma**.

### 5. Colombatto, Birch & Fleming (2025), *The influence of mental state attributions on trust in large language models*

Relevant point: mental-state attributions to LLMs are distinct from intelligence attributions and affect users' trust behavior in different ways. Demonstrates that anthropomorphic attribution and observed/reported behavior are separable empirical variables.

Classification: **neighboring anthropomorphism/mental-attribution family**.

### 6. Howdle (2026), *Anthropomorphising AI: Two Modes, Two Errors*

Relevant point: philosophical work treats mental-state language about AI as potentially involving mistaken ontological commitment. This overlaps the anthropomorphic-promotion horn.

Classification: **neighboring philosophical attribution problem**.

### 7. Johnson & Obradovich (2022), *Measuring an artificial intelligence agent's trust in humans using machine incentives*

Relevant point: experiments on an AI as trustor conclude that its choices display **behavior consistent with trust** toward humans. The wording itself preserves a distinction between observed decisions and the stronger metaphysical property claim.

Classification: **direct empirical neighbor for AI-as-trustor behavioral operationalization**.

### 8. Mei et al. (2023), *A Turing Test: Are AI Chatbots Behaviorally Similar to Humans?*

Relevant point: AI chatbots can be statistically indistinguishable from humans in behavioral games intended to elicit trust, cooperation, fairness, risk aversion, and related traits.

Classification: **direct neighbor for human/AI behavioral equivalence; does not by itself settle hidden-property identity**.

### 9. Buechner-related digital-trust discussion / *Some Circumstances Under Which It Is Rational for Human Agents Not to Trust Artificial Agents* (2025)

Relevant point: explicitly notes a category-error objection according to which artificial agents may not be eligible bearers of genuine trust; but if trust is defined as mere reliance or expectation, artificial agents can fall under the concept.

Classification: **close precedent for the behavioral-definition versus bearer-applicability fork**.

### 10. Budnik (2025), *Can We Trust Artificial Intelligence?*

Relevant point: separates evidential/reliance-style accounts from stronger normative trust and argues that stronger trust in AI faces additional requirements.

Classification: **neighboring domain/applicability debate**.

## Search comparison

The dedicated search found strong prior art for each of these components:

- trust state versus trusting behavior;
- trust versus trustworthiness;
- behavioral indicators versus latent/internal construct;
- humanlike AI behavior and anthropomorphic attribution;
- AI as an alleged bearer or non-bearer of genuine trust;
- operational machine-trust experiments that intentionally make weaker behavioral claims.

The retrieved sources did **not** provide an exact match to the full combined DSD structure:

1. identical admitted behavior for one human bearer and one AI bearer;
2. symmetric rejection of both `same behavior -> same hidden trust` and `AI substrate -> trust=0` shortcuts;
3. explicit three-way split among behavioral constitution, bearer/type gating, and unresolved assignment;
4. explicit separation of `inapplicable / unavailable input / undefined / defined zero / defined nonzero`;
5. explicit reconstruction/injectivity condition before reduced output is treated as a complete hidden-property identifier.

This absence is not proof of historical novelty.

## Final precedent classification

Conservative classification:

**new DSD-constructed rebuttal format and formal synthesis/sharpening of established trust, AI-attribution, and behavioral-identification problems; historical novelty unproven.**

## Comparison rule

Broad similarities to the problem of other minds, Turing-style behavioral tests, anthropomorphism, trust calibration, or interpretability are not enough for an exact-match verdict.

Do not convert a failure to locate an exact precedent into a priority claim.
