# LAW-001 / Global Case 012

## Title
Nonparticipation, Uncast Votes, and the Presumption of Innocence

## Primary purpose
reinterpretation / application

## Secondary purpose
coherence / predefinition audit

## Question
Can the DSD Formation distinction between candidate or eligible status, realized participation, defined assignment, admitted channel, and downstream result provide a non-circular explanation of three standard institutional separations?

1. A member who is absent and expresses no opposition is not thereby a recorded opponent.
2. A person who possesses a voting right but does not exercise it has not thereby cast an invalid ballot.
3. A criminally accused person is not to be promoted to a guilty legal status before the prosecution satisfies the applicable proof requirement.

## Anti-bias rule
The institutional rule is reconstructed first from external sources. DSD is then used only as an interpretive decomposition. No legal or parliamentary rule is claimed to be derived from DSD.

## DSD sources
- Formation Axiom System, especially the staged distinction among admission, realization, configuration describability, partial assignment, channel formation, and composition.
- Axis-property system only where role- or tag-sensitive institutional properties become relevant; it is not needed for the core three-state separation.

## External source families
- Robert's Rules of Order official FAQ and Official Interpretations for voting/abstention distinctions.
- UN Human Rights Committee General Comment No. 32, ICCPR Article 14(2), for presumption of innocence and burden of proof.
- Constitution of the Republic of Korea Article 27(4) as a domestic corroborating instance.

## Result classes
- structural correspondence
- conditional correspondence
- non-correspondence
- DSD overreach / unsupported derivation
