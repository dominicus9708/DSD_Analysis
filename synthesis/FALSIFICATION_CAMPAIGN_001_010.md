# DSD Falsification-Oriented Stress-Test Campaign — Cases 001–010

## Status

Initial 10-case campaign complete.

No claim of absolute consistency, completeness, empirical validation, or universal applicability is made.

The campaign records a narrower result: ten deliberately adversarial analyses did not produce an internal contradiction in the tested classes, while repeatedly identifying scope limits, non-correspondences, pre-axiom choices, and missing bridge laws.

## Proposed paper role

This synthesis is intended as preparation for a separate falsification-oriented analysis paper rather than as a running revision list for the source axiom papers.

Possible working titles:

- *Model-Theoretic Stress Tests of Dimensional-Structural Describability Axiom Systems*
- *Falsification-Oriented Stress Tests for the Formation and Axis-Property Axiom Systems of Dimensional-Structural Describability*

## Case map

### 001 — Partial functions and undefinedness

Tested whether undefined assignment may be collapsed into defined zero.

Result: not falsified. Zero-totalization loses domain/status information unless the encoding preserves it explicitly.

### 002 — Many-Sorted Logic and typed applicability

Tested wrong-sort application, unavailable carrier, partial undefinedness, and defined zero.

Result: not falsified. A roadmap error was corrected: wrong sort is outside the typed product, not DSD `unavailable input`.

### 003 — Institution Theory and strict equivalence

Tested renaming, same aggregate with different structure, and satisfaction-preserving translation.

Result: not falsified. Satisfaction preservation is weaker/different than DSD strict base-fixed formation isomorphism.

### 004 — Free Logic and predefinition non-justification

Tested hidden promotion rules such as candidate -> admitted, admitted -> describable, and realized -> describable configuration.

Result: not falsified. Adding those rules strictly removes models already admitted by the Formation system, so they are genuine extra assumptions.

### 005 — Strong Kleene K3

Tested whether DSD undefinedness is simply a third semantic value.

Result: not falsified, with an important non-correspondence. DSD undefinedness is outside the original partial-map domain; an external sentinel encoding is possible but must not be fed back as an ordinary Stage-V value.

### 006 — Public Announcement Logic

Tested whether external truth/knowledge leaks into DSD describability and whether epistemic model restriction equals DSD restriction/candidate deletion.

Result: not falsified. DSD regime-relative describability is not automatically an epistemic-state semantics; information update requires an additional bridge.

### 007 — Linear Logic

Tested resource multiplicity, weakening/contraction analogies, and set-indexed Stage-VII composition.

Result: not falsified. Distinct operational tags are preserved, but same-tag occurrence multiplicity is outside the current `Pfin(C_L)` core.

### 008 — Primitive PI direct attack

Tested total realization of selected axis channels.

Result: PI not falsified. The strongest vulnerability is earlier than PI: single-line functionality is already imposed by the partial-function type of `AxLine`; PI adds totality. Axis eligibility itself is primitive extension data.

### 009 — Primitive PII direct attack

Tested bilinear-dependency compatibility.

Result: PII not falsified. PII guarantees dependency-data availability, not semantic correctness of arbitrary property values. Symmetric-bilinear dependency and kind-level dependency classification are pre-axiom choices.

### 010 — Integrated Formation + Axis-Property countermodel search

Tested the full Stage-VI interface, P1–P8, PI/PII, and explicit completion.

Result: no integrated countermodel found in the tested class. A deliberately hostile model with coincident lines, tag-sensitive values, degenerate bilinear data, failed formal closure, and failed nondegeneracy still completes uniquely because those negative statuses are legitimate derived records rather than model collapse.

## Cross-case findings

### A. Predefinition non-justification has multiple layers

The campaign now distinguishes at least four mechanisms:

1. **hidden promotion rule** — an earlier status is treated as automatically implying a stronger later status;
2. **signature/type exclusion** — a candidate structure is removed before axioms are tested;
3. **dependency declaration** — a dependency class is chosen rather than derived;
4. **bridge assumption** — abstract records are assigned external/physical meaning without a constitutive map.

This is broader and more precise than treating predefinition non-justification as only a complaint about informal definitions.

### B. Negative outcomes inside a model are not automatically model failure

Several layers intentionally record distinctions such as:

- undefined vs defined zero;
- tag-sensitive vs line-invariant;
- satisfied vs failed closure;
- satisfied vs failed nondegeneracy.

The model can remain formally complete while containing a negative derived status.

### C. The core survives partly by limiting what it claims

The stress tests repeatedly show that the source axioms do not prove several stronger propositions that could easily be overread into them:

- Formation does not decide which channels are axes;
- PI does not prove single-valuedness of axis realization;
- PII does not validate property semantics;
- the Axis-Property core does not depend on Formation Stage VII;
- the current finite-set composition does not encode same-tag occurrence multiplicity;
- observer-information change is not generated by Formation closure alone.

These are scope boundaries, not defects, so long as the stronger claims are not asserted.

### D. Failure modes found by the campaign

The campaign found no false theorem requiring correction, but it did find several classes of would-be failure:

- lossy zero-totalization;
- type erasure;
- aggregate-only equivalence;
- unjustified promotion rules;
- semantic-value identification of undefinedness;
- observer-knowledge leakage;
- occurrence-multiplicity loss;
- pre-axiom functional restriction;
- dependency-availability vs semantic-value mismatch;
- backward cross-layer admissibility planning in PII.

These are useful because a future application can be checked against them before claiming to instantiate DSD faithfully.

## Claim discipline for the eventual paper

Preferred conclusion:

> No contradiction or countermodel was found within the tested classes.

Paired with:

> Several scope limitations, non-correspondences, pre-axiomatic commitments, and missing bridge laws were identified.

Avoid claims such as:

- DSD has been proved consistent in an absolute sense;
- the tests empirically validate DSD;
- external logical systems prove the DSD axioms;
- failure to find a countermodel proves universal correctness.

## Next research phase

The initial falsification campaign is complete. The next work should be paper construction and independent re-audit of the ten case records, not immediate addition of more similar logic cases unless a reviewer or new application exposes a specific untested vulnerability.
