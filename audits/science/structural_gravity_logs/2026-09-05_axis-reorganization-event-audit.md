## Experiment 432 — Projector commutator flow is rank-preserving for each realized axis

Let a realized unoriented axis be represented by a rank-one projector

\[
P_a^2=P_a,
\qquad
P_a^{\mathsf T}=P_a,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}P_a=1.
\]

For

\[
\boxed{\dot P_a=[\Omega_a,P_a]},
\qquad
\Omega_a^{\mathsf T}=-\Omega_a,
\]

the solution can be written

\[
P_a(t)=R_a(t)P_a(0)R_a(t)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\dot R_a=\Omega_aR_a,
\]

with orthogonal \(R_a\). Therefore symmetry, idempotence, trace, and the individual projector rank are preserved exactly.

### Verdict

The commutator equation is a kinematic admissibility law for realized-line reorientation, not yet a physical driving law.

## Experiment 433 — Common rigid rotation is not internal relative reorganization

If every axis undergoes the same rotation,

\[
P_a(t)=R(t)P_a(0)R(t)^{\mathsf T},
\]

then all pair invariants remain fixed:

\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_aP_b)=\text{constant}.
\]

For fixed weights,

\[
Q(t)=R(t)Q(0)R(t)^{\mathsf T},
\]

so the eigenvalues of \(Q\), and hence invariants such as

\[
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal A^2,
\qquad
\mathcal A=Q-I/3,
\]

are unchanged.

A common rotation can still matter relative to an external field or boundary, but it does not change the axes' internal pair geometry.

### Verdict

Distinguish **rigid common reorientation** from **relative structural reorganization**.

## Experiment 434 — Relative reorientation can change the structural-gravity anisotropy

With fixed weights,

\[
\boxed{
\dot Q=\sum_aw_a[\Omega_a,P_a]
}
\]

for general relative motion, while a common generator gives

\[
\dot Q=[\Omega,Q].
\]

The latter changes only orientation of the eigenspaces; relative \(\Omega_a\) can change the eigenvalues of \(Q\), and therefore the magnitude of \(\mathcal A\) and the axis metric

\[
h_A=e^{\beta_A\mathcal A}.
\]

Thus the structural-gravity branch is sensitive primarily to reorganization that changes the second moment \(Q\), not merely to the fact that individual axes moved.

## Experiment 435 — Collective axis rank is a Gram-matrix diagnostic

Choose arbitrary unit representatives \(n_a\) for the unoriented lines and define

\[
G_{ab}=n_a\cdot n_b.
\]

Changing a representative sign multiplies the corresponding row and column by \(-1\), so \(\operatorname{rank}G\) and \(\det G\) are orientation-independent.

The collective span rank is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{span}\{n_a\}
=\operatorname{rank}G.
}
\]

For three axes in three dimensions,

\[
\boxed{
\det G
=\bigl[n_1\cdot(n_2\times n_3)\bigr]^2.
}
\]

Hence

- \(\det G>0\): collective rank 3;
- \(\det G=0\): collective rank at most 2.

The integer rank can therefore change at a geometrically well-defined boundary even when the individual projectors remain rank one.

## Experiment 436 — Individual rank preservation does not imply collective rank preservation

Under projector commutator dynamics each \(P_a\) remains rank one. Nevertheless two initially independent axes may continuously align, causing

\[
\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{span}\{n_1,n_2\}:2\to1.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{individual axis rank preservation}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{collective span-rank preservation}.
}
\]

`Axis reorganization` and `rank transition` are related but distinct event classes.

## Experiment 437 — Rank transition and support loss are not equivalent

### Support loss without rank transition

The coupled Hessian may acquire a zero/negative eigenvalue while \(\det G>0\). Then the system loses support/stability while the axes remain collectively full rank.

### Rank transition without support loss

A pair potential that energetically favors alignment can have a stable aligned minimum. The collective span rank can then decrease as the system approaches that minimum without the transition being generated by a negative stability mode.

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{
\text{rank transition}\neq\text{support loss}.
}
\]

No black-hole/collapse interpretation follows from rank change alone.

## Experiment 438 — Collective rank is encoded by a positive weighted second moment

For

\[
Q=\sum_aw_aP_a,
\qquad
w_a>0,
\]

one has

\[
v^{\mathsf T}Qv=\sum_aw_a|n_a\cdot v|^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\ker Q
=\left(\operatorname{span}\{n_a\}\right)^\perp,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}Q
=\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{span}\{n_a\}.
}
\]

So the second moment detects collective span-rank loss when all admitted weights are strictly positive.

However the axis metric

\[
h_A=e^{\beta_A(Q-I/3)}
\]

remains positive definite even when \(Q\) becomes rank deficient. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{collective rank loss}\not\Rightarrow\text{metric singularity}.
}
\]

## Experiment 439 — Minimal dynamic bridge separates kinematics from dynamics

A first-order overdamped specialization can be written

\[
\dot P_a=[\Omega_a,P_a],
\]

with

\[
\Omega_a=\Gamma_a\mathcal G_a,
\]

where \(\mathcal G_a\) is the skew energetic/constitutive drive and \(\Gamma_a\) is a positive mobility operator.

For an energy gradient \(H_a=\partial E/\partial P_a\), one convenient drive is

\[
\mathcal G_a=[P_a,H_a].
\]

With the Frobenius metric this sign convention gives energy descent when \(\Gamma_a\) is positive.

A second-order inertial specialization instead requires an angular/inertial equation such as

\[
\mathsf M_{\Omega,a}\dot\Omega_a
+\mathsf D_{\Omega,a}\Omega_a
=\mathcal T_a(P,\psi,\ldots),
\]

followed by the same kinematic projector equation.

### Verdict

`Axis reorganization` should be split into:

1. admissible kinematics;
2. drive/torque functional;
3. mobility or inertia/damping law.

## Experiment 440 — A stationary axis state does not identify its cause

The observation

\[
\dot P_a=0
\]

is compatible with multiple descriptions:

- zero drive / energetic equilibrium;
- cancellation of several drives;
- symmetry locking such as \([P_a,H_a]=0\);
- zero mobility or very large inertial timescale;
- boundary/admissibility saturation;
- exact placement on an unstable equilibrium.

Therefore `no observed reorientation` does not imply support saturation or a unique physical mechanism.

---

## Experiment 441 — Refined stasis taxonomy

The earlier `stasis` intuition should not be identified with one property. Distinguish at least:

- **kinematic stasis**: relevant rates such as \(\|\dot P\|\) are small;
- **geometric stasis**: pair invariants and \(Q\) are stationary;
- **dynamic equilibrium**: constitutive generalized drives vanish;
- **kinetic arrest**: nonzero drive but mobility is suppressed / timescale diverges;
- **marginal stasis**: support margin approaches zero while the instantaneous state may remain stationary.

These descriptions can coincide but are not logically equivalent.

### Verdict

`Stasis` is best treated as a derived regime/diagnostic with a specified comparison timescale, not as a primitive axis property.

---

## Experiment 442 — Velocity information alone does not determine support

The support margin

\[
m_{\rm sup}=\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal H)
\]

and the instantaneous rate \(\dot P\) are independent diagnostics.

Examples:

- stable equilibrium: \(m_{\rm sup}>0\), \(\dot P=0\);
- marginal equilibrium: \(m_{\rm sup}=0\), \(\dot P=0\);
- unstable exact equilibrium before perturbation: \(m_{\rm sup}<0\), \(\dot P=0\).

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\dot P=0\not\Rightarrow\text{support/stability verdict}.
}
\]

## Experiment 443 — The axis-metric branch has a describability kernel

The current structural-gravity metric branch uses

\[
\{P_a,w_a\}\longmapsto Q=\sum_aw_aP_a\longmapsto h_A.
\]

Different microscopic axis arrangements can have the same \(Q\). Therefore a reorganization can satisfy

\[
\Delta Q=0
\]

while changing individual pair relations or higher-order axis structure.

Such a reorganization is **silent to the present \(h_A(Q)\) branch** but can remain visible to pair-coupling energy, crossing data, or higher moments.

### Verdict

\[
\boxed{
\text{axis reorganization}\not\Rightarrow\Delta h_A\neq0.
}
\]

The present axis metric is a coarse second-moment representation, not a complete descriptor of axis microstructure.

## Experiment 444 — Common rotation can be externally visible while internally invariant

If \(Q\) is anisotropic, common rigid rotation changes its orientation relative to an external progression-field gradient or boundary even though its eigenvalues remain fixed.

Thus a common rotation can be

- internally geometry-preserving;
- externally dynamically relevant.

This distinction must be recorded explicitly rather than classifying all rigid rotation as physically null.

## Experiment 445 — Reorganization-induced structural-gravity change requires a visible bridge

For the current branch

\[
h_A=e^{\beta_A\mathcal A},
\qquad
\mathcal A=Q-I/3,
\]

an intrinsic anisotropy-magnitude change requires a change in the spectrum of \(Q\). A pure common rotation only rotates \(h_A\)'s eigendirections.

Therefore structural-gravity consequences divide into:

1. **orientation effect**: eigendirections change relative to \(\nabla\psi\)/boundary;
2. **anisotropy-strength effect**: eigenvalues of \(Q\) change;
3. **metric-silent micro-reorganization**: \(Q\) unchanged.

## Experiment 446 — Rank transition is a boundary event in a continuous orientation dynamics

The orientations/projectors may vary continuously while a determinant diagnostic such as

\[
\Delta_G=\det G
\]

approaches zero continuously. The collective rank itself is integer-valued and changes when the trajectory reaches the boundary \(\Delta_G=0\).

Therefore `rank transition` should be represented as an event triggered by a continuous geometric state crossing an admissibility/degeneracy surface, not as a continuously varying rank property.

---

## Experiment 447 — Rank transition need not dominate the structural-gravity response

Because \(h_A=e^{\beta_A\mathcal A}\) remains regular at \(\det G=0\), a rank transition can alter anisotropy and coupling geometry without creating a singular progression metric.

A strong gravity/collapse claim still requires an independent instability, lapse/progression degeneration, self-field threshold, or another explicit mechanism.

## Experiment 448 — Reorganization may also occur through weight changes, but that is a separate bridge

If

\[
Q=\sum_aw_aP_a,
\]

then

\[
\dot Q
=\sum_aw_a[\Omega_a,P_a]
+\sum_a\dot w_aP_a.
\]

Thus orientation change and weight redistribution can both alter \(Q\). The second mechanism must not be called axis reorientation unless the weight has a separately justified physical meaning and evolution law.

### Verdict

Do not hide weight dynamics inside the reorientation label.

---

## Experiment 449 — Migration classification for `axis reorganization`

The old vocabulary should migrate as follows:

- static `reorganization tendency` or candidate property: possible input only;
- actual change of realized axes: **dynamic event**;
- \(\dot P=[\Omega,P]\): **kinematic law**;
- \(\Omega\) or its equation: **constitutive dynamic bridge**;
- collective rank change: **derived geometric event**;
- support loss: **derived stability event**;
- stasis: **derived regime diagnostic**.

These layers must remain separate in the structural-gravity paper.
